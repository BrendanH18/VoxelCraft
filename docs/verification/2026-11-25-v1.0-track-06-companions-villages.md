## What changed

### Companions (MOB-03b)

- **Order and pose.** `animals::State` now separates Java's
  `orderedToSit` (`sitting`, saved as before) from `isInSittingPose`
  (`sit_pose`, not saved; a load sits the pet, as `readAdditionalSaveData`
  does). `SitWhenOrderedToGoal` sets the pose only on the ground and out
  of water and keeps it while the order stands. The model, the wolf armor
  repair (`isInSittingPose`), cats on chests and the LAN replica read the
  pose; following, breeding and the defend goals read the order.
- **Taming** (`tryToTame`): `nextInt(3)` for wolves and cats and
  `nextInt(10)` for parrots (was a float chance). Wolves and cats are
  ordered to sit; parrots no longer are. Only a wolf's health changes
  (`applyTamingSideEffects`, 40); a tamed cat or parrot kept to full
  health before. Taming stops the pet's navigation and target.
- **`mobInteract`** for tamed pets, rewritten from `Wolf`, `Cat` and
  `Parrot`:
  - a wolf heals by twice the food's nutrition (`feed(..., 2, 2)`), from
    anyone; a cat by its nutrition, from its owner only;
  - only the owner dyes a collar; the same colour does nothing for a wolf
    and orders a cat to sit or stand, as in Java;
  - otherwise `Animal.mobInteract` (love or growth), and if that passes the
    owner's order to sit toggles, which also stops the pet. A wolf already
    in love now toggles instead of ignoring the owner;
  - a wild wolf eats meat into love mode (it still can't breed untamed);
  - a parrot's order toggles only while it isn't flying (on the ground).
- **Hurt:** wolves and parrots stand up when hurt (`hurtServer`); cats
  don't (unchanged for wolves, new for parrots).
- **Breeding:** the wolf's extra full-health condition is gone
  (`Wolf.canMate` has none; feeding a hurt wolf still heals it first).
- **Following and teleporting:** `unableToMoveToOwner` now counts a lead
  even while its holder is missing (`mayBeLeashed`); the teleport's
  collision test moves the pet's box by the block offset
  (`getBoundingBox().move(...)`) and the teleport keeps the pet's motion
  (`snapTo`). `TamableAnimalPanicGoal` is new: a panicking wolf, cat or
  parrot more than 12 blocks from its owner teleports to it.

### Leads (MOB-03d)

[`src/entity/leash.rs`](../../src/entity/leash.rs) is rewritten from
`Leashable`, and boats are new in
[`src/entity/leash/boats.rs`](../../src/entity/leash/boats.rs):

- **Distances:** a lead snaps past 12 blocks between box centres
  (`LEASH_TOO_FAR_DIST`, was 10 from an approximate anchor), 16 for a
  happy ghast; the elastic reach is 6 less both widths (10 for a happy
  ghast).
- **Spring:** `checkElasticInteractions` exactly: attachment points
  (`(0, ½h, ½w)` turned by the mob's yaw, the holder's `(0, ½h, 0)`), the
  displacement past the slack, 0.3 of it when the leashee already moves
  that way, torque from the lever arm into the angular momentum (×10),
  `AXIS_SPECIFIC_ELASTICITY` (0.8, 0.2, 0.8), 0.11 of the relative
  velocity, and the yaw turned by the momentum with the ground, liquid or
  air friction. A happy ghast holding a horse, camel, sniffer or boat uses
  the four shared corners at a quarter each. `Target` gained the player's
  velocity for this.
- **Close range:** within the elastic reach a leashed mob that isn't
  panicking walks to 2 blocks short of the holder at `followLeashSpeed`
  (llamas 2); allays and happy ghasts don't.
- **Holders:** players, fence knots and, new, other mobs (`Leash::Mob`,
  saved as `{"mob": uid}`). A sneaking player using a grown, leashable mob
  ties every mob they lead within 12 blocks to it (`Entity.interact`).
- **Use rules** (`Entity.interact` before `mobInteract`): the player
  leading a mob unties it with any item or an empty hand (dropping the
  lead, except with infinite materials); a lead moves a mob tied to a
  fence or another mob to the player and drops the old lead; shears cut
  the mob's lead and every lead tied to it (and wear the shears).
- **Knots:** using a fence ties the player's mobs (and boats) within
  12 blocks to its knot (`bindPlayerMobs`, searching a 32-block cube);
  with nothing to tie, using a knot gives its mobs back to the player
  without dropping anything, unless the player sneaks (it used to drop
  every lead). A broken or sheared knot drops each lead at its mob.
- **Leashability:** Java's `canBeLeashed`: bees, squid, glow squid,
  skeleton horses, zombie horses (tame or not carrying a mob), camel
  husks (not carrying a mob), happy ghasts, nautiluses (not angry),
  zombie nautiluses and sulfur cubes (holding a block) are added; an angry
  wolf refuses.
- **Missing holders:** a lead whose holder is gone (dead, spectating, a
  removed fence or a dead holder mob) drops at once; one whose holder is
  missing (a player not in the game, a mob not loaded) drops after
  100 ticks (`restoreLeashFromSave`). Offline players' leads used to be
  kept forever.
- **Riding:** a mob a boat or minecart picks up drops its lead
  (`Mob.startRiding`).
- **Boats:** a lead ties a boat to the player or a fence, the player
  leading it unties it, the spring tows and turns it and it snaps past
  12 blocks; a destroyed boat drops its lead. The lead is saved after the
  riders on the boat's cart line (`p<id>`, `f<x>:<y>:<z>` or `m<uid>`).
- Persistence across chunk unload uses the existing entity storage, which
  saves the lead with the mob (`chunk_storage` tests).

### Farm animals (MOB-03a)

Checked against the jar data, both ways, by
`foods_temptations_and_drops_match_the_jar`: breeding foods of cows,
mooshrooms, pigs, sheep, chickens, rabbits, goats, wolves, cats and camels;
the temptations of cows, sheep, chickens, rabbits, goats, horses and
llamas; parrot and cat taming foods; the cookie; and the cow, pig, sheep,
chicken and rabbit drops. Fixed:

- rabbits always drop one raw rabbit (`entities/rabbit`'s `set_count` 1;
  was 0–1);
- horses, donkeys and mules also follow an enchanted golden apple
  (`horse_tempt_items`);
- a grazing sheep eats when 4 of the 40 animation ticks are left
  (`EatBlockGoal`), not at the end.

Confirmed unchanged: the grazing odds (1 in 1000 adult, 50 baby, per
goal update), the 6000–12000-tick egg timer, babies growing by a tenth per
feeding, the 6000-tick breeding cooldown and Sheep wool regrowth.

### Structure spawn overrides (MOB-01c)

- Swamp huts: the witch and cat overrides (`bounding_box: piece`) now use
  `SwampHutPiece`'s 7 × 7 × 9 box, from Java's y 0 (one below the floor)
  to the top of the roof. The old box ran from the foot of the stilts to
  two blocks over the roof, so witches spawned on the roof and under the
  hut.
- Outposts: generation 3 outposts already used the union of their jigsaw
  piece boxes, Java's `full` box. Ancient cities (`full`, every category
  empty) use the union of their pieces; trial chambers (`piece`, every
  category empty) their piece boxes; generation 5 monuments the
  building's box (v0.16 Track F); fortresses their pieces (v0.16
  Track E). The fixture records each structure's box kind and list.

### Raids (MOB-05d)

- **Witches heal raiders** (`NearestHealableRaiderTargetGoal` and
  `performRangedAttack`): on a target-selector update with a coin flip,
  a witch in an active raid picks the nearest raider other than a witch
  within its 16-block follow range that it can see, and waits 200 ticks
  (100 updates, counted down each tick) before picking again; meanwhile
  it can't target players. It walks until it sees the raider within
  10 blocks, then throws Healing at a raider with 4 health or less and
  Regeneration otherwise, aimed at the raider's next position, and drops
  the target.
- **Pre-raid bell** (`SetRaidStatus`, `getPreRaidPackage`, `RingBell`):
  one tick in 20 a villager near a raid takes the pre-raid activity before
  the first wave and between waves, or the raid activity otherwise. In
  the pre-raid activity it walks to its meeting point and, one tick in 20
  within 3 blocks of it, rings the bell there with itself as the source
  (`EntityEvent::RingBell`, which the game turns into
  `World::ring_bell_by`). The ring is the existing bell path: villagers
  hide, and raiders within 32 make it resonate and glow (v0.15 Track E).
- **Fireworks** (`CelebrateVillagersSurvivedRaid`): after a won raid a
  villager in the raid activity under open sky celebrates until the raid
  stops: the celebration sound one tick in 100 and, under open sky, one
  tick in 200 a rocket from its eyes with one burst star of a random dye
  colour and flight 0–2, which never hits the villager that launched it
  (`firework::Rocket.shooter`, as `canHitEntity` skips the owner). The
  sound used to play for every villager near a won raid, roofed or not.
- **Statistics:** players now keep Java's custom statistics
  (`session::raid::Stats`), saved as `stats` in the level and in agent
  profiles. A raid started from a player's omen awards `raid_trigger`; a
  hero of a won raid gets `raid_win`.
- The LAN raid bar is multiplayer and stays out of scope.

## Differences from Java and remaining gaps

- **Sounds:** `LEAD_TIED`, `LEAD_UNTIED`, `LEAD_BREAK`, `SHEARS_SNIP`
  for leads, the witch's throw and the parrot's eating sound aren't
  played; adding sound cues is an appended ordering no track reserved.
- **Knots** are still derived from the mobs tied to a fence rather than
  entities, so a broken fence drops its leads at once; Java's knot checks
  `survives()` every 100 ticks. A knot whose chunk isn't loaded holds its
  mobs instead of dropping them.
- **Boats:** the rope to a boat isn't drawn, and mobs can't be tied to a
  boat (`Entity.interact` allows any leashable holder).
- `Mob.leashTooFarBehaviour` also disables the MOVE goal flag until the
  next close-range tick; not modelled.
- `SitWhenOrderedToGoal.canUse`'s "owner attacked by a mob within
  12 blocks" condition and its sitting of unordered pets whose owner is
  away aren't modelled (no last-hurt-by-mob on `Target`).
- Open sky for the celebration uses the light-blocking surface, not
  `MOTION_BLOCKING`.
- The swamp hut's layout is still VoxelCraft's (a block taller than
  Java's); only its spawn box follows `SwampHutPiece`. Its witch and cat
  stay a block apart, not both at the piece's (2, 2, 5), because saved
  worlds mark a populated hut by that pair
  (`cat_spawner::legacy_hut_marker`). Its
  height comes from the ground at the centre, not the average over the
  footprint (`updateAverageGroundHeight`). Rewriting the layout is
  generation work outside this track.
- Statistics: only `raid_trigger` and `raid_win` are counted; there is
  no statistics screen.
- The headless simulation ignores `EntityEvent::RingBell` (as it ignores
  the other villager block events).
