## What is implemented

**Shulker boxes** (`world::shulker_box`, `content::shulker_box`). The plain
box and the sixteen dyed ones, six facings each; placed facing away from
the clicked face. The 27 slots are an ordinary chest-table entry, so the
desktop screen (titled "Shulker Box"), controller menus, the CLI's `chest
take/put`, LAN peers' screens, hoppers, droppers and comparators all work
on them; nothing that is a shulker box goes in (`ShulkerBoxSlot.mayPlace`
on clicks and shift-clicks, `canPlaceItemThroughFace` for hoppers, the CLI
refuses it) and bundles refuse boxes too. A box only opens when the half
block in front of its lid is free of collision (`canOpen`); its lid rises
half a block and turns 270° over ten ticks while anyone has it open, with
the open and close sounds on the first viewer and the last, and the
`container_close` game event. Removing a box never spills its slots: the
drop carries them as the item's `container` component (`Payload::Container`,
trailing empty slots trimmed as `fromItems` does), whether it was mined,
blown up (no explosion condition on its loot) or popped by a piston
(`PushReaction.POPPED`); a Creative player breaking a box that holds
something gets it too. Placing a box restores the contents. Hardness and
resistance 2, mined with a pickaxe without needing one, piglins guard them,
stacks of one. Dyeing (`<colour>_shulker_box.json` transmutes) and washing
in a water cauldron keep the contents. A dispenser places a box in front
(facing its own way, or up when something is under the spot) and keeps it
when it can't. The item tooltip lists the first five stacks as "name x
count" and "and N more...". Boxes are drawn as models with their lids
within 64 blocks (`ShulkerBoxRenderer`), never in chunk meshes; icons,
dropped items and break particles use the closed cube. LAN clients get
open and moving lids in the snapshot. Saves: contents in the chest table,
item contents in the stack string.

**Elytra** (`content::elytra`, `player::travel`). Item 1675: durability 432,
epic rarity, the chest slot (right-click, armor-slot clicks, shift-click and
the CLI's `place` equip it, with its own equip sound), repaired with
phantom membrane at the anvil, and in `#enchantable/durability` and
`#enchantable/equippable` (Unbreaking, Mending, the curses). A fresh jump
press in the air, not on a ladder or in a liquid, starts a glide when the
worn elytra has more than one durability left (`canGlideUsing`,
`Player.canGlide`: not creative-flying, riding or levitating). Each tick
runs Java's `updateFallFlyingMovement` exactly (lift by the pitch, the dive
conversion, the climb, steering toward the look and the 0.99/0.98 drag,
with Java's `Mth` sine table and float arithmetic), then the move; a wall
that stopped it hurts by ten times the horizontal speed lost less three
(`fly_into_wall`, past armor). The glide ends on the ground, on a ladder or
when the glider can't go on; the fall distance is capped at one block while
it isn't diving steeply, so ordinary landings don't hurt. Every twentieth
tick of flight uses one durability (Unbreaking's non-armor chance, none in
Creative); gliding stops at the last point, which keeps the elytra.
Gliding takes the 0.6-block pose with eyes at 0.4, no block speed factor.
The player model tips toward the look, rolls into turns, damps its limbs
and raises its head, and worn elytra draw their wings, which spread with
`ElytraAnimationState`. The local player hears the glide wind. All of this
is in the shared player tick, so the host, controller seats, CLI agents and
LAN peers (with `glide` in their state) behave alike.

**Fireworks** (`content::fireworks`, `entity::firework`, `particles::firework`).
Firework stars (1677) from gunpowder, dyes and at most one shape ingredient
(fire charge, gold nugget, feather, or any registered `#skulls` item), one
diamond (trail) and one glowstone dust (twinkle); fading with dyes; rockets
(1676) from paper, one to three gunpowder (the flight duration) and any
stars, three per craft, plus the plain `firework_rocket_simple`. Their
tooltips follow `Fireworks.addToTooltip` and the star's lines; a star's icon
takes the `Firework` tint. Using a rocket on a block launches it 0.15 off
the face; using one while gliding attaches it to the player, which pushes
the glide every tick toward 1.5 blocks a tick along the look
(`FireworkRocketEntity.tick`). A free rocket drifts, speeds up 1.15 times
horizontally and climbs 0.04 a tick, lives `10 * (1 + flight) + rand(6) +
rand(7)` ticks, bursts on a mob or player at once and on a block only with
stars. A burst with stars hurts its holder by `5 + 2 * stars` and every
other player and mob within five blocks it can see (the feet or the middle
of the body) by that times `sqrt((5 - d) / 5)`, as `fireworks` damage (an
explosion type, never scaled by difficulty) with the rocket's pull-toward
knockback. Bursts draw Java's starter: each star two ticks apart in its
shape (small and large balls, star, creeper face, burst), sparks in the
star's colours that fade to its fade colours, leave trails and twinkle,
and a flash; the launch, blast (large for three stars or a large ball),
far variants past 16 blocks and the delayed twinkle play. Dispensers shoot
rockets (`asProjectile`: just outside the face, power 0.5, uncertainty 1,
the shoot sound). Crossbows load a rocket held in the off-hand
(`ARROW_OR_FIREWORK` for held items) and fire it shot-at-angle at 1.6, one
per Multishot direction, three uses of wear each; rockets don't pierce.
LAN clients see rockets, bursts and sounds.

## Differences from Java

- The shulker box recipe isn't registered on this branch: the shulker shell
  is Track A's. `crafting::flight` adds it as soon as `shulker_shell`
  resolves, and `tests/java_flight.rs` checks it then. The integrator only
  needs to merge both tracks.
- The creeper star shape takes any registered `#skulls` item; until Track C
  registers the heads only the wither skeleton skull counts.
- Shulker boxes keep a full-cube collision and outline while open (Java's
  shape grows with the lid) and don't push entities as the lid rises
  (`moveCollidedEntities`); `canOpen` checks blocks, not entities in front
  of the lid. Custom names, locks and loot-table contents aren't carried
  between box and item. The model is an original stand-in for Java's
  textured lid and base, drawn only within 64 blocks as Java's is.
- The tooltip uses the older `%s x %s` wording: 26.3 names the line
  `item.container.item_count`, which `en_us.json` doesn't translate.
- Rockets aren't saved (each lives under four seconds, like evoker fangs);
  held rockets don't hit entities on their own, and a crossbow or
  dispenser rocket's entity hits use its box against mob and player boxes
  rather than `ProjectileUtil`'s inflated sweep.
- Firework sparks use the crit sprite and the flash an existing sprite,
  in Java's colours, lifetimes and motion; sparks stop on the ground as
  other particles here do. Trail copies keep their parent's lifetime.
- The glide wind is played as overlapping stretches at Java's volume and
  pitch, not one tickable loop, and as a positioned sound LAN peers can
  also hear. The elytra has no separate broken icon and the crossbow no
  rocket-loaded icon; worn wings use an original texture.
- Dispensers don't equip elytra onto players, and gliding doesn't award
  statistics or advancements.
