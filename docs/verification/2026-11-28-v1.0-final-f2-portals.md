`t3/v1.0-final-f2-portals`, from `2b6bf95` (the integration branch

PLY-01b (entity storage, here across dimensions). No IDs were used.

## Scope

1. Mobs, dropped items, experience orbs, projectiles, minecarts and boats
   travelling through Nether portals, End portals and End gateways as
   Java's `Entity.setAsInsidePortal`, `handlePortal` and
   `TeleportTransition` do, with their portal cooldowns and Java's
   `canUsePortal` exceptions. An entity sent to a dimension that isn't
   loaded waits for that dimension and arrives there when it next loads.
2. A player riding a cart, boat or mount travelling with it through
   portals, as Java's passengers do, instead of being refused.

Both are done; what remains is [listed below](#remaining-differences),
including what the engine's single loaded dimension means for each case.

## What changed

### Entities through portals (item 1)

[`entity::portal_travel`](../../src/entity/portal_travel.rs) gives mobs,
dropped items, experience orbs, arrows, tridents, thrown items (snowballs,
eggs and the like), thrown potions, minecarts and boats a portal cooldown
(`portal_cooldown`, Java's `portalCooldown`) and runs their portal step
once a tick, before they move ([`Game::entity_portals`](../../src/app/portal_travel.rs),
called from `update_mobs` on the host). The step is Java's
`entityInside` of the entity's last move followed by the next tick's
`handlePortal`: the portal block the entity's box is in (a Nether portal
or End gateway anywhere in its cell, an End portal in its slab from 6 to
12 sixteenths up) marks it, or, on cooldown, restarts the cooldown; the
cooldown counts down; a marked entity that can use portals goes at once
(a transition time of 0) with its cooldown set to 300 ticks. An End
gateway that isn't cooling down starts its cooldown as it lets an entity
in, as for the player. Entities that can't use portals: a passenger, the
Wither (the Ender Dragon isn't a mob here and never takes the step), a
heart-bound creaking, a sleeping villager, a mob a player rides or a
parrot on a shoulder, a cart or boat a player rides (the host's is the
host's trip, below), and cushions. A leashed mob goes and its leash comes
off without a lead (`removeLeash`).

An entity that goes leaves the level with the mobs riding it, as a batch
of the level file's entity keys, the form a region record's entity batch
holds. Then:

- **Nether portal**: the trip is the host's (`nether_trip`, shared with
  the player's own): the scaled target at the entity's height inside the
  world border, the portal's axis, and the entity's place in the entry
  portal (`getRelativePortalPosition` with its own size). With the
  `allow_entering_nether_using_portals` rule off a trip into the Nether is
  refused, the entity stays on cooldown, and the trip owes the Nether its
  exit portal as a refused player trip does (Track 8c's `OwedPortal`).
- **End portal**: into the End from the Overworld or the Nether, with its
  motion turned to face west (`end_arrival_motion`); out of the End to the
  world spawn's top.
- **End gateway**: in the End, to the gateway's exit at once (the linked
  exit, a generation 7 return gateway's exact exit, or a first exit found
  now, built with its island and linked both ways), keeping the motion,
  with the arrival spot the player's (`gateway_arrival`).

A traveller bound for another dimension waits in the root level file
(`portal_arrivals`, [saves](../saves.md#region-files)) until that dimension
next loads. Arriving there, before the host and behind the arrival screen
(after any owed portals), each one, oldest first, waits for the ground
around its arrival to load and then makes the arrival Java made at the
time of the trip: for a Nether portal, `PortalForcer`'s search (16 blocks
into the Nether, 128 out), else `createPortal` along the entry's axis,
then the same place in the exit portal, free of collisions, turned a
quarter if the axes differ (`exit_position`, `collision_free_position`,
`quarter_turn_motion`); into the End, the obsidian platform rebuilt
(`createEndPlatform`, now one helper the player's arrival shares) and the
bottom centre of `END_SPAWN_POINT`, a block above a player's arrival, so
it drops onto the platform; out of the End, the world spawn's column one
above its highest motion-blocking block or fluid, leaves aside
(`adjustSpawnLocation`). The entity joins the level there, with the
destination's next mob uids, cart ids and merchant ids (passengers riding
their vehicle again), its cooldown at 300 so it doesn't step straight
back, and goes into its chunk's region record when that chunk unloads
like any other entity. Travellers waiting for the dimension a world opens
in arrive as their ground loads.

### Riding through portals (item 2)

A player riding a cart, boat or mount no other player rides now travels
with it ([`dimension.rs`](../../src/app/dimension.rs)). Java's vehicle is
the entity in the portal and its passengers go with it, so: the vehicle's
box marks the host's portal count (`update_vehicle_portal`, before
`process_portal`, as the next tick's `handlePortal`), which stands in for
the vehicle's (the cooldown is the player's 10 ticks, Java's
`getDimensionChangingDelay` with a player aboard); a ridden vehicle goes
through a Nether portal at once (a vehicle isn't a `Player`, so the
transition time is 0); the trip is the vehicle's (its position, size and
motion in the portal it was marked in). The vehicle leaves the level with
any mobs riding it, waits with the host while the destination loads (and
is saved with the travellers if the game saves then), and arrives where
the trip puts it, with the host aboard again. Into the End the vehicle
comes out a block above the platform, as any non-player entity does; out
of the End a ridden vehicle goes only once the host has seen the credits
(`canTeleport`) and goes to the world spawn's top (`Arrival::SpawnTop`)
rather than the host's bed; through a gateway it goes with the host to
the exit. A save that fails at the departure puts the vehicle back.

## Remaining differences

**The other dimension doesn't run while the host is away** (an engine
difference: VoxelCraft loads only the host's dimension). What that means
for each case:

- A traveller waits, unchanged and out of every world, from the moment it
  leaves until the host next opens its dimension; Java's arrives at once
  and lives on there (its arrival keeps the exit portal's chunks loaded
  for 300 ticks, `PLACE_PORTAL_TICKET`, and longer if anything else
  does). So a mob doesn't walk away from the exit portal, an item's
  5-minute despawn clock and pickup delay don't run, an arrow doesn't
  land, a lit TNT cart's fuse doesn't burn, a thrown potion doesn't
  break and a boat doesn't drift until the host arrives; then each one
  starts from its arrival as Java's did at the time of the trip.
- The arrival itself is Java's: the destination hasn't changed since the
  trip (nothing there runs), so the same portal search over the same
  blocks finds, or `createPortal` builds, the same exit portal, and the
  End platform and world spawn are as they were. A destination never
  generated generates the same terrain whenever it is first loaded.
- An entity that goes through a portal and straight back can't: on
  arrival it waits out its cooldown in the exit portal, as in Java, but
  only once the host is there.
- Travellers to a dimension arrive, oldest first, before the host does;
  the host waits while each arrival's ground loads (behind the arrival
  screen), so many travellers to far-apart portals make the host's
  arrival slower. At most 4,096 wait; past that an entity in a portal
  stays where it is.
- LAN guests follow the host as before, so nobody is in another dimension
  to see a traveller arrive there.

Also:

- Ender pearls (bound to their owners since Track 8f; Java's travel and
  then teleport their owner across), fireballs, wither skulls, shulker
  bullets, wind charges, firework rockets, eyes of ender, primed TNT,
  falling blocks, area effect clouds, armour stands, item frames,
  paintings and evoker fangs don't use portals here; most of them rarely
  meet one. Fishing hooks can't in Java either.
- A portal cooldown isn't saved (Java's `PortalCooldown`): an entity that
  is saved and reloaded within 15 seconds of arriving in a portal can go
  straight back.
- A villager arriving in another dimension forgets its bed, job site and
  their claims; Java keeps the memories (positions in the old dimension)
  and the old dimension's POI tickets, which only its death releases.
- A vehicle another player rides (a LAN guest or a controller seat)
  doesn't use portals; Java's takes them along.
- Through a Nether portal lit in the End an entity stays, as the host
  does here; Java's go to the Nether.
- An entity entering an End gateway whose exit's ground isn't loaded
  stays where it is (its gateway cooldown spent); Java loads the exit
  synchronously and sends it.
- Dropped items have no yaw here, so their motion entering the End turns
  as for a yaw of 0; Java turns it by each item's own random yaw.
- Entities in loaded chunks beyond the simulation distance take the
  portal step too; Java's step runs only in entity-ticking chunks.

## What remains unverified

Everything in play: no one has sent a mob, item or cart through a portal,
or ridden one through, in the running game. The arrival paths
(`resolve_portal_arrivals`, the host's vehicle in `update_arrival`) have
no automated test that drives a whole `Game`; their parts (the trip, the
batch, the arrival's ids and riders, the saved key) do.
