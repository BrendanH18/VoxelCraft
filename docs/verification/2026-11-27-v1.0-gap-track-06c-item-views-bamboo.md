## What changed

### 1. Every item's display transforms (BLD-05c)

[`content::item_display`](../../src/content/item_display.rs) holds Java
26.3's display transforms for every item: the 42 distinct sets the
client's items resolve to (`block/block`, `block/stairs`,
`fence_inventory`/`wall_inventory`, `template_fence_gate`, `thin_block`,
the shelves, chests and shulker boxes, `template_skull`, beds, banners,
the shield, `item/generated`, `item/handheld`, the rods, bow, crossbow,
brush, mace, goat horn, music discs, amethyst buds, pointed dripstone and
the single-item models), each with its `gui`, `ground`, `fixed`,
`firstperson_righthand`, `thirdperson_righthand` and `on_shelf` transforms
and its `gui_light`. Java merges `display` per context down a model's
parent chain; [`tools/extract_java_item_model_fixtures.py`](../../tools/extract_java_item_model_fixtures.py)
now records each item's merged set in
[`item_models.json`](../../tests/fixtures/java/26.3/item_models.json)
(`displays`, and a `display` index per item), and
`tests/java_item_models.rs::every_item_has_javas_display_transforms`
checks the game's choice against it for all 1,634 items the game
resolves, in all six contexts, with no exceptions.

### 2. Slot icons in Java's orientation (BLD-05c, owner: match Java)

`GuiItemAtlas.drawToSlot` places an item at the slot's centre, scaled by
(16, −16, 16) and flipped back when the atlas is sampled, so model +x is
screen right, +y up and +z toward the viewer, with no mirror. With
`block/block`'s GUI turn (30, 225, 0) the **east face shows on the left
and the north face on the right**; the game had drawn a hand-made
isometric cube with north on the left, a mirror image of Java's. Every 3D
slot icon is now drawn by [`Ui::gui_model`](../../src/render/ui.rs): the
item's model quads through its own GUI transform (so stairs, fences and
walls turn 135°, chests, gates, heads, pots and the conduit 45°, beds
340°, the shield its own), far faces first, each face lit as Java's GUI
lights it (`Lighting.Entry.ITEMS_3D` for block models, `ITEMS_FLAT` for
`gui_light: front`): `minecraft_mix_light`'s 0.4 ambient plus 0.6 of the
two turned diffuse lights. For a block that is **1.0 on top, 0.65 on the
left and 0.40 on the right** (the game had 1.0, 0.8 and 0.62). Faces carry
Java's default UVs, each texture upright and unmirrored as seen from
outside. The true projection makes a cube 14.1 pixels wide and 15.7 tall
in a 16-pixel slot (the game's cube was a 14 × 14 2:1 diamond).

This one path draws every slot: inventories, the hotbar, the creative
palette, the recipe book and its toast, every container and workstation,
the controller menus, tooltips and the bundle tooltip (all go through
`hud::draw_model_sized`). A flat item's GUI transform is applied too (the
amethyst buds sit two pixels up, `item/amethyst_bud`'s translation).

To make Java's transforms meaningful the models sit as Java's item models
do: an orientable block's front faces north (it shows on the right now);
chests face south (on the right); a head's face moved from −Z to +Z (the
skull item's `transformation` turns the model half about x, so its face
is south, on the right) and its cube is Java's 8 × 8 × 8 skull box in the
slot; fence gates face south; the lectern, big dripleaf, dried ghast and
calibrated sculk sensor north (their blockstates' unrotated facing);
stairs stay east (`block/stairs`); fences and walls now run north to south
as `fence_inventory` and `wall_inventory` do. Copper golem statues are
entity models in Java, upside down until each transform's half turn about
z; the game's upright statue boxes take the same transforms without that
half turn (`item_display::drawn`), which places them identically.

**Glint on 3D items in slots**: an enchanted (or otherwise foil) 3D item
draws each face again as the glint's texture-shaped sheen with the band
sweeping along the face's texture, as Track 6's flat-icon glint does.

### 3. Items in third-person and mob hands, and on shelves (BLD-05c)

A player seen from outside (in third person, split screen, LAN and CLI
players) and the humanoid mobs (zombies, husks, drowned, skeletons,
strays, bogged, illagers, vexes, piglins) now hold the item's own model,
placed by Java's chain: `ItemInHandLayer`'s `translateToHand` on the arm,
−90° about x, 180° about y and (1, 2, −10) sixteenths ((0, 1, −4.5) on a
baby model, `useBabyOffset`), then the item's `thirdperson_righthand`
transform. The game's model frame is Java's turned half about x, which
the chain converts (`player_model::third_person_hand`). Block items show
their boxes (`block/block`: 75°, 45°, 0.375, raised 2.5/16), the 3D meshes
(beds, banners with their patterns, the shield, small dripleaf, spore
blossom) their models, and flat items their extruded sprite with
`item/generated`'s, `item/handheld`'s or their own transform (the rods,
bow, crossbow, brush, mace, goat horn). They were a small cube or a
1-pixel-thick box. The entity mesh now hands these to the renderer
(`EntityMesh::push_held`, `HeldItemModel`), which draws them in the item
model pass, so they **glint** when enchanted and **translucent blocks
blend**, as dropped and framed items do.

**Shelves** draw their items by `ShelfRenderer`'s chain: the cell's
centre, turned to the shelf's facing (`-toYRot`), the slot's 5/16 offset
and a quarter back, a quarter size, then the item's **`on_shelf`**
transform, raised so the middle of its model's bounds sits there
(`getModelBoundingBox`; `align_items_to_bottom` is only read from saved
data in Java, so items are always centred). They used the ground
transform.

Item frames turn fences, walls and anvils a quarter, their `fixed`
transforms' 90° about y, so the north-to-south fence lies across the
frame as Java's does.

### 4. Bamboo and Java's model offsets (UX-01, owner: extend the terrain format)

**Model offsets.** Java 26.3 gives 39 blocks an `offsetType`
(`BlockBehaviour.Properties.offsetType`): flowers, the tall and short
grasses and ferns, dry grass, tall seagrass, the tall flowers, pitcher
plant, bamboo and its sapling, mangrove propagules, roots and sprouts,
hanging roots, eyeblossoms, pointed dripstone, sulfur spikes and the
small dripleaf. Their model moves within the cell by an amount seeded from
the column (`Mth.getSeed(x, 0, z)`): x and z in sixteen steps of 1/30
between ±0.25, clamped to ±0.125 for the speleothems
(`SpeleothemBlock.MAX_HORIZONTAL_OFFSET`); the `XYZ` blocks (short grass,
fern, short and tall dry grass, small dripleaf) also drop by up to 0.2
(0.1 for the dripleaf). [`world::offset`](../../src/world/offset.rs) is
Java's function. The game drew them all centred.

The terrain shader now computes the offset itself
(`chunk.wgsl`'s `block_offset`): each chunk draw's instance carries its
world origin (x, z) beside its camera-relative origin, and since only bits
16–27 of `getSeed`'s 64-bit result are read and the low bits of a product
depend only on the low bits of its factors, 32-bit arithmetic gives Java's
seed exactly (`render::shader_equivalence::the_shaders_model_offset_is_javas`
checks it against `world::offset` over millions of columns). An upright
cross quad names its offset kind in word 0 bits 24–26 (unused before), so
**no quad grew and none was added**.

**Bamboo.** Java's bamboo has `age` 0 or 1: the stalk is 2 texels across
(7 to 9, `bamboo*_age0`) or 3 (6.5 to 9.5, `bamboo*_age1`), and the leaf
planes run from 0.8 to 15.2 through the middle of the cell
(`bamboo_small_leaves`, `bamboo_large_leaves`: no ambient occlusion,
shaded as a face up, the whole texture over the plane). The game had one
4-texel post and planes from 1 to 15. Bamboo is now drawn with **fine
quads** (module docs of [`mesh`](../../src/mesh.rs)): a detail quad with a
cross's face code (a combination no quad used), holding its real face,
a 4-bit plane code and 3-bit bound codes for the off-grid coordinates
(0, 0.8, 6.5, 7, 9, 9.5, 15.2, 16 and 8 sixteenths), a leaf-plane flag and
the stalk's own ambient occlusion and corner light; the shader moves it
by bamboo's offset. A fine quad is still one 12-byte record.

The game had no `age`. States **14038–14040** (Track 6's reservation) are
bamboo `age=0` with no, small and large leaves; the existing states
2022–2024 are `age=1`, which is what Java's `BambooFeature` generates, so
world generation, the golden chunk hashes and saves are unchanged (a
save's bamboo all reads as thick, as generated bamboo is). The thin states
answer every rule as the thick ones (block aliases: drops, mining,
survival, fire, names). Growth follows Java's `growBamboo`: a shoot puts a
thin stalk with small leaves above itself and becomes a bare thin stalk
(its `updateShape`); a stalk puts one with small leaves above a bare
stalk, or with large leaves above a leafy one, which then has small leaves
and the one under it none; the new stalk is thick above a thick stalk or
once two stalks are below the top, so a grown shoot's lowest three stay
thin. Placing bamboo follows `BambooStalkBlock.getStateForPlacement`: on
the ground it is planted as a shoot (it placed a bare stalk), on a shoot a
bare thin stalk, on a stalk a bare stalk of its thickness, under a stalk
one of that stalk's.

**Outlines and collision.** As Java's `getShape` and `getCollisionShape`
do, the outlines of the flowers (with the torchflower, wither rose and
eyeblossoms), mangrove propagules, the bamboo sapling and stalk and the
speleothems move by their offset (the crosshair and selection box), and
bamboo's and the speleothems' collision too. Bamboo outlines as Java's
6-wide column (10-wide with large leaves) and collides as its 3-wide
column (6.5 to 9.5); it outlined and collided as the 4-wide post. Grass,
ferns, tall plants, roots, sprouts, hanging roots and the small dripleaf
keep a centred outline, as in Java.

### 5. Leftovers (BLD-05b)

- **A bundle in a workstation's input slot** is now pickable with the
  right stick on controller seats: the furnace's input and fuel, the
  brewing stand's bottles, ingredient and fuel, the enchanting table's,
  anvil's and smithing table's inputs and the v0.15 workstations' slots
  (`pad_slot_stack` reads them as the controller screen draws them).
- **F3+H's feedback is a chat line**, as Java's `debugFeedbackTranslated`
  puts it in chat (`Minecraft.showDebugChat`): "[Debug]: Advanced
  tooltips: shown" or "hidden". The game has chat (the console's lines,
  shown over the hotbar in a LAN game); a single-player game now shows its
  recent chat lines for Java's 10 seconds (`ChatComponent`'s 200 ticks)
  after a line arrives, so the feedback is seen there too.
- **F3 and the debug modifier are separate bindings**, as Java's
  `key.debug.overlay` (order −2) and `key.debug.modifier` (order −1) are,
  both F3 by default, listed under Debug as "Toggle Overlay" and "Debug
  Modifier Key" (saved `key_key.debug.modifier`). Bound to one key, the
  overlay toggles when it is let go unless a debug key was used meanwhile
  (`usedDebugKeyAsModifier`); bound apart, the overlay key toggles it when
  pressed and H takes the modifier, as `KeyboardHandler.keyPress` does.

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens (and
every other slot: hotbar, creative palette, recipe book, workstations,
controller menus, tooltips, the bundle tooltip):

- **Every 3D icon is turned as Java turns it**: a block's east face on
  the left and north face on the right, the mirror image of before, so a
  furnace's, pumpkin's, dispenser's or hive's front is now on the right;
  chests show their latch on the right, heads their face on the right,
  stairs their profile on the left and their steps on the right, fences
  and walls their length on the right; gates, pots, the conduit, statues,
  beds, banners and the shield their own Java turns.
- **Icons are Java's true projection**: a cube is a little taller (15.7
  pixels in a 16-pixel slot, from 14), and side faces are **darker**: the
  left at 0.65 of the top and the right at 0.40 (from 0.8 and 0.62), as
  Java lights them. Item meshes (beds, banners, shield, plants) take the
  same lighting.
- **Heads** are their 8 × 8 × 8 skull at Java's GUI size and lift, not a
  cube filling the slot.
- **Enchanted 3D items glint** in slots.
- The amethyst buds' icons sit two pixels higher.
- Controller players can pick a bundle's stacks in workstation input
  slots; F3+H's message appears in the chat area; Controls lists "Debug
  Modifier Key" under Debug.

## Measurements

The headless meshing benchmark (`voxelcraft --bench --rd 4`, CI's step)
on Boat, the base and this branch's binaries run alternately six times in
one sandbox session (`ci` profile, single thread, `snapshot+mesh`):

| Seed | Quads (before = after) | GPU bytes/chunk (before = after) | ms/chunk before: median (min–max) | after: median (min–max) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 (123 dense chunks) | 359,070 | 35,031 | 0.901 (0.841–1.040) | 0.943 (0.902–1.719) |
| 12345 (120 dense chunks) | 432,253 | 43,225 | 0.956 (0.916–1.048) | 1.069 (0.937–1.158) |

**Mesh memory is unchanged**: the same quads, 12 bytes each, in every
chunk (offset kinds use spare bits, bamboo's fine quads replace its detail
quads one for one). The meshing cost moved by +5% and +12% in the medians,
inside these runs' spread (the base itself ranged 0.84 to 1.05 ms/chunk);
the new per-cell work is one table lookup per cross cell and one match per
shaped cell. The shader's offset is a few integer operations per plant
vertex. Earlier separate runs (`target/bench-before.log`,
`target/bench-after.log`) agree within the same spread. Chunk instances
grew from 12 to 20 bytes (per visible chunk, per frame). Generation is
untouched; no frame-time run (none is required).

## Remaining differences

- **Terrain textures on north and east faces** are mirrored from Java's
  UVs in the world mesh (its planar UVs run the same way on opposite
  faces; Java's run unmirrored as seen from outside on every face). Slot
  icons use Java's UVs; held, dropped and framed blocks keep the world's
  mapping. Pre-existing, outside this track.
- **First-person, dropped and framed block items** still use Track 6's
  scales rather than their whole display transforms (an orientable block's
  first-person 135°, chests' 315°, chests' and heads' framed 180° are not
  applied; fences, walls and anvils do turn in frames). Their positions
  are Java's for `block/block`.
- **The allay and the copper golem** hold their items with their own
  grips as before (small boxes in the entity pipeline, no glint or
  translucency): their models are Track 7c's. The held-item outline under
  Glowing no longer includes a held item drawn by the item pass.
- The trident and spyglass in a hand use their GUI model's transforms;
  Java switches them to their in-hand models.
- Fences' item geometry is the game's post with bars, not Java's
  two-post `fence_inventory` (its bars reach 2/16 beyond the block, which
  the game's boxes can't hold); only its direction changed.
- Bamboo's random model choice (four stalk models per age with different
  UV columns of Java's texture) is not drawn: the game's original stalk
  art keeps its planar UVs. Bamboo's `stage` (when a stalk stops growing)
  is still the game's height roll. Pointed dripstone drips from the
  centre of the cell, not its offset tip.
- LAN players seen from outside hold their item as a plain stack (the
  host doesn't receive their stack's components), so no glint on theirs.
- Debug chat lines are plain white text (Java's "[Debug]:" is yellow and
  bold); a LAN game's chat still shows its last four lines without
  fading.
- The glint is Track 6's original pattern, not Java's texture.
