## What is implemented

**Shipwrecks** (`world::shipwreck`, generation 5 only). The `shipwrecks`
set (linear spread 24/4, salt 165745295) holds `shipwreck` and
`shipwreck_beached`; the start chunk draws one from its
`setLargeFeatureSeed` random and tries the other if it fails, as
`ChunkGenerator.createStructures` does, so an ocean start (`#is_ocean`) is
an ocean wreck and a beach start (`#is_beach`) a beached one, with the
biome checked at the chunk centre on the `OCEAN_FLOOR_WG` (or
`WORLD_SURFACE_WG`) height. From the start's random: a rotation, then a
template from Java's list (20 ocean, 11 beached, in Java's order and so
Java's weights), placed at the start chunk's corner at y 90 and turned
about the pivot (4, 0, 15). The height is `postProcess`'s: the mean
`OCEAN_FLOOR_WG` height over the template's unturned footprint for an
ocean wreck, the lowest `WORLD_SURFACE_WG` height less half the
template's height less `nextInt(3)` for a beached one. The palette is
`RandomSource.create(Mth.getSeed(position)).nextInt(8)` at the adjusted
position, each palette swapping the hull's and trim's woods as Java's do
(oak/spruce, jungle/spruce, dark oak/jungle, dark oak/spruce,
spruce/oak, spruce/jungle, spruce/dark oak, oak/birch). Template air and
markers aren't placed, so the sea stays in the hull, and waterloggable
blocks placed in water are waterlogged. The `supply_chest`, `map_chest`
and `treasure_chest` markers make chests one block below with
`chests/shipwreck_supply`, `shipwreck_map` and `shipwreck_treasure`.

The 20 templates are original (FE-02, `shipwreck::templates`): one ship of
VoxelCraft's own design in Java's frame (keel and bilge, a two-high hold
under a trim deck with hatches over its chests, rails, a stern cabin a
step down with a roof walk and windows; the masted ship adds a mast, two
yards and a crow's nest), laid as each Java entry lies: right side up,
turned over (upside down), or rolled onto its side; whole, or broken to
its front or back part with a ragged break; degraded ones missing about
one block in seven. Each has the size of Java's template of that name and
holds its chests at Java's marker cells with Java's facings, so loot sits
where Java's does.

**Ocean monuments** (`world::monument`, generation 5 only). A port of
`OceanMonumentStructure` and `OceanMonumentPieces`: the biome checks
(every biome on the quart grid within 29 blocks of the start chunk's
block (9, 9) at sea level in `#required_ocean_monument_surrounding`, then
the centre on the ocean floor in `#has_structure/ocean_monument`); the
58 × 23 × 58 building at y 39, 29 blocks west and north of the start
chunk, facing a random horizontal direction; the 5 × 5 × 3 room graph with
the entry, the core room's eight cells claimed, two closed openings per
room while the source stays reachable (`findSource`), and the room
fitters in Java's order (double XY, double YZ, double Z, double X, double
Y, simple top, simple); the wings with their designs from one
`nextInt()`, and the penthouse. All of it is drawn from the start's
`setLargeFeatureSeed` random, and four starts are pinned against Java's
own constructor.

`postProcess` runs once per 16 × 16 Java chunk the building's box crosses,
clipped to that chunk, with the chunk's own decoration random
(`setDecorationSeed`, `setFeatureSeed` with index 24 of
`surface_structures`, step 4; the Xoroshiro draws are pinned against
Java's): the walls, wings, arches, roof, entrance wall and pillars, the
water skirt sloping out five blocks, and every room's walls, doorways,
lamps, floors and ceilings (`generateBoxOnFillOnly`). As in Java, each part
only draws in chunks that cross its gate box, the simple rooms' centre
pillars and the top rooms' wet sponges come from the chunk's random, the
legs fill down through water to the sea floor, and the water boxes keep
ice and turn cells at or above sea level to air. The core room holds its
eight gold blocks in dark prismarine; prismarine, prismarine bricks, dark
prismarine, sea lanterns and wet sponges are Java's.

Residents: the three elder guardians (one per wing room, one in the
penthouse) spawn at Java's cells once their chunks load. The spawn
overrides use the building's box (Java's `full` box): monsters there are
guardians, and no axolotls or underground water creatures spawn.

**Elder guardians.** Their Mining Fatigue III (6000 ticks, every 1200, to
survival players within 50) now skips players who already have it at III
with more than 1199 ticks left (`addEffectToPlayersAround`'s display
limit); the rest was already in place.

**The conduit** (`world::conduit`, `app::conduit`,
`entity::conduit_hunt`, `entity::model::conduit`). Crafted from eight
nautilus shells round a heart of the sea; shells come from fishing,
drowned and the wandering trader and the heart of the sea from buried
treasure, all of which already existed. The block is the 6 × 6 × 6 shell
(`Block.cube(6)`), strength 3, light 15, the `HAT` note block instrument,
mined faster with a pickaxe but dropping itself with any tool or none and
in blasts, unmovable by pistons (a block entity), uncommon (yellow name),
and waterlogged when placed in a water source. It is in the creative
palette.

Each conduit is a block entity kept by position and saved in the
dimension's `conduits` property. Every 40 game ticks (`updateShape`) it
checks that the 3 × 3 × 3 around it is all water (water, flowing water or
a block holding it) and counts the prismarine, prismarine bricks, dark
prismarine and sea lanterns on its 42 frame cells: 16 or more make it
active, 42 make it hunt. While active, the same pulse gives Conduit Power
(260 ticks, ambient, with particles) to every player in water or rain
whose block is closer than `frame / 7 × 16` blocks (32 for 16 blocks to 96
for 42), and a hunting conduit keeps its target while it is alive and its
block is closer than 8, or with none picks a random hostile mob (Java's
`Enemy`s) in water or rain in the cell grown by 8, and hurts it for 4
(magic). It plays its activation and deactivation sounds when that
changes, an ambient sound every 80 ticks and a short one every 60–99
ticks, and the strike's sound at the target. Its cage turns each tick at
Java's rate; motes fly in from each frame block one tick in 50 and leave
the target's eyes every tick.

**Conduit Power** now acts: it is a water-breathing effect for the air
supply (`MobEffectUtil.hasWaterBreathing`), it speeds mining like Haste
(`getDigSpeedAmplification`: the larger of the two amplifiers, 1 + 0.2 ×
(amplifier + 1)), and with the eyes in water it lights the view like
Night Vision.

Input paths: placing, breaking and crafting the conduit are the ordinary
block, mining and crafting paths on desktop, controller, LAN and the CLI
agent; LAN clients animate conduits from the replicated blocks.

## Differences from Java

- Shipwreck templates are original (FE-02): stairs and slabs on a wreck
  lying on its side become planks, and there are no doors, trapdoors or
  logs beyond the keel, stem and mast. Sizes, markers and chest facings are
  Java's.
- Java's first chunk to decorate a beached wreck draws its `nextInt(3)`;
  the start chunk's decoration random always does here. The structure's
  `couldValidBiomeExistOnTopOfChunkCenter` reads the column's surface
  biome.
- The monument reads the base terrain (column heights, the exact column
  where the sea can freeze, `solid_below` for the legs), not the
  fully generated chunk: Java's `postProcess` sees carvers and lakes
  already placed. VoxelCraft paints structures after its vegetation, so
  the water boxes clear seagrass and kelp that Java would grow afterwards.
- Elder guardians don't show the ghostly curse overlay or play its sound
  (`GUARDIAN_ELDER_EFFECT`), and pulse on their own one-minute timer rather
  than `(tickCount + id) % 1200`.
- A conduit's target isn't saved (mob uids aren't persistent); a reloaded
  conduit picks again at its next pulse. LAN clients draw the frame's motes
  but not the target's (they don't know it).
- The conduit's model is original: the shell block stays drawn inside the
  turning cage while active (Java's renderer swaps it for the eye and
  wind), the eye is a camera-facing disc of boxes, and there is no wind.
  Sounds are original synths.
- Conduit Power's underwater vision is full at once; Java ramps it in over
  the first 30 seconds under water (`LocalPlayer.getWaterVision`, client
  code, not in the server jar).
