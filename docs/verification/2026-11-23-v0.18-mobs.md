## What was built

- **Ground shadows** (`src/entity/shadow.rs`) for mobs, players, dropped
  items and experience orbs, following `EntityRenderer.extractShadow` and
  `ShadowFeatureRenderer`: per-renderer radius and strength, the scan of
  every block column under the radius down to `min(power / 0.5 − 1,
  radius)` below the feet, one quad per block top that is drawn
  (`RenderShape` not invisible), has a full-block collision shape and is
  lit above 3, alpha `(power − 0.5 × depth) × 0.5 × brightness`, power
  `(1 − distance² / 256) × strength`, brightness `Lightmap.getBrightness`,
  disc coordinates `−x / 2 / radius + 0.5`, and Java's view-offset scale
  against z-fighting. Invisible mobs (the Invisibility effect, invisible
  wandering traders) and invisible or spectating players cast none; the
  local player casts one in third person only, as its body is drawn only
  then. Split-screen views and LAN replicas get them through the same call.
- **Entity Shadows** (`entityShadows`, label "Entity Shadows", default on),
  saved in `options.txt` and placed after Particles in Video Settings'
  Quality & Performance list, as Java places it after Particles and Mipmap
  Levels.
- **Mob art.** Ten original tileable 16 × 16 grey patterns in the reserved
  texture layers 1280–1289 (wool, fur, hide, feather, mottle, bone, speckle,
  bristle, cloth, rot; `src/render/mob_textures.rs`), sampled one texel per
  model pixel by patterned boxes (entity shader material 6). The pattern
  number travels in a spare `BoxInstance` byte, so patterned boxes are
  still GPU instances. New faces and markings, all original cuboids:
  - pig: hide, darker cloven hooves, a mouth and blush under the eyes;
  - cow: fur on the hide and its patches, three more patches, an udder,
    hooves, nostrils, eye whites, horn tips;
  - sheep: tufted wool, skin, eye whites, a pink nose, hooves (sheared too);
  - chicken: layered feathers, darker wing edges and tail tips, a beak line;
  - villager (and zombie villager's body): woven robe with a belt and a
    darker hem, skin, white eyes with green irises;
  - zombie (husk and drowned through the shared macro): rotting skin,
    woven clothes, a torn shirt showing skin, a waistband, eye glints, a
    nose, a ragged mouth;
  - skeleton (stray, bogged, wither skeleton): bone texture, a darker skull
    with four teeth;
  - creeper: mottled skin, darker feet;
  - spider: bristly hair, a faint abdomen marking, eight eyes, a mouth;
  - enderman: speckled hide (jaw too);
  - wolf: fur, a mouth, inner ears and eye glints.
  Silhouettes and cuboid construction are kept; the cow's udder is the one
  new protrusion (Java's cow has one).
- **Motion.** Mobs simulate at 20 Hz and their position was interpolated
  per frame, but their body yaw, head yaw and pitch and walk cycle were
  not, so turning and leg swing stepped at 20 Hz while the body glided.
  `Mob::render_pose` now lerps them with the frame's partial tick (angles
  the short way round, the walk phase across its wrap), as Java's
  `rotLerp` and `WalkAnimationState` do. `Entities::mesh` draws the
  interpolated pose and restores the simulation's values; LAN replicas
  keep the previous snapshot's pose to interpolate from.
- **Entity submission.** The mesh keeps blended triangles (slime shells,
  shadows) in their own list, so `Renderer::set_entities` uploads the two
  lists as they are; it used to scan every triangle vertex each frame and,
  when any was blended, copy them all into a scratch list to put those
  last. Each model part's box map and face shades are now worked out once
  per part instead of once per box.
- **Capture harness.** `--still-mobs` (capture-only): a mob that has landed
  stops updating, faces the starting camera and keeps its lighting current.
  Two pinned scenes appended to the `mobs` group: `mobs-still-farm` and
  `mobs-still-hostile`, at noon with natural spawning off.

## Fixture and tests

`tools/extract_java_shadow_fixtures.py client.jar --output
tests/fixtures/java/26.3/entity_shadows.json` resolves each registered
entity type's renderer from `EntityRenderers`' bootstrap methods (lambdas
included), follows its constructors up the class chain to the
`shadowRadius`/`shadowStrength` it ends with, lists the classes in that
chain that override `getShadowRadius`/`getShadowStrength` with their
literals, and records the babies' `getAgeScale` values, `extractShadow`'s
constants (checked against the bytecode's literals) and the option's key,
label, default, place in the quality list and graphics presets (Fast off,
Fancy and Fabulous on). Rerunning it reproduces the file byte for byte.

- `tests/entity_shadows.rs`: every `MobKind`'s base radius and strength
  against its Java renderer; the size, puff and baby rules (age scale times
  the renderer's own baby factor) against the overrides' literals; items,
  orbs and players; the constants; the option's key, label, default and
  position.
- `entity::shadow` unit tests: quads lie on the block tops under the
  radius, at alpha 0.5 at full power and light; they fade with camera
  distance, height above the ground and darkness, and vanish at 16 blocks
  and at light 3; zero-radius and invisible casters draw nothing; drawn
  meshes keep shadows in the blended list.
- `app::settings::tests::entity_shadows_round_trip` (persistence, old files,
  bad values) and `app::options::tests::entity_shadows_toggles_like_java`.
- `entity::tests::mobs_turn_and_walk_smoothly_between_ticks`,
  `entity::tests::still_mobs_land_then_hold_their_pose_facing_the_camera`.
- `render::textures::mob::tests`: each pattern averages near neutral, has

| `Entities::shadows` | — | 52.6 (73.4): about 94 casters within 16 blocks, 513 quads |

After the second round and the merge (`7888a00`): model build 81.7

Third round, measured back to back on one sandbox that runs everything
about twice as slowly as the earlier ones (its pure-copy partition step
takes 7.8 µs instead of 3.5): at `e50121d`, before the round, model build
181.3 (212.4) µs with 3,684 boxes and shadows 99.5 (140.2) µs; at
`6f889a2`, after it, 172.0–175.6 (247.9–306.1) µs with 3,690 boxes and
shadows 103.5–104.5 (161.5–169.9) µs over two runs. The new detail adds
six boxes in this scene and no measurable cost.

So the model build is 18 µs cheaper with 40% more boxes, the shadows add
53 µs, and the frame's entity CPU work goes from about 99 to 134 µs in
this scene. The [entity frame-time record](2026-11-09-entity-frame-time.md)
measured the entity phase at 0.68–0.76 ms of a tick frame on the M5 before
instancing; the gate (120 FPS 1% low, p99 ≤ 8.33 ms) is the owner's run on
the reference Mac and has not been run with this work. The GPU draws 40%
more box instances and up to a few hundred blended quads, which a
software renderer can't measure.

## Differences from Java

- The shadow disc is an original 16-step disc with a soft rim drawn in the
  shader, not Java's `textures/misc/shadow.png`. Its alpha is converted for
  the linear-light blend (above), so it darkens the screen as Java's does.
- Receivers: the full-block collision test uses `physics::full_collision`
  (VoxelCraft's collision boxes), and the quad always spans the whole block
  top (a full collision shape's outline is the full cube for every
  VoxelCraft block). A cell above the receiver that is an opaque block is
  skipped before the light lookup: its light is 0 (or the piece is hidden
  inside an emitting block), so nothing visible changes.
- Brightness uses `Dimension::ambient_light` (0, 0.1 and 0.25) for
  `dimensionType.ambientLight()`; those values were not re-extracted here.
  Where skylight is not yet known the light falls back to block light.
- Every entity with a Java shadow radius that VoxelCraft draws casts one;
  display entities (`DisplayRenderer`, whose radius comes from data) aren't
  implemented. No attribute scale exists, so `LivingEntityRenderer`'s
  `× scale` is 1. Java's `EntityRenderDispatcher.shouldRenderShadow`
  (off in the inventory preview) has no counterpart because no entity
  preview draws shadows here.
- Graphics presets: VoxelCraft has no Fast/Fancy/Fabulous preset; Java's
  Fast turns Entity Shadows off.
- Mob art is original and not a copy of Java's skins. Patterns replace the
  old per-texel noise on every mob; only the mobs listed above have new
  hand-drawn faces and markings.
- Motion: only the interpolation above was changed. The walk cycle's
  constants (`limb_phase` += speed × 3.2 per second, swing sin × 0.9),
  Java's `WalkAnimationState` (speed lerp 0.4, legs cos(position × 0.6662) ×
  1.4), the death topple, hurt flash timing and attack swings were read in
  the code, not compared frame by frame; static captures can't show motion.
