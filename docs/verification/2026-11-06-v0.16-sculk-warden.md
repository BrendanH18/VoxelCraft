## Implemented behaviour

**Game events and vibrations** (`src/world/vibration.rs`). Every Java
game event with its frequency (1–15), notification radius and tags.
`World::emit_game_event(pos, event, source, affected)` (and the free
`vibration::emit_game_event`) is the public API: block listeners in range
consider the event at once; wardens get it on their next tick. A listener
rejects an event while a vibration travels, outside its tag, from a
spectator, from a sneaking source for the sneak-ignored events, from a
source that dampens vibrations (wardens), on a wool or carpet
(`affected`), or when wool occludes all six nudged rays. Candidates are
selected per tick (nearest, then higher frequency), chosen on a later
tick and travel `floor(distance)` ticks. Sensor power is
`max(1, 15 - floor(15 / radius * distance))`.

Events posted now: player steps, swims and landings (every 1/0.6 blocks
walked, `player_moved`, host and agents), mob steps, swims, landings,
damage and deaths (`entity/sculk_events.rs`), block place and destroy
(shared placement and mining paths, with the block as affected state),
doors, trapdoors, gates, levers, buttons, repeaters and comparators
(shared use path), TNT priming, cake, chest opening (player) and closing,
composters, harvests, eye insertion, lighting fire, eating and drinking
with `item_interact_finish` (shared consumption), buckets and bottles
(`fluid_pickup`, `fluid_place`), thrown snowballs, eggs, pearls and
potions (`projectile_shoot`), mounting, note blocks, lightning,
explosions, bow and mob arrow shots and arrows landing, sensor tendril
clicks, amethyst resonance and shrieks.

**Sculk sensors and calibrated sensors** (`src/world/sculk.rs`). Listen
at radius 8 (16), activate for 30 (10) ticks then cool down 10, emit weak
power to every side (not the calibrated sensor's input side) and strong
power into the block below, read their last frequency to comparators
while active, resonate amethyst blocks next to them (`resonate_N` at the
frequency, with the note-pitched chime), post tendril clicks, and hear an
entity stepping on them (except wardens), sneaking or not. A calibrated
sensor's input (the redstone signal into the side opposite its facing)
filters events to that frequency. The calibrated sensor faces where its
placer looks, is waterloggable, mines like the sensor (1.5, hoe), drops
only with Silk Touch, gives 5 experience otherwise, emits light 1, and is
crafted from a sensor and three amethyst shards.

**Shriekers.** Listen only to tendril clicks (radius 8) from a player
source (a projectile's owner first), and to a player stepping on them.
One that can't summon (every player-placed or catalyst-grown shrieker)
shrieks at once; a summoning one asks the game to warn the players
(`World::resolve_shrieks`): nothing with a warden in the 48-block box or
anyone within 16 on cooldown, otherwise the highest warning level rises by
one and every player in the group takes it. Shrieking lasts 90 ticks; then
(or when broken mid-shriek) a summoning shrieker with a warning answers:
level 4 summons a warden, anything else (or a failed summon) plays the
warden's distant reply, and survival players within 40 get Darkness. The
`spawn_wardens` gamerule and Peaceful stop answers. Warning levels decay
every 12000 ticks and are saved with each player.

**Catalysts.** The nearest loaded catalyst within 8 blocks of a mob's
death takes the experience it would drop (none when nothing would drop),
which no longer drops, blooms for 8 ticks with its sound and soul
particles, and spreads the charge with Java's cursor algorithm: veins
creep over faces, veins on replaceable blocks turn them into sculk, sculk
far enough from the catalyst grows sensors (and one shrieker in eleven,
never summoning) with at most two others nearby, and charge decays with
distance. Cursors merge, move to sculk or veins nearby and save with the
catalyst.

**The warden** (`src/entity/warden.rs`). 500 health, 0.9 × 2.9, movement
0.3, knockback resistance 1, fire immune, never despawns by distance,
removed in Peaceful, drops one sculk catalyst and 5 experience. Its brain
runs once a tick with Java's activities in priority order (emerge, dig,
roar, fight, investigate, sniff, idle) and memory expiries: it hears
vibrations at its eyes (radius 16, 40-tick cooldown, tendril clicks);
`AngerManagement` keeps per-entity anger (cap 150, angry at 80, agitated
at 40, decaying by one every 20 ticks, players first, absent entities
remembered), 35 anger per vibration or sniff, 10 for a first projectile
then 35, 20 more when it roars, 100 when hurt; disturbances it investigates
at 0.7 speed; sniffing for 84 ticks with a 100–200 tick cooldown; roaring
for 84 ticks before attacking; melee (30 damage, attack knockback 1.5,
18-tick cooldown, Java's attack reach) and the sonic boom (target within
15 across and 20 up or down, after 200 ticks without a landed melee or 40
after the last boom; sound and damage 34 ticks in: 10 damage that armor
and Protection don't reduce, a 2.5 / 0.5 push scaled by knockback
resistance, a beam of particles). It emerges over 134 ticks when summoned
(invulnerable, ignoring explosions, one block tall), and digs away over
100 ticks after a minute without disturbance unless persistent (no loot or
experience). Every 120 ticks it gives survival players within 20 Darkness
(260 ticks, renewed below 200). Touching it angers it. Heartbeat (faster
with anger), ambient, agitated, angry, listening, roar, sniff, emerge, dig,
attack, sonic charge and boom, hurt and death sounds are synthesized.
The original model (eyeless head, glowing ribcage, twitching tendrils,
beating heart) rises out of and sinks into the ground, roars, sniffs,
swings and charges its boom. State (pose, running action, anger, listener,
memories, targets) saves with the mob; pose, timers and anger reach LAN
clients.

**Darkness.** `Effect::Darkness` with Java's id and colour. The fog closes
to 15 blocks (start at three quarters), blending out over the last 22
ticks, and the fog colour pulses with `0.45 * cos(ticks * pi / 40)`.

## Differences from Java

- Randomness uses VoxelCraft's generators, not Java's `RandomSource`
  sequences: spreads, sniff cooldowns, idle strolls and summon positions
  follow Java's odds and ranges but not its exact rolls.
- Listener lookup uses the listener radius for every event; Java visits
  sections by the event's notification radius first, which differs only
  for the jukebox's radius-10 events (not vibrations).
- Events emitted outside a world transaction (eating, bow shots, arrows
  landing) are queued and delivered at the next entity update, a part of a
  tick later. Note blocks, chest closing, lightning and explosions post
  with no source entity (their player or entity isn't passed down), so a
  shrieker can't trace them to a player and wardens only investigate.
  Player bow shots don't carry the shooter's sneaking.
- Not yet posted: entity interaction, dismounting, shearing, equipping,
  elytra gliding, flapping, splashes, teleports, bounces, projectiles
  hitting entities, fishing casts, and block changes from other sources
  (pistons, crops). These events and frequencies exist for when
  their systems call the API.
- Darkness is a fog with a pulsing colour; Java also darkens the lightmap.
- The warden's walk targets use the shared ground navigation (no
  `distanceToXZ` path cost), give up after 200 ticks, and investigate the
  disturbance cell itself rather than a nearby random cell. Its touch anger
  is an overlap test rather than collision pushing. Shield disabling (5
  seconds) on its melee isn't modelled. Sonic boom knockback omits the
  hurt knockback's default 0.4 push.
- Experience from player deaths isn't offered to catalysts; only mob
  deaths are.
- Mobs other than wardens emit steps only while on the ground; Java's
  per-type movement emission (some flyers emit none) isn't distinguished.
- Calibrated sensors draw as the sensor's model with amethyst textures;
  no extra crystal geometry.
- Wool stairs and slabs from Java's `dampens_vibrations` tag don't exist
  in VoxelCraft.

## Checks run

`cargo fmt` locally, then `scripts/remote-check.sh --full` on a Namespace
Linux instance (rustc 1.99.1), which runs:

```sh
cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings
cargo test --release
cargo clippy --release --no-default-features --all-targets -- -D warnings
cargo test --release --no-default-features
```

The final run passed all four: 2265 tests passed (21 ignored) with default
features and 2060 passed (20 ignored) without, 0 failed. Earlier runs
caught a temporary borrowed from the warden's loot table, three
`needless_range_loop` lints in the vein spreader, the warden model standing
taller than its 2.9-block box, the calibrated sensor's default state not
being its first registered state (name lookup), the v0.13 sculk state-count
and emission tests not knowing the calibrated sensor, and two of the new
tests' own timing assumptions; all were fixed before the passing run.

The 30 new tests cover: the frequency table, tags, selector order, travel
and listener save data (`world::vibration`); a sensor's activation by
distance, power, comparator frequency, active and cooldown timing; wool
occlusion, wool dampening and sneaking; radius and its own block events;
nearest-candidate selection; calibrated input filtering, radius 16 and its
10-tick activation; amethyst resonance reaching a second sensor; shriekers
that can't summon; warning levels, cooldowns, the 16-block group, the
nearby-warden block and the gamerule; warning decay; catalyst experience,
bloom and sculk spreading; listener save/reload; player steps, sneaking,
stepping on sensors and shriekers and spectators (`world::sculk`); the
calibrated recipe; and for the warden its registry and attributes, anger
ordering and decay, summoning at warning 4 with emerging and Darkness,
vibrations to roar to melee, creative and sneaking sources, the sonic boom
on an unreachable target, digging away without loot, persistent wardens,
Darkness pulses, save/reload and LAN look state, and catalyst experience
from a kill.

Not run: the game itself (no screenshot, no hands-on session), the
headless benchmark and a frame-time scene. World generation is unchanged
(no generator code was touched), so generation 1 and 2 golden hashes are
unaffected; the existing golden tests passed.
