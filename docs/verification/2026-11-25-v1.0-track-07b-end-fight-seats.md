## What changed

### One route-finder for both dragons (`entity::dragon::path`)

Track 7 wrote Java's nodes and A* for the egg-spawned dragon only
(`dragon::wild`); the End fight's dragon kept a node ring with a floor of
10 and a simplified walk. Both dragons now run the same phases and the same
path code, moved into `src/entity/dragon/path.rs`:

- **Nodes** (`findClosestNode()`): 24 whole-block points, absolute around
  (0, 0): twelve at radius 60 (`π/12` steps), eight at radius 40 (`π/8`),
  four at radius 20 (`π/4`), with `Mth.cos`/`Mth.sin` table values and
  `Mth.floor`, at `max(73, MOTION_BLOCKING_NO_LEAVES height + 5)` (+ 15
  for the radius-40 ring). The heightmap comes from the loaded world, so in
  the End the inner nodes over a pillar stand 15 above its obsidian, cap or
  cage, as in Java. Before, the fight read the island's terrain noise
  (pillars ignored), floored the height at **10**, and centred nodes on
  half blocks. Each dragon works its nodes out the first time it needs one
  and keeps them once every column was loaded (until then unloaded columns
  sit at 73 and are recomputed). They are no longer stored in the fight.
- **Closest node** (`findClosestNode(x, y, z)`): float squared distances
  from the floored point; only nodes closer than 100 blocks count (Java's
  starting `closestDist` of 10000), and node 0 is the answer when none is.
  With no crystal alive (or no fight) only nodes 12–23 count.
- **A\*** (`findPath`): `nodeAdjacency` (`6146, 8197, …, 8257536`), float
  `Node.distanceTo` costs, Java's `BinaryHeap` (insert, pop and
  `changeCost` with its `upHeap`/`downHeap`, so equal costs come off in
  Java's order), never through nodes below 12 with no crystal alive. A goal
  it can't reach ends the path at the node nearest it; a start that can't
  get nearer is `null` (`None`), which the phases treat as Java does (no
  new target, try again next time). The path is held as Java's
  `currentPath` is: none, nodes still ahead, or done.
- **`navigateToNextPathNode`**: the next node, up to 20 above it
  (`y + nextFloat() × 20F`, in floats).

The fight's phases now follow Java instead of VoxelCraft's ring walk:

- **Holding pattern**: at the end of a path, land one time in
  `aliveCrystals + 3`; else strafe the targetable player nearest the egg
  (no range; was 150) one time in `distToCenterSqr / 512 + 2` or in
  `aliveCrystals + 2`; else a new path from the nearest node to the next
  node round (one time in eight it turns and aims six further on). A
  fight's dragon always picks outer-ring numbers (`aliveCrystals() >= 0`
  holds even with none alive), so with every crystal gone it flies the
  inner nodes toward an outer goal it may not use — Java's behaviour. A
  dragon with no fight folds the goal onto the radius-40 ring.
- **Strafe**: on its first tick (`setTarget`) a path from the nearest node
  to the node nearest the player, ending at the player's block, `floor(y +
  min(0.4F + dist / 80 − 1, 10))` up; once it's flown, the target is the
  player, a little above them; on reaching that, a new circling path with
  the strafe's own direction. The fireball rules are unchanged. VoxelCraft's
  400-tick strafe timeout is gone (Java has none).
- **Landing approach**: from the nearest node to the node nearest
  `(−x, 105, −z) × 40` of the player nearest the egg (no range; was 150),
  or nearest `(40, egg y, 0)` with no player, then the egg's cell; landing
  starts when the egg becomes the target.
- **Takeoff**: on its first tick, a path out along `getHeadLookVector`
  (pitched by the distance from the egg) to the outer ring with crystals
  alive, the radius-40 ring without; holding pattern once the dragon is 10
  blocks from the egg's centre. Before, the fight's dragon climbed 12 and
  flew to one outer node.
- **A phase handing over** runs the new phase's first tick at once, as
  `EnderDragon.aiStep` does (so a dragon leaving the holding pattern for a
  strafe has its path and target that tick).
- Hovering moved into the shared phase code; `wild.rs` keeps only what is
  particular to a dragon with no fight (spawning, death, saves, replicas).

Crystals, healing, perching, the breath, the exit portal, the boss bar,
death, the respawn ritual and saves are unchanged; no save format changed
(Java saves no path or node either).

### Minecart seats (`entity::minecart`)

`Entity.positionRider` puts a passenger's feet at the vehicle's passenger
attachment less the passenger's own vehicle attachment. A minecart's
attachment is 0.1875 above its feet (`passengerAttachments(0.1875F)`, for
every minecart type); a player's vehicle attachment is 0.6
(`Avatar.DEFAULT_VEHICLE_ATTACHMENT`). So:

- A player in a minecart now has their feet **0.4125 under** the cart's
  (was 0.1875 above), and a standing player's eyes (1.62) sit 1.2075 above
  the cart: the camera is 0.6 lower, as in Java. Nothing renders the rider
  with an offset of its own, so the model drops with it.
- Mobs sit on the attachment less their own (`−ridingOffset`, halved for
  babies): a zombie or skeleton 0.7 lower, a pig on the point. Villagers
  and wandering traders sit on the cart's feet
  (`LOWERED_PASSENGER_ATTACHMENT`). Before, every mob sat on the player's
  seat. A mob a cart scoops up lands on that seat too.
- Cushions keep their own seat.

## Differences and remaining gaps

Found while reading the phases, left as they were (outside this track's
scope, which was the route):

- **Landing**: Java's `DragonLandingPhase` flies to the egg's bottom
  centre and, within 1 block, resets the flame count and goes to
  `SITTING_SCANNING`. VoxelCraft goes to the flaming perch, counts it as a
  flame, snaps the dragon onto the perch, and lands anyway after 400 ticks.
- **Strafe target**: Java keeps strafing a player who turns creative or
  leaves (`attackTarget` stays set); VoxelCraft returns to the holding
  pattern.
- **Death**: still rises in place (Java's `DragonDeathPhase` first flies
  toward the portal); Track 7 recorded this.
- The A* and node choices use Java's arithmetic; the dragon's flight
  toward each target is still VoxelCraft's `fly` (Track 7 and earlier),
  so its exact track between nodes isn't claimed to match Java's tick for
  tick.
- Node heights come from the loaded world; until every node column is
  loaded, unloaded ones sit at 73 and are recomputed.
- Minecart seats: the seat is the cart's position plus the attachment;
  whether VoxelCraft's cart height on a rail matches Java's cart position
  on every rail shape wasn't re-checked here (FOO-04b's rail fixtures
  cover cart movement).
