## What changed

### 1. Work sounds and the composter's effects (`src/entity/villager.rs`, `src/entity/mob/brain_walk.rs`, `src/world/composter.rs`, `src/audio/work.rs`, `src/app/crafter.rs`)

- **`playWorkSound`**: every `WorkAtPoi.start` (each 300 ticks at most, on
  a coin flip, within 1.73 of the job site) plays the profession's
  `entity.villager.work_<job>` at the villager, `NEUTRAL`, volume 1
  (`Profession::work_sound`; none for the unemployed and nitwits). The
  thirteen sounds are original procedural stand-ins in the established
  style (`src/audio/work.rs`): the armorer's furnace roar, the butcher's
  sizzle, the cartographer's pen, the cleric's bubbling, the farmer's
  compost, the fisherman's barrel, the fletcher's taps, the
  leatherworker's flaps, the librarian's page, the mason's grind, the
  shepherd's loom, the toolsmith's hammer and the weaponsmith's rasp.
- **Level event 1500** (`ComposterBlock.handleFill`) for every fill:
  `block.composter.fill_success` when a layer went in, else
  `block.composter.fill` (`BLOCKS`, volume and pitch 1), and ten
  `composter` particles (`Request::Composter`, LAN `"t": "cf"`) within the
  inner 0.625 across, from half a pixel above the compost's centre
  (`centre_height`: the hollow starts at `max(2, 1 + 2 * level)` pixels) to
  the top, each moving by a gaussian 0.02. The player's use
  (`useItemOn`), a hopper's (`InputContainer.setChanged`) and a farmer's
  `WorkAtComposter` all post it; a farmer's `compostItems` ends with it
  every time, a plain fill when nothing went in (`FarmAct::Compost` is now
  sent always).
- **`block.composter.empty`** when a player (`useWithoutItem`) or a farmer
  (`extractProduce`) takes the bone meal out; a hopper below takes it
  silently (`OutputContainer.setChanged` calls only `empty`).
- **`block.composter.ready`** when the scheduled tick ripens level 7 to 8.
- The composter sounds are original procedural stand-ins (a wet crumbly
  squelch, its settling crackle for a success, a long scoop, a soft
  bloop).

### 2. Bone meal and torchflowers (`src/world/growth.rs`, `src/world/bees.rs`, `src/world/automation.rs`, `src/session/interact.rs`, `src/particles/`, `src/entity/model/villager_outfit.rs`)

- **`World::use_bone_meal`** is bone meal used by anyone: the growth
  (`apply_bone_meal`), then level event 1505: `Request::Growth`
  (LAN `"t": "gr"`) and the bone meal sound, which the game plays from the
  world's queue for players, farmers' `UseBonemeal` and now dispensers too
  (still the game's grass-placing stand-in for `BONE_MEAL_USE`).
- **`BoneMealItem.addGrowthParticles`**: by the block there after the
  growth (`growth::bone_meal_kind`), a neighbour spreader (grass, nylium,
  netherrack, moss, pale moss) scatters 45 happy-villager particles from 2.5
  blocks either side of the block above it, 1 high, keeping only those over
  a non-air block; water does the same from its own cell; any other
  bone-mealable block gets 15 within its cell up to its shape's top (a
  whole block over air), rooted dirt and mangrove leaves in the cell below
  them. Each moves by a gaussian 0.02.
- **Level event 2011**: a bee growing a crop (`BeeGrowCropGoal`) sheds 15
  within the crop's cell.
- **`HAPPY_VILLAGER`** and **`COMPOSTER`** particles
  (`SuspendedTownParticle`): the base random motion with the given one at a
  fiftieth, no collisions or gravity, 0.99 drag, 0.5 to 1.1 times the base
  size; a happy villager lives 20 / (0.2 to 1.0) ticks, a composter
  particle 3 to 7. Two original sprites: a green four-point glint and a
  brown crumb (`Kind::HappyVillager`, `Kind::Composter`).
- **Torchflower crops** are `CropBlock`s whose maximum age (2, the
  torchflower itself) their states never reach: `UseBonemeal` now picks
  them (each use one age, as the game's bone meal already grew them), and
  `HarvestFarmland` never breaks them, as in Java.
- **The bone-meal hand**: while `UseBonemeal` runs (`using_bone_meal`), the
  villager's `CrossedArmsItemLayer` draws bone meal (Java's
  `setItemSlot(MAINHAND, bone meal)` in `start`, emptied in `stop`), in
  place of a shown trade.

### 3. Eating, fireballs and the goat (`src/session/consumption.rs`, `src/app/hands.rs`, `src/agent.rs`, `src/app/split.rs`, `src/entity/panda.rs`, `src/entity/fireball.rs`, `src/entity/lifecycle.rs`, `src/app/mobs.rs`, `src/entity/animals/long_jump.rs`)

- **`shouldEmitParticlesAndSounds`**: the session's consumption step says
  when Java's `onUseTick` emits (`Step::effects`): past the first 21.875% of
  the use, every fourth tick by the ticks left (`session::use_effects`; a
  32-tick meal chews with 24, 20, 16, 12, 8 and 4 ticks left). The chewing
  or drinking sound and food's five item particles follow it for every
  player through the shared adapter (the host, split-screen controller
  and LAN players) and for CLI agents (`agent::Event::Chew` and the new
  `Event::EatParticles`, drawn by the host); finishing still sheds 16. It
  replaces the game's chew every 5 ticks from the start.
- **The panda's `addEatingParticles`**: every fifth tick of eating, six
  item particles of what it holds, 0.8 to 1.2 ahead of its body and 0.3 to
  0.9 below a block over its eyes, turned by its pitch and facing, and the
  chewing sound (the game's stand-in for `PANDA_EAT`, `NEUTRAL`). The
  other eating mobs Java has don't shed item particles: the fox's eating
  (Track 2g) is the only other one; sheep and others graze with block
  particles.
- **Fireballs' shooters**: a blaze's small fireball hitting a player
  records the blaze as the player's attacker (`OwnerHurt`, so trusting
  foxes and the player's wolves defend), and a blast a mob is behind (a
  ghast's fireball, a creeper's explosion) does the same for every player
  it hurts (`Entities::player_hurt_by_mob`, as `hurtServer` records any
  living source entity). The fireballs already carried their owner
  (Track 8e).
- **The goat's launch**: its heading snaps to its body's
  (`setYRot(yBodyRot)`), and Jump Boost lengthens the jump by
  `getJumpBoostPower` (0.1 a level).

## Differences and remaining gaps

- **Bone-mealable blocks**: the particles read Java's `BonemealableBlock`
  class from the block: the spreaders, water, rooted dirt and mangrove
  leaves by name, and any other crossed-plane or shaped block as a grower
  (excluding the torchflower and the double plants tall grass, large fern
  and tall seagrass). A shaped block that isn't a Java `BonemealableBlock`
  can't be bone-mealed, so it never shows them, but a plant Java's
  growth leaves as another non-bone-mealable plant (beyond those excluded)
  would show particles Java doesn't.
- **Level event 2011's order**: Java posts the bee's event before setting
  the grown state, so the client reads the old crop's height; the game
  reads the new one.
- **The bone-meal hand on LAN**: guests don't see it (the hold isn't in a
  villager's replicated look; Java's main-hand item is).
- **Sounds**: the work, composter and panda eating sounds and bone meal's
  are original procedural stand-ins; the panda's chew plays at volume 0.75
  in place of Java's 0.5 or 1 and the game's pitch.
- **Mob use effects**: Java's wandering trader drinking (`UseItemGoal`)
  plays its drink sound on the same rhythm; the game keeps its own drink
  sound timing there (no particles either way).
- 2g's other remaining differences stand (pickup reach, the goat's path
  type and look, 2f's list).
