## What is implemented

**Placement (generation 4 only).** `temples::Kind::Mansion` uses the
`woodland_mansions` placement (triangular spread like the monument's).
`mansion::start` reproduces `findGenerationPoint`: no randomness unless a
dark forest or pale garden is at block (7, 7) of the start chunk; the
rotation is the first draw of `setLargeFeatureSeed(seed, chunkX, chunkZ)`;
the start y is the lowest `WORLD_SURFACE_WG` height (top block + 1 of the
base terrain column) of four corners five blocks apart, turned by the
rotation; starts below y 60 are dropped; then the biome at the start must
be in the tag. Generations 1–3 never start a mansion.

**Layout.** `mansion::pieces` ports `MansionGrid` (corridors from the
entrance, edge cleaning, room identification with 1×1, 1×2 and 2×2 rooms,
door flags, the third floor and its stairs room) and `MansionPiecePlacer`
(entrance, outer walls on two floors, the third floor's walls, both roofs
with fronts, corners, inner corners and parapets, corridor floors and
their carpets, indoor walls and doors, and every room with its template
choice, rotation and mirror) from the same random sequence. Pieces carry
Java's template names and positions.

**Templates (FE-02).** `mansion::rooms` holds, for every one of Java's 73
template names, the size, whether every cell is stored, and Java's data
markers; the blocks are original. Each room is a room of Java's kind for
that name (a flower room, pumpkin shrine, study, hearth room, the four
secret 1×1 rooms including the spider spawner room, farms, a smithy with a
lava hearth, a sapling nursery, an indoor tree, a mushroom cellar, the
storerooms of empty chests, iron-door cells, wool statues, bedrooms,
libraries, an illager altar, council rooms, the allay jail, a pond, a hay
loft, the lava treasure vault, the two stairs rooms), held to the block
families Java's room of that name contains. The secret spawner (spider,
default spawner settings as in Java) and the four chests of set contents
(alliums, dark oak saplings, an Efficiency I iron axe, a trapped chest of
ender pearls over TNT) are at Java's positions with Java's contents.

**Residents and loot.** `Mage` markers place evokers, `Warrior`
vindicators (with `finalizeSpawn`'s iron axe), `Group of Allays` one to
three allays; all are persistent and spawn once per marker through the
existing structure population. Chest markers become chests turned by the
piece rotation only (Java ignores the mirror there) and filled from
`chests/woodland_mansion`, which now carries the vex armor trim template
(`loot_tables_carry_the_new_items`).

**Terrain.** No terrain adaptation, as in Java: templates replace the cells
they store, including air. `afterPlace` is reproduced: every column whose
first-floor block is not air gets cobblestone down to the first solid block
of base terrain.

**Locating.** `/locate structure mansion` (or `woodland_mansion`) finds
mansions through the existing temple search, from the start alone (no
layout is built to locate).

**Explorer maps.** Items 1629–1633: woodland mansion, desert pyramid, warm
ocean ruins, buried mineshaft and buried ancient city maps
(`world::explorer_maps`), with original icons and map decorations under
Java's decoration ids (`mansion`, `desert_pyramid`, `ocean_ruin_warm`,
`mineshaft`, `ancient_city`). The search is `findNearestMapStructure` for
one random-spread placement: rings of placement regions out from the
origin's chunk, the first start on the first ring with one, the radius in
rings, the target the start chunk's corner, zoom 2 with a biome preview.
`skip_existing_chunks` is modelled: the map storage remembers referenced
starts (saved with the maps), skips them, and adds the found one. The
master cartographer sells the mansion map (14 emeralds and a compass, 12
uses, 30 xp, discount 0.2, radius 100), the only map trade among the five
in Java 26.3.

## Generation cost

The layout is built once per start and cached with the other surface
structures (`temples`' cache, rebuilt deterministically after eviction);
the 73 templates are built once per process. A chunk walks only the pieces
whose boxes meet it, and a mansion's start check runs only where a dark
forest or pale garden sits at the start chunk's block (7, 7), so other
regions pay one biome sample. Measured by the ignored
`mansion_generation_cost` test on a Namespace 8×16 instance (not the
reference M5; one run, no frame-time claim), seed 42's mansion at
(1975, 63, 4583):

| What | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Layout (487 pieces, foundation columns) | 7.0 ms, once per mansion |
| The 18 engine chunks the mansion meets, generation 4 | 53.4 ms |
| The same chunks, generation 3 (no mansion) | 61.1 ms |
| Painting the mansion alone into those chunks | 0.96 ms (0.054 ms per chunk) |

The generation 3 and 4 runs differ by noise and cache warm-up; the
mansion's own paint is under a tenth of a millisecond per chunk, so worker
generation stays bounded. The layout build lands on whichever worker first
touches the mansion. No frame-time run was made; the integrator's
offscreen suite covers that.

## Differences from Java

- Templates are original (FE-02): only sizes, stored-cell flags, markers,
  fixed containers and room kinds follow Java's.
- The two infested cobblestone families of Java's TNT trap room are plain
  cobblestone: infested blocks are a v0.16 registry gap.
- A group of allays spawns one per block up the marker's cell rather than
  all on the marker block, because residents are remembered by position;
  their count is a per-position roll rather than the level random.
- Chest loot seeds are hashed per position, as for the other temples,
  rather than drawn from the piece random.
- The foundation stops at the first solid block of base terrain; Java's
  stops at the first non-air, non-liquid block of the generated world, so
  a carver cave under the floor is not filled here.
- `claimStructureAt` (referencing a destination the map is created inside)
  is not modelled: nothing in the game makes these maps inside their
  destinations. The older explorer maps still ignore references.
- The buried mineshaft map skips badlands chunks in place of Java's
  separate `mineshaft_mesa`; the game's mineshaft placement itself is not
  Java's (recorded under GEN-05).
- The four non-mansion maps have no source in play until abandoned camps
  (v0.16); they exist, locate and draw, and can be given by command.
- Biome checks use the column's surface biome rather than Java's 4×4×4
  quart sampling.
