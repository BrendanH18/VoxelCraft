## What was built

1. **One component style** ([`gui/style.rs`](../../src/app/gui/style.rs)).
   The [guide's](../art-and-ui.md) palette as sRGB tokens, converted in one
   place (`style::srgb`, the seam with Track 1's colour helper), and the
   components every screen now draws through:
   - `panel`: Java's container panel (black outline with stepped corners,
     2-pixel white and `#555555` bevels, `#C6C6C6` fill); `inset`, `slot`
     (18 × 18, `#373737`/`#FFFFFF` edges, `#8B8B8B` fill) and `big_slot`
     (the 26 × 26 result frame); `slot_highlight` (white at 50 %).
   - `button` and `track` in three states that differ beyond colour:
     normal (black outline, bevel), hot (white outline, lighter sprite) and
     disabled (flat `#2C2C2C`, `#A0A0A0` text, no bevel). The menu
     toolkit's buttons and sliders use them; hover and focus no longer turn
     the text yellow or the button blue.
   - `tooltip_box`: Java's `#100010` fill at 94 % with a purple-to-dark
     border at 31 %, used by every item, offer, potion, trim, firework,
     recipe and widget tooltip.
   - Original pixel art for the furnace flame (14 × 14, Java's
     `ceil(left × 13) + 1` rows), the progress arrow (24 × 16; 16 × 13 in
     the survival inventory), the brewing stand's 9 × 28 arrow and 18 × 4
     fuel gauge, the anvil's plus and an error cross, each over its
     `#8B8B8B` empty shape; the 182 × 5 experience bar in Java's greens;
     effect backgrounds (stepped frame, cyan when ambient); the hotbar's
     strip, per-slot frames and a 24 × 24 two-pixel white selection frame
     in a black edge.
   - Equipment hints: the expected piece's silhouette at 50 % in empty
     armour, off-hand, lapis and smithing slots.
   Applied to the survival, chest, furnace, crafting, brewing, enchanting,
   anvil, grindstone and smithing screens, `slots_ui` (Track 4's creative
   screen and every container share it), the creative panel, its tabs and
   scrollbar, the recipe book's panel and tabs (Track 5), the HUD, and the
   menu toolkit (pause, Options, LAN, error notices, confirmations and the
   updater dialogs, whose wording, confirmations and signed-update steps are
   unchanged). Controller screens keep their dark overlay panel but gain
   slot frames, a white-outlined cursor and the shared flame, arrow and
   keyboard buttons, so the cursor and lit key no longer show by colour
   alone.
2. **Backdrops and blur.** Container and workstation screens get
   `Screen.extractTransparentBackground`'s `#101010` gradient (75 % to
   82 %). Menus over a world blur the world and HUD, then draw Java's 25 %
   black `inworld_menu_background`; the title screen's menus blur the sky
   behind them. The blur is a new renderer pass
   ([`render/blur.rs`](../../src/render/blur.rs),
   [`blur.wgsl`](../../src/render/shaders/blur.wgsl)): while a blurring menu
   is up, the world and the HUD's vertices are drawn into an offscreen
   target, six passes of Java's `box_blur` (horizontal and vertical, three
   times, radius = Menu Background Blur, bilinear taps two texels apart)
   bring it back onto the frame, and the menu is drawn sharp over it.
   `FrameParams` gained `blur` and `ui_under_blur`.
3. **Menu Background Blur** (`menuBackgroundBlurriness`, 0 to 10, default
   5, "OFF" at 0, tooltip "Changes the blurriness of menu backgrounds."),
   in Video Settings (after Particles, where Java lists it after Entity
   Distance) and Accessibility Settings (after Closed Captions, Java's
   High Contrast slot), saved in `options.txt` under Java's key. Old files
   without the line get 5.
4. **FPS counter off by default** only without a saved preference:
   `Settings::default().show_fps` is false; every `options.txt` the game has
   written has a `show_fps=` line, and a saved `true` or `false` loads and
   saves unchanged. F3 and the Video Settings toggle stay.
5. **Java's death screen** ([`death.rs`](../../src/app/death.rs)) on the menu
   toolkit, on its own stack (`Game::death`) so it doesn't pause the game
   (Java's `isPauseScreen` is false) or blur:
   - "You Died!" at twice the size 60 pixels down, the cause at 85 and
     "Score: n" (the number yellow) at 100; Respawn and Title Screen,
     200 × 20, at a quarter of the height plus 72 and 96; the red
     `#500000`→`#803030` gradient.
   - Hardcore: "Game Over!", Spectate World and Title Screen. Spectate
     World runs the shared respawn and then Spectator mode, as Java's server
     does for the respawn packet, so the spectator starts at the bed or
     world spawn. Title Screen leaves at once.
   - Both buttons are disabled for 20 client ticks (one second) and Escape
     does nothing. Outside Hardcore, Title Screen opens "Are you sure you
     want to quit?" with Title Screen and Respawn (150 × 20, 4 apart,
     Java's `ConfirmScreen` column centred on screen) over the same red,
     disabled for another 20 ticks; once live, Escape answers Respawn.
   - Input paths: mouse, keyboard (Tab, arrows, Enter, Space) and a
     controller that hasn't joined (D-pad, A; B does nothing), as the pause
     menu takes them. Title Screen saves and quits as Save and Quit does.
   - Immediate respawn: with `immediate_respawn` on, the host respawns at
     once, in Hardcore as a spectator (Java's `shouldShowDeathScreen` is the
     game rule alone). Previously Hardcore turned the host into a spectator
     where they died with a popup and no screen.
   - Unchanged (PLY-01c): pause rules (the death screen never pauses and
     Escape can't open the pause menu over it), focus loss while dead,
     session ownership, split-screen seats ("Press A to respawn" on their
     own views), LAN guests (Respawn sends the guest's `respawn` command;
     Title Screen disconnects) and CLI agents.
6. **The anvil's name box** (left from v0.17; the core and CLI already
   renamed). The box's text now lives in the player's session
   (`PlayerSession::anvil_name`, not saved), tied to the stack in the left
   input so a new item resets it (`AnvilScreen.slotChanged`), and the shared
   anvil result reads it, so every adapter gets Java's `createResult`:
   - Desktop: Java's 110 × 16 box at (59, 20) with the text at (62, 24),
     always focused while the left slot holds an item, taking every key but
     Escape (so E and the number keys type), 50 characters, filtered by
     `validateName`; the item's own name typed back counts as no rename
     (`onNameChanged`). The title is "Repair & Name" at x 60, and the
     anvil's slots moved down to Java's row (46) to make room. The cost line
     uses Java's colours and dark backing at (…, 67..79), and the error
     cross shows whenever there's an input and no result.
   - Controller: the anvil row gains a fourth entry (a name-tag mark); A
     opens the sign editor's on-screen keyboard for the name (A types, X
     erases, Y shifts, B back to the anvil), and the name shows beside the
     row.
   - LAN guests: typing sends `anvil_name <text>` to the host
     (`ServerboundRenameItemPacket`), which sets the guest's session name
     when its anvil screen is open; closing the screen clears it.
7. **Dispensers dressing LAN guests** (left from v0.17): by reading the code,
   nothing to add. Since v0.17 the tick lends the host and every *active*
   agent player to the dispenser (`tick.rs`), and LAN guests are active agent
   players on the host, so they are already lent. Not covered by a test or
   seen in play; the v0.17 dispensing record's note predates this check.

## Differences from Java

- Drawn, not Java's sprites: every component is original rectangles and
  masks with Java's sizes and colours. The button has no texture noise; the
  flame, arrows, plus, cross and name-tag mark are original shapes.
- The blur runs on linear colour (the surface is sRGB); Java blurs sRGB
  values, so bright edges spread a little more. The radius is in physical
  pixels like Java's.
- Veils: Java blends sRGB values; `style::veil` matches the darkening of a
  black veil exactly and tinted ones (the death screen's red) within a
  few levels.
- The death screen's delay counts real time (a second) rather than client
  ticks; the game has no separate client tick while the world runs.
- The death screen's draft-report icon and clickable link styles don't
  exist (no chat reporting).
- Controller seats in split-screen keep their own death view ("Press A to
  respawn"), and CLI agents and LAN guests in Hardcore still become
  spectators where they died (their adapters' behaviour, unchanged).
- The anvil box is ASCII until Track 1's font lands (the core already takes
  any name `validateName` allows). The desktop box doesn't place the caret
  by mouse click.
- The controller's anvil name entry and keyboard are VoxelCraft's (Java has
  no controller screens).
- The creative and recipe book tabs keep their own geometry; only their
  colours follow the panel.
- Title-screen menus keep their existing darkening (Track 7's panorama
  replaces the sky they blur).
- Workstation lists (stonecutter, loom, cartography, beacon), books, signs
  and trading keep their own drawings apart from the shared slots,
  tooltips and backdrop.

## Measurements

The blur adds six full-screen passes and one more render pass only on
frames with a blurring menu open (pause, Options and the like); the
world's frames are unchanged. Each pass takes `radius + 1` bilinear samples
per pixel (6 at the default 5). Its two targets (window-sized, the surface
format) are made the first time a menu blurs. Software captures on Boat
show appearance only; the frame-time gate is the owner's run on the
reference Mac. The HUD's added vertices are a few dozen quads (slot frames,
selection, flame and arrow masks).
