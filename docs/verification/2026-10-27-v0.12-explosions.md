## Explosions

`src/world/explosion.rs` replaces the old ragged sphere and its hard-coded
list of resistant blocks.

- **Rays.** `World::exploded_cells` casts Java's 1,352 rays from the 16 x 16
  x 16 grid's surface: intensity `radius * (0.7 + 0.6 * nextFloat)`, 0.3-block
  steps, minus 0.225 a step and `(resistance + 0.3) * 0.3` in each cell with
  a block or fluid (the larger of the two; water and lava are 100). Rays stop
  at the top and bottom of the world and at unloaded chunks. The cells are
  shuffled before breaking, as `Util.shuffle` does.
- **Blast resistance.** `Block::blast_resistance` is Java's
  `explosionResistance`: the block's hardness unless a table keyed by block
  name says otherwise (344 names). `tests/java_block_mining.rs` now compares
  every registered state's resistance with `block_mining.json`; all match.
  A TNT blast in stone now leaves a small crater, and obsidian, water and
  bedrock shield what is behind them.
- **Order.** Entities are hurt before any block breaks, so walls about to
  go still shield them.
- **Entities.** Damage is `getEntityDamageAmount` without rounding, scaled
  by how much of the entity's box sees the blast (`getSeenPercent`, through
  the existing collision-shape line test) and measured from its feet. Mobs
  take no hit knockback (`#no_knockback`) and are pushed by
  `(1 - distance / diameter) * exposure` blocks per tick from their eyes.
  Players are pushed the same way, less 0.15 per level of Blast Protection
  worn (Netherite's knockback resistance no longer counts, as in 26.3),
  and spectators and flying Creative players aren't pushed. Minecarts
  use exposure too; frames, paintings, armor stands and End crystals break
  on any damage, as before.
- **Drops.** Each block's drops are an empty-handed break's loot, thinned by
  its table's rules when the blast decays drops: `survives_explosion` keeps a
  whole stack with chance `1 / radius`, `explosion_decay` each item. Leaves
  mix the two (saplings and apples survive, sticks decay) and a full
  composter's bone meal has no rule. Drops are merged into piles of up to 16
  at the first cell that dropped the item. TNT is primed, never dropped.
- **Fire.** Beds and respawn anchors always light fires, ghast fireballs with
  `mob_griefing`, creepers, TNT and End crystals never: one in three
  exploded cells that is air over a solid block catches fire
  (`GameRules::explosion_fire`).

The loot fixture gained an `explosion` scenario
(`tools/extract_java_block_loot_fixtures.py`): each table evaluated exactly at
radius 4 with an empty tool, with `ExplosionCondition` and
`ApplyExplosionDecay` checked in bytecode first. `tests/java_block_loot.rs`
samples `World::spill_exploded` for every registered state against it; all
match, with no allow-list entries. The extractor still reproduces the rest of
the fixture byte for byte (only the new scenario's rows and metadata changed).

### Behaviour a player will notice

- TNT and creepers barely dent stone and deepslate but still clear dirt,
  sand and wood; obsidian, water and other high-resistance blocks protect
  whatever stands behind them.
- Hiding behind a wall now reduces or removes blast damage and knockback.
- Knockback is Java's: up to one block per tick straight away from the
  blast, with no fixed upward pop.
- Blast drops vary by block: ores, slabs and crops lose items one by one,
  other blocks drop whole or not at all; stacks pile up instead of
  scattering one per block.
- Bed and respawn anchor explosions start fires.

## Item frame comparator output

A comparator reading through a solid block takes the larger of the block
behind it and an item frame hung on that block's far side, facing away:
0 when empty, else the rotation plus one. The entity tick refreshes each
frame's reading (`World::refresh_frame_signals`), waking nearby circuits only
when one changes. Test: `comparators_read_an_item_frame_on_the_far_side_of_a_conductor`.

## LAN decorations

Host snapshots carry up to 256 item frames, paintings and armor stands within
160 blocks of each client (shed before dropped items when a frame is too
large); clients replace their copy, keeping armor stands' motion smooth.
Interacting with them already ran on the host through the shared session code.
Test: `lan_clients_mirror_nearby_decor_and_frame_comparator_output`.

## Ender chests for every player

- Controller players open their own ender inventory (a new `EnderChest`
  container and controller tab), not the keyboard player's.
- LAN players get the same container; the client shows it as a chest.
- CLI agents use `chest take/put` on an aimed ender chest.
- Each agent and LAN profile now saves its ender inventory
  (`ender_chest` in the profile record, optional, so older saves load with an
  empty one). A conductor on top keeps the chest shut for everyone.

### Timing

`examples/explosion_bench.rs` blasts power 4 at 64 surface spots and 64 spots
24 blocks underground in generated terrain (seed 263), then blasts each block
of a 4 x 4 x 4 TNT cube in turn at its fixed position (the TNT a blast primes
is discarded rather than ticked, so this isn't a fused chain). Apple M5, three runs of each build
back to back, `--no-default-features`:

| Scene | This branch: median / mean / max per blast | `main` (`c24dc8f`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface | 0.75–1.02 / 0.80–1.20 / 10.5–18.0 ms (5,583 blocks) | 0.47–0.51 / 0.61–0.68 / 11.3–12.3 ms (5,081 blocks) |
| Underground | 0.04 / 0.20–0.22 / 10.2–10.9 ms (218 blocks) | 0.81–0.89 / 0.99–1.06 / 11.2–11.8 ms (11,034 blocks) |
| 64-TNT cube | 0.18–0.19 / 0.27–0.29 / 1.6–1.8 ms; 17.0–18.5 ms in all (1,158 blocks) | 0.02 / 0.04 / 0.8 ms; 2.5 ms in all (342 blocks) |

The rays cost about 0.3 ms more per open-air blast. Underground blasts got
cheaper because stone stops the rays. The cube costs more because
TNT no longer resists, so the blasts break far more around them. The maxima
of 10–18 ms happen on both builds (the relighting and settling after a large
blast) and are not new. The per-state tables take 10–15 ms to build once;
`World` builds them while loading so the first blast doesn't pay for it.
A real chain spreads its blasts over the fuses' 10–30 ticks.

## Not done

- Explosions don't destroy dropped items or XP orbs yet (`ItemEntity` has no
  health), and only mobs, players, minecarts and decorations are hurt.
- Wind charges, the Wither and Wither skulls, and TNT minecarts' rail rule
  come with their sources.
- The ray's random intensity uses VoxelCraft's world RNG, so craters differ
  from Java's for the same seed and position; their size and shape follow the
  same distribution.
- Owner play, and save/reload in play (no saved format changed apart from
  the new optional profile field).
