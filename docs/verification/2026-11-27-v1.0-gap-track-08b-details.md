## Scope

The remaining differences [Track 8](2026-11-27-v1.0-gap-track-08-leftovers.md#remaining-differences)
left in portal, projectile and dragon code:

1. the Nether portal countdown as Java's `PortalProcessor` (travel on the
   81st tick, 4 off the count a tick outside), the purple overlay and the
   trigger sound on the client's own count, and a player who turns
   spectator the tick before travelling;
2. projectiles: a bounced arrow's 170 to 190° turn, rain and water putting
   out burning arrows, tridents carrying fire as Java's do, llama spit
   flying on through players and mobs, and Java's blame for the blast of a
   crystal the spit breaks;
3. the dragon in bubble columns.

All three are done. What remains is [listed below](#remaining-differences).

## What changed

### The portal countdown (item 1, GEN-04c)

`world::portal::PortalProcessor` is Java's class: `enter` is
`setAsInsidePortal` off cooldown (a new count at 0, or the running one
marked inside), `process` is `processPortalTeleportation` and `hasExpired`
(`allowed && portalTime++ >= delay`, or 4 off outside; a count at 0 goes).

- `Game::process_portal` runs at the start of the tick, before the player
  moves, as `handlePortal` runs in `baseTick`: the cooldown counts down,
  then the count. `Game::update_portal`, after the move, marks the player
  inside (`entityInside`). A survivor who steps in on tick 0 goes on tick
  81 (the game went on the 80th), and outside the count loses 4 a tick
  (it lost 2). `canUsePortal(false)` gates the count: riding, dead or
  asleep, the mark is used up and nothing counts.
- The count carries on across the trip, as the same `ServerPlayer` keeps
  its `portalProcess`: a player who steps straight back in after the
  cooldown carries on from where it has run down to. An End portal trip
  or a respawn starts a fresh one.
- Arriving sets Java's 10-tick cooldown, and standing in a portal on
  cooldown restarts it (`setAsInsidePortal`), so the player has to be out
  of the exit portal for half a second before it takes them again. Before,
  stepping out for one tick rearmed it. The save-failure lock stays.
- With `allow_entering_nether_using_portals` off the count still completes
  and starts the cooldown, then the trip is refused
  (`teleportToPortalDestination`); before, the count ran on without end.
- **The spectator case.** Because the count now runs before the move and
  game-mode changes land between ticks, a player who turns spectator after
  the count's last tick in the portal still goes on the next tick, as in
  Java (the `ServerPlayer` handles its move packet, then the command, then
  ticks). As in `getExitPortal`, a spectator goes only to a portal already
  there: `travel` asks the destination's save without loading it (Track
  5b's `saved_portal_near`, restored with its test, and `find_portal_in`
  public again), doesn't travel when there is none, and the arrival never
  builds one for a trip that set off as a spectator (`PortalTrip::spectator`).
- **The client's side.** The purple and the trigger sound read the
  client's own intensity, which `Distortion` already kept: +0.0125 a tick
  inside, −0.05 outside. `Game::portal_effect` uses up the client's mark
  each tick (`handlePortalTransitionEffect`), plays `PORTAL_TRIGGER` when
  the intensity is 0, and closes the inventory or any open container
  (`isAllowedInPortal`). The overlay's strength is
  `extractPortalOverlay`'s curve (`a⁴ × 0.8 + 0.2` below 1) on the flat
  purple, where it was the server count over the delay (full at once in
  creative, and 40 ticks to fade). As in Java the intensity carries
  through the trip and fades out on arrival.

### Arrows and tridents turn as Java's (item 2, PRO-05)

Both are `AbstractArrow`s: `yRot` and `xRot` take the launch motion and
then, each tick in flight, move a fifth of the way toward the motion
(`lerpRotation`, the short way round), so a falling arrow's nose lags its
path a little. `ProjectileDeflection.REVERSE` adds 170 to 190° to the yaw
and keeps the pitch, so a bounced arrow or trident now flies back turned
roughly about and eases round to its motion over the next ticks; before,
it pointed along its motion at once. This covers the perched or dying
dragon, decorations that refuse a mob's arrow, breezes, and every trident
bounce. `Arrow::dir` and `Trident::dir`, which the renderer and sculk
read, point along the new rotation; a returning trident's yaw looks back
along its motion while its pitch doesn't, as Java's.

A decoration that refuses a mob's arrow now sends it back at a tenth of
its speed (`deflect(REVERSE, .., 0.2)`: −0.5 × 0.2), not a fifth, and an
arrow left almost still drops as its item, as off the dragon.

### Burning arrows and tridents (item 2, PRO-05)

- Fire and lava light both (`FIRE_IGNITE`'s `igniteForSeconds(8)`,
  `LAVA_IGNITE`'s 15), checked over the cells their 0.5 × 0.5 box sweeps
  through in flight and touches while stuck (every cell is a full block
  to `checkInsideBlocks` for fire, lava and water). A perched dragon now
  sets a trident alight too (`Boxes::perched`, the dragons perched as the
  tick's boxes were taken).
- Water and rain put both out: at the start of each tick
  (`isInWaterOrRain`) and after each move (`EXTINGUISH`, then `isInRain`),
  rain falling on the cell or on the top of the box. Flame is Java's
  `igniteForSeconds(100)` at the shot, so water or rain puts a Flame arrow
  out for good (`Arrow::doused`); before, nothing did.
- The fire burns down only in flight: stuck, an `AbstractArrow` skips
  `super.tick()`, so a burning arrow in a block burns until rain or water
  reaches it. Before, it burned down while stuck too.
- A burning arrow or trident lights a campfire (unless waterlogged) and
  primes TNT it hits, as well as candles; a mob's only with `mob_griefing`
  on (`Projectile.mayInteract`). Before, only a burning arrow's candle
  worked. `ThrownTrident.onHitEntity` sets nothing alight, so a burning
  trident's hits on mobs and players don't.
- A trident's fire is saved with it (`fire`, Java's `Fire`); older saves
  load with none. Arrows still aren't saved.

### Llama spit and the crystal's blame (item 2, PRO-05)

- `LlamaSpit.onHitEntity` never discards the spit: it now hurts a player
  or mob in its way by 1 and flies on, as it already did through End
  crystals. Each is hurt once (`Thrown::spat_on` keeps the last four it
  hit, standing in for their hurt cooldown, which refuses the same 1
  again). With its llama gone it hurts no one, players included (before,
  players were hurt regardless).
- `EndCrystal.hurtServer` blows up as `explosion(crystal,
  source.getEntity())`. A crystal broken by a mob's projectile (a llama's
  or trader llama's spit, a skeleton's, stray's, bogged's, parched's,
  pillager's, piglin's or illusioner's arrow, a drowned's trident, a snow
  golem's snowball, a breeze's wind charge, a shulker's bullet, a Wither's
  skull) now blows up as a `player_explosion` naming that mob ("was blown
  up by a llama"), through `dragon::Blow::blame` and `crystal::Blow::Mob`.
  An ownerless projectile's blast is still the crystal's own
  (`END_CRYSTAL`).

### The dragon in bubble columns (item 3, PRO-05)

`checkWalls`' `removeBlock` replaces a bubble column cell with its fluid's
legacy block, a water source, which is a change, so it counts as broken
(level event 2008's particle). The dragon now breaks bubble column cells
with `mob_griefing` on (they are walls with it off, as before), and
`World::break_blocks` turns them into water sources, from which the
column re-forms on the next bubble tick (0.25 s, Java's five-tick
`scheduleTick`). Still and flowing water stay as they were.

## Remaining differences

- **End portals** still take the player in the tick they touch one; Java
  sends them through the same `PortalProcessor` (delay 0), so on the next
  tick, and an End portal touched on cooldown restarts the cooldown
  instead. Gateways already follow the cooldown.
- **A refused trip into the Nether** (`allow_entering_nether_using_portals`
  off): Java builds the Nether's exit portal before refusing it
  (`getPortalDestination` comes first); here the Nether is left alone.
- **Other screens in a portal:** the inventory and containers close; the
  sign editor, the only other non-pause screen, stays open.
- **Mobs that refuse an arrow** otherwise than the dragon, decorations and
  breezes (Java's `hurtOrSimulate` false on a living target, such as an
  invulnerable mob) still take the arrow away rather than bouncing it
  back turned about; and VoxelCraft's mobs have no hurt cooldown for
  arrows to bounce off.
- **Drawing fire:** Java draws flames on a burning arrow or trident
  (`displayFireAnimation`); VoxelCraft draws none, Flame arrows included.
  That is model and renderer work (Track 7c's area).
- **Fire on the box's sweep:** fire, lava and water are found over the
  cells the box touches at quarter-block samples along the move, where
  Java walks every cell the swept box crosses; a corner clipped between
  samples can be missed.
- **A player's crystal blast** keeps the game's "was blown up by an End
  crystal" and its `explosion` type, where Java's `explosion(crystal,
  player)` is a `player_explosion` naming the player; mobs hurt by a
  mob-blamed blast don't turn on that mob, and a primed TNT from a burning
  projectile names no igniter (`prime(.., owner)`).
- Track 8's other remaining differences (particles and rain near the
  portal, the portal texture and sounds, projectile geometry, non-players
  through End portals) stay as recorded there.
