## Implemented paths

Hearts retain axis, activity and natural state in chunks. Two pale oak log-tag
blocks with matching axes activate a heart; an existing protector retains its
binding when the logs are removed. The authoritative day clock uses the pinned
12600-inclusive / 23401-exclusive creaking-active interval. Both natural and
player-built hearts can spawn a non-persistent protector near a player at night;
Peaceful and the existing spawning switch prevent new protectors. There are five
spawn attempts, within 16 blocks horizontally and eight vertically, on solid
non-leaf support. Hearts retain one protector UID, compute comparator output
`15 - floor(clamp(distance, 0, 32) / 32 * 15)` and notify the circuit on changes.

The dimension saves `creaking_hearts`; the mob component saves its home position
and appearance state through the existing name-based mob store. Chunk registration
and edits maintain a loaded-heart set; unloaded records remain saved and do not
tick. Console/agent summons use the common name registry. Mob kind and component
appearance replicate through the existing LAN snapshots.

Creakings have one health, a 0.9 × 2.7 collision box, movement attribute 0.4,
attack damage 3, follow range 32 and navigation step 1.0625. A survival player's
look can activate one within 12 blocks; an active one freezes when any eligible
player within 32 looks through its fixed 60-degree cone at one of three heights.
Frozen creakings do not travel or attack and cannot be pushed by mob separation.
Bound creakings ignore ordinary damage and knockback; player-credited hits produce
an eight-tick twitch and notify the heart. They do not distance-despawn while
bound. Breaking the heart initiates a 45-tick twitch/teardown without mob loot.
Original cuboids, procedural bark and amber eyes render the creature; its ambient,
hurt and death calls use original synthesized wood resonances and crackles.

An awake hurt heart tries 2–3 resin placements, then waits 100 ticks before another
emission. A shuffled breadth-first search visits pale oak logs up to depth two,
with a 64-position bound, adding one face to a neighboring clump, air cell or
water source. Clumps reuse glow lichen's placement, support loss, selection,
non-collision and paired waterlogged-state code. Mining gives one clump per face.
A heart gives 1–3 clumps plus a uniform Fortune bonus (maximum nine), or the heart
under Silk Touch. Resin construction has creative entries, shared placement and
mining, recipes, brick smelting (0.1 XP, Java's `smelting/resin_brick`; the first slice
had 0.3, which `tests/java_recipes.rs` caught), stonecutting recipe data, the complete
stairs/slab state families and the project's four-state wall representation.

### Eyeblossoms (2026-11-06)

Open and closed eyeblossoms are cross-model flowers with Java's
`#supports_vegetation`-style soil rule (the shared `dirt_like` set, which
includes moss and pale moss), zero hardness, fire odds 60/100, compost
chance 0.65, the orange/gray dye recipes and the two suspicious-stew recipes
(the existing mooshroom and stew table already maps them to nausea 7 s and
blindness 11 s), the bee flower hooks and the creative palette. Flower pots
take both; the potted states drop the pot and the flower, give the flower on
pick-block and empty by hand through the shared pot code.

`EyeblossomBlock.tryChangingState` runs on random ticks and scheduled ticks.
The Overworld clock supplies Java's `gameplay/eyeblossom_open` keyframes (open
from tick 12600, closed from 23401); other dimensions leave the attribute
undefined, so flowers keep their state there. A flower that changes schedules
each flower of its old kind within ±3/±2/±3 to tick after
`nextIntBetweenInclusive(5d, 10d)` ticks (`d` the distance) through the
existing saved scheduled-tick queue, so patches ripple open at dusk and shut
at dawn.

### Generation 3 pale gardens (2026-11-06)

Generation 3 pale garden trees now use Java's `pale_oak` feature shape:
`DarkOakTrunkPlacer(6, 2, 1)` (2×2 trunk 6–9 tall bending up to two blocks
from a random height, side branches of 2–4 logs at each of the twelve ring
positions one time in three), `DarkOakFoliagePlacer(0, 0)` rows for the
double-trunk and branch attachments with its skip rules, and
`PaleMossDecorator` trunk (0.4) and leaf (0.15) hangers that grow while the
cell below is free and a 50% roll continues. One tree in ten is
`pale_oak_creaking` (`pale_garden_vegetation`'s 0.1 selector); its
`CreakingHeartDecorator(1.0)` puts a dormant, natural, Y-axis heart in the
first shuffled log whose six neighbours are logs. About a third of creaking
trees have such a log (the unit test counts 40–400 hearts over 400 seeded
trees; branches enclose a trunk cell often but not always).

Closed eyeblossoms generate from `pale_garden_flowers` (one chunk in eight,
a pale-garden centre, 96 tries offset by trapezoid ±7/±3/±7) and
`flower_pale_garden` (one chunk in 32, one flower), placed only in air on a
valid soil. Attempts from the neighbouring chunks' patches are replayed so
the result does not depend on generation order. Generations 1 and 2 are
untouched; their golden chunk hashes still pass.

## Unfinished work and differences

- Random draws use the engine's SplitMix64 streams (world, entity and
  positional generation hashes), not Java's random sequences. Counts, ranges,
  odds and shapes follow the reference.
- Generation 3 keeps the engine's tree-cell placement density for pale
  gardens (85% of 5×5 cells) rather than `pale_garden_vegetation`'s count of
  16 per chunk with its own placement filters; only the tree shape, the
  creaking selector and the decorators follow Java. The decorator's
  six-log test and the moss hangers see only the tree's own blocks, not
  neighbouring trees or terrain. `PaleMossDecorator`'s ground moss patch is
  left to the biome's existing pale moss surface. Sapling-grown pale oaks still
  use the older shape (Java's `pale_oak_bonemeal` is the same placers without
  decorators).
- Eyeblossom patches use the column height as the `MOTION_BLOCKING_NO_LEAVES`
  heightmap and skip attempts on the lowest cell of a storage chunk, whose soil
  lies in the chunk below. Generated flowers do not get Java's `schedule_tick`
  first tick; they flip on their next random or neighbour tick instead.
- Eyeblossom ambient sounds (`EYEBLOSSOM_IDLE` on pale moss), the switch
  sounds, transform particles, the open flower's emissive layer and the bee
  poison effect from `entityInside` are not implemented. The wandering
  trader's eyeblossom offer is not added (the engine's trader has no Java
  trade table yet).
- The creaking brain is a bounded pursuit implementation using existing ground
  navigation. It does not port Java's complete brain activity scheduling, idle
  stroll/look selectors, home path evaluation or player-stuck sensor.
- The watch sensor clips the shared collision shapes rather than Java's
  VISUAL shape mode; transparent-block and disguise-item cases remain open.
  Activation and watch sensing run each simulation step rather than Java's
  sensor cadence.
- Heart state refresh and day/range removal run each simulation step rather
  than on Java's 20-tick countdown; spawn retries use a 20–24-tick delay.
  Spawn placement tests three air cells instead of Java's complete SpawnUtil
  strategy. Day/range removal shares the heart-break teardown rather than
  Java's immediate discard path, and teardown keeps health until removal.
- Heart unresolved-UID grace is loaded time. Cross-chunk protector
  unload/reload, replacement hearts, naming persistence and dimension
  transitions still need broader lifecycle evidence.
- Resin trails are original transient particle requests, not Java's exact
  bidirectional trail/heart-call timings, and are not LAN replicated.
  Specialized spawn/attack/step/heart-call sounds and emissive eye rendering
  remain unfinished; only ambient/hurt/death calls exist.
- Natural-heart mining awards the reference 20–24 XP, including Silk Touch.
  Player-credited explosion XP is not wired.
- Damage-source distinctions (projectile owner, pet credit, invulnerability
  bypass, potion effects and attacks lacking a knockback source) need further
  parity work.
- The stonecutter's interactive screen is deferred to v0.17; this slice
  provides its resin recipe data.
- Owner play, sound inspection, process-restart and representative mob-scene
  performance evidence remain unverified. No feature is marked Verified.
