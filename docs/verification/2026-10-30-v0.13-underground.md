## Blocks (gameplay; every world)

`world::cave_blocks` registers every Java 26.3 state of six blocks in
track 3's reservation, with original procedural textures
(`render/cave_textures.rs`) and item forms through `Item::from_block`.

| Block | States | Light | Mining | Drops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glow lichen | 63 face sets × waterlogged | 7 | 0.2, axe (shears 2, sword 1.5 by tag) | shears: one per face; nothing otherwise |
| Sculk vein | 63 face sets × waterlogged | 0 | 0.2, hoe | silk touch: one per face; nothing otherwise |
| Sculk | 1 | 0 | 0.2, hoe | silk touch: itself; otherwise 1 XP |
| Sculk catalyst | `bloom` | 6 | 3.0, hoe | silk touch: itself; otherwise 5 XP |
| Sculk sensor | phase (inactive, active, cooldown) × waterlogged | 1 | 1.5, hoe | silk touch: itself; otherwise 5 XP |
| Sculk shrieker | `shrieking` × `can_summon` × waterlogged | 0 | 3.0, hoe | silk touch: itself; otherwise 5 XP |

- **Multiface blocks** (glow lichen, sculk vein) have one bit per face in
  Java's `Direction` order (down, up, north, south, west, east). They are
  drawn and outlined as one-sixteenth plates on their faces, have no
  collision, are replaceable and non-solid, and take the alpha-tested pass.
  Placing one adds the first face, in the player's nearest-looking order
  (`Direction.orderedByNearest`), that is free and has a full face behind
  it; placing into an existing block of the same kind adds a face. A face
  whose support goes falls off silently, and a block with no face left
  leaves its water (`MultifaceBlock.updateShape`). Bone meal spreads glow
  lichen by one face (same cell, same plane or around the edge, as
  `MultifaceSpreader` does); veins don't take bone meal. Glow lichen
  composts with chance 50% (`compost.json`). Glow lichen burns
  (15, 100) and sounds like plants; the sculk blocks use the dirt sounds
  (the nearest shared material).
- **Sensor and shrieker** are half-block (`Block.column(16, 0, 8)`) shaped
  blocks: the sensor with two crossed tendril planes, the shrieker with its
  bony rim. **Sculk and the catalyst** are full cubes.
- **Inert until v0.16.** The catalyst doesn't bloom or spread sculk when
  mobs die, the sensor doesn't hear vibrations or give redstone power, and
  the shrieker doesn't shriek, warn or summon. Their states exist so v0.16
  needs no new ids. The calibrated sculk sensor isn't registered (v0.16).
- Creative palette and `give`/`setblock` by name work as for other blocks
  (`glow_lichen`, `sculk`, `sculk_vein`, `sculk_catalyst`, `sculk_sensor`,
  `sculk_shrieker`).

## Deep dark (generation 2)

Java places the deep dark as a bottom cave biome under the two lowest
erosion bands (`-1.0..-0.375`) at a depth parameter of about 1.1. Under
FE-01 VoxelCraft applies that rule to its own climate: erosion below
-0.375, at least 120 blocks under the column's surface, at or below y = 0.
There it takes precedence over lush and dripstone caves (also in
`biome_at`, `/locate` and the cave decoration, generation 2 only).
`Biome::DeepDark` is appended to the enum; biomes are never saved, so no
save format changes. It has no mob spawns, like Java's.

The roadmap's scope text says "high-erosion inland ground". Java's rule is
the *low* erosion parameter (un-eroded, mountainous ground) with no
continentalness limit, and that is what is implemented. **Owner decision
wanted** if the roadmap wording was meant literally.

Sculk: Java grows patches by sending charges out from random origins
(`SculkPatchFeature`). That is order-dependent across chunks here, so each
cell decides from world coordinates instead: a 3D patch noise turns
exposed replaceable stone into sculk where it is high (about 60% of
exposed stone in the test area: 687 of 1,081), veins line stone faces in a band under
the threshold plus 2.5% of other faces, catalysts sit in patch cores
(1 in 220 sculk floors), and sensors stand on sculk floors (1.2% of open
cells over sculk), one in eleven a shrieker with `can_summon` set (Java's
world-generated shriekers can summon). No glow lichen or cave plants grow
in deep-dark cells. Ancient cities are v0.16.

## Glow lichen (generation 2)

Java: `glow_lichen` placed feature, 104–157 tries per chunk, at least 13
blocks under the surface, walls and ceilings only, on stone, andesite,
diorite, granite, dripstone, calcite, tuff and deepslate, spreading with
chance 0.5. Here: clusters from a 3D noise over open cave cells at least 13
blocks under the surface and outside the deep dark; each wall or ceiling
face of a listed stone gets lichen with chance 0.7; waterlogged in water.
Density was tuned by eye from the test areas rather than matched to a
Java count (FE-01).

## Springs (generation 2)

Java's `spring_water` (25 tries per chunk, uniform from the bottom to
y = 192) and `spring_lava` (20 tries, `very_biased_to_bottom` from the
bottom to 8 under the top, inner 8), with `SpringFeature.place`'s rule:
valid stone above and below, the origin stone or air, and of the four
sides and the block below exactly four valid stone and one air. Tries are
hashed per Java 16×16 cell. A spring needs all its checked cells in the
chunk being generated (tries on a chunk's outer layer are skipped, a
small loss). Generated chunks schedule their springs' first fluid tick
(`GeneratedChunk::new`), so they pour out when the chunk is simulated.
The frozen lava spring variant (snowy slopes, frozen peaks) is not
implemented.

## Ore pass (generation 2)

Checked every placed feature in `world::ore` against Java's placed
features: counts, height providers, sizes and air-exposure discard
chances already match (coal upper/lower, iron upper/middle/small, copper,
gold, gold lower, badlands gold, redstone, redstone lower, lapis, buried
lapis, diamond, rare, medium and buried diamond, mountain emeralds,
granite/diorite/andesite, tuff, dirt and gravel). Gaps found and fixed for
generation 2:

1. **Large copper only in dripstone caves.** Java gives dripstone caves
   `ore_copper_large` (size 20) *instead of* `ore_copper` (size 10).
   Generation 1 placed both everywhere. Generation 2 picks by the cave
   biome at the vein's origin.
2. **Large ore veins** (Java's `OreVeinifier`, missing entirely): copper
   ore in granite at y 0..=50 and deepslate iron ore in tuff at y -60..=-8,
   with raw copper and raw iron blocks. Java's thresholds are kept
   (veininess 0.4, edge roundoff over 20 blocks, solidness 0.7, richness
   0.1–0.3, raw block chance 0.02, gap -0.3, ridge 0.08), applied to this
   engine's Perlin noise with original frequencies (toggle period about 170
   blocks, ridges about 24, gap 32).

Known differences kept: ore air-exposure checks see only the chunk being
generated, so a vein face exposed only across a chunk border isn't
discarded; infested stone (windswept hills) isn't registered. Biome
filters for copper read the 3D cave biome; mountain emeralds and badlands
gold still read the surface biome, as in generation 1.

## Continuity and determinism

Decisions are per cell from the seed and world position. Exposure of
stone across a chunk border reads the pristine terrain
(`Generator::natural_block`); attachments (lichen and vein faces, sensors,
springs) only use support inside the chunk being generated, so nothing
floats if the pristine-terrain guess is wrong (aquifer barriers, ravines).
The pass runs after structures, as Java's decoration steps do.
