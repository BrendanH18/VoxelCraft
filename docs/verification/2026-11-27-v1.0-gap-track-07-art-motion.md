## What changed

### Motion (MOB-06)

The shared code is [`src/entity/motion.rs`](../../src/entity/motion.rs);
[player rendering](../player-rendering.md#mob-motion-against-javas-client-v10-gap-track-7)
summarises it.

1. **Walk state.** `Mob::limb_phase`/`limb_amp` now hold Java's
   `WalkAnimationState` position and speed, advanced once a tick from the
   distance the mob moved in that tick (it used to follow the velocity in
   radians a second): speed `+= (min(distance * 4, 1) - speed) * 0.4`,
   position `+= speed`; the camel's (`* 6`, factor 0.2), frog's (`* 25`)
   and creaking's (`* 25`, capped at 3) overrides; bees and parrots (and a
   sulfur cube holding a block) count vertical movement. A hit sets the
   speed to 1.5 (`handleDamageEvent`); a dead or riding mob's walk stops.
   `Mob::render_pose` hands models Java's `walkAnimationPos` (`position -
   speed * (1 - partial)`, times 3 for babies) and `walkAnimationSpeed`
   (lerped, capped at 1). LAN replicas run the same from each snapshot's
   movement, as Java's client does. The copper golem's step sound keeps a
   step each half leg cycle in the new units.
2. **Limbs per family**, each on its Java model's formula (signs and
   phases included; this game's models are Java's turned through their
   origin, so every rotation keeps its sign):
   - quadrupeds (pig, cow, mooshroom, sheep, polar bear, panda, goat),
     creeper, llama and trader llama, wolf and fox: `cos(pos * 0.6662) *
     1.4 * speed`, diagonal pairs together (the wolf, fox and goat used to
     pace, both legs of a side together); the wolf's tail wags on the
     same wave unless angry;
   - humanoids (zombie, husk, drowned, zombie villager, skeleton, stray,
     bogged, parched, wither skeleton, piglin, piglin brute, zombified
     piglin, enderman): legs as above, arms `cos * speed` opposite, the
     idle arm bob (`bobModelPart`); skeletons hold the bow out
     (`ArmPose.ITEM`) and aim with both arms (`BOW_AND_ARROW`); the
     enderman halves and clamps every limb to 0.4 and lifts an angry head
     5 pixels (it used to hold its arms forward, which Java's doesn't);
   - villagers, wandering traders and witches: legs at half stride;
     illagers: arms `cos * speed`, legs at half stride, the riding pose on
     a ravager, and every arm pose (`IllagerModel`);
   - cats and ocelots (`AdultFelineModel`): amplitude 1, the left hind
     leading, a gallop with pairs 0.3 apart when sprinting (the cat's hind
     legs used to stay still and its front legs swung while standing);
   - horses, donkeys, mules and undead horses: front 0.8 and hind 0.5 of
     `cos(pos * 0.6662 + pi) * speed`, a fifth as fast in water, the head
     nodding above speed 0.2;
   - hoglin and zoglin `cos(pos) * 1.2`; ravager 0.4; strider (speed capped
     at 0.25, body rocking and bobbing, legs lifting, the body following
     the look when unridden); spider (sweep at twice the frequency, lift
     at the frequency, the four pairs a quarter cycle apart); iron golem
     (13-unit triangle waves); turtle (sweeping on land, rowing off it);
     warden (`animateWalk`);
   - keyframe-animated mobs (armadillo, camel, copper golem, creaking,
     frog on land and swimming, sniffer): their own drawn limbs, run on
     Java's `applyWalk` loop length (`pos * 50 * speedFactor` ms over the
     definition's length) and strength (`min(speed * scaleFactor, 1)`).
3. **Attack swings.** Java's `SwingState` (6 ticks, no restart in its
   first half, `getAnimation` lerping on through a restart) starts when an
   attack sets `attack_anim`. Zombies and zombified piglins raise their
   arms (higher while chasing, `animateZombieArms`) and swing them;
   other humanoids twist the body and sweep the arm (`WHACK`); armed
   illagers, piglins and brutes swing the weapon down (`swingWeaponDown`),
   holding it high between strikes; unarmed attacking illagers swing like
   zombies. The iron golem throws both arms over its 10-tick
   `attackAnimationTick` (it had no attack pose; its `attack_anim` is now
   10 ticks, not 8); the hoglin and zoglin butt the head from 50 degrees
   down to 20 up and back over 10 ticks (their `attack_anim` is 10 ticks,
   not 7); the ravager's neck lunges up to 12 pixels and its mouth opens,
   a stunned ravager droops and sways, a roaring one gapes, each counter
   less the partial tick. `attack_anim` is visual only.
4. **Death topple.** `sqrt((deathTime - 1) / 20 * 1.6)`, capped at 1,
   turns the mob about its feet onto its left side (spiders, cave
   spiders, silverfish and endermites onto their backs); `deathTime`
   counts the ticks since death with the partial tick. It used to rise
   linearly over 0.54 s onto the right side, lifted half a width off the
   ground. Removal follows Java's 20 ticks: `DEATH_TIME` is 18.5 ticks of
   `dying`, so the last frame drawn is `deathTime` 19, since this game
   removes the dead at the start of a tick (it was 18 ticks).
5. **Hurt flash.** Java's red overlay is on or off, not faded: on for the
   10 ticks of `hurtTime` (counted in whole ticks) and from death on. The
   entity shader mixes it as `entity.fsh` does with `OverlayTexture`'s red
   row (alpha 0xB2): 70 % of the face-shaded texel and 30 % red, in sRGB,
   then the light. It used to fade from a 60 % dark red over half a second.

### Art (UX-01)

A new child of Track 10's generator,
[`src/render/final_art_c_textures.rs`](../../src/render/final_art_c_textures.rs),
with Track 10's primitives (light from the upper left, cut-out sprites):

- **Amethyst buds and cluster** (layers 1475-1482: small, medium, large
  and cluster standing, then hanging): violet prism shards with a lit left
  face, a pale glint streak, a shaded right face and a pointed, faceted
  tip; one stage's shards stay in place in the next, which adds taller
  ones (5, 8, 11 and 14 texels tall). The hanging tiles are the standing
  ones upside down; wall buds use the standing tiles as before. The
  cluster was drawn by the same old generator as the buds, so it is
  redrawn with them.
- **Bamboo leaves as crossed planes.** Leafy bamboo (states 2023 and
  2024) now draws Java's `bamboo_small_leaves`/`bamboo_large_leaves`
  geometry: two planes crossing at the middle of the cell beside the post
  (Java's run 0.8 to 15.2; these 1 to 15), in the alpha-tested pass. The
  post shows the bare stalk, as Java's always does; layers 1568 and 1569
  are redrawn from 10b's leafy stalks into leaf sprites (narrow leaves
  fanning up and out from the middle; the large ones more, longer and
  lower). The planes are mesh-only (`mesh::shaped_boxes`): collision and
  the outline stay the post.
- **Potted bamboo** (layer 2612): the pot shared with every potted plant
  (`utility::potted_side`) and the cane its post samples drawn like the
  bamboo stalk (lit left, shaded right, a node at row 2), replacing the
  flat striped green. Its **single leaf** is Java's `potted_bamboo` leaf
  plane from (0, 2, 8) to (16, 18, 8), cut at the top of the cell, with a
  leaf sprite on the one new layer, **4066**; it is mesh-only too.
- The review sheet: ignored test `render::textures::tests::final_art_c_sheet`
  writes these 12 layers to `$VC_FINAL_ART_C_SHEET`.
- Asset manifest (`procedural_resources.material_art`): provenance gains
  this round; 772 tiles; the cache budget rises from 768 to 800 KiB; the
  block array grows by one layer (5,547,388 bytes).
- A capture scene, `material-amethyst-bamboo` (`materials` group): the
  four stages standing on budding amethyst, hanging from an amethyst
  ledge and on its wall; bamboo bare and with small and large leaves on
  posts one to three blocks tall; the potted bamboo beside a potted fern.

## Remaining differences

- **Model shapes.** Motion follows each Java model's formulas, but on this
  game's own models, whose parts and pivots differ from Java's (a
  hand-made zombie villager without arms, a ravager with its own neck).
  The keyframe-animated mobs keep their drawn limbs on Java's loop, not
  Java's keyframes. Not converted: the chicken's and parrot's flap state
  (`Chicken.flap`, `flapSpeed`: the wings still flap on a timer), the
  rabbit's hop, fish, squid, dolphins, guardians, bats, bees, the allay,
  vex, phantom, blaze rods, silverfish, ghast, breeze, shulker and the
  Wither, whose animations aren't walk-driven; the pillager's crossbow
  draw stays at its start (no use timer is kept); the drowned's trident
  and swimming poses; humanoid swimming (`setupSwimAnimation`); the
  equine tail and rearing; Java 26.3's separate baby models (babies are
  this game's adults scaled).
- **Mirroring.** This game's humanoid models hold the weapon on the
  model's +X side, which is the mob's own left in the world; limb phases
  follow the models' own right and left. The death topple turns toward
  the mob's left in the world, as Java's does.
- **`ageInTicks`** for the idle bob comes from the shared clock, so mobs
  bob in step; Java counts each mob's own ticks.
- **Overlay.** The red part of the overlay takes the face shade here (the
  light and shade are one factor in this shader); Java's red is unshaded.
  The creeper's white fuse flash is unchanged.
- **Bamboo.** The planes run 1 to 15 rather than 0.8 to 15.2, and the
  post is this game's single 4 × 4 post (Java's is 2 or 3 texels by age,
  offset at random); the potted bamboo's leaf plane stops at the top of
  the cell instead of 2/16 above it, and its post starts at the soil.
