## 1. What changed

### A headless long-session soak

`src/soak.rs`, run by `examples/soak.rs` (CSV under `target/`) and, for a
few simulated minutes, by `tests/soak.rs`. The
[development guide](../development.md#long-session-soak) says how to run it.

- **The player** is a creative-mode `Agent`: the CLI-agent player, with its
  real session tick (`Agent::tick_rules`) every game tick. It moves
  kinematically along each leg's route and flies (creative flight), so it
  can't get stuck or die. In the Overworld it follows the generator's
  surface above sea level, as the benchmark routes do. In the Nether and
  End it follows the first floor under a two-block gap in the loaded world.
- **The world** runs the library's steps of `Game::tick` in its order,
  following the headless tick benchmark (`src/tick_bench.rs`). Each tick:
  - simulation players, the world rules and difficulty, rain and thunder;
  - the agent's tick;
  - redstone daylight and contacts, and the container viewers;
  - `tick_world_rules` at random tick speed 3, with fire spread on;
  - snow pushes and piston pushes;
  - the automation entity pass;
  - beacons, crafter events, conduits and potent sulfur;
  - the mob update (`sync_chunk_entities`, primed TNT, silverfish, golems,
    frog and turtle hatches, lightning, `update_world` with natural
    spawning);
  - block drops and experience;
  - the day clock.

  What the desktop's audio, particles and HUD take each frame is drained.
  Rain falls a quarter of the time, with thunder within it now and then.
- **Streaming** goes through `World::update_players` with the desktop's
  2 ms light budget. Meshes are built by the workers and dropped. Ticks run
  as fast as the machine allows, but the player only moves once every chunk
  within 4 (128 blocks) of it, one row below to one row above, has loaded.
  Without that, unpaced ticks outran the workers and simulated an almost
  empty world (the first run did; see §3).
- **The base** is in the Overworld at spawn. It holds:
  - a 7 x 7 wheat farm around a water source, replanted on each visit;
  - the reference contraptions' observer-and-piston sugar cane farm;
  - an observer clock driving dust and a lamp;
  - a repeater loop started by a button;
  - a chest–hopper–hopper–chest chain and four furnaces, refilled on each
    visit;
  - a zombie spawner in the open.
- **The itinerary** repeats six legs of `--leg-minutes` (default 10) each:
  1. idle at the base;
  2. walk out at sprint speed (5.612 blocks/s) on this cycle's heading,
     the golden angle on from the last;
  3. fly on at creative sprint-flight speed (21.6 blocks/s), 24 blocks up;
  4. walk in the Nether;
  5. fly in the Nether (10.92 blocks/s, 10 up, under the roof);
  6. fly out across the End from the obsidian platform.
- **Saving.** It autosaves every 5 simulated minutes (Java's 6000 ticks),
  through `Storage::save` and the same properties as `Game::save`. Before
  each Overworld walk it quits and reopens the world: the world, its
  workers and entities are dropped, and new ones load from disk. Every
  change of dimension saves and reopens the same way, as `switch_dimension`
  does. Dimension properties are copied from `Game::dimension_props` and
  `restore_dimension`, which live in the binary.
- **Samples** are taken every `--sample-seconds` (default 60) of simulated
  time:
  - resident memory, its peak and the thread count (`/proc/self/status`);
  - mean and worst tick time, ticks over 50 ms and streaming time per tick;
  - wall time spent waiting for terrain;
  - the last save's time and the save folder's size;
  - the last reopen's time;
  - every entry count `World::diagnostics` and `Entities::diagnostics`
    report (below);
  - the size of each saved property.

### Diagnostics

`World::diagnostics` (`src/world/diagnostics.rs`) and
`Entities::diagnostics` (`src/entity/diagnostics.rs`) name and count every
queue, cache and retained collection either holds. Small `soak_counts`
helpers read private state in `lighting`, `redstone`, `redstone_order`,
`fluid`, `fire`, `lightning`, `automation`, `piston`, `sculk`,
`chunk_storage`, `mob_index` and `mob`. `particles::Requests` gained `len`.

They cover:

- loaded chunks (and how many are modified), modified unloaded chunks
  (`saved`, with heap bytes) and columns;
- the streaming queues;
- block and sky light;
- water, lava and bubble cells, including those held for unloaded chunks;
- fire schedules and lightning rods;
- every redstone and update-order map and queue, and automation;
- moving pistons and sculk;
- falling blocks, leaf decay and the per-position block-entity maps;
- every per-tick event list the game drains;
- every live entity list, entity storage (chunks, entities, mobs, items,
  claims) and the entity bookkeeping sets;
- the mob index, separation grid and mob path nodes.

The generator's structure caches were checked by reading the code instead.
Each is capped (`CACHE_LIMIT` 256 or 512, column caches 8,192, the chorus
cache 256), and the stronghold cache holds at most the world's 128
strongholds.

### Fixes from the soak

1. **Fluid cells of unloaded chunks stayed queued** (`src/world/fluid.rs`).
   `wake_fluids` queued cells in any chunk, and nothing removed a cell
   whose chunk had unloaded. Each chunk the player streamed past left its
   generated springs' and lava's cells in `FluidState::pending`. Every
   water tick (and `World::active_fluids`, which `is_idle` calls) scanned
   them all: 362 to 26,460 cells after 20 Overworld minutes in the
   before-fix run, and 33,098 at most. Now:
   - an unloading chunk's water, lava and bubble cells leave the queues;
   - a player-modified chunk keeps them in `FluidState::suspended` for its
     return, as Java keeps a chunk's fluid ticks with it;
   - a generated chunk keeps none, because generating it again wakes the
     same cells;
   - `wake_fluids` schedules nothing in an unloaded chunk, as Java's
     `LevelTicks.schedule` refuses a position without a loaded container.

   Fluid ticks are still not saved across quitting (unchanged; Java saves
   them).
2. **Fire schedules of unloaded chunks stayed queued**
   (`src/world/fire.rs`). Their `LevelTicks`-style containers were peeked
   at every tick and never ran, because the chunk was outside the ticking
   region. They reached 355 entries in 229 columns in the before-fix run.
   An unloading chunk now drops its entries, as Java's `LevelTicks` drops
   the chunk's container. Its fires reschedule when it loads, through the
   existing `load_fires`.
3. **A cell rewritten with its own state marked its chunk modified**
   (`World::edit_exact_inner`). Fluid, redstone and growth paths rewrite
   unchanged cells, so the chunk counted as player-modified (saved, and
   kept once unloaded) and its shared data was copied. Java's
   `LevelChunk.setBlockState` changes nothing in that case, and now
   neither does this. The soak showed this wasn't what fills the save: the
   two 30-minute samples differ by 1% (7,153 and 7,089 modified unloaded
   chunks), within run-to-run noise. Finding 1 in §5 explains the real
   cause.

### The startup sound bank (owner decision, 2026-11-25)

v0.18 Track 9 had the audio thread synthesize every block family's sounds
at startup: 229 s of audio, 44 MB of the 150 MB bank. Now:

- `Bank` holds `Buffer`s, each a `OnceLock<Vec<f32>>` that reads as an
  empty slice until it is set. `Bank::empty` lays out every sound's
  buffers. `render_startup` fills every sound but the lazy ones on the
  audio thread at startup. `Bank::lazy` names the lazy sounds, which are
  the blocks' own (`Sound::Block`).
- **First use.** `Audio::play_in` checks `Bank::is_ready`. A lazy sound
  that isn't ready goes to the audio thread (`audio::serve`) as a `Synth`
  request. That thread renders its buffers (`Bank::ensure`) and only then
  hands the play to the mixer. The device callback never synthesizes and
  never blocks: it reads a `OnceLock` with `get`. If a not-ready play ever
  reached the mixer, the mixer would skip it.
- **Ahead of first use.** When the player steps onto a new block, or any
  of a block's sounds plays, `Audio::prefetch_block` asks for that block's
  step, break and place sounds without playing them. Most first uses then
  find the buffers ready.
- **Bit-identical.** Both paths run the same `Sound::render(variant)`, and
  the pop still uses its recorded sample. The digest of every buffer's bits
  (FNV-1a over `Sound::all` order) is `d1161dadbfcdaf97` for v0.18's bank,
  built from `3685593` on Boat. It is the same for this branch's eager bank
  and for its lazy bank once every lazy sound has been rendered.

Measured on Boat with `startup_bank_memory_and_time` (`--ignored`, run
twice in one process):

| | v0.18 eager bank | Startup bank now |
|---|---|---|
| Samples held | 149.9 MB | 105.9 MB |
| Synthesis on the audio thread | 478 ms, 363 ms | 268 ms, 251 ms |
| Process RSS growth while building it (first pass) | +168.3 MB (7.7 → 176.0 MB) | +112.5 MB (14.1 → 126.6 MB) |

- Rendering every lazy sound afterwards took 594 and 676 ms in total. The
  slowest single first use was `block_amethyst_cluster_break` at 21.8 and
  19.2 ms. That is the longest a never-prefetched first sound waits, on
  the audio thread and not the game's.
- Sounds already playing never stall: the mixer keeps mixing while the
  audio thread renders.
- The game holds the lazy buffers it has used for the rest of the session,
  at most the same 44 MB.

## 5. What still grows, and why

The fixed leaks were queues: entries that outlived their chunk and were
scanned every tick. What still grows is state the game keeps on purpose.
Java keeps it too, but on disk with each chunk, while VoxelCraft keeps it
in memory for the whole session. Over run C's 4 hours, which spend two of
every six legs travelling fast through new Overworld terrain, resident
memory rose about 450 MB an hour.

1. **Modified unloaded chunks (`World::saved`).** These are the largest
   share:
   - 28,223 chunks and 939 MB in memory after the third Overworld
     flight, and 505 MB on disk;
   - every autosave re-encodes and rewrites all of them on the main thread
     (2.2 s at the end);
   - every reopen loads them all (1.3 s).

   A diff of 4,852 of them against freshly generated chunks found:
   - at least 1 changed cell in every one, a median of 6, and 1,594 at
     most;
   - air turning to falling or flowing water (about 20,000 cells), as
     springs and aquifer edges flow after loading;
   - air to flowing or falling lava (about 1,000), and lava to obsidian
     (824);
   - grass to dirt under the new water (1,368), washed-away plants and
     kelp growth.

   Java changes these too, and saves every chunk anyway. The difference is
   only that VoxelCraft holds every modified chunk of the dimension in
   memory and rewrites one `chunks.bin` each save.
2. **Per-position block-entity state.** Chests (5,831, 745 KB saved),
   spawners (2,617), trial spawners and vaults (278 KB), archaeology,
   beehives, utilities and villager births are kept for every chunk ever
   loaded, and saved in `level.txt`.
3. **Stored entities.** 7,859 in unloaded chunks at 210 minutes:
   - 1,568 mobs: generation herds, which are persistent as in Java, and
     bastion and structure residents;
   - 6,291 items: drops from plants that flowing water washed away, which
     don't age while their chunk is unloaded (as in Java).

   Requirement PLY-01b already records that stored entities stay in memory
   rather than in `entities/` region files.
4. **Suspended fluid cells** of modified chunks (up to 42,474 cells, about
   1 MB). They follow the modified chunks and are lost on quit, as fluid
   ticks already were.

The generator's caches, the light, mesh and streaming queues, the entity
lists, the scheduled redstone, fire and fluid ticks, threads and tick time
did not grow.

## 7. Remaining gaps and open decisions

1. **Open decision: keep modified chunks, block entities and stored
   entities on disk, not in memory.** The Java-faithful default is Java's
   own: write each chunk, its block entities, entities and scheduled ticks
   to region storage when it unloads, read them back when it loads, and
   make autosave incremental. That bounds memory by what is loaded and save
   time by what changed. It changes the save format, and Track 1 owns the
   1.0 save policy (UX-05b), so this track measured it and did not
   implement it. Without it, memory and autosave time grow with the area a
   player has changed, roughly with the distance travelled.
2. **Autosave runs on the main thread.** `Game::save` encodes and writes
   every modified chunk synchronously: 2.2 s at the end of run C, so a
   long session's autosave becomes a visible hitch. Moving the write to a
   background thread (the chunk data is already shared `Arc` snapshots)
   would fix the hitch without the format change. It is left for the
   owner's decision with item 1, because it changes how save errors reach
   the player.
3. **A tick hot spot** at x 10,400 to 11,600, z −2,400 to −2,750 in seed
   12345's Overworld: 783 ticks over 50 ms in two minutes, the worst 1.7 s.
   It isn't investigated. Its counts show no queue growth there: 706 water
   cells, 275 lava cells, no TNT, no falling blocks. It could be checked
   with `--bench-tick` and `perf` there.
4. **Generation herds lag a moving player in the soak.** While the walker
   travels, live mobs stay near zero and the set of populated columns
   barely grows. Herds need a column fully loaded with settled light, and
   the walker only waits for the rows near it. This makes the soak's travel
   legs lighter on entities than play. It may also happen in play when
   generation lags; it wasn't looked into further.
5. **What the soak leaves out:**
   - the renderer, GPU memory, audio and particles (drained, not drawn or
     played);
   - survival damage and the player's inventory screens;
   - LAN, controllers and split-screen;
   - the desktop-side consumers of world events. Each list the game
     drains was checked to have a drain site in `src/app` or the entity
     update.

   Run C's memory includes the meshes built for the 2 ms light budget's
   streaming. It doesn't include a renderer's copies.
6. **Fluid ticks are still not saved across quitting** (unchanged): a
   modified chunk mid-flow at quit stays as it was until something wakes
   it.
7. **Sound:**
   - A block sound first heard with no prefetch waits for synthesis on the
     audio thread, up to about 22 ms on Boat for the slowest. Whether
     that can be heard is for the owner's ears.
   - The rendered lazy sounds stay for the session, up to the same 44 MB.
   - The other 106 MB of the bank are still built at startup. The same
     mechanism could defer them, and that would be the owner's call.
