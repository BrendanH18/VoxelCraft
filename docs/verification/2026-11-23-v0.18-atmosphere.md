## What changed

- **Benchmark scenes first.** The frame-time runner has three more scenes
  (`benchmarks/scenes.json`, `performance::SCENES`, `app::benchmark`):
  `weather` (the survival orbit in a full thunderstorm held at noon),
  `underwater` (the camera 5.5 blocks down in a 33 x 33 x 12 glass tank of
  still water built at spawn) and `nether` (the Nether where its portal
  arrives, the camera turning in place). See the
  [development guide](../development.md#live-performance-scene-suite). They
  landed in the first commit, before any tuning.
- **Fog** (`voxelcraft::atmosphere::fog`): Java 26.3's `FogRenderer`.
  Environments in Java's order (lava, powder snow, Blindness, Darkness,
  water, atmospheric), each with Java's distances; Java's colour layers
  (dimension, the biomes around the eye blended with Java's Gaussian
  weights, the day timeline, the rain and thunder layers, the look-at-the-sun
  sunset blend, the sky-colour mix, void darkness, the effects' darkening,
  water vision and night vision brightening); the rain fog's smoothing and
  its open-sky and precipitation rules. Shaders fog by Java's
  `total_fog_value`: the larger of a linear environmental fog on spherical
  distance and a linear render-distance fog (the last tenth, 4 to 64
  blocks) on cylindrical distance, blended in sRGB as Java blends it.
  Entities, beams and conduits are built out to where the fog makes them
  invisible.
- **Sky**: Java's sky plane 16 blocks overhead in the sky colour, fogged by
  distance up to the sky fog end, with the timeline's sunrise/sunset colour
  around the sun's side of the horizon. VoxelCraft's square sun, moon and
  stars stay. Lightning flashes the sky as Java does (the sky colour lerped
  0.22 toward (0.8, 0.8, 1) and the sky light factor at 1) unless Hide Sky
  Flashes is on. The End's mottled sky takes Java's End fog colour.
- **Clouds** (`atmosphere::clouds`, `render::clouds`): Java's
  `CloudRenderer`. 12 x 12 cells, 4 blocks thick, at y 192.33, drifting east
  0.03 blocks a tick (on the animation clock, so held captures keep them
  still); Fast draws one downward face per cell with the top colour; Fancy
  draws boxes with only the faces the camera can see and inside faces for
  the 3 x 3 cells around it; faces are built in rings from the camera
  outward and rebuilt only when the camera enters another cell, crosses the
  layer, or an option changes. Face shades (0.7 bottom, 1.0 top, 0.8
  north/south, 0.9 east/west), the dimension's cloud colour through the day
  timeline and weather (grey and dark in storms, near-black at night), none
  in the Nether and the End, and alpha fading linearly to the cloud fog end.
  A depth pass then a colour pass draws only the nearest cloud surface.
- **Options**: Clouds (OFF, Fast, Fancy; default Fancy; `renderClouds`,
  with Java's `false`/`fast`/`true`, and its old booleans read) and Cloud
  Distance (2 to 128 chunks, default 128; `cloudRange`) in Video Settings'
  Quality section in Java's order; Hide Sky Flashes (off;
  `hideLightningFlashes`) with Java's tooltip in Accessibility Settings after
  Damage Tilt. Older `options.txt` files load with Java's defaults.
- **Rain**: the streaks are drawn on a 64-texel grid across each sheet, as
  Java's rain texture is, instead of a 16-texel one, so each streak is a
  quarter as wide; columns use Java's alpha (full at the camera to half at
  the weather radius; snow 0.8).
- **Water**: the existing waves, Fresnel and sky reflection stay; the
  ripples are sampled per texel, so their highlights follow the block art's
  16-pixel grid, and the reflection is the same sky gradient the sky draws.
- **Interior light**: the Overworld's terrain, entity and block-model
  lighting follow Java's `lightmap.fsh` in Java's gamma space and convert
  once for the sRGB target: the dimension's ambient colour (#0a0a0a), sky
  light times `visual/sky_light_factor` and `visual/sky_light_color` (the
  timeline's 0.24 and #7a7aff at night, pulled toward them by rain and
  thunder), and block light with `visual/block_light_tint` (#FFD88C) mixed
  toward white near the source, times Java's block factor 1.4, then night
  vision and the Brightness option. Face and corner shading and
  VoxelCraft's sun-facing tint stay outside the conversion (the tint
  weighted by the sky's share of the light), so fully sunlit faces look as
  before while shade, night, caves and torch-lit rooms take Java's darkness
  and warm torchlight. The Nether and the End keep their lighting path.

Gameplay light is untouched: spawning, burning, detection and redstone read
`simulation::lighting::SpawnLight` and the world's light levels, which take
nothing from these colours, factors or options
(`gameplay_light_does_not_follow_the_visual_model`).

## Differences from Java

- **Mixing space for the lightmap.** Java's lightmap multiplies textures in
  gamma space. VoxelCraft converts the Overworld lightmap once, but face and
  corner shading and its own sun-facing tint (a VoxelCraft addition Java
  doesn't have) multiply after the conversion, so sunlit faces keep their
  current look rather than Java's darker side faces. The Nether and the End
  keep VoxelCraft's earlier lighting path. Particles and rain keep their
  own lighting.
- **Render-distance fog radius.** Java fogs the last tenth before its render
  distance. VoxelCraft's chunks are 32 blocks, so the drawn terrain can stop
  up to about a chunk and a half short of Java's distance; the
  render-distance fog ends where the terrain actually stops around the eye
  (`meshed_radius`) so its ragged edge never shows. In the comparison scenes
  (render distance 6, 192 blocks) that is 176.7 blocks at a chunk's centre.
- **Biome blend in 2D.** Java blends biome attributes over a 6 x 6 x 6 block
  of quarts around the eye; VoxelCraft blends the 6 x 6 quart columns at the
  eye's height (as its Nether fog already did).
- **Clouds**: VoxelCraft's own pattern. Clouds drift on the animation clock
  rather than game time, so they move while the game is paused. Java blends
  clouds with order-independent transparency; VoxelCraft draws the nearest
  surface (Java's pre-OIT method), so water and weather behind a cloud don't
  show through its 20 % transparency. Cloud colours are blended in linear
  space by the sRGB target.
- **The sky** keeps VoxelCraft's square sun, moon, stars and its own sunset
  glow shape (now in Java's timeline colour); Java draws textured sun and
  moon, its star field and a sunrise fan. The End's sky is VoxelCraft's
  mottled pattern in Java's End fog colour, not Java's end sky texture.
- **Water vision** counts frame time in ticks for every view, so split-screen
  followers adapt too. Darkness's fade uses the existing approximation (its
  last 22 ticks), not `MobEffectInstance.getBlendFactor`'s fade-in.
- **Rain** keeps VoxelCraft's procedural streaks and radius 10 (Java's
  Weather Radius default); Java's Weather Radius option (3 to 10) is not
  added.
- **Not drawn, so not added**: Java 26.3's End flashes (which Hide Sky
  Flashes also hides), the boss-bar world fog and darkening, and the
  Darkness lightmap pulse beyond the existing colour pulse.

## Underwater follow-up (2026-11-23)

The owner saw the `underwater` capture turn an almost pure blue, about
`#1010F0` over the whole view, against an expectation of Java's muted
blue-teal. What the jar says, and what changed:

- **Java's arithmetic.** `WaterFogEnvironment.getBaseColor` reads the
  biome's `visual/water_fog_color`; oceans, deep oceans and rivers set none,
  so it is the attribute default `#050533` (`EnvironmentAttributes`, int
  -16448205). `FogRenderer.computeFogColor` then lerps it toward itself
  scaled to its brightest channel by `LocalPlayer.getWaterVision()` (0.6
  after 100 ticks under water, 1 after 600). Java's main target is
  `RGBA8_UNORM` (`MainTarget`), so the float colour is the displayed colour.
  A fully fogged pixel is therefore (5, 5, 51) on entering the water, (17,
  17, 173) after 5 seconds and (25, 25, 255) after 30 seconds, in sRGB
  bytes. A warm ocean's `#041F33` becomes (20, 155, 255) and a lukewarm
  ocean's `#041633` (20, 110, 255): the teal water is in warm oceans, not in
  ordinary ones. Lava's fog is (153, 25, 0) and powder snow's (159, 187,
  204), never brightened.
- **The bug.** The fog, sky and cloud colours went to the renderer through
  a 2.2 power, while the sRGB target encodes with the exact curve, so full
  water vision showed (18, 18, 255) instead of (25, 25, 255), and dark fog
  colours were off by several levels. Both directions now use the exact sRGB
  transfer (`atmosphere::linear` and `encode`; the shaders' `to_srgb` and
  `from_srgb`), and the night and interior lightmap uses the same decode.
  `fog_colours_show_on_screen_as_java_computes_them` pins the on-screen
  bytes for water at three water-vision levels in three biomes, a warm
  ocean, lava and powder snow against Java's arithmetic from the fixture,
  and checks the conversion round-trips every byte.
- **The scene.** The `underwater` tank floats 75 blocks up and its sand
  floor is a gravity block: it falls during the capture's wait, so the view
  is a glass box with floating seagrass and nothing behind it but the clear
  colour, which is the fog colour. After the capture's long load the eye has
  full water vision, so almost every pixel is Java's (25, 25, 255). Java
  draws the same scene the same way. `underwater-ocean` (appended) looks at
  the seed's ocean monument (24, 48, 712) from 11 blocks under the surface,
  so the comparison shows the sea floor, the monument and water at ordinary
  underwater distances.

Recaptured at the merged head (`target/captures/merged`, the whole
`atmosphere` group): the tank's background measures (25, 25, 255), Java's
value, where the earlier capture had (18, 18, 255). In `underwater-ocean`
the monument stands in a lukewarm ocean, so its distant water measures (20,
110, 255), Java's lukewarm value; the sea floor, seagrass and the monument
read clearly at ordinary distances. Night is a little lighter than the
earlier capture (the exact decode lifts dark lightmap values toward Java's),
and the End is unchanged.

The fog colour is still Java's: the background of an ordinary ocean after
30 seconds under water is a saturated blue. If the owner wants it muted
beyond Java, that is a scope decision this record does not make.
