## What is built

**Infested blocks** (`world::infested`, states 30720–30728). Infested
stone, cobblestone, stone bricks, mossy, cracked and chiseled stone bricks,
and infested deepslate with its three `axis` states (placed along the
clicked face, as `InfestedRotatedPillarBlock`). Each draws its host's
textures, mines in half its host's time (`#mineable/pickaxe`, no tool
needed), resists explosions at 0.75, and drops nothing unless mined with
Silk Touch, which drops the host (`blocks/infested_*`). Broken in Survival
with `blockDrops` on and no Silk Touch, or caught in an explosion that
drops blocks, it lets a silverfish out at the block's bottom centre with
the spawn poof (`spawnAfterBreak`, `spawnInfestation`); the release goes
through `World::released_silverfish`, drained by the game (and the LAN
headless harness) every update. As in Java there is no survival recipe or
drop: they are found in mountains and strongholds.

**Silverfish** (`entity::silverfish`, MOB-01c). `SilverfishMergeWithStoneGoal`:
with no target and standing idle, on the goal selector's every-other-tick
update, under `mobGriefing`, one time in five (`reducedTickDelay(10)`) a
silverfish picks one of the six directions and, when the block that way
from `containing(x, y + 0.5, z)` is a host, turns it into the infested
block and vanishes with the spawn poof. `SilverfishWakeUpFriendsGoal`: a
hit from an entity arms a countdown of ten goal ticks (20 game ticks);
then it scans y 0, 1, −1 … ±5 and x, z 0, 1, −1 … ±10 in Java's order,
breaking each infested block found (its silverfish comes out) under
`mobGriefing` or turning it back into its host without it, and stops after
each one with probability ½. The Infested effect was already complete
(v0.15 Track D).

**`ore_infested`** (generation 5, `world::ore`). Fourteen tries a chunk,
uniform from the bottom of the world to y 63, size 9, no air discard,
infested stone in stone and infested deepslate in deepslate, in the ten
biomes whose feature lists carry it (meadow, cherry grove, grove, snowy
slopes, jagged, frozen and stony peaks, windswept hills, windswept
gravelly hills, windswept forest — exactly `Biome::is_mountain`, checked
against the fixture).

**Nether fortresses** (generation 5, `Fortress::generate_java`). Java's
layout from `setLargeFeatureSeed(seed, chunk)`: the start crossing's
direction from `Plane.HORIZONTAL`'s order, every weighted pick of
`BRIDGE_PIECE_WEIGHTS` and `CASTLE_PIECE_WEIGHTS` (limits, `allowInRow`,
the shared previous piece, falling through to later pieces, the bridge
end when five tries fail), each bridge end's `selfSeed` (also drawn when
one is made past the 112-block reach and dropped), the turns' chest roll,
the T balcony's z offset by orientation and its two `nextInt(8) > 0`
castle rolls, the stalk room's second doorway, the pending queue (every
piece, bridge ends included, removed in place by `nextInt(size)`) and
`moveInsideHeights(48, 70)`. Bridge ends crumble from
`RandomSource.create(selfSeed)` as Java paints them. Placement was already
Java's (`nether_complexes`: spacing 27, separation 4, salt 30084232,
fortress weight 2 of 5).

**Strongholds** (generation 5, `Stronghold::generate_java`). Java's
retry loop (`setLargeFeatureSeed(seed + tries, chunk)` until a layout has
a portal room), the spiral staircase start, `STRONGHOLD_PIECE_WEIGHTS`
with the library and portal room depth rules and the imposed five-way
crossing, every constructor's draws in order (entry door, straight
corridor exits, five-way crossing exits, room crossing type, tall or low
library), Java's world-axis doorway offsets (turns grow left or right by
orientation, the five-way crossing's swapped offsets for west and north),
`FillerCorridor` stubs (new `Kind::FillerCorridor`, painted as Java does)
where a corridor would run into a piece at the same height, the 50-step
and 112-block limits and `moveBelowSeaLevel(63, -64, random, 10)`. Walls
use `SmoothStoneSelector`'s bands, now with infested stone bricks in
0.5–0.55 (5%), and turns open the side Java opens. Ring positions follow
`generateRingPositions`' random sequence (seeded with the world seed:
first angle, each distance and its biome-search `fork`, each ring's turn)
and its `spread = min(spread, count - i)`.

**Mineshafts** (generation 5, `Mineshaft::generate_java` and
`start_java`). Placement by `legacy_type_3` (the start chunk's
`setLargeFeatureSeed` random's first `nextDouble` below 0.004, every chunk
a candidate), then `tryGenerateStructure`'s weighted pick between
`mineshaft` and `mineshaft_mesa`, each kept only where its start (the
chunk's middle x, y 50 moved with the pieces, its north edge) is in its
biomes: the Overworld but badlands and the deep dark for the normal type,
badlands for mesa. Layouts follow `MineshaftPieces` depth first: the room
from three draws, its doorways per side, `createRandomShaftPiece`'s
crossing/stairs/corridor split and box searches, the corridor's
`hasRails` and spider rolls, each corridor end (`nextInt(4)` then the
height draw), the side branches (passed `depth + 1`, so two deeper),
two-floored crossings' four `nextBoolean`s, the depth-8 and 80-block
limits, then `moveBelowSeaLevel(63, -64, random, 10)`; the mesa type
instead moves the layout's centre to a random height between sea level and
the `WORLD_SURFACE_WG` surface there, and builds in dark oak planks, fence
and logs.

**Fortress spawns** (all generations: a spawn rule, not terrain).
`fortress.json`'s monster override holds inside a piece
(`BoundingBoxType.PIECE`, as before), and `isInNetherFortressBounds` now
also gives `FORTRESS_ENEMIES` on nether bricks anywhere inside the
fortress's whole bounding box (`getStructureAt`), which Java checks first.

**Dungeons** (`MonsterRoomFeature`) were compared and need no generation 5
change: the radii (2–3), validity (solid floor and roof, one to five
two-high openings), the downward paint with mossy floor three times in
four, the two chest passes of three tries over `-r..=r` needing exactly
one solid neighbour, the spawner's `[skeleton, zombie, zombie, spider]`,
`simple_dungeon` loot, and the placed features' counts and bands
(10 a chunk over the world, 4 in `above_bottom 6..=-1`) already match.

## Differences from Java

- **Terrain-dependent placement.** VoxelCraft's biomes and terrain are its
  own (FE-01), so a seed's structures don't land where Java's do even
  where the placement is Java's: fortress and mineshaft starts use Java's
  random sequence and Java's biome rules over VoxelCraft's biomes;
  stronghold rings skip Java's 112-block search for a
  `#stronghold_biased_to` biome (the fork that search uses is still drawn,
  so the ring math stays Java's), keeping each ring position as is.
- **Painting randomness.** Pieces paint with VoxelCraft's positional hashes
  where Java draws from each chunk's decoration random (stronghold wall
  stones, cobwebs, mineshaft supports, rails and torches, chest loot
  seeds); only the bridge end's crumbling uses Java's own seed. The
  proportions are Java's.
- **Minecart chests.** Mineshaft corridors still place plain chests filled
  from `abandoned_mineshaft` where Java spawns chest minecarts on a rail.
- **Fortress and stronghold layouts before generation 5** keep the older
  builders (own random source, mirrored side doorways, no filler
  corridors or second stalk room doorway).
- **Silverfish.** The merge goal's idle check is VoxelCraft's `Ai::Idle`
  (Java: the random stroll's navigation is done), and its stroll keeps the
  generic interval rather than `RandomStrollGoal(1.0, 10)` and the walk
  target value of 10 over host blocks. Block changes from both goals are
  applied by the game later in the same update; the wake-up's broken
  blocks show break particles but no break sound. Magic damage
  (`#always_triggers_silverfish`) doesn't wake friends; hits with
  knockback do.
- **Infested deepslate** from a silverfish is always upright: VoxelCraft's
  deepslate has one state.
- **Ore targets.** `ore_infested`, like the other ores here, replaces only
  stone and deepslate; Java's targets also take granite, diorite, andesite
  and tuff (`stone_ore_replaceables`, `deepslate_ore_replaceables`).
