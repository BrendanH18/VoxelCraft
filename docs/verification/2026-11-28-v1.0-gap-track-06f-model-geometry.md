**Families now Java's:** iron and copper bars (all eight copper states),

no model UVs). **The rest remain** (listed under
[remaining differences](#remaining-differences) with the reason for each):
the game's 12-byte terrain quad has no room for the per-face texel offsets
those models' ambient-occluded faces need, and several need rotated planes.

## What changed

### 1. A detail quad for whole Java model faces (plane code 29)

Track 6e's quads carry Java's UVs as one of the square's eight symmetries
plus one of eight fixed offsets, and only on faces at the cell's edge. Java's
other models need any texel offset on any face, planes inside the cell's
edge (bars' end strips at 0.001 and 15.999) and faces of elements the game's
boxes don't have. Detail plane code 29, never used before, is now *a face of
a Java model drawn without ambient occlusion*:

- word 1's AO bits hold its depth code: 0–16 sixteenths, 17 for 0.001 and 18
  for 15.999 (Java's inset planes facing into the cell);
- the three spare bound bits hold its UV symmetry;
- word 2 (the light) holds its one light in the low byte and its UV offset
  in texels in bits 8–11 (u) and 12–15 (v).

Java draws these models without ambient occlusion (bars and hoppers by
their `ambientocclusion: false`, end rods and lanterns because they emit light:
`ModelBlockRenderer` turns AO off for any block with light emission), lit
flat: the neighbour's light for a face on the cell's edge
(`faceCubic`), the block's own otherwise. So the AO bits and three of the
four light bytes are free, and the quad stays 12 bytes. The shader
(`chunk.wgsl`) decodes code 29: depth, flat light (`uniform_light`), AO
off, symmetry and texel offset.

`tools/extract_java_model_uvs.py` now also writes, per blockstate row, every
face of the jar's model (`ModelFace`: face, rect, symmetry, offset, texture
slot, turned cullface, inset), emulating `FaceBakery` as before, and the
fixture lists every face's corners and Java UVs. The mesher draws such a
block from the table instead of its boxes, culling a face by its cullface
(the neighbour in that direction opaque) or when it lies on the cell's edge
against an opaque neighbour.

### 2. Iron and copper bars (`template_bars_*`)

Java's bars are planes, not the pane-like boxes the game drew: a lone post
is two crossed planes 2 wide at the centre (`template_bars_post`), each
joined side a plane at 8 across its half of the cell with a 2-wide edge
strip on the cell's edge (`_side`, `_side_alt`), one joined side adds a
cap plane (`_cap`, `_cap_alt`), and every state draws the end strips at
0.001 and 15.999 (`_post_ends`, and the sides' strips), all with the jar's
UVs: each plane's back mirrored, the strips' rows offset. All 16 sets of
connections come from the multipart blockstate; copper bars (every
oxidation and waxed state) share iron bars' blockstate and templates.

**Art:** the post shows only the texture's columns 7–8, where the game's
bars texture had a gap, so a lone post was nearly invisible. The bars
texture (`material_textures`, iron and every copper stage) now has its
bars on columns 3–4, 7–8 and 11–12 and half bars on the two edges, so a
post shows a bar and a row of bars joins into whole ones; rails unchanged.

**Collision and outline** are unchanged: Java's `IronBarsBlock` shapes are
the same 2-wide post and arms the game already had.

### 3. Hoppers (`hopper`, `hopper_side`)

Java's hopper is a 16 × 1 × 16 plate at 10–11 with the *inside* texture on
both faces, four rim walls from 11 to 16 with the *top* texture on their
tops, the 8 × 6 × 8 funnel and a 4 × 4 × 4 spout (down, or 4 long to the
side, `y`-turned without `uvlock`). The game drew rim walls from 10 with no
plate and an 8-long spout, every face in one texture. Now:

- the model's faces from the jar for all five facings, the plate's top and
  the rims' inner faces culled by an opaque block above as Java's
  `cullface: up` does;
- **two new textures** (original art, Track 6's last reserved layers):
  `HOPPER_TOP` (4086), the rim's top, a ring two texels wide in the
  hopper's grey, lit on its outer edge; `HOPPER_INSIDE` (4087), dark iron
  in four panels with seams and rivets, for the plate and the funnel's and
  spout's undersides. The outside keeps layer 1138;
- **collision and outline** follow Java's `HopperBlock` shapes (the 16-wide
  top from 10 less its 12-wide inside from 11, the funnel and the 4-long
  spout), so an entity stands on the plate at 11 inside the rim rather than
  falling past the funnel's edge, and the side spout is 4 long.

### 4. End rods (`end_rod`)

The game's end rod boxes were already Java's elements (a 4 × 1 × 4 base, a
2 × 15 × 2 rod) but drew each face from a plain glow texture and the base's
underside from a second one. Java maps every face from one texture laid out
as an atlas. Now every face takes the jar's UVs in all six facings (`x` and
`y` turns without `uvlock`), and the rod's layer is redrawn in that layout
(original art): the rod down columns 0–1 (rows 0–14, lit on the left), the
glowing cap at (2, 0), the base's top and underside at (2, 2)–(5, 5) in pale
purple with a darker rim, and the base's sides along row 6. The second
layer (`T_ROD_BASE`) is no longer used by the end rod. The item (Java's
unturned `end_rod`) takes the same UVs.

### 5. Lanterns (`template_lantern`, `template_hanging_lantern`)

The game's lantern boxes were already Java's body and cap (one texel higher
hanging), but every face showed the planar texture. Java's lanterns map an
atlas: the body's sides from (0, 2) to (6, 9), its top and bottom from (0, 9)
to (6, 15), the cap's sides from (1, 0) to (5, 2) and its top from (1, 10) to
(5, 14). Now every face of the body and cap takes the jar's UVs (and the
hanging model's cap gets its underside, which Java draws), for iron, soul
and all eight copper lanterns, and the lantern texture (one pattern, every
lantern's palette) is redrawn in that layout (original art): a frame of
posts and rims round the flame on the sides, a dark top plate with the
cap's lit square inside it, and the cap's sides lit on top. The item stays
Java's flat sprite (its own art).

### 6. Trapdoor items

Java's trapdoor item is the unturned `*_trapdoor_bottom` block model, so a
slot icon, held, dropped or framed trapdoor shows its rims as the jar maps
them: oak, dark oak, iron and the copper trapdoors' bottom rows upside
down; the orientable wood trapdoors' top rows. `mesh::item_face_uvs` applies
the blockstate's north-facing, bottom, closed row to the item's box in
`render::ui::block_item_quads` (slots, hands) and `render::block_model`
(dropped and framed items).

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens:

- **Trapdoor icons**: the rims (the slot icon's left and right edges) show
  the strip Java's item model maps there, as above, instead of the default.
- **End rod icon**: the rod, cap and base show the redrawn texture through
  Java's UVs: a pale rod lit on one side, a bright cap and a purple base.
- **Bars, hoppers, lanterns and panes** are flat sprites in slots, as in
  Java, unchanged (the bars sprite comes from the block texture, so its
  bars sit on the new columns; lanterns have their own sprite art).

Outside the inventory: bars are Java's crossed planes and strips; hoppers
have their plate, rim top and inside textures and short spout, and stand
and collide as Java's; end rods and lanterns show Java's layouts; held,
dropped and framed trapdoors and end rods take the jar's UVs.

## Remaining differences

A scan of the listed families' blockstates on the jar, face by face,
against what the terrain quad can store (code 29 handles any symmetry and
offset, but only for models Java draws without ambient occlusion):

| Family | Why it stays |
| --- | --- |
| Anvils, grindstones, lecterns, stonecutters, bells, lightning rods, beds, sniffer eggs, the heavy core, scaffolding | Ambient-occluded faces inside the cell need texel offsets (for example the anvil's 32 such faces, the grindstone's 204), which the quad can't carry beside per-corner AO and light; their textures are also Java atlas layouts the game's art would need redrawing for |
| Chains, lanterns' handles (still not drawn, as before), campfires (the fire), lecterns (the tilted top), candles, brewing stands (the bottles), tripwire hooks, sea pickles, small and big dripleaves, levers, signs | Elements turned 22.5 or 45 degrees, which detail quads (axis-aligned) can't draw |
| Lit campfires, lit candles, brewing stands | Flat-lit (light-emitting) so code 29 could hold their offsets, but each also has a rotated element above (the lantern's body and cap are done; its handle is the rotated part) |
| Turtle eggs, cocoa, crops and stems, repeaters and comparators, flower pots and potted plants, fence gates, buttons | Not attempted in the time (repeaters, comparators, cocoa, crops, stems and flower pots are flat-lit and would fit code 29 with art in Java's layouts) |
| Composters, cauldrons, enchanting tables, cakes, the dragon egg, daylight detectors, slabs | Every face already maps by Java's default UVs; their element layouts were not compared in this track |
| Chests | Java draws them as block entities (`ChestRenderer`), as the game does; no block model |
| Stairs, pistons, observers, crafters, vaults, jigsaws, end portal frames | Their turned faces (`uvlock` or blockstate turns) need checking against the game's turned layers; not attempted |

Also: Java's random `y` turns of stone, grass, sand and others, and the
`cube_mirrored` variants, are still not drawn (Track 6e's note stands).
