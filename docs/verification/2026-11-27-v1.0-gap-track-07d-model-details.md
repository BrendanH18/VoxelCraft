## What changed

### 1. Scale keys and `skipDraw` on the rig (MOB-06)

A rig's parts now scale (`xScale`, `yScale`, `zScale`) as Java's do:
after the part's turn, carried to its children's offsets and, through
their turns, their boxes (`Rig::parts` keeps each part's world transform
as a pivot, a turn and a linear stretch, exact for any mix of scales and
turns; this game's `Part` gained the stretch, identity for every model
that isn't a scaled rig). Keyframe `SCALE` channels add their factor less
one, times the strength, as `offsetScale` does. Every scale key in the
converted tables now runs: `NautilusAnimation.SWIMMING`'s body and mouth
pulses, `FOX_BABY_WALK`'s legs stretching 15 percent with the walk, and
the sniffer's nose and body (the axolotl's, the camel calf's root and the
rabbits' body keys are all 1, and change nothing, as in Java). A part can
also skip its own boxes while its children draw (`skipDraw`), which the
armadillo pup's shell needs.

### 2. The adult nautilus shell first (MOB-06; owner, 2026-11-27)

The adult nautilus and zombie nautilus are rebuilt on
`NautilusModel.createBodyMesh`'s parts: the coiled shell (14 by 18 by 20
pixels, its flat face forward) leads, and the soft body with the upper,
inner and lower mouth trails out of the shell's back, so a nautilus swims
shell first as Java's does and as Track 7c's baby already did. The model
and its swim are the baby's: the body turns toward the look within 10
degrees and `SWIMMING` runs on the walk position plus a fifth of
`ageInTicks` (`applyWalk(.., speed + 0.2, 2, 3)`), opening and closing the
mouth and pulsing the body and mouth with its scale keys (the old
model's faster tentacle wave while dashing is gone: Java has none). The shell is
Java's height, 18 pixels, the rider's seat (1.1375 blocks); this game's
old adult's shell stood about 15. The layers are Java's: the saddle is
`NautilusSaddleModel`'s upper shell grown by 0.2 (here a seat on top with
straps down the sides), the armor `NautilusArmorModel`'s shell (plates
over the upper shell and the front of the lower, in the material's
colour), and a warm zombie nautilus carries `ZombieNautilusCoralModel`'s
eight coral fans (yellow, pink, blue and red, on Java's part tree), hidden
under armor. Colours, the coil's bands, the eyes and the dark lip of the
shell's opening are this game's own, in the old adult's palette, which
the zombie nautilus's temperate and warm recolouring still use.

### 3. Baby keyframes (MOB-06)

- **Camel calf**: `CAMEL_BABY_IDLE` (ears flicking, the tail swinging, the
  head nodding) from the calf's `idleAnimationState`, which `Mob::tick_anim`
  restarts every 80 to 119 ticks (`Camel.setupAnimationStates`; the roll on
  Track 7b's per-mob, per-tick hash). The walk keyframes now run whatever
  else plays, as `CamelModel.setupAnim` applies them (Track 7c skipped them
  while sitting or dashing; a sitting calf doesn't move, so only the dash
  changes).
- **Armadillo pup**: `ArmadilloModel.setupAnim` in full. Hidden in its shell
  (`shouldHideInShell`: rolling after its fifth tick, scared, unrolling for
  its first 26) the body skips its own boxes, the hind legs and tail hide
  and the ball shows, while the head and front legs stay drawn where the
  keyframes tuck them; `ARMADILLO_BABY_ROLL_UP` runs while it rolls,
  `ARMADILLO_BABY_ROLL_OUT` while it unrolls and, scared,
  `ARMADILLO_BABY_PEEK`, restarted by each peek (this game's `peeking`
  countdown) and otherwise held on its last keyframe, as Java's
  fast-forward to the scared state's 50 ticks leaves it. Track 7c drew only
  the ball.
- **Snifflet**: `SnifferModel.setupAnim`'s keyframes on the snifflet's parts:
  `SNIFFER_WALK` (or `SNIFFER_SNIFF_SEARCH` while searching) on the walk
  (`applyWalk(pos, speed, 9, 100)`), and `SNIFFER_DIG`, `SNIFFER_LONGSNIFF`,
  `SNIFFER_STAND_UP`, `SNIFFER_HAPPY` and `SNIFFER_SNIFFSNIFF` from the time
  in its state (`Sniffer`'s `pose` and `elapsed`, which LAN guests already
  get). **Correction to Track 7c**: `SnifferRenderer` draws the snifflet
  with `SnifferModel` itself (`new SnifferModel(bakeLayer(SNIFFER_BABY))`);
  `SniffletModel`, whose `setupAnim` applies `BABY_TRANSFORM` (the head 1.2
  times larger, a pixel up and back), is never constructed in 26.3. The
  snifflet's head is now its mesh's size, at its mesh's place.
- **Scale keys**: section 1.

### 4. Baby layers (MOB-06)

- **Foal markings**: `HorseMarkingLayer` draws a horse foal's markings (its
  `*_baby` marking textures), so a foal with white, white-field,
  white-dot or black-dot markings now wears them on its flanks, as the
  adult does here, in the foal's proportions (horses only, as Java's
  layer).
- **The cria's decor**: `LlamaDecorLayer` never draws a carpet on a baby
  (`!state.isBaby`; babies can't wear one either), but a trader llama's
  cria wears `trader_llama_baby` on the decor mesh (the cria's boxes grown
  by 0.2): now the adult trader llama's blue blanket and gold panels over
  its body.
- **Zombie villager cub's armor**: `HumanoidArmorLayer` with
  `ZOMBIE_VILLAGER_BABY_ARMOR`. The cub's rig is the six-part humanoid, so
  it wears its armor (this game's armor pieces, in their texture layout,
  on the cub's pivots and sized to Java's baby armor mesh: a helmet about 9
  pixels across, half a pixel proud of the head, a chestplate a little
  wider than the body, arms and legs as long as the cub's, the boots at its
  feet).
- **Zombie villager cub's profession**: nothing to draw. Java's
  `VillagerProfessionLayer` skips the profession and level layers for
  babies (`!state.isBaby`) and draws only the biome type's baby outfit
  (`baby/<type>.png`); this game draws no villager type outfits on any
  villager, adult or baby (see Remaining differences).

### 5. Track 2e's states: not landed

Track 2e's fox crouch, pounce, faceplant, sleep and interest states and
the villager's shown trade item were not on the integration branch when
this track finished (`origin/t3/prep-release-1-0` still at `674f872`), so
the fox's poses and the villager holding up its trade item are not drawn
here: a handoff to whichever track takes them after 2e merges.

## Remaining differences

- **Villager type outfits**: Java draws each villager's and zombie
  villager's biome outfit (`type/<type>.png`, `baby/<type>.png`, with the
  type hats); this game draws none on any villager. For the cub that is
  its whole profession layer. Outside this track's model-detail scope: it
  is new art for every villager type (a scope decision for the owner).
- **Zombie villager armor**: the adult zombie villager still draws no
  armor (its model isn't the six-part armor rig); the cub now does. The
  cub's (and, from Track 7b, the baby zombie's) armor uses this game's
  armor pieces in the adult texture layout, sized to the baby, not
  `createBabyArmorMeshSet`'s own boxes and baby texture layout.
- **Textures' shapes**: Java's nautilus textures leave parts of its boxes
  transparent; here the body box ends at its face plane (Java's zero-depth
  box at z 7) so the mouth parts show, the saddle and armor are
  shaped boxes rather than texture cut-outs, and the coral fans are a
  tenth of a pixel thick. The foal's markings cover its flanks, as the
  adult's here, where Java's marking textures also mark legs and face.
- **Track 2e's states** (section 5): the fox's crouch, pounce, faceplant,
  sleep and interest poses and the villager's shown trade item.
- **Unchanged from Track 7c** (outside this track): the guardian's body and
  pitch, the horses' head pivot, the bamboo stalk and leaf planes, the
  armadillo, sniffer and camel adults' walk keyframes per part, LAN
  guests' piglin fighting state.
