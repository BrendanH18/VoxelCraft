## Implemented behavior

- Thunder rolls once per active, loaded Java **16×16** chunk per game tick,
  1/100000 during faded rain and thunder. It picks a uniform column,
  uses motion-blocking height, prefers the closest exposed rod within 128
  blocks, otherwise samples exposed living mobs/players above the column.
  The climate precipitation/sky check rejects snow, dry biomes and roofs.
- Bolts run Java's initial life 2 and 1–3 repeat flashes, random inter-flash
  pause, original segmented emissive geometry, sky flash, synthesized
  weather-category thunder. The shared headless entry point is
  `Entities::tick_lightning`; desktop/agent/LAN sessions use the same logic.
  Bolts and charged/shaking render state replicate to LAN clients.
- Real bolts damage and ignite intersecting mobs and players in Java's
  6×12×6 strike box; players take five armored points without difficulty
  scaling. Initial fire uses the existing Normal/Hard helper; later flashes
  can ignite on Easy too, matching 26.3. Fire obeys the player's fire radius.
- Creepers charge and explode at double power, with an original moving blue
  aura; pigs become persistent gold-sword zombified piglins and villagers
  persistent witches outside Peaceful. Adults killed by lightning drop a
  bowl as well as normal turtle loot. `Mob::thunder_transform(bolt_id, ...)`
  is the mooshroom integration hook; the bolt id is constant across flashes.
  Villager conversions immediately release their shared bed/job claims;
  lightning deaths retain recent player kill credit.
- Rods power for eight ticks, weakly emit 15 everywhere and strongly emit
  into their support. Their existing scheduled-redstone serialization
  preserves pulses. A loaded powered state without a pending tick schedules
  one. Lightning strips the struck unwaxed copper to unaffected and makes
  3–5 walks of 1–8 steps; each step samples up to ten radius-one positions,
  cleaning one oxidation stage while retaining properties and door halves.
- `summon lightning_bolt` (also namespaced, with relative coordinates) works
  through console and agent command adapters. It is a bolt, not a MobKind.
- A natural lightning strike has effective-local-difficulty × 0.01 trap
  probability with `spawn_mobs` on and no rod beneath. Trap bolts are visual
  only. A skeleton trap horse expires after 18000 ticking ticks unless
  persistent, and an alive non-spectator player within ten blocks triggers
  four tamed horses with persistent skeleton riders and iron helmets, plus
  a visual bolt. Additional horses/riders get the 60-tick protection.
- Skeleton and zombie horses share saddle, inventory, horse armor, steering,
  jumping, death spilling, owner/rider saves and ordinary mount interaction
  paths. Skeleton horses require taming from the trap (summoned untamed ones
  cannot be mounted); zombie horses tame by riding and eat red mushrooms.
  Neither breeds or grows out of a baby state. The 26.3 entity registrations
  allow both in Peaceful (natural monster spawning still stops there).
  Zombie horses burn in daylight unless wearing body armor, as `Mob.burnUndead`,
  the `burn_in_daylight` tag and `ZombieHorse.sunProtectionSlot` specify.
  Health is 15/25, skeleton
  speed 0.2 with ordinary horse jump draws, zombie jump 0.5–0.7 and speed
  `(9 + U + U + U) / float(42.16)`. Drops are 0–2 bones / 2–3 rotten flesh,
  plus ordinary Looting. Models are original horse rigs, including exposed
  skeletal ribs. Natural zombie horses create zombie riders; mob riders use
  tagged mount ids and follow the horse when positioned, saved or unloaded.
- Snowfall attempts follow `random_tick_speed` rolls at 1/48 per chunk per
  tick, independently of section block ticks. The implemented/saved
  `max_snow_accumulation_height` rule defaults to 1, accepts 0–8 and gates
  accumulation above cold, supported, block-light-below-10 surfaces.
  Each snow block grows toward the cap, with entity displacement for added
  collision layers. All layer counts, including eight, are excluded from
  Java 26.3's motion heightmap; snowfall never creates a second block of snow
  above a completed eight-layer block.
- Zombies and husks track eye-water exposure, start shaking at 600 ticks,
  then count 300 down through zero before converting. Husks first become
  zombies; those can later become drowned. **26.3 cancels the countdown
  when the eyes leave water** (unlike some older descriptions). The dry
  reset is -1, so re-entry needs 601 exposure ticks. Names, persistence,
  age/baby state, identity, equipment, effects and rider links are retained.
  Natural zombies, husks and drowned now save without setting persistence
  required, so wild conversions resume and their results survive restart.
  Baby eye-water checks use Zombie's explicit 0.775f eye height.
  New saved armor material/glint fields retain exact equipment through
  conversion and still read legacy boolean armor entries. Additions are
  omitted when default, preserving the v0.8 fixture's ordinary records.

## Differences and remaining work

- **Mooshroom colour swaps and waxed-copper root recognition** need the
  integrator's track-3/track-2 content. The conversion hook is explicit;
  unwaxed copper neighbors clean correctly today. Waxed blocks should start
  the random walk but must not themselves lose wax or oxidation.
- **Zombie jockey iron spears** are absent from the existing equipment and
  combat model. Riders currently use ordinary zombie melee. Trap helmets
  and bows lack Java's local-difficulty enchantment-provider rolls.
- Equine travel, mob passenger pursuit, original horse voices, horse armor
  rendering, and procedural bolt/aura appearance retain shared engine
  approximations. Exact skeleton-horse underwater movement (0.96 drag),
  and rider pathfinding/control parity are not
  complete. Undead babies use the 0.7 reference scale, and skeleton horse
  speed widens the reference float 0.2. Conversion-specific sound events are
  not synthesized separately yet.
- Existing climate snow lines, collision approximations and shared
  spawn light/sky visibility are used; the weather RNG sequence is not
  Java's LCG, and local difficulty still lacks chunk inhabited-time tracking.
  No new freezing-water behavior is claimed. Snow pushes cover player,
  agent and mob bodies; dropped items and vehicles are not pushed yet.
- Thunder is synthesized and played throughout the local weather session;
  Java's separate impact event and exact long-distance spatial attenuation
  are not reproduced. Bolts currently affect living mobs/players and world
  fire, not every nonliving entity (items, vehicles, decorative entities).
- The scripted model capture above is artifact evidence, with no interactive
  desktop play. Ordinary owner play, charged aura and bolt GPU appearance,
  end-to-end natural trap frequency, undead mounted combat and sustained
  storm performance remain unverified. Hands-on agent play still requires
  Terra under the project instructions.
