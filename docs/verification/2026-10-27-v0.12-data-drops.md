## What was compared

`tools/extract_java_block_loot_fixtures.py` evaluates each table exactly, as
probability distributions with no sampling. It does this for every
combination of the state values its conditions name and for seven mining
scenarios: bare hand, plain pickaxe, shears, Silk Touch, and Fortune I, II
and III. It writes `tests/fixtures/java/26.3/block_loot.json`, which covers
all 1,286 Java blocks. The 63 wall variants point to their standing block's
table. The 22 other blocks with no table drop nothing: air, the fluids, the
portals, bedrock, and the command, structure and test blocks.

`tests/java_block_loot.rs` (with `tests/block_loot/mod.rs`) works through
each of the 13,866 named VoxelCraft states. It maps the state to its Java
block and to the state values that block's table names. Then, for each
scenario with a tool that can harvest it, it breaks the state through
`World::spill_with_item`, the mining drop path. In all it makes 84,105
state-and-scenario checks. A scenario is skipped 11,039 times because no tool
of that kind can harvest the state, mostly the bare hand on pickaxe blocks;
Java's `requiresCorrectToolForDrops` is outside the tables and belongs to
`mining::can_harvest`. Fixed drops must match exactly. Random drops are
sampled 4,000 times with the seeded world RNG; the chance of each item and
its mean must fall within five standard errors of Java's, and every count
must lie within Java's range. Further states of a case that has already been
sampled get a range check only.

Two whole-structure tests break blocks through the real mining session:

- Either half of a bed drops exactly one bed, at the head.
- Breaking the upper half of double tall grass or a large fern rolls its
  table once: with shears it gives two of the plant and never seeds, and
  without them the seed share stays at 1 in 8.

`examples/block_loot_audit.rs` prints the counts, any failures and the
unregistered blocks as JSON.

## Behaviour changes a player would notice

- **Leaves** of every kind now drop 1–2 sticks 2% of the time (2.2%, 2.5%
  and 3.3% with Fortune I–III), as Java does. Before, they never dropped
  sticks.
- **Beds** drop from the head. Breaking either half gives one bed, which now
  appears at the head rather than the foot. A bed that explodes in the
  Nether or the End drops nothing (`BedBlock.useWithoutItem` removes both
  halves); before, clicking its head dropped a bed.
- **Cake** drops nothing; Java's table is empty.
- **Ripe carrots and potatoes** drop 2–5, not 1–4: the table's second pool
  adds one more before the 4-in-7 rolls.
- **A full composter** (level 8) also drops its bone meal.
- **Dead bushes** drop 0–2 sticks, not always 1.
- **Nether quartz ore** takes Fortune (ore bonus).
- **Sea lanterns** drop 2–3 prismarine crystals (plus 0 to the Fortune
  level, at most 5), and themselves only with Silk Touch. Before, they
  dropped both themselves and the crystals.
- **Silk Touch on shears-only plants** (hanging roots, seagrass, tall
  seagrass, small dripleaf, vines and nether sprouts) drops nothing. On tall
  grass and large ferns it rolls seeds as a plain tool does. Pale hanging
  moss keeps dropping itself.
- **Eight snow layers** broken with Silk Touch give one snow block.
- **Snow layers that melt**, wash away or lose their support drop nothing:
  Java's snow table needs a breaking entity, and `EntityPredicate.matches`
  is false without one.
- **Sweet berry bushes** at ages 0–1 drop nothing. Ripe bushes add 0 up to
  the Fortune level.
- **Amethyst clusters** give 4 shards (with Fortune's ore bonus) only to a
  pickaxe; anything else, including a hand or shears, gets 2.
- **Double tall grass and large ferns**: breaking the upper half no longer
  makes the orphaned lower half roll seeds a second time.

## Not done

- **Explosion loot.** `survives_explosion` and `explosion_decay` are recorded
  per table but not compared. VoxelCraft's blasts keep a whole block with
  chance `1 / power` (`World::explode_with_decay`). Java rolls per table and
  per item. Snow layers in a blast drop snowballs, as Java's do when
  an entity (TNT, a creeper, a fireball) causes the blast; the integrator
  routed blasts through `spill_mined` for this
  (`blasts_drop_snowballs_from_snow_layers`). Bed and respawn anchor blasts
  have no source entity in Java and drop no snowballs; VoxelCraft's blasts
  don't record a source, so those drop them too.
- **XP from mining** is not part of the loot tables and was not compared.
- **Block-entity contents** (containers, shelves, the chiseled bookshelf,
  decorated pot contents) and the `copy_components` data (names, banner
  patterns) are outside item identity and were not compared.
- **The harvest rule** (`requiresCorrectToolForDrops`) is assumed correct in
  `mining::can_harvest` and is owned by the hardness and tools slice.
- **Owner play.**
