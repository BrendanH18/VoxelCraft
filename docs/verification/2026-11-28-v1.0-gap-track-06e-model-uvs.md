## What changed

### 1. Explicit model UVs: doors, trapdoors, glass panes (UX-01)

Java maps a face without explicit UVs by `FaceBakery.defaultFaceUV`, which
the terrain shader already is (Track 6d). The door, trapdoor and pane
models give faces explicit `uv` rectangles and `rotation`s, and their
blockstates turn the models by `x` and `y` **without `uvlock`**, so each
face's texture follows the model. Every such face's Java UVs turn out to be
the game's planar UVs under one of the square's eight symmetries plus a
whole-texel offset, read from the jar for every state:

- **Doors** (`door_bottom_left` to `door_top_right_open`, all 32 states):
  the thin edges show the texture's left three columns (reversed on a
  closed door's edges, upright on an open door's outer edge), the top and
  bottom ends their own rotated strips (`rotation` 90 or 270), and one broad
  face mirrored (Track 6d's rule, now from the same table).
- **Trapdoors**, both of Java's templates: oak, dark oak, iron and the
  copper trapdoors use `template_trapdoor_*` (closed models that don't turn
  with the facing; rims showing the bottom three rows upside down); the
  other wood trapdoors (acacia, bamboo, birch, cherry, crimson, jungle,
  mangrove, pale oak, poplar, spruce, warped) `template_orientable_trapdoor_*`,
  whose broad faces turn with the facing (`y` 0 to 270, and `x` 180 for an
  open top trapdoor) and whose rims show the top rows.
- **Glass panes** (clear and stained): the edge texture's middle columns on
  the arms' tops and bottoms (`glass_pane_side` `[7, 0, 9, 7]` and the rest)
  and the arm sides and unjoined post sides mirrored, as `glass_pane_side`,
  `_side_alt`, `_noside` and `_noside_alt` give them.

`tools/extract_java_model_uvs.py` emulates `FaceBakery`'s vertex order and
UV assignment (`FaceInfo`, `CuboidFace.UVs.getVertexU/V`,
`Quadrant.rotateVertexIndex`, the blockstate turn) for every variant and
writes `src/mesh/java_uv_tables.rs` (per state and world face rectangle,
the face's *op*: symmetry, offset and whether it lies on the cell's edge)
and the fixture `tests/fixtures/java/26.3/block_model_uvs.json` (every
face's corners and their Java UVs).

**Mesh format** (still 12 bytes a quad): a detail quad's upper bounds
(1 to 16) are stored in four bits (16 as 0), and the lower u bound is at
most 15, which leaves three spare bits (word 0 bit 25, word 1 bits 20 and
25). Plane codes 17 to 24, never used before, are a face on the cell's edge
with symmetry `code - 17`, the spare bits its offset; on any other face the
spare bits are the symmetry. A rail's slope moved from its lower-u tag to
plane codes 25 to 28 (its plane is always at 1/16). Track 6d's door mirror
bit is symmetry 1 of the same field. The shader's `java_uv` applies the
symmetry and offset (`chunk.wgsl`'s two small tables).

**Frogspawn** is now Java's plane: its top and bottom both at 1.5/16 and
no sides (it drew a 2/16 slab's four sides), which also keeps the 1.5/16
code out of the bounds.

### 2. Glazed terracotta's turned faces (UX-01, BLD-05c)

`template_glazed_terracotta` turns its north face 90 degrees, south 270 and
east 180, and the blockstate turns the whole model by facing (`y` 0, 180,
270, 90) without `uvlock`, so the top and bottom turn with the facing and
each side keeps its model face's turn. The game drew all six faces with one
layer turned by the facing. Each face now takes the layer of its own turn
(`colors::GLAZED_FACE_TURNS`; the four turned layers per colour already
existed), so no texture or quad changed. The item is the south-facing model,
as Java's item is the unturned block model.

### 3. The trident thrown back (BLD-05c)

While a trident is charged (`using_item`):

- **First person**: `FirstPersonHandsAndItemsRenderer`'s `TRIDENT` use
  animation: no swing, the arm's placement, then (-0.5, 0.7, 0.1), -55
  degrees about x, 35.3 about y, -9.785 about z, a shake once charged past a
  tenth (`sin((t - 0.1) * 1.3) * (power - 0.1)`, 0.004 up), drawn back 0.2
  and stretched 1.2 along z toward full charge at 10 ticks, and -45 degrees
  about y (`render::hand`), with the held time
  `useDuration - (remaining - partialTicks + 1)`.
- **Third person**: `HumanoidModel`'s `THROW_TRIDENT` arm (half the walk
  swing less a half turn, no yaw) and the item definition's
  `item/trident_throwing` model: `thirdperson_righthand` (0, 90, 180)
  degrees and (8, -17, 9) pixels, the point down behind the raised arm
  (`item_display::TRIDENT_THROWING`). First person's transform is the same
  in both models.

### 4. Pointed dripstone's landing (UX-02)

`DripstoneFallAndLandParticle.postMoveUpdate`: a dripstone drop that
lands is removed, leaves its landing particle and plays
`block.pointed_dripstone.drip_water` or `drip_lava` (blocks, volume 0.3 to
1 at random, pitch 1) where it fell:

- a **water** drop leaves Java's `SPLASH` (`SplashParticle` with no
  velocity: `WaterDropParticle`'s random rise) instead of turning into a
  splash with a fixed rise of 0.2 (Track 6d);
- a **lava** drop leaves `LANDING_LAVA` as before (the lava drip's resting
  stage), now with the sound; lava dripping from under a lava block is
  unchanged (Java's `LavaFallProvider` has no sound).

The two sounds are new cues with original synthesized audio
(`Sound::DripstoneDrip`, appended after the portal cues). **Cauldrons below
already fill as Java's**: `PointedDripstoneBlock.maybeTransferFluid` on the
stalactite's random tick (water 0.17578125, lava 0.05859375, through mud
turning to clay) was implemented before this track
(`world::utilities::cauldron::dripstone_transfer`, tested by
`stalactite_drips_fill_a_cauldron_below`); nothing changed there.

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens:

- **Glazed terracotta icons**: the slot icon's sides show the pattern
  turned as Java's item model turns them (its south face 270 degrees, east
  180), instead of every face unturned.
- Everything else in a slot is unchanged: door items are flat sprites, and
  trapdoor and pane icons keep the default UVs (see remaining differences);
  the trident keeps its flat slot icon.

Outside the inventory: door edges, trapdoor rims and broad faces, and pane
tops and sides show their textures as Java's models do; glazed terracotta
turns each face as Java does; frogspawn is a flat plane; a charging trident
is drawn thrown back in first and third person; dripstone drops splash and
sound as they land.

## Remaining differences

- **Other explicit-UV models**: the scan finds 268 block models whose faces
  differ from the default UVs. This track maps doors, trapdoors, glass panes
  and glazed terracotta. The rest keep the default mapping, among them
  iron and copper bars (`template_bars_*`: planes rather than the game's
  pane boxes, and offsets the op table lacks), stairs' and slabs' `uvlock`
  turns, the anvil, stonecutter, grindstone, lectern, campfire, lanterns,
  chains, bells, scaffolding, vines, tripwire and hooks, signs and hanging
  signs, cocoa, stems and crops, pistons, observers, repeaters and
  comparators, rails' curves, candles, sea pickles, turtle eggs, dripleaves,
  flower pots, beds, fences' and gates' arms, sniffer eggs, the vault and
  heavy core, the crafter, `cube_mirrored` and `cube_north_west_mirrored`
  (Java's mirrored random variants), and the cross models' tinted forms.
  Java's random `y` turns of stone, grass, sand and others are not drawn
  either.
- **Item views** of trapdoors and panes (slots, hands, the ground, frames)
  keep the default UVs; only the world mesh takes the tables.
- **A drowned's raised trident** is unchanged: Track 7b's `THROW_TRIDENT`
  arm with the in-hand model, which is Java's (`DrownedRenderer` raises the
  arm while aggressive, and a mob's `using_item` is false then), so Track
  6d's note on it closes as no difference.
- **The lava drop's landing particle** is the game's resting lava drip, not
  a separate `LANDING_LAVA` particle with its own lifetime roll.
- The drip sounds are original synthesized cues, not Java's recordings.
