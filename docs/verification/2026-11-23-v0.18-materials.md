## What was built

1. **Stone, redrawn** (the authored asset `assets/textures/stone.png`, from
   `stone.source.json`). The inset-panel composition the
   [visual review](2026-11-05-graphics-ui-review.md) flagged is gone. Now:
   soft patches of three greys, small irregular pits (three with a deeper
   centre) and a few raised flecks lit from the top left, no frame, tileable
   both ways. It was laid out with the generator's cluster rules and
   finished by hand. `tools/texture_from_source.py` builds the PNG from the
   source, and `tools/validate_assets.py` (run in CI) now fails if the PNG's
   pixels and the source differ.
2. **A material generator**, [`src/render/material_textures.rs`](../../src/render/material_textures.rs):
   integer maths over small palettes, so every platform draws the same
   texels. Base shades come from tileable value noise plus a per-texel
   jitter. Pits and bumps are 1–5 texel random walks. Cluster `k` starts in
   its own cell of a 4 × 4 grid, so clusters never clump into a repeating
   glyph. Bumps are lit at the top left and shaded at the bottom right. A
   last pass breaks any straight run of one colour longer than four texels.
   Tiles are built once, on first use, and cached. It draws 59 layers, all
   redrawn in place:
   - deepslate (horizontal strata), andesite, diorite, granite, tuff,
     cobblestone (rounded stones on a one-texel joint), gravel;
   - dirt, grass top, grass side and snowy grass side (a lip of uneven
     two-texel drips over the dirt tile, with a shade under it), sand, red
     sand;
   - the eight stone ores and eight deepslate ores. Each keeps its
     background's texels exactly and adds 3–5 mineral clusters from eight
     hand-drawn shapes, each with a lit corner and a shaded rim;
   - oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia and dark oak leaves (cut-out holes
     with a shade under each);
   - logs (bark ridges and furrows; birch's pale bark with short dark marks;
     jungle's with moss), log ends (rings around a pith inside a bark rim)
     and planks (four boards, each lit along its top, shaded along its
     bottom, with one butt joint) of oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia,
     dark oak, mangrove and cherry.
3. **Greens toned down.** Grass at the plains colour has HSV saturation
   at most 0.55 (it was 0.67). Tall grass uses the grass top's mid shade. Oak leaves
   are 23 % darker than the grass. The biome tints (`tint_foliage`) are
   unchanged, so the swamp, savanna, jungle and taiga groups stay distinct
   (see Gaps).
4. **Podzol and mycelium sides** draw the new dirt below their lip, so they
   match a dirt block beneath them.
5. **Mipmap Levels**, Java's Video Settings slider: 0 to 4, default 4,
   "Mipmap Levels: OFF" at 0 and "Mipmap Levels: N" otherwise, right after
   Particles in Quality & Performance, saved as `mipmapLevels` in
   `options.txt`. The block sampler's `lod_max_clamp` honours it: all five
   levels stay uploaded, and a change only replaces the sampler and its bind
   group. An older `options.txt` loads with 4. The slider is drawn by the
   shared menu toolkit, so mouse, keyboard and controller reach it the same
   way.
6. **Asset manifest.** The stone entry's provenance records the redraw.
   `procedural_resources.material_art` records the generator, its
   provenance, the 59 layers, no new layers and the memory budget the tests
   check.
7. **Capture scenes.** The `materials` group is captured at 1920 × 1080 and
   1280 × 720. A new `material-items` scene shows material blocks in the
   hotbar, stone held, and nine dropped blocks on grass and stone.
   `tools/compare_captures.py` (standard library only) puts each before and
   after capture side by side.

## Tests

In `src/render/material_textures.rs`:

- `redrawn_tiles_are_deterministic_and_pinned`: an FNV-1a checksum of each
  of the 59 tiles and the stone asset, and a second build equal to the
  first.
- `natural_materials_follow_the_art_guide`: stone's mean luminance
  (0.17–0.24) and contrast (90 % within ±25 %); no straight run over four
  texels in the stones, ground, grass and leaves; dirt, sand, red sand and
  gravel 90 % within ±18 %; grass saturation at most 0.55; oak leaves 10–25 %
  darker than grass; 8–15 % holes in every leaf; no darker border (only the
  side columns for deepslate, whose strata run along rows).
- `far_mips_keep_the_families_apart`: stone, cobblestone, andesite, dirt and
  gravel differ by at least 4 % luminance (or by hue, for colours).
- `ores_keep_the_stone_and_deepslate_texels`: every changed texel is one of
  the ore's own colours, 8–40 per ore.
- `icons_draw_the_redrawn_faces`: the redrawn blocks draw isometric icons
  (not flat sprites) from their redrawn faces. Held and dropped blocks use
  the same faces.
- `texture_memory_stays_within_the_recorded_budget`: the cache within the
  manifest's `cpu_cache_bytes`, and the block array exactly the manifest's
  `block_array_bytes`.

Elsewhere: `app::settings::tests::mipmap_levels_match_java_and_persist` and
`app::options::tests::mipmap_levels_is_javas_slider` compare the option with
the fixture, and `assets::tests` checks the new stone texel.

## Second round

After the owner approved the first round (2026-11-23), the owner asked for

### What changed

1. **No pattern at a distance.** The faint diagonal on the far cave wall
   came from low-frequency structure inside one tile: a lighter and a
   darker half, and pits whose cells followed `k * 7 mod 16`, which lines
   them up from tile to tile. Now:
   - clusters take the sixteen 4 × 4 cells in a shuffled order per tile
     (`cell_order`), still one cluster per cell;
   - a `balance` pass nudges base shades a step at a time until every
     4 × 4 block's brightness (squared bytes, close to luminance) is within
     4 % of the tile's mean;
   - the stone asset was re-authored from the balanced layout, with three
     deep pit centres placed by hand.

   A new guide rule, checked by the tests: in natural stone, ground, grass
   and sandstone tops every 4 × 4 block's mean luminance is within 8 % of
   the tile's. Stone measures 5.7 % (it was 9.3 %).
2. **Gravel** has 25 small pebbles with dark gaps, lit at their top left,
   one in five warm brown, with centres jittered so the gaps don't line up.
   The first round's ±18 % band made it flat, so gravel now has its own
   rule in the guide: 75 % of texels within ±25 % (it measures 80 %).
3. **45 more tiles, all on existing layers** (104 in all; none of the
   reserved 1211–1259 used):
   - sandstone side and top, red sandstone side, top and bottom, cut
     sandstone and cut red sandstone (smooth sandstone uses the top):
     horizontal layers split by two broken seams, or a slab with a groove;
   - coarse dirt (grey pebbles), podzol top and side, mycelium top and side
     (their lips over the dirt tile, like grass), rooted dirt (pale roots
     with a shade on their right), clay, bedrock (the strongest contrast,
     on purpose);
   - polished andesite, diorite, granite and tuff (one bevelled slab) and
     polished deepslate (8 × 8 tiles); cobbled deepslate (stones on a
     joint, like cobblestone);
   - pale oak, mangrove and cherry leaves;
   - pale oak log side, end and planks;
   - the stripped logs and ends of oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark
     oak, mangrove, cherry and pale oak (long grain lines, no bark; their
     quarter-turned faces follow automatically). Stripped bamboo keeps its
     own drawing.

   The old drawings of these layers are removed from `textures.rs`,
   `overworld_textures.rs` and `stone_textures.rs`.
4. **A new comparison scene**, `material-families`: 32 of these blocks as
   2 × 2 × 2 cubes in four rising rows, seen from above, so tops and sides
   both show.

OBSERVATIONS

## Differences from Java

- Java reloads its resources when Video Settings closes after a mipmap
  change. VoxelCraft changes the sampler at once, without a reload.
- Java marks its graphics preset Custom when Mipmap Levels changes.
  VoxelCraft has no graphics preset (its Graphics button is
  Enhanced/Classic), so nothing else changes.
- Java's 0 builds no smaller levels at all; VoxelCraft keeps them uploaded
  and doesn't sample them. The picture is the same.
- The art is original and differs from Java's textures by design.
