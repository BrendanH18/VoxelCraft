## What is implemented

**Furnaces, smokers and blast furnaces** (`src/world/furnace.rs`). The
furnace is now Java's integer tick model: `litTimeRemaining`,
`litTotalTime`, `cookingTimer`, `cookingTotalTime` and `speedMultiplier`.
In 26.3 every smelting, smoking and blasting recipe takes 200 ticks; what
makes a smoker or blast furnace fast is the fuel: `cooking/speed_default`
is 2 in `#block/fast_cooking` blocks (1 elsewhere) and divides the recipe
time, rounded up, and each `cooking/time_*` provider integer-divides the
fuel's burn time by the block's reduction factor (2 or 1). `step` follows
`serverTick` line for line: the lit timer counts down first, so a fuel of N
ticks cooks for exactly N ticks; lighting sets the speed from the fuel (1
for a non-fuel such as an empty bucket) and rescales unfinished progress
(`ceil(ratio * newTotal)`); progress falls two ticks a tick
(`BURN_COOL_SPEED`) when nothing can light it and resets when the furnace
burns without a usable input or the output is full; an input with no
recipe here leaves it alone. `set_input` is Java's `setItem` (a different
item, or a refill of an emptied slot, restarts cooking with a recomputed
total; adding the same item doesn't), and the screens, the CLI and hopper
insertion use it. The fuel's crafting remainder (a lava bucket's bucket)
takes the emptied slot, a wet sponge fills a bucket waiting in the fuel
slot, a renamed result in the output stops smelting, and the lit block
state follows `isLit` each tick.

Experience is Java's `RecipesUsed` tally: one entry per recipe (the
26.3 recipes with a tag or list ingredient, `charcoal`, `glass`,
`leaf_litter` and the nugget melt-downs, are one recipe for all of their
inputs), each paying `floor(count * xp)` plus one with the fraction as its
chance, rolled separately. A player taking from the output (click, shift
click, the CLI) pays the whole tally, even for a partial take, and a take
that moves nothing pays nothing; breaking the furnace pays it at the block;
a hopper's take pays nothing and leaves it. `RecipeManager`'s quick-check
cache is a lookup optimisation with no behaviour of its own; the game's
recipe lookup is a match on the item.

Saves: the furnace record now holds the ticks, speed and tally
(`input;fuel;output;lit;lit_total;cook;cook_total;speed;kind;recipes`).
Older records (6 to 8 fields, seconds, doubled in fast furnaces, one
experience total) still load: their timers convert to ticks and the total
becomes one tally entry that pays the same.

**Brewing stands** (`src/world/brewing.rs`). Ticks are whole game ticks:
a brew starts on a tick with a brewable ingredient and fuel, then counts
down 400 ticks (`ceil(400 / speed)`, blaze powder's speed being 1); a spent
stand takes one blaze powder (the only item with a `brewing_fuel`
component: `brewing/uses_default`, 20 brews). The brew stops if the
ingredient item changes or nothing can be brewed, except on its last tick,
where Java brews whatever ingredient is there if it brews something. The
ingredient's crafting remainder takes its emptied slot or drops (dragon's
breath leaves glass bottles). `can_place` is Java's `canPlaceItem` (bottle
slots take a potion input only while empty, the ingredient slot a reagent,
the fuel slot brewing fuel). Placing a bottle by hand now keeps its
components (a lingering potion lost its contents before). The save format
is unchanged (the brew time stays in seconds, exact to the tick).

**Comparators** read furnaces and brewing stands as
`getRedstoneSignalFromContainer` does; the game's `f64` sum gives the same
signal as Java's float sum for every furnace and brewing stand fill
(checked exhaustively for 64-stacks and potions while writing the
fixture), and the fixture's cases pass.

**Enchanting tables** (`src/enchant.rs`, `src/app/screen.rs`,
`src/agent.rs`). The offers already used Java's algorithm; they now match
the jar-derived vectors: 8 seeds (including 0, -1 and both int extremes) ×
5 bookshelf counts (0, 1, 8, 15, 32) × 16 items give the three costs, the
clues and each offer's full roll. The bookshelf scan is Java's 32 places on
the ring at the table's height and one up, each needing a power transmitter
(`#replaceable`) halfway, at most 15 counted; the transmitter test is
`enchant::transmits_power`, checked against the tag over every registered
block. That check found the game's `Block::is_replaceable` differs from
`#replaceable` in four blocks (Java's tag has bushes and leaf litter, not
pale hanging moss or sculk veins); `transmits_power` corrects them, and
`is_replaceable` itself, which placement uses, is left for its owner. Taking an offer now takes the levels in creative too
(`onEnchantmentPerformed` does; `consume` spares only the lapis), and the
new enchantment seed is a full 32-bit draw (it was a float scaled to an
int, so only 2^24 seeds came up).

**Anvils.** `enchant::anvil_menu` is `AnvilMenu.createResult` with the name
box: a non-blank name other than the item's shown name costs a level and
names it, a blank box clears a custom name for a level, a rename alone is
capped at 39 levels, doesn't raise the prior-work penalty and leaves the
right-hand item where it is (even an identical spare, as in Java), and
names follow `validateName` (control characters and section signs
filtered, at most 50 characters). Enchanted books on the right count as
books even when empty. The CLI renames with `anvil name <text>`. Anvil
wear is unchanged (12% per survival use). A falling anvil now lands one
step more worn with Java's chance (`0.05 + 0.05 * blocks`, when the fall
deals damage, i.e. more than one block), or breaks without placing.

**Grindstones** (`src/grindstone.rs`). Already Java's apart from one rule:
two of an item that doesn't wear out merge only if they stack, so two
enchanted books no longer merge. The 11 cases (disenchanting, repairs,
books, curses kept at the first item's level, the repair-cost reset) and
their experience ranges match.

**Smithing.** The upgrade and trim recipes were already compared both ways
in `tests/java_recipes.rs`; `tests/workstations.rs` adds the slots: the
template, base and addition predicates accept exactly the items Java's
smithing recipes name, over every registered item.

## Differences from Java

- The anvil's name box exists in the core and on the CLI only: the desktop
  and controller anvil screens have no text field yet, so players there
  can't rename (or clear a name). LAN guests likewise.
- Falling anvils wear on landing but don't hurt entities (Java deals 2 per
  block past the first, at most 40, with the `falling_anvil` damage type).
- Stack names hold at most 64 UTF-8 bytes, so a 50-character name of
  multi-byte characters may be refused where Java takes it.
- The prior-work penalty is stored in 16 bits (Java: an int), so it stops
  at 65535 after 16 uses instead of 2^31 - 1.
- An anvil merging a right-hand item whose own enchantments clash with each
  other (only possible with commands) charges penalties in the game's
  enchantment order, where Java uses its hash map's order.
- Furnace slots written directly (world generation, the benchmark) never
  pass through `setItem`; such a furnace gets its cooking total when it
  first lights instead of Java's zero total (which would smelt one item on
  the first lit tick).
- Each furnace recipe's experience rounds with its own roll from the game's
  random sources, not the level's `RandomSource`; orbs aren't split per
  recipe the way `ExperienceOrb.award` splits them.
- When a creative player takes an offer with fewer levels than its cost,
  the level drops to 0 but keeps its progress (Java also zeroes progress
  and total experience).
- `Block::is_replaceable` (placement) still differs from Java's
  `#replaceable` for bushes, leaf litter, pale hanging moss and sculk veins;
  only the enchanting table's check is corrected.
- The enchanting table uses the level-up sound, not `ENCHANTMENT_TABLE_USE`
  (no dedicated sound exists).

## Gaps and seams

- Hopper and dropper sides are AUT-03's (Track C2). The Java rules they
  need are here: `Furnace::set_input` (slot 0's `setItem`) and
  `BrewingStand::can_place` (`canPlaceItem`; bottle slots only while
  empty). `world/automation.rs` now calls `set_input` for slot 0 (it used
  to reset the cook on every slot-0 write); its brewing insertion still
  uses its own check, which lets a hopper stack glass bottles into an
  occupied bottle slot where Java refuses.
- Not checked here: the furnace's and brewing stand's client screens (the
  progress arrows read `cook_progress`, `burn_progress` and
  `brew_progress` now), sounds, and anything in play. The owner's play is
  the evidence still to come.
