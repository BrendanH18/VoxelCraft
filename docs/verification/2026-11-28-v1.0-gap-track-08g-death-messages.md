# v1.0 gap-closing Track 8g: death messages as Java words them — 2026-11-28

## Scope

What [Track 8f left](2026-11-28-v1.0-gap-track-08f-details.md#remaining-differences)
in death messages:

1. Mobs named as Java names them (their kind's display name, "Zombie", or
   their custom name from a name tag) wherever a message names an
   attacker.
2. Java's message variants: `.item` (the attacker's named main-hand item,
   "using [Item]") and the `.player` kill credit ("while trying to escape
   …", "while fighting …") for falls, fire, lava, drowning and the rest, as
   `CombatTracker.getDeathMessage` builds them.
3. A player's Harming potion, lingering cloud or tipped arrow naming the
   thrower or shooter, and a fire charge used on its own fire (Java's
   crosshair stops at the fire).

All three are done; what remains is [listed below](#remaining-differences).

## What changed

### Mobs named as Java names them (item 1)

`simulation::death_message::java_words` puts the entity a cause's text
names VoxelCraft's way ("was slain by a zombie", "was killed by an evoker
using magic", "was slain by the Ender Dragon") as Java names it ("was
slain by Zombie", "was killed by Evoker using magic", "was slain by Ender
Dragon"): every mob kind's `entity.minecraft.*` name (its words
capitalised, which is Java's name for each kind VoxelCraft has), the
dragon, and the projectiles and clouds an ownerless hit names itself by
(Arrow, Wind Charge, Splash Potion, Area Effect Cloud). The cause texts
themselves keep their old form, so saved arrows' causes and the fixtures
don't change; the message is worded when the player dies.

The mob is the hit's attacker (`Cause::by`, `Owner::Mob`) for every mob
melee hit (`Mob::melee_attack`, which the zombies, spiders, piglins,
illagers, endermites, foxes and the rest use), wolves, iron golems,
creakings, dolphins, the ravager's roar, mobs' arrows, the drowned's
trident and the blaze's fireball. When the message is finished
(`Vitals::name_killer`, at the death), the game looks the attacker up
(`Entities::attacker`): a mob with a custom name (a name tag) is named by
it, "was slain by Grumbles"; one that's gone keeps its kind's name. A
player is named by their profile name, as Track 8f did, now for every
attacker shape below.

Seven causes VoxelCraft worded its own way now say Java's message (the
fixture test's `WORDING` list of differences is empty):

| Cause | Was | Now (Java) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ghast fireball's blast | was fireballed by a ghast | was blown up by Ghast (`explosion.player`; a player who punched it back is named) |
| Llama spit | was spat on by a llama | was slain by Llama (`mob`) |
| Ownerless wind charge | was blasted by a wind charge | was slain by Wind Charge (`mob`) |
| Guardian spikes | was pricked by a guardian | was killed while trying to hurt Guardian (`thorns`) |
| Bee sting | was stung by a bee | was stung to death (`sting`, which doesn't name the bee) |
| Pufferfish | was stung by a pufferfish | was slain by Pufferfish (`mob`) |
| Goat ram | was rammed by a goat | was slain by Goat (`mob`) |
| Dragon's breath | was killed by dragon's breath | was killed by Ender Dragon using magic (`indirectMagic`) |

### Old saves' texts read back

A saved death message is still parsed back to its cause
(`Cause::saved`): an old text the game no longer uses is in
`Cause::RETIRED` (the eight above join Track 8e's and 8f's), and a text
saved in Java's words ("was slain by Zombie") reads back as the cause it
was worded from. A player loaded dead has their message in Java's words
(`Vitals::restore`: "was slain by a zombie" comes back "was slain by
Zombie"). The v0.8 fixture holds no dead player and resaves unchanged.

### The variants (item 2)

Each player keeps Java's `CombatTracker` (`Vitals::combat`): every hit that
takes health, with the fall distance for a fall's, forgotten 5 seconds
after the last hit (15 once a living attacker hit them) or at death. And
their kill credit (`lastHurtByPlayer`, else `lastHurtByMob`): whoever's
hit landed in the last 5 seconds (a mob's only for damage that isn't
`#no_anger`, and only while it lives). At death,
`CombatTracker::death_message` picks the message as
`getDeathMessage` does:

- **A fall** (`fall`, `ender_pearl`): the hit before the biggest fall over
  five blocks makes it "was doomed to fall by Zombie" (`death.fell.assist`)
  or, when the fall was the killer's, "fell too far and was finished by"
  them; a hit with no one behind it, "was doomed to fall"; a fall on its
  own, "fell from a high place"; a fall under six blocks, Java's
  `death.attack.fall`, "hit the ground too hard" (it said "fell from a
  high place").
- **The kill credit** (`.player`): a death from a source with no entity
  (drowning, lava, fire, burning, magma, campfires, starving, freezing,
  suffocation, cactus, berry bushes, `/kill`, the void, lightning,
  withering, magic, a short fall) names whoever the player was escaping:
  "drowned while trying to escape Zombie", "tried to swim in lava to escape
  Alex", "walked into fire while fighting Zombie", "was killed by magic
  while trying to escape Alex".
- **A named weapon** (`.item`): when the attacker's main-hand item has a
  custom name (an anvil-renamed sword, a mob's picked-up one), "was slain by
  Alex using [Excalibur]", "was impaled by Alex with [Poseidon]", "was
  doomed to fall by Zombie using [Shove]".

`VARIANTS` holds the variant texts, checked against the fixture.

### Potions, clouds and tipped arrows (item 3)

A splash potion's Instant Damage is `indirectMagic(potion, owner)`: "was
killed by Alex using magic" for a player's, "was killed by Witch using
magic" for a witch's (the thrown potion now remembers its witch, not
saved), "was killed by Splash Potion using magic" for a dispensed one.
A lingering cloud's is `indirectMagic(cloud, owner)`: its thrower, or
"Area Effect Cloud". Before, all three were the drunk potion's "was
killed by magic". A tipped arrow of Harming is Java's `magic` (the arrow
adds the effect, whose tick hurts), so it stays "was killed by magic",
which the arrow's hit now turns into "was killed by magic while trying to
escape" its shooter through the kill credit.

### A fire charge on its own fire (item 3)

Fire and soul fire are now a sixteenth-high floor to the crosshair
(`BaseFireBlock.SHAPE`), as in Java; they were a full cell. A fire charge
used again on the fire it just set clicks the fire's top, so
`FireChargeItem.useOn` tries the cell above, where fire can't go, and
does nothing, keeping the charge; before, the crosshair met the fire's
full-cell side and lit the cell beside it. Flint and steel does the same.

## Remaining differences

- **Fall locations** aren't tracked: every fall is Java's `GENERIC`
  ("fell from a high place"), never "fell off a ladder", "fell off some
  vines", "fell off scaffolding" or "fell while climbing", and the hardest
  hit while climbing or in water never stands in for the fall
  (`getMostSignificantFall`'s alternative).
- **A creeper's own blast** names its kind, never a custom name: the
  creeper is gone by the time the message is put together (Java keeps the
  source). The same for any attacker removed before the death is handled.
- **A tamed wolf's** hit on a player doesn't give its owner the kill
  credit (`resolvePlayerResponsibleForDamage`), and the remaining hits
  that don't carry their mob (a goat's ram, the dragon's bite and wings,
  evoker fangs, the guardian's beam and spikes, a
  ghast fireball's direct hit) name the kind but no custom name and give
  no credit.
- **Witches' potions** saved mid-flight forget their witch (as does a
  reloaded game's), and so name the potion.
- Java's `.player` variants for `sulfurCubeHot` put the attacker first
  ("Alex showed … that not just the floor is lava"); VoxelCraft's message
  always starts with the victim, and the sulfur cube's damage keeps its
  base message.
- From earlier tracks, still open: a pearl in another dimension waits for
  the host there; the credits' chunks stay loaded; the villager's "no" is
  original audio nobody has listened to.
