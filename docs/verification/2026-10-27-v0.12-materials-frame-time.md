# v0.12 materials scene: first frame-time runs (2026-10-27)

[Documentation index](../README.md) · [Roadmap v0.12](../roadmap.md#v012-scope-building-materials) · [Development: scene suite](../development.md#live-performance-scene-suite)

**Status: not acceptance evidence.** One of three runs was valid, and that
run missed the 240 FPS gate. The scene and the readiness timing fix are in
place; a clean three-run set on an idle M5 is still owed.

## The scene

`materials` (in `benchmarks/scenes.json` and `src/app/benchmark.rs`) clears
the ground ahead of the camera and builds a stone floor. On it, one state of
every block in the v0.12 state ranges (16384–29695) is cycled over a 25 x 25
grid, three blocks apart. A block that pops off without support gets stone
on one side at a time until it stays. Every sign gets text on both sides. A
25 x 4 wall of item frames and glow item frames holding items stands in front
of the grid. The camera turns in place, as in the building scene. Item drops
from blocks that still couldn't stand are removed before sampling.

Building the scene takes about 6–7 seconds of `set_block` calls. Readiness
was timed from the start of the frame that built it, so with a short warm-up
the build time landed in the first sampled frame (a 7.5-second "frame" in a
smoke run). Readiness, warm-up and samples are now timed from after the
build, for every scene. Older scenes build in milliseconds, so their earlier
results are unaffected.

A screenshot taken during development (with a local, uncommitted build that
allowed `--screenshot` alongside a scene) showed the full grid, signs with
text and the frame wall.

## Runs

`python3 tools/benchmark_scenes.py --scenes materials,building --output target/bench-v012`
at `4198f5d`'s source: Apple M5, AC power, 1920 x 1080, rd 8, enhanced
graphics, `Immediate` presentation, 10 s warm-up and 60 s samples.

| Run | Average FPS | 1% low FPS | p99 ms | Worst ms | Valid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| materials 1 | 118.8 | 69.2 | 11.97 | 20.5 | No: window hidden or occluded |
| materials 2 | 114.3 | 40.4 | 14.45 | 547.2 | No: occluded; ticks short |
| materials 3 | 548.8 | 206.8 | 4.33 | 15.0 | Yes; misses the gate (1% low below 240, p99 above 4.17 ms) |
| building 1–3 | 76–181 | 33–46 | 20.0–20.9 | up to 1,009.6 | No: window hidden or occluded |

The machine was in use during the runs: the benchmark window was covered for
most of them, which the runner detects and rejects. For comparison, the
2026-10-16 M5 baseline building scene averaged about 630 FPS with 1% lows of
261–273 FPS.

## What this shows and doesn't

- The one valid materials run suggests a scene full of v0.12 blocks costs
  more than the older building scene, enough to drop the 1% low below the
  240 FPS gate. One run on a machine in use is not enough to say so.
- The texture-paging cost on its own isn't isolated. The M5 binds two
  texture pages once `tex::COUNT` passes 2048 and can't be forced back to
  one array, so separating paging from the scene's extra geometry (signs,
  candles, banners, frames) needs a profile, not this suite.
- Owed: three valid runs of `materials` (and the building scene to compare)
  on an idle M5 on AC power with the window uncovered. If the gate still
  fails, profile the frame for texture paging versus model geometry. The
  gate itself belongs to v0.20.
