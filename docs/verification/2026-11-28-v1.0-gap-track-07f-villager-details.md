What they say:

- `FoxHeldItemLayer`: translate to the posed head's `x, y, z` (in
  pixels / 16), scale 0.75 for a baby, rotate z by `headRollAngle`, y by
  the look's yaw and x by its pitch (the render state's, not the head
  part's), translate `(0.06, 0.27, -0.5)` (asleep `(0.46, 0.26, 0.22)`;
  the baby's `(0.06, 0.26, -0.5)` and `(0.4, 0.26, 0.15)`), rotate x 90
  degrees (and z 90 asleep), then draw the main-hand item resolved for
  `GROUND`. It runs inside `FoxRenderer.setupRotations`' pitch.
- `FoxModel.legMotionPos` is a field of the model, `+= 0.67` in every
  `setupAnim` of a faceplanted fox; the renderer keeps one adult and one
  baby model.
- `VillagerModel.setupAnim`: unhappy, `head.zRot = 0.3 * sin(0.45 *
  ageInTicks)` and `head.xRot = 0.4` (the look's yaw kept); otherwise
  `zRot = 0`.
- `Villager.mobInteract`: unless the item is a villager spawn egg or the
  villager is dead, trading or asleep: a baby gets `setUnhappy` (the use
  succeeds, no trading); an adult with no offers gets `setUnhappy` for
  the main hand and the use is consumed. `setUnhappy` sets the counter
  to 40 and plays `entity.villager.no` on the server; `Villager.tick`
  counts it down. `WanderingTrader.mobInteract` never sets it.

## What changed

### 1. Profession outfits (MOB-06)

`VillagerProfessionLayer`'s profession layer is now each profession's own
clothes, this game's original art in the box style (7e had one plain
apron colour per job and hats for six of them). Each is a set of boxes on
the robe, plus pieces on the face, sleeves or gloves on the arms, and the
hat; all sit between the type's outfit and the level badge, in Java's
layer order:

| Profession | Robe | Head | Arms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Armorer | iron-grey bib apron, straps, rivets | welding mask with a visor slit | dark gloves |
| Butcher | white bib apron with red stripes at the hem | red headband (partial hat; was white) | — |
| Cartographer | tan waistcoat, gold buttons, a rolled map in a hip pouch | gold monocle on a chain | — |
| Cleric | purple vestments (robe, hem, gold belt), mantle, gold stole | — | purple sleeves, gold cuffs |
| Farmer | brown bib overalls with a pocket | straw hat (7e's) | — |
| Fisherman | waxed yellow apron with a fish, blue neckerchief | bucket hat (7e's) | — |
| Fletcher | green tunic, a quiver of arrows on the back | cap with a feather (7e's) | — |
| Leatherworker | stitched leather apron with pockets | — | leather gloves |
| Librarian | cream tabard with red edges, red collar, a book patch on the chest | tall cap (7e's) | — |
| Mason | stone-grey apron, a pocket and a chisel | — | grey gloves |
| Shepherd | woolly cape, satchel on its strap | wool cap (7e's) | — |

| Weaponsmith | dark leather apron, studded hem | eyepatch on its strap | dark gloves |

- The cleric's and nitwit's clothes repaint the whole robe and sleeves
  (boxes grown over the type's), so they hide the type's touches on the
  robe (the snow coat's fur, the taiga shawl and so on) as an opaque
  profession texture covers the type's pixels; the type's headwear still
  follows `typeHatVisible`.
- Zombie villagers wear the same clothes on their humanoid arms and
  thinner robe, rotted (the 7e fade) as their own `profession/` textures
  fade them; 7e drew their aprons and hats unfaded. Babies and the cub
  wear no profession, as before.
- Every robe colour is a fixed colour of the profession, not a key, so
  the type's palette shows only where the profession leaves the robe
  bare.

### 2. The fox's mouth item and paddle (MOB-06)

- **`FoxHeldItemLayer`.** A fox's (or kit's) main-hand item is drawn in its
  mouth with Java's transform chain above, in its `ground` display
  transform, through the renderer's item model pass (as 7e's shown trade
  is). The pounce and faceplant pitch carry it with the head. This game's
  foxes get a main-hand item only from `--spawn fox,x,y,z,item` (and now
  `fox,x,y,z,baby,item`) today; picking items up and spawning with one are
  Track 2f's.
- **`legMotionPos`.** The faceplanted paddle now reads a counter kept per
  model (adult and kit), advanced 0.67 each time a faceplanted fox is
  posed, so it runs per rendered frame and every faceplanted fox shares
  it, as Java's model field does (7e ran it on each fox's own clock at
  0.67 a tick).

### 3. The unhappy head shake (MOB-06)

- **Simulation.** Villagers keep `unhappyCounter` (`Villager::unhappy`,
  never saved, as Java's synched data isn't). A use on a villager with no
  offers (jobless or a nitwit) sets it to 40 before the refusal
  (`Used::MerchantRefused`); a use on a villager child that is neither
  asleep nor trading sets it to 40 and is spent on it (also
  `MerchantRefused`; children used to let the use fall through to other
  interactions). Wandering traders never shake. `Mob::update` counts it
  down by one a tick for villagers (`Villager.tick`).
- **Model.** `VillagerModel.setupAnim`'s shake on the adult and the child
  (whose `BabyVillagerModel` runs the same code): the head bowed to 0.4,
  rolling `0.3 * sin(0.45 * ageInTicks)`, the look's yaw kept. The
  wandering trader's model reads it too, as `WanderingTraderRenderer`
  does.
- **LAN.** The host sends a villager's running counter with each pose
  (`sad`, only while above 0); guests draw the shake from it.

### 4. Capture tooling

`--spawn kind,x,y,z,baby,item` spawns a baby holding an item. Three new
scenes in `tools/capture_scenes.json`: `mobs-still-villager-professions`,
`mobs-still-zombie-villager-professions` and `mobs-still-villager-faces`.

## Remaining differences

- **Textures, not boxes** (as 7e): Java paints each profession's texture
  over the same boxes; here the clothes are this game's boxes and
  colours, designed after the professions' looks, not copied. The level
  badge keeps 7e's one shape in five materials.
- **`entity.villager.no`.** `setUnhappy` plays the villager's "no" sound;
  this game's villager voice has only ambient, hurt and death calls, so
  the shake is silent (sound is outside this track).
- **The villager spawn egg.** Java's `mobInteract` lets a villager spawn
  egg through before the shake or the trade; this game's spawn-egg use on
  a mob already runs before every villager interaction, so nothing
  differs in practice.
- **The fox's item in the simulation.** Foxes spawning with an item
  (`populateDefaultEquipmentSlots`, 20 percent), picking items up, eating
  and dropping them are fox behaviours (Track 2f); until then only capture
  scenes give a fox an item.
- **`legMotionPos` across renders.** It advances once per fox pose; this
  game poses a mob once per frame in the entity pass, as Java does. The
  counter also advances in tests that pose foxes; nothing reads it but the
  paddle.
