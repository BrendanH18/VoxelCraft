## Scope

The remaining differences the Track 5b, 5c and 4c records list in portals,
travel and the dragon, with the owner's decision of 2026-11-27 (match
Java: spectators can't use portals):

1. spectators pass through Nether portals, End portals and End gateways
   without travelling, and Track 5b's spectator cancel path goes;
2. a Nether portal whose exit stands along the other axis turns the
   player's momentum along with their view;
3. llama spit breaks End crystals; a perched dragon sets arrows alight; a
   waterlogged block the dragon breaks leaves what Java leaves;
4. whatever else those records list that Java's code closes.

Items 1 to 3 are done. Item 3's waterlogged blocks were already right (see
below). Item 4 closed four more: the dragon's block breaking posts level
event 2008 instead of a sound, its wing beats post the `flap` game event,
an arrow bounced off it nearly still drops as an item, and every dragon's
fireballs meet every dragon's parts. What remains is
[listed below](#remaining-differences).

## What changed

### Spectators and portals (item 1, GEN-04c)

Java never lets a spectator touch a portal: `Player.tick` sets `noPhysics`
for spectators before `super.tick()`, `isAffectedByBlocks` is
`!noPhysics`, so `checkInsideBlocks` never calls `NetherPortalBlock`'s,
`EndPortalBlock`'s or `EndGatewayBlock`'s `entityInside`, and nothing
calls `setAsInsidePortal` (Track 5c's finding; MC-46421).

- `GameMode::uses_portals` (false for spectators) gates
  `Game::update_portal`: a spectator in a Nether portal is treated as out
  of it (a countdown already begun runs down, the portal look fades and no
  trigger sound plays), and End portals (including the credits from the
  End) and End gateways do nothing.
- Track 5b's spectator path goes: `travel` no longer asks the
  destination's save for a portal (`saved_portal_near` and its test are
  removed, and `find_portal_in` is private to `world::portal` again), and
  an arrival no longer declines to build a portal for a spectator. Java's
  `getExitPortal` check only matters for a player who turns spectator
  between the tick their countdown last counted and the trip itself; here
  the mode at departure counts, and a player who left as a survivor gets
  the portal Java would have built for them.
- LAN: guests' portal travel stays host-controlled, unchanged. A spectator
  guest standing in a portal no longer gets the "Portal travel is
  host-controlled" notice, since in Java the portal does nothing for them.

### The quarter turn turns the motion (item 2, GEN-04c)

`createDimensionTransition` returns `outputRotation` 90 when the exit
portal stands along the other axis, with `Relative.DELTA` (which includes
`ROTATE_DELTA`) and `Relative.ROTATION`. `calculateAbsolute` then turns
the motion by `yRot(old yaw − new yaw)` after an `xRot` by the pitch's
change (zero), in float radians through `Mth`'s tables. The arrival now
does the same (`world::portal::quarter_turn_motion`, sharing
`rotate_delta` with Track 4c's `end_arrival_motion`), with the yaw the
player has as they arrive: what was ahead of them is still ahead.

### Llama spit and End crystals (item 3, PRO-05)

`LlamaSpit.onHitEntity` hurts whatever it hits by 1 (`spit`) while its
llama exists (`getOwner()` is a `LivingEntity`) and never discards
itself there; only blocks, water and non-air cells stop it. An End crystal
breaks to any damage but a dragon's (`EndCrystal.hurtServer`). Spit now
breaks a crystal it passes through while its llama is alive, once per
crystal, and flies on (`Thrown::update`, `Boxes::crystal_at`). It still
passes dragon parts (it can't hurt a dragon, Track 4c).

### Arrows on a perched dragon (item 3, PRO-05)

`AbstractDragonSittingPhase.onHurt` takes an arrow's or wind charge's
damage away and calls `igniteForSeconds(1)` on it; `EnderDragon.hurt`
asks it in every phase but `DYING`. `AbstractArrow.onHitEntity` then
deflects the refused arrow (`REVERSE`, 0.2: back at a tenth of its speed),
and drops it as an item (`spawnAtLocation(.., 0.1)`, if it may be picked
up) and discards it when its motion is under `1.0E-7` squared blocks a
tick.

- Arrows carry their own fire (`Arrow::fire`, ticks, not saved like the
  rest of their flight) beside Flame: `Arrow::burning` is either. The
  dragon's `scorches` (an arrow-like blow on a perching phase, not dying,
  not fire) sets 20 ticks; the fire burns down a tick at a time and water
  puts it out (`AbstractArrow.tick`'s `clearFire`). Everything Flame did,
  a burning arrow does: it sets what it hits alight for five seconds and
  lights candles.
- The bounce now starts from outside the part (the sub-step's start),
  where Java leaves the arrow at the point it met the box. Before, the
  arrow stayed inside the box and met it again the next tick, bouncing
  back and forth.
- A bounced arrow left almost still drops as its item.

### The dragon's walls (item 3, item 4, PRO-05)

`checkWalls` calls `level.removeBlock(pos, false)`, which sets the cell's
`fluidState.createLegacyBlock()`: a waterlogged block leaves its water
source. That was already the case here: `World::break_blocks` goes through
`World::edit`, whose `waterlog::carried` leaves the source when a block
that holds water is removed (since v0.9). Track 4c's record was wrong; a
test now covers it. Sources and flowing water stay (removing them changes
nothing, so Java doesn't count them as broken either), as before.

`checkWalls` runs on the head, neck and body boxes in turn, and each that
broke a block posts level event 2008 at a random cell of its own box
(`nextInt` per axis); the client draws one `EXPLOSION` particle at that
cell's centre and plays nothing. The game played its own smash sound (the
explosion at a quarter volume) once per tick instead. Now each part posts
`EntityEvent::DragonSmashed`, which draws that particle, and the sound is
gone (`MobSound::DragonSmash` removed).

### Wing beats and fireballs (item 4, PRO-05)

- `EnderDragon.aiStep` starts with `processFlappingMovement`, which posts
  the `flap` game event at the dragon on each beat on the server (the
  dragon's `MovementEmission` is `ALL`; `noPhysics` only skips `move`'s
  own emission). Each `DragonFlap` sound now also posts `flap`
  (`Entities::update`), which sculk sensors hear.
- Every `EnderDragonPart` is pickable, so a dragon fireball meets the
  parts of every dragon, not only those its owner shares a list with. The
  fight's fireballs now tick with the wild dragons' boxes too
  (`Fight::update_beside`) and the wild dragons' with the fight's
  (`Wild::update`'s `fight`).

## Remaining differences

- **A player who turns spectator mid-countdown.** Java's last
  `entityInside` can land in the tick before the trip, so a player who
  switches to spectator in that one tick still travels (to an existing
  portal; none is built). Here the mode is checked in the same tick, so
  they don't.
- **The portal countdown** (found here, not in the records): Java takes a
  traveller on the 81st tick after entering (`portalTime++ >= 80`, counted
  from the tick after `setAsInsidePortal`) and, outside, takes 4 ticks off
  the count each tick (`decayTick`). The game travels on the 80th tick
  inside and takes off 2 a tick. The HUD's purple and the trigger sound
  read the same count, so changing it is a small separate change.
- **A bounced arrow's look.** Java turns its yaw 170–190° and then eases it
  20% a tick toward its motion; VoxelCraft's arrows always point along
  their motion, so a bounced one points straight back.
- **Rain** doesn't put out a burning arrow (Java's `isInWaterOrRain`);
  water does.
- **Tridents** bounced off a perched dragon are set alight in Java, which
  lets one light a candle or campfire or prime TNT within the second;
  VoxelCraft's tridents carry no fire and their block hits don't do those.
  A wind charge's ignition is invisible in both (it bursts at once).
- **Spit elsewhere** (found here, outside this track's code): Java's spit
  flies on after hitting a player or mob as it does after a crystal;
  VoxelCraft's stops on them. A crystal the spit breaks explodes with no
  attacker here, where Java names the llama (`explosion(crystal,
  llama)`), as for a skeleton's arrow already.
- **Bubble columns.** The dragon skips them as water; Java's
  `removeBlock` turns one into a water source (counted as a break, so a
  2008 particle) and the column re-forms from the block below.
- **Particles and rain** near the portal, the portal texture's frames and
  face shading, sounds, the client lightmap: Track 5c's remaining
  differences are rendering and art, not portal behaviour, and stay.
- **Projectile geometry, arrival of non-players through End portals**:
  Track 4c's remaining differences outside this scope stay as recorded
  there.
