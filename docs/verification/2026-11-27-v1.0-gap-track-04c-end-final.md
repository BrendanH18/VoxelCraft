## Scope

The remaining differences of [Track 4b](2026-11-27-v1.0-gap-track-04b-end-leftovers.md#remaining-differences):

1. every projectile Java lets hit the Ender Dragon's parts does so, with
   Java's outcome per projectile and phase;
2. with `mobGriefing` off, the dragon's `inWall` slowdown and block
   handling as Java's;
3. arriving in the End keeps the player's motion as Java 26.3 does;
4. decoration beside End cities checked against the running jar.

All four are done. Item 4 found a real difference (every generation 7 city
stood one block too high), fixed for generation 7.

## What changed

### Projectiles and the dragon (item 1, PRO-05)

`EnderDragon.hurt` is now `Dragon::hurt(part, Blow)`, a `Blow` carrying
what the `DamageSource` tells Java: damage, whether a player caused it,
whether it is `#always_hurts_ender_dragons` (`#is_explosion`: explosions
and fireworks), whether its direct entity is an `AbstractArrow` or a
`WindCharge`, and whether it is `#is_fire`. In Java's order: fire is
refused (the dragon is fire immune, `isInvulnerableToBase`); nothing lands
on a dying dragon; perched (`SITTING_*`, not hovering) an arrow, trident or
player's wind charge does nothing; the head **and neck** take the damage
(26.3 added the neck), the rest a quarter plus up to one; under 0.01 the
hit is refused; otherwise it **lands**, and only a player's hit or an
explosion takes health. A hit that lands without health (a skeleton's
arrow, or one inside the immunity window) is gone, not deflected: before,
a mob's arrow took health and a weaker player's arrow inside the window
bounced off.

Every projectile now meets the parts of the fight's dragon and of dragons
no fight owns (and End crystals), with Java's outcome:

| Projectile | Java's outcome on a dragon part | On an End crystal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrow, spectral, tipped | a player's: its damage (now with the critical bonus); a mob's or a dispenser's: lands, nothing; perched or dying: bounces back at a tenth (already) | breaks it (already) |
| Trident | `trident` 8 (no Impaling: the dragon isn't `#sensitive_to_impaling`), a player's only, nothing perched or dying; always bounces (`× (-0.01, -0.1, -0.01)`) with the hit sound and falls to its owner | breaks it, bounces |
| Snowball, eggs | `hurt(thrown, 0)`: refused; the projectile is gone (an egg may hatch where it hit) | breaks it |
| Ender pearl | refused; the thrower goes to where the pearl was when the tick began (`oldPosition()`) | breaks it, thrower teleported |
| Bottle o' Enchanting | bursts, orbs thrown back along its flight | bursts, crystal stands |
| Splash potion | shatters there; Harming reaches the dragon as a `LivingEntity` (its own 16 × 8 box, `hurtServer` through its body, a player's only), Healing heals it, every lasting effect is refused (`addEffect` is false) — near misses too, as in Java | shatters, crystal stands |
| Lingering potion | shatters; its cloud doses the dragon (feet within the radius) with Harming (3 per amplifier step, a player's only) or Healing | shatters |
| Wind charge | a player's: 1 (1.25 on the body), nothing perched; a breeze's: lands, nothing; both burst | passes it by (`canHitEntity`) |
| Ghast fireball | fire: nothing; it explodes there, and the blast hurts and pushes the dragon (credited to a player who punched it back) | explodes, which breaks it |
| Blaze fireball | fire: nothing; gone, no block set alight | gone |
| Dragon fireball | bursts into breath, placed on the nearest player or dragon within four blocks; its own dragon included (the dragon isn't pickable, so `leftOwner` holds at once, and a part isn't its owner); the breath never hurts its dragon | bursts |
| Wither skull | `witherSkull` 8 from the Wither: lands, nothing (no Wither effect: a part isn't living); its blast follows and hurts | breaks it, then the blast |
| Shulker bullet | `mobProjectile` 4 from the shulker: lands, nothing, no levitation; gone | breaks it |
| Firework rocket | bursts on hitting it; a burst with stars hurts every `LivingEntity` within 5 blocks of the rocket measured from its feet, in sight of its feet or middle: the dragon through its body, whoever launched it (`#is_explosion`) | bursts, crystal stands (only the living are hurt) |
| Fishing hook | hooks the part and rides it at 80% of its height; reeling in wears the rod by 5 (the pull is lost when the dragon places its parts again) | hooks it |
| Llama spit | `spit` 1 from the llama: nothing, and spit isn't stopped by what it hits | (see remaining differences) |

Java 26.3 lets no one turn the dragon's own fireball back: a punch turns
only `#redirectable_projectile` (ghast fireballs and wind charges), and
`AbstractHurtingProjectile.hurtServer` refuses everything else. So "its
own reflected" fireball never happens in Java; a ghast fireball punched
back is credited to the player, as before.

Two new pieces carry these hits: `dragon::Boxes`, a snapshot of every
crystal's and part's box (and each living dragon as a whole) taken once
the dragons have moved, which the projectiles test against; and
`EntityEvent::DragonStruck`/`DragonHealed`, which they push and the
entities apply once all projectiles have moved, in the same tick (none of
these projectiles acts on whether its hit landed; arrows, which do, keep
striking at once).

Explosions: Java's `ServerExplosion.hurtEntities` reaches the dragon
itself and each of its parts, each measured from its feet with its own
box's exposure. `Dragon::blast` now does so (it used the parts' centres,
full exposure), and pushes the dragon itself
(`(1 - d) × exposure × multiplier` along its eye, 6.8 up, from the
centre), which carries it once it next flies; wind bursts push it with
their own radius and multiplier (1.22 for a player's charge).

The dragon's fireball now starts where Java's does: a block ahead of the
head, level with its top (`head − viewVector`, `getY(0.5) + 0.5`; the
dragon's view vector points away from its head). VoxelCraft started it a
block behind the head's centre, which would have burst it on the neck now
that fireballs meet parts.

### `checkWalls` and `mobGriefing` (item 2, PRO-05)

`checkWalls` runs over the head, neck and body in every phase (VoxelCraft
skipped it while perched). Each block that is not air or
`#dragon_transparent` (`light`, `#fire`) breaks without drops when
`mobGriefing` is on and it isn't `#dragon_immune`; a fluid is left
(`removeBlock` puts the fluid back); every other such block is a wall.
With `mobGriefing` off every such block, fluids included, is a wall. The
tags are Java 26.3's: the immune list gained crying obsidian, End
gateways, moving pistons and respawn anchors, and Nether portals now
break, as Java's do (barriers, command, structure, jigsaw and test blocks
aren't registered). The game rule reaches the dragons each tick
(`Fight::griefing_off`, `Wild::griefing_off`).

The wing beat follows `aiStep`: 0.1 a tick perched, `0.2 / (10 v + 1) ×
2^vy` in flight, halved only in a wall. VoxelCraft halved it always, so
the wings beat at half Java's rate (and 0.05 perched). The flap sound is
Java's `isFlapping`: as the last tick's beat took its cosine up through
−0.3, perched or not (it was downward, in flight only).

### Arrival in the End (item 3, GEN-04b)

`EndPortalBlock.getPortalDestination` uses `Relative.DELTA` (with
`ROTATE_DELTA`) and `X_ROT` and sets the yaw to west; the client keeps
its player's motion across the respawn (`handleRespawn` with
`KEEP_ENTITY_DATA`) and `PositionMoveRotation.calculateAbsolute` turns it
by the old yaw less the new. `Arrival::EndSpawn` now carries that motion,
computed when the player enters the portal
(`world::portal::end_arrival_motion`, Java's float radians and `Mth`
tables); the player arrives facing west, keeps their pitch (already) and
keeps moving as they were relative to where they faced.

### Decoration beside End cities (item 4, GEN-04b)

The fixture's four cities start in the chunks VoxelCraft's do, and all
173 pieces have Java's template, rotation, `OW` and box, in Java's order,
but every one stood one block higher. Java measures a start from
`getFirstOccupiedHeight` (`WORLD_SURFACE_WG` less one: the top block),
VoxelCraft from the first free block above it. Generation 7 now uses
Java's height (and, as Java does, drops a start whose lowest corner tops
out at y 59); generations 5 and 6 keep theirs, so their golden hashes
don't move. With that, generation 7 matches the jar: the end stone outside
the pieces' boxes in all 676 chunks, the end stone of every chunk clear of
the cities, and the chorus of all 595 chunks whose plants can't meet a
neighbour's, 114 of them beside or under a city. The city blocks are
original (FE-02), yet no column a piece covers grows a plant in either
game, so the chorus draws stay in step. Eleven chunks' end stone inside a
piece's box differs, where the templates' own blocks and air differ
(FE-02). The grids hold no return gateway.

## Remaining differences

- **Projectile geometry.** Thrown projectiles test a point at each
  sub-step against the boxes (VoxelCraft's existing model), not Java's
  swept box with its margin; where a mob and a part are met in the same
  sub-step the mob wins. Hits other than arrows' are applied after every
  projectile of the tick has moved, still in that tick.
- **Arrows on a perched dragon** aren't set alight for a second
  (`igniteForSeconds(1)` in `onHurt`), a bounced arrow isn't turned
  170–190° for its one tick, and one left almost still isn't dropped as an
  item.
- **Llama spit** doesn't break End crystals (Java's `spit` does, and flies
  on); on the dragon it already behaves as Java's.
- **Dragon fireballs** meet the parts of the dragons their own owner
  shares a list with: the fight's fireballs the fight's dragon, a wild
  dragon's the wild dragons; never the other kind.
- **Waterlogged blocks** the dragon breaks become air (Java leaves the
  water); the break plays VoxelCraft's smash sound rather than level event
  2008's particles; the flap posts no `flap` game event.
- **Arrival** keeps the motion for players; VoxelCraft still sends no
  entity but players through an End portal.
- **Outside this scope, for the owner**: a Nether portal whose exit faces
  the other way turns the player a quarter but not their motion (Java's
  `ROTATE_DELTA` turns both); that is Track 5's area.
