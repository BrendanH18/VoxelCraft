## Scope

The five gaps the [gap-closing plan](../roadmap.md#v10-gap-plan-2026-11-27)
gives Track 1:

1. Darkness darkening the lightmap as Java's client does, not only the fog
   (PLY-03b).
2. Smite and Bane of Arthropods on mobs' weapons when mobs hit (MOB-02d).
3. A creeper's fall adding to its fuse (`Creeper.causeFallDamage`, MOB-02d).
4. Pointed dripstone's doubled fall damage (`PointedDripstoneBlock.fallOn`)
   for players and mobs (MOB-02d).
5. Mobs' fall distance saved and loaded (MOB-02d).

Mobs using spears belong to Track 2 and weren't touched.

## What changed

### PLY-03b: Darkness darkens the lightmap

Java 26.3's `lightmap.fsh` builds each light level as ambient (or night
vision) + sky + block light, darkens it for the boss overlay, then
subtracts `DarknessScale` **before** clamping to 0–1, and finally moves it
toward `notGamma` by `BrightnessFactor`. `LightmapRenderStateExtractor`
sets, from the player's Darkness blend factor `b`
(`getEffectBlendFactor(DARKNESS, partialTick)` times the Darkness Pulsing
option, 1 by default):

- `brightness = max(0, gamma − b)`: the Brightness option loses the blend;
- `darknessEffectScale = max(0, cos((tickCount − partialTick) · π · 0.025)
  · 0.45 · b)`: a pulse every 80 ticks that rests at 0 half the time.

The game now does the same:

- **The blend** (`src/simulation/effects.rs`): `Effects` keeps Darkness's
  `BlendState` (not saved, as in Java). It ticks after the countdown, as
  `tickClient` does: a step of 1/22 toward 1 while the effect has more than
  22 ticks left, toward 0 within its last 22, eased between ticks by the
  frame's partial tick. A re-application keeps the blend
  (`copyBlendState`); a newly given Darkness fades in; one loaded with the
  world is full at once (the server sends join-time effects without
  blending, so the client calls `skipBlending`); milk or `/effect clear`
  drops it. `Effects::darkness_blend` and `Effects::darkness_scale` give the
  two numbers; the pulse runs on the effects' tick counter, the player's
  `tickCount`.
- **The fog** uses the same blend. Before, it approximated the blend as
  `ticks left / 22`, which faded out right but didn't fade in.
- **The lightmap** (`src/render/shaders/{chunk,entity,block_model}.wgsl`):
  `g.effects.z` carries the scale and `g.effects.y` the lowered Brightness
  (`src/render/mod.rs`, `FrameParams::darkness`). `night_vision` now takes
  the light both clamped and unclamped: it adds back what lay above full,
  subtracts the scale, and `brightness` clamps, so the subtraction comes
  before the clamp as in Java. Bright light (daylight with a torch, whose
  sum is above 1.45) stays at full through the pulse while dim corners go
  black. With no Darkness the result is the same as before (the extra terms
  are exact zeros). The skyless (Nether and End) path now hands the
  unclamped sum to the same function. The chunk shader's per-vertex
  lightmap runs the same code, so uniformly lit quads still match
  per-fragment lighting.
- **Particles and rain** (`particles.wgsl`, `weather.wgsl`) take the scale
  off their own light the same way; full-bright particles keep their light.
- The split-screen views pass each player's own Darkness; the title screen
  passes none.

The game's night vision is still its older formula (scaling the light
toward full) rather than 26.3's `max(AmbientColor, NightVisionColor ·
factor)`; this track left it alone (see below).

### MOB-02d: Smite and Bane of Arthropods on mobs' weapons

`Mob.doHurtTarget` runs `EnchantmentHelper.modifyDamage` with the target,
so a mob's main-hand Smite adds 2.5 a level against `#sensitive_to_smite`
(`#undead`: skeletons, strays, wither skeletons, skeleton horses, bogged,
parched, the zombies tag's nine kinds, the Wither and phantoms) and Bane
of Arthropods against `#sensitive_to_bane_of_arthropods` (bees, endermites,
silverfish, spiders, cave spiders), and `doPostAttackEffects` then gives an
arthropod Bane's Slowness IV for 1.5 to 1.5 + 0.5 per level above I seconds.

- `EntityEvent::MobHit` gained `melee`: true for every `doHurtTarget`
  hit (all mob melee, the warden's included), false for projectiles and
  the warden's sonic boom.
- `Entities::resolve_mob_hits` (`src/entity/nether.rs`) adds the attacker's
  main-hand `creature_damage_bonus` (new in `src/enchant.rs`, the
  target-dependent part of `damage_bonus`) for the target's kind on a melee
  hit, and on a hit that hurt runs the existing `bane_slowness`.
- `MobKind::creature` already matched the 26.3 tags; checked against the
  jar's `skeletons`, `zombies` and `undead` lists (17 kinds). Players are in
  neither tag, so hits on players are unchanged.

### MOB-02d: a creeper's fall adds to its fuse

`Creeper.causeFallDamage` adds `(int) (fallDistance · 1.5)` ticks to the
swell, then holds it to `maxSwell − 5` (25 of 30 ticks), which also cuts
back a swell already past it. The distance is the one `fallOn` passes, so a
dripstone tip's extra 2.5 counts; powder snow doesn't call
`causeFallDamage`, so landing in it adds nothing.

- `hazards::creeper_fall_fuse` and `Mob::track_fall` (`src/entity/mob/hazards.rs`).
- The swell then burns down, as Java's `swellDir = −1` does. Because the
  game used `fuse > 0` to mean "swelling", the creeper now has `swelling`
  (Java's `swellDir > 0`, set by its swell goal and not saved): the swell
  goal starts within 3 blocks and keeps going within 7, the avoid goal is
  held off while swelling, and the sculk `prime_fuse` event fires only when
  a swell starts from nothing, as `Creeper.tick` does. A fallen creeper
  therefore flashes and inflates (the model reads the fuse) but hisses and
  primes only when it starts swelling at a player.

### MOB-02d: pointed dripstone's doubled fall damage

`PointedDripstoneBlock.fallOn` on an upward tip (`tip_direction` up,
`thickness` tip, waterlogged or not) calls `causeFallDamage(fallDistance +
2.5, 2, stalagmite)`. Merged tips, downward tips and sulfur spikes keep
`Block.fallOn`.

- `speleothem::stalagmite_tip` with the two constants (`src/world/speleothem.rs`).
- **Mobs**: `hazards::landing` replaces `landing_modifier` and returns the
  block's extra distance, modifier and damage type, or nothing for powder
  snow. Ordinary mobs queue the damage with its type
  (`Mob::pending_fall_cause`), so a mob killed by it dies of `stalagmite`.
  Wolves and frogs, which keep their own fall trackers, now use the same
  landing and `hazards::fall_damage` (Java's floored formula, with Frog's
  5 less and Jump Boost); before, they used a ceiling and knew only slime.
- **Players** (`src/simulation/survival.rs`, `Env::on_stalagmite` from the
  block 0.2 under the feet, `getOnPosLegacy`): a landing on a tip adds 2.5
  blocks (before the mace impulse clamp, as Java passes it) and deals
  `floor((distance + 1e-6 − 3) · 2)` as `Hurts::stalagmite`, which the game
  and the agents apply as the new `Cause::STALAGMITE` ("was impaled on a
  stalagmite", `minecraft:stalagmite`: armor points don't reduce it,
  Protection and Feather Falling do, and the `fall_damage` rule covers it
  through `#is_fall`). No fall damage is dealt beside it.

### MOB-02d: mobs' fall distance is saved

Java saves `fall_distance` with every entity. `Mob::saved_fall_distance`
reads whichever tracker moves the mob (a mount's, a wolf's, a frog's or the
ordinary one) and `restore_fall_distance` puts it back. Both mob records
write it as `fall_distance`, only when above 0, so older records and mobs at
rest save byte for byte as before: `save_mob`/`load_mob` (most saved mobs,
bees in hives included) and the villager and golem records
(`villagers_to_string`/`load_villagers`). A negative or non-finite value
loads as 0. A mob saved mid-fall now lands with the whole fall.

## Differences from Java and open items

- **Night vision** stays the game's older lightmap formula (the light
  scaled toward full after the clamp, with a flicker), not 26.3's
  `max(AmbientColor, NightVisionColor · factor)` added before sky and block
  light. With both effects the Darkness pulse still comes off before the
  clamp. Not in this track's scope; it changes every night-vision frame,
  so it would need the owner's look.
- **The Darkness Pulsing option** (Accessibility, `darknessEffectScale`)
  isn't in the game, so its scale is Java's default 1 (the options screen
  lists Darkness among the options left out).
- **Full-bright particles and entity parts** (glowing eyes) keep full
  light through the pulse. Java samples the lightmap at (15, 15), whose sum
  is about 1.4 or more, so they lose at most a few percent at the peak.
- **Java's lightmap updates once a tick** (with that frame's partial tick);
  the game computes it every frame. The difference is under a tick of
  easing.
- **Mob arrows and thrown tridents** don't take the shooter's Smite or Bane
  (Java's arrows run `modifyDamage` with the bow, which can't carry either
  in survival); the player's arrows don't either. Bane's slowness needs a
  direct hit in Java too.
- **Mounts** keep their own fall code (an unridden mount's ceiling formula
  and the ridden horse's landing in `equine.rs`, which Track 3 is changing),
  so a mount landing on a dripstone tip takes its ordinary fall. Their fall
  distance is saved like every other mob's.
- **Wolves and frogs** now floor their fall damage, as 26.3's
  `calculateFallDamage` does, and take hay, honey and powder snow into
  account; before, they rounded up. A wolf falling 4.5 blocks takes 1, not
  2.
- **Mobs that aren't saved** (natural hostiles the game doesn't persist)
  lose their fall with themselves, as before.
