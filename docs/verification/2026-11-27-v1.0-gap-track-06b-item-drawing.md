## What changed

### Items Java draws flat (59 items)

Java 26.3 draws every door, lantern and chain, the cauldron, hopper,
brewing stand, comparator, repeater, both campfires, flower pot, bell,
sniffer and turtle eggs, pink petals, wildflowers, leaf litter, bamboo,
lever, redstone torch and tripwire hook as flat `item/generated` sprites;
the game drew their world shapes in 3D. Each now has an original 16 × 16
item sprite ([`content::block_item_sprites`](../../src/content/block_item_sprites.rs)
lists them, [`render::block_item_sprites`](../../src/render/block_item_sprites.rs)
draws them), shown flat in a slot and extruded held, dropped, framed and
shelved, as Java does. The art takes its materials from the game's own
block textures, so an icon matches the block it places:

- **Doors** (13 woods, iron, copper ×8): the door's upper and lower faces
  narrowed into Java's footprint (columns 3–12, rows 2–15), each texel the
  average of the block texels it covers, see-through where most of them are
  (iron and copper door windows), with the item art's light rim.
- **Lanterns** (lantern, soul lantern, copper ×8) and **chains** (iron,
  copper ×8): the placed block's front face (the lantern's cage and cap,
  the chain's three middle columns) in Java's footprint, the lantern with a
  loop handle in its cage's darkest colour. Waxed copper shares the
  unwaxed sprite, as Java's item models do.
- **The rest**: simple shapes lit from the top left in their block's
  colours (the cauldron's iron, the hopper's, the flower pot's clay, the
  bell's gold, the eggs' shells, bamboo's green, cobblestone, smooth stone,
  oak log and planks for bases and logs): a pot with its dark inside and
  legs; a funnel; a blaze rod on a stone base with two bottles; the
  repeater's and comparator's slabs with their torches; logs under a flame
  (blue for the soul campfire); a clay pot; a bell; a large mottled egg and
  two small speckled ones; four blossoms on stems; four fallen leaves; a
  bamboo stalk; the lever's handle on its base; the game's torch with a
  redstone head; an iron ring on a stake.

Only each Java sprite's footprint (its bounding box, measured from the 26.3
client) was used. No texture was copied or traced. Java draws the lever,
redstone torch and tripwire hook with block textures that are sprites
(`block/lever` and so on); the game's blocks of those names are box
materials, so the game uses item sprites of its own for them.

### Items Java draws in 3D (35 items)

[`content::item_models`](../../src/content/item_models.rs) gives these
items Java's 3D models and their base models' display transforms
(`gui`, `ground`, `fixed`, `firstperson_righthand`), read from the client:

- **Beds** (16): the head half with the foot one block behind it, the
  composite the item model makes of `block/<colour>_bed_head` and
  `_bed_foot` translated by (0, 0, 1), drawn with the game's bed halves'
  faces and `block/template_bed`'s transforms (in a slot turned 30° and
  340°, scale 0.5325).
- **Banners** (16): `BannerSpecialRenderer`, the standing banner (pole, bar
  and flag at angle 0) at the item's `transformation` (the world banner's
  2/3 scale and (0.5, 0, 0.5)), its patterns over both faces of the flag,
  with `item/template_banner`'s transforms.
- **The shield**: `ShieldModel` (a 12 × 22 × 1 plate and a 2 × 6 × 6 grip)
  at the item's (1, −1, −1) scale, planks with an iron rim; a patterned
  shield shows its base colour's cloth inside the rim with the pattern
  layers over it (`ShieldSpecialRenderer`), with `item/shield`'s transforms.
- **The small dripleaf** (`block/small_dripleaf_top`: three leaves on two
  crossed stems, with `item/small_dripleaf`'s first-person override) and
  **the spore blossom** (`block/spore_blossom`: a base and four petals
  hanging at 22.5°), with `block/block`'s transforms. Their leaf, stem,
  petal and base are four original block layers (4080–4083, Track 6's
  reservation) used by the item models only; the placed plants keep their
  crossed sprites.

The same quads and transforms draw the item in a slot (`Ui::item_mesh`,
far quads first, lit from the side or, for the banner and shield, from the
front), dropped (its lowest point hovering 1/16 above its feet as
`ItemEntityRenderer` places it), in a frame (Java's 0.5 scale and `fixed`
transform), on a shelf (the ground transform, as other shelved items use)
and in the first-person hand (`arm` pose then Java's display transform).
The banner and shield icons Track 6's slots drew flat (with
`app::patterns` over them) are now these models.

### Drops, glint and translucency

1. **Dropped and framed sprites are extruded** as Java's `item/generated`
   model is: the sprite 1/16 of the model deep (7.5 to 8.5), so 1/32 of a
   block for a dropped item's half-size model, its front and back faces
   over the whole 16 × 16 and a strip along every pixel edge between solid
   and clear (`render::hand::extrude`, shared with the held item). It was a
   single quad.
2. **The enchantment glint** shows on held, dropped, framed and shelved
   stacks (`Stack::has_foil`, Java's `ItemStack.hasFoil`: enchanted stacks
   and enchanted books, lodestone compasses, written books, the enchanted
   golden apple, the experience bottle, the Nether star, the End crystal).
   The model shader adds an original pattern of two soft purple streaks
   drifting on Java's two glint periods (27.5 s and 7.5 s of
   `millis × glintSpeed 0.5 × 8`), turned 10°, over the item's colour; it
   doesn't sample Java's glint texture. Slots keep Track 6's glint.
3. **Translucent items blend**: stained glass and stained panes, tinted
   glass, ice and slime and honey blocks (Java gives a block item its
   block's chunk layer, `ItemBlockRenderTypes`) draw held, dropped and
   framed through a blended pipeline after the opaque models, without
   writing depth. They were cut out at half alpha. Plain glass and panes
   stay cut out, as in Java.

### Bundles, tooltips and spills

1. **Controller seats pick a bundle's stack.** Java 26.3 has no gamepad
   input at all (no gamepad or joystick classes in the client), so there
   is no Java binding to match. The right stick, which controller screens
   don't otherwise use, takes the wheel's place over a bundle that shows
   stacks: down picks the next stack and up the previous, wrapping, one on
   a flick then repeating like the menu moves
   (`ScrollWheelHandler.getNextScrollWheelSelection`, as the desktop wheel
   uses it). A (left click) and X (right click) go with the pick, so X with
   nothing held takes the picked stack out (`Mutable.removeOne`); moving
   the cursor off the bundle, the bundle losing the stack or any click but
   putting a stack in clears it, as on the desktop.
2. **Controller screens show the bundle's contents tooltip** (the grid, the
   fullness bar, the picked cell highlighted and its name above), beside
   the cursor, also while a stack is held.
3. **The CLI** (`bundle <slot> [stack]`): takes the front stack, or the
   contents tooltip's cell 0–11 as the wheel would pick it, out of the
   bundle in inventory slot 0–35 into the inventory (what doesn't fit is
   dropped). LAN guests already pick with the wheel (Track 6, protocol 7);
   controller seats are the host's own players.
4. **Tooltips with a held stack**: `AbstractContainerScreen.extractTooltip`
   shows a hovered stack's tooltip only when nothing is held or when its
   tooltip image asks to (`showTooltipWithItemInHand`, only the bundle's).
   The game showed every tooltip while a stack was held.
5. **Advanced tooltips (F3+H)** exist in 26.3: Java's
   `key.debug.showAdvancedTooltips` (H) with the debug key held toggles
   `advancedItemTooltips` (saved), and tooltips then end with
   "Durability: left / max" for a damaged stack, the item's id
   (`minecraft:stone`, dark grey) and "N component(s)" (dark grey): the
   item's default component count from the data generator's reports
   ([`item_components.json`](../../tests/fixtures/java/26.3/item_components.json),
   [`tools/extract_java_item_component_fixtures.py`](../../tools/extract_java_item_component_fixtures.py))
   plus each component the stack sets that the item lacks by default
   ([`content::advanced_tooltip`](../../src/content/advanced_tooltip.rs)).
   The F3 key now toggles the debug overlay when it is let go, unless a
   debug key was used while it was held (Java's `usedDebugKeyAsModifier`,
   with the modifier and overlay on one key as Java binds them). The
   feedback shows as a popup ("[Debug]: Advanced tooltips: shown"; Java
   puts it in chat). **Java has no bundle fullness line**: the lang file
   keeps `item.minecraft.bundle.fullness` ("%s/%s"), but nothing in the
   26.3 client uses it, so the bundle's advanced tooltip shows only the
   three lines above, as Java's does.
6. **Shulker boxes destroyed as items spill** (`BlockItem.onDestroyed`,
   `ItemUtils.onContainerDestroyed`): burnt or blown up, a block item's
   `container` contents come out where it lay, slot by slot with empty
   slots skipped, as Track 6's bundles do.

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens:

- 59 slot icons are new flat sprites (the doors, lanterns, chains,
  redstone parts, campfires, eggs and plants above) instead of small 3D
  shapes, in every slot, the hotbar, the creative palette and the recipe
  book.
- Beds, banners and the shield are 3D in their slots (a bed seen from the
  side and above, a banner standing on its pole, the shield turned
  slightly), and the small dripleaf and spore blossom are their 3D plants
  instead of crossed sprites. A patterned banner or shield shows its
  patterns on the model instead of a flat flag drawn over a sprite.
- Holding a stack on the cursor hides other items' tooltips; a bundle's
  still shows.
- With advanced tooltips on (F3+H, saved in the options), every item
  tooltip ends with the durability (when damaged), the id and the
  component count. Controls lists "Show Advanced Tooltips" (H) under Debug.
- Controller players see a hovered bundle's contents tooltip and pick its
  stacks with the right stick.

## Remaining differences

- Third-person and mob-held items: the beds, banners, shield and two
  plants still draw as their flat sprites there, and held items in third
  person show no glint or translucency (the entity pipeline draws them).
- Enchanted 3D block items show no glint in a slot (Track 6's slot glint
  covers flat icons only); Java glints both.
- Shelves draw a 3D item with its ground transform, not Java's `on_shelf`
  one.
- The GUI view of a 3D item is mirrored left to right from Java's
  transform, as the game's block icons are (their north face on the left);
  the beds, banners and shield follow that convention.
- The glint is an original procedural pattern on Java's timing, not
  Java's texture; dropped translucent items aren't sorted back to front
  among themselves.
- The F3 debug key acts as Java's separate debug modifier key too (Java
  binds both to F3 by default and lets them be split); only F3+H among
  Java's debug combinations exists. The F3+H feedback is a popup, not a
  chat line.
- Controller seats pick bundle stacks in inventory, container, ender
  chest, vehicle and palette slots; a bundle in a workstation's input slot
  isn't pickable there.
