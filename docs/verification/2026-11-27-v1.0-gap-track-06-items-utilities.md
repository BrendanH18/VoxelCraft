## What changed

### Bundles (BLD-05b)

1. **Sounds.** `Sound::Bundle(content::bundle::Cue)` with Java's four events
   (`item.bundle.insert`, `insert_fail`, `remove_one`, `drop_contents`,
   subtitles "Item packed", "Bundle full", "Item unpacked", "Bundle
   empties"), all `PLAYERS`, at `BundleItem`'s volumes and pitches (1 and 1
   for a failed insert, 0.8 and 0.8–1.2 for the rest). The audio is an
   original procedural leather rustle with a thump, a pop or a double pat
   ([`src/audio/bundle.rs`](../../src/audio/bundle.rs)). Every container
   click goes through `screen::Screen::click_slot_selected`, which runs the
   click inside `bundle::clicking` and plays what the bundle override did,
   on the desktop, controller seats and LAN guests (the host plays it at the
   guest).
2. **Throwing the contents out** (`BundleItem.use`, `onUseTick`,
   `dropContent`; [`src/session/bundles.rs`](../../src/session/bundles.rs)).
   Use with a bundle in the main hand, or in the off hand behind a hand
   whose item takes no use first, starts a 200-tick use (`getUseDuration`)
   after any block that takes the use. Each use tick calls Java's rule with
   the ticks remaining before counting down: the first tick throws the front
   stack, then every tick with fewer than 190 remaining and an even count
   (188, 186, …, 2), so at most 95 stacks per use. A throw is the drop
   toss (`Player.drop(stack, true)`), swings the main hand, plays the
   remove-one sound at the player and the drop-contents sound at the
   player's block. Letting go, switching slot or bundle colour
   (`isSameItem`), or the end of the use stops it; start and stop post
   `item_interact_start` and `_finish`. Using a bundle slows movement like
   any held use. Desktop, controller seats and LAN peers share
   `hands::Actor`; the CLI's `eat` (hold use) starts it and its ticks
   throw, as an agent event the host sounds. A LAN guest predicts the
   slow-down for up to 200 ticks.
3. **A bundle destroyed as an item spills its contents**
   (`BundleItem.onDestroyed`, `ItemUtils.onContainerDestroyed`): burnt in
   fire or lava, or blown up, its stacks come out where it lay with
   `ItemEntity`'s own start (0.2 up and up to 0.1 sideways a tick) and no
   pickup delay. Despawning spills nothing, as in Java.
4. **Contents tooltip** (`ClientBundleTooltip`;
   [`src/app/bundles.rs`](../../src/app/bundles.rs)): under the name, a
   grid of up to twelve 24-pixel cells four across, filled from the bottom
   right with the front stack last (`getNumberOfItemsToShow`: eleven and a
   "+n" cell when more don't fit, trimmed to whole rows), over a 96 × 13
   fullness bar (blue, red once full, filled `mulAndTruncate(weight, 94)`)
   reading "Empty" or "Full" at the ends. An empty bundle shows "Can hold a
   mixed stack of items" in grey over its empty bar. The cell and bar art is
   original (the game's slot bevels), not Java's sprites.
5. **Selection** (`BundleMouseActions`, `ScrollWheelHandler`): the wheel
   over a hovered bundle that shows stacks takes the wheel first (in the
   creative screen too) and moves the selection — down the next stack, up
   the previous, wrapping, from none down to the front and up to the last
   shown — in whole notches with its own accumulator. The selected cell is
   highlighted, its name shows in a box 15 above the tooltip, and the slot
   draws the bundle open with the stack held up in it (original open-back
   and open-front icons, `bundle_open_back`/`_front` in Java). A right click
   with an empty cursor takes the selected stack out instead of the front
   one (`Mutable.removeOne`). Moving off the bundle, closing the screen,
   taking a stack out, a shift-click or any click but putting a stack in
   clears it, as Java's `toggleSelectedItem(-1)` and `onStopHovering` do;
   putting one in keeps the index, as Java does. LAN guests send the
   selection with the click (`Packet::Click::selected`, protocol 7).

### Item models (BLD-05c)

1. **One set of item boxes** (`shape::item_boxes`): the icon, the held
   model and the dropped, framed and shelved models all draw a block item
   with the same boxes. Until now the dropped model (and frames and
   shelves) drew slabs, carpets, beds' shapes, farmland and paths — every
   block whose world shape is a whole cube cut lower — as **full cubes**,
   and the third-person, mob, allay and copper golem held models drew
   every block as a full cube with one texture on all faces. They now draw
   the item's boxes with each face's texture (`player_model::held_block`).
2. **Dropped items sit as Java's do** (`ItemEntityRenderer`): the model's
   lowest point hovers `ITEM_MIN_HOVER_HEIGHT` (1/16) above the item's feet
   and bobs `sin(ageInTicks / 10 + offset) * 0.1 + 0.1` above that. Before,
   the bob was 0.07 around 0.1 with no hover, so items dipped to 0.03 and
   models with a raised lowest box floated by that box.
3. **Per-box layers**: candle wicks, the straw bed's pillow and the
   beacon's obsidian plinth and glowing core now show on every item model
   (`shape::item_box_layer`, the world mesh's rule). The beacon item drew
   three glass boxes, an empty shell.
4. **Fronts to the north** (`Block::item_facing`): Java's item models are
   the block models with the front to the north, which a slot shows on the
   left. Whole-cube blocks with a front (furnaces, smokers, blast furnaces,
   pumpkins, jack o'lanterns, looms, hives, dispensers, droppers, observers)
   now show it; their items' states faced another way, so slots showed a
   plain side. Pistons and sticky pistons point up (`piston_inventory`).
   Heads show their face on the side a slot shows (it was on the far side).
5. **Java's display scales** (`shape::item_scale`, from each model's
   display transforms over `block/block`'s): the conduit (GUI 1, ground 0.5,
   first person 0.8) and the heavy core (GUI 1, ground 0.5) draw at 1.6 to
   2 times a block item's size, fence gates and the dried ghast at 1.28 in
   a slot, copper golem statues at 0.88 in a slot, 1.4 dropped and 1.25 held,
   and shelves at 1.25 held. The conduit and heavy core were tiny.
6. **Buttons** use Java's `button_inventory` box (6 × 4 × 4) instead of the
   2-high floor button.
7. **Java's flat items with block textures**: glass panes (and the 16
   stained panes), iron and copper bars (8), the lily pad, vine and
   frogspawn are now flat sprites of their block's texture, as Java's
   `item/generated` models with a `block/...` layer; they drew their world
   shape in 3D.
8. **Sprites**: spear shafts stopped six pixels short of the head (every
   spear's head floated), shovel shafts one pixel short; both now reach.
9. **Comparison with Java for every item**
   ([`tests/java_item_models.rs`](../../tests/java_item_models.rs)): a new
   fixture, `item_models.json`, records whether Java draws each of its 1,658
   items as a flat sprite or in 3D, and the test checks each of the 1,600+
   ids the game resolves, with an exact list of what still differs (below).

## Remaining differences

- **Items Java draws flat but the game draws in 3D** (it has no sprites
  for them; each needs original item art): every door (13 woods, copper
  ×8, iron), lantern (2, copper ×8), chain (iron, copper ×8), the
  cauldron, hopper, brewing stand, comparator, repeater, campfire, soul
  campfire, flower pot, bell, sniffer egg, turtle egg, pink petals,
  wildflowers, leaf litter and bamboo; and the lever, redstone torch and
  tripwire hook, whose block textures are their boxes' materials rather
  than Java's sprites. **Owner decision**: draw ~50 sprites for them, or
  keep their world shapes as items.
- **Items Java draws in 3D but the game draws flat**: the 16 beds, 16
  banners, the shield, the small dripleaf and the spore blossom. **Owner
  decision** with the above.
- Flat dropped items are a single quad, not Java's extruded 1/32-thick
  sprite (the held item is extruded), and dropped and held items have no
  enchantment glint (written books, enchanted items).
- Stained glass dropped or held draws opaque; in a slot it is translucent.
- The bundle tooltip's slot, highlight and bar art is original, not Java's
  sprites; text widths follow the game's font. Advanced tooltips (F3+H)
  don't exist in the game, so the bundle shows no "n/64" fullness line.
- Controller seats have no input to pick a bundle's stack (the front comes
  out, Java's no-selection behaviour); the controller screens show no
  contents tooltip. LAN guests and the CLI can't select either except by
  the desktop wheel on a guest.
- The game shows item tooltips while a stack is held (Java shows only the
  bundle's then); unchanged.
- Shulker boxes destroyed as items still don't spill (their
  `onDestroyed`), outside this track.
