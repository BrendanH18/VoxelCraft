from Track 6f's head (`939ab9d`, not yet merged into the integration

**Families now Java's:** repeaters (every delay, facing and power, and
Java's locked bar), comparators (every facing, mode and power), cocoa
(every age and facing) and cauldrons' bodies, all drawn whole from the
jar's element layout. **Checked and already Java's:** enchanting tables,
cakes and stairs' turned faces (below). **Most of the scope remains** (listed under
[remaining differences](#remaining-differences)): the work started at
05:37 with a 07:00 push deadline, so the two pieces of mesh support 6f
lacked (texel offsets on ambient-occluded faces, and planes turned 22.5°
and 45°) were not built, and every family that needs them stays as it was.
Repeaters, comparators, cocoa and cauldrons were chosen first because their models are
`ambientocclusion: false`, so 6f's plane code 29 draws them without new
mesh support.

## What changed

### 1. Repeaters (`repeater_{1..4}tick[_on][_locked]`)

The game drew a repeater as its plate and two 6-high torch boxes, every
face in one placeholder texture (grey with a red stripe down columns 7–8).
Java's model is the 16 × 2 × 16 plate (`#slab` smooth stone on the sides
and underside, the sides from the texture's bottom two rows), the `#top`
texture over the whole plate, and two 2 × 5 × 2 torches from 2 to 7 with
the redstone torch's columns 7–8, rows 6–11 (`#unlit` or `#lit`); a locked
repeater replaces the moving torch with a 12 × 2 × 2 bedrock bar. Now:

- every face of the jar's model for all 64 blockstates (four facings, four
  delays, locked, powered), from `tools/extract_java_model_uvs.py`'s new
  `REPEATERS` table, drawn in plane code 29 (flat lit, as Java's
  `ambientocclusion: false`), the plate's sides and underside culled by
  their cullfaces;
- **locked** as Java keeps the `locked` state (`RepeaterBlock.isLocked`,
  `getAlternateSignal` with diodes only): the mesher shows the bar when a
  powered repeater or comparator beside it points into it. The game's
  simulation already locked repeaters this way (`World::repeater_locked`);
  only the look was missing;
- the game's facing is the output side and Java's the input, so the row is
  Java's facing, the opposite of the game's.

### 2. Comparators (`comparator[_on][_subtract]`)

Java's comparator is the same plate, two back torches at 4–6 and 10–12
(2 × 5 × 2) and a 2 × 3 × 2 front torch, lit by mode and power. The game
drew 6-high boxes in the placeholder texture. Now every face of the jar's
model for all 16 blockstates (`COMPARATORS`), the torches lit as Java's
models light them (the front torch lit in subtract mode, the back ones
when powered).

### 3. Diode tops (original art, layers 4092–4095)

**Four new layers**, the integrator's spare block layers for this wave:
`content::item_models::REPEATER_TOP`, `REPEATER_TOP_ON`,
`COMPARATOR_TOP` and `COMPARATOR_TOP_ON`, Java's `repeater`,
`repeater_on`, `comparator` and `comparator_on` drawn as the game's own
art in Java's layout (the up face maps the whole texture, the output end
at row 0): a smooth stone plate with a darker rim and the redstone dust's
grooves, dark or bright red when powered: the repeater's line down columns
7–8 with the output's arrow at rows 1–3; the comparator's front socket
(columns 7–8) down to a bar on rows 7–8 joining the back torches' columns
4–5 and 10–11. The torches use the redstone torch's own layers (1116,
1117) and the plate smooth stone; layers 1118 and 1119 stay registered as
the blocks' particle and icon texture.

### 4. Cocoa (`cocoa_stage0`–`2`)

The game's cocoa boxes were already Java's pods (and its outline and
collision Java's `CocoaBlock` shapes), but drew every face from a ridged
fill and had no stem. Java maps an atlas: the pod's top and underside from
the top-left square (4, 6 or 8 texels), its sides from the right of row 4
(4 × 5 at 11, 6 × 7 at 9, 8 × 9 at 8) and a stem plane (4 × 4 at 8 across,
from the pod's top to the log) from the top-right corner. Now every face of
the jar's model for all 12 states (`COCOA`), and the cocoa texture (all
three ages) is redrawn in that layout (original art): the pod's ends with
a darker middle, ridged sides with darker edges, a twig in the stem's
corner and the rest clear. Cocoa moves to the cutout pass for the stem's
clear texels.

### 5. Cauldrons (`cauldron` and the filled templates' body)

Java's cauldron is `ambientocclusion: false`: four walls from 3 to 16, the
floor at 3–4, and four L-shaped legs of two elements each (4 × 3 × 2 and
2 × 3 × 2), the walls' and floor's undersides in `#inside`. The game drew
nine boxes with ambient occlusion, each leg a 4 × 4 square. Now the body is
every face of the jar's model (`CAULDRONS`, the same 13 elements in every
filled template), lit flat, culled by its cullfaces; the contents (water,
lava, powder snow) are still the game's box, which draws only its top, at
Java's heights. Textures: `#side`, `#top` and `#bottom` the game's
cauldron layers; `#inside` (Java's `cauldron_inner`, which the game has no
layer for) the top layer's middle, which is what the game showed on the
floor before (a remaining difference).

### 6. Outline and collision

Java's `DiodeBlock.SHAPE` is the plate alone (0–2), for both the outline
and collision; the game's included the torches. `shape::outline_with` and
`collision_with` now return the plate for repeaters and comparators.

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens: **none.**
Repeaters, comparators and cocoa beans are flat item sprites in slots, as
in Java (`item/generated`), and their sprites don't come from the changed
block layers.

Outside the inventory: placed repeaters and comparators show Java's
thinner torches, smooth stone sides and the redrawn tops, a locked
repeater its bedrock bar; their outline is the plate only, and entities
collide with the plate only (an item or mob no longer catches on the
torches). Cocoa pods show their stem and Java's texture layout. Cauldrons
show Java's L-shaped legs and are lit flat, as Java draws them.

## Families checked against Java's shapes

From the jar's models and blockstates (on Boat), against the game's boxes:

| Family | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Enchanting tables | Already Java's: one 16 × 12 × 16 element whose explicit UVs (`[0, 4, 16, 16]` on the sides, whole top and bottom) are the default ones; the game's box is the same |
| Cakes (every bite) | Already Java's: `[1 + 2 × bites, 0, 1]`–`[15, 8, 15]` with default UVs, as the game's box; the bitten face's `#inside` is the game's cake layers' business (not changed) |
| Stairs (all 3,880 variants of every `*_stairs`) | Already Java's: every turned variant has `uvlock: true`, which keeps the texture world-aligned, which is the game's planar default |
| Composters | Geometry the same in look: Java's floor (0–2) and four full-height walls, contents a 12 × 12 top at 3, 5, … 15 by level; the game's floor box grows to the same height under its walls. Ambient-occluded in both. Not converted |
| Cauldrons | Converted (above) |
| Pistons and observers | **Different, not fixed:** Java turns them by facing without `uvlock` and turns the side faces (`rotation` 90, 180, 270 in `template_piston`; the observer's top `[0, 16, 16, 0]`), so the piston side's head band and the observer's arrows point with the facing. The game draws every side upright (planar) on direction-free art (layer 1130: grey edges and a wooden middle; the observer's sides plain stone). Matching needs Java's side art and a turned full-cube face in the greedy mesh, which wasn't built |

## Remaining differences

| Family | Why it stays |
| --- | --- |
| Anvils, grindstones, lecterns, stonecutters, bells, lightning rods, beds (and sniffer eggs, the heavy core, scaffolding) | They need texel offsets on ambient-occluded faces inside the cell (6f: the quad's AO and light words have no room beside per-corner AO); that mesh support wasn't built in the time. Their textures are Java atlas layouts the game's art would also need redrawing in |
| Chains, campfires (the fire), candles, brewing stands (the bottles), lantern handles, stems (`stem_growth*`, turned 45°), lecterns' tilted top | Planes turned 22.5° or 45°; detail quads are axis-aligned and the turned-plane support wasn't built |
| Lit repeaters' and comparators' glow planes | Java's lit models add 1-texel glow faces round each lit torch on the half-sixteenth grid (6.5–9.5); detail quads place sixteenths, so they're left out |
| Crops | Java's `crop` planes run from −1 to 15 (below the cell); the game's crop planes weren't compared |
| Flower pots and potted plants | The game's pot uses three textures (side, top, bottom) where Java maps one atlas (`flower_pot`); its boxes are already Java's elements, but taking Java's UVs needs the pot art redrawn as one atlas, not done in the time |
| Pistons' and observers' turned sides | See [families checked](#families-checked-against-javas-shapes): the game's art has no direction and full-cube faces can't turn |
| Cauldrons' `#inside` | No `cauldron_inner` layer in the game (no spare layer left after the diode tops); drawn with the top layer's middle |

Also as 6f: Java's random `y` turns of stone, grass and others (F3's) and
the `cube_mirrored` variants.
