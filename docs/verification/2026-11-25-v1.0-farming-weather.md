## What changed

### FOO-02: farmland, crops and pale oaks

- **Farmland moisture 0–7** (`src/world/farmland.rs`), as Java's
  `FarmlandBlock`:
  - a random tick sets moisture to 7 when a fluid in `#water` (water,
    flowing water, waterlogged blocks) lies in `pos.offset(-4, 0, -4)` to
    `pos.offset(4, 1, 4)` (`isNearWater`), or when it rains on the block
    above (`isRainingAt(pos.above())`);
  - otherwise moisture falls by one per random tick (`setBlock(..., 2)`,
    no neighbour updates), and at 0 the farmland turns to dirt unless
    `#maintains_farmland` is above;
  - `#maintains_farmland` is the 26.3 tag: wheat, carrots, potatoes,
    beetroots, melon and pumpkin stems (attached too), the torchflower crop
    and torchflower, the pitcher crop, `moving_piston` and every fence gate;
  - `canSurvive`: a solid block above that doesn't maintain it turns it to
    dirt (Java's `updateShape` schedules a one-tick check; see the
    differences);
  - `turnToBaseBlock`: dirt, a `block_change` game event, and
    `Block.pushEntitiesUp` for whatever stood on the 15/16 top (the new
    slice 15/16..1 lifts players, agents, mobs, items, orbs, vehicles,
    primed TNT and armor stands onto the full block);
  - trampling (`fallOn`): `nextFloat() < fallDistance - 0.5`, from the
    world's random, and only for bodies over 0.512 cubic blocks
    (`width² × height`); players always may (single-player has no spawn
    protection). The host and CLI agents now share it
    (`World::player_landed`); before, only the host trampled, with a
    clock-seeded hash.
- **Old saves:** moisture 0 is the original state 57 (`farmland`) and 7 is
  the original 58 (`wet farmland`), so a dry block loads as moisture 0 and
  a wet one as 7 with no migration step. Moistures 1–6 are new states
  13968–13973; they look like dry farmland (Java draws `farmland_moist`
  only at 7) and answer as it for name, drops, hardness, sounds, shape,
  light occlusion and pick-block.
- **Crop growth speed** (`CropBlock.getGrowthSpeed`): farmland counts 3
  at any moisture above 0 and 1 at 0. Before, only the wet state counted.
- **Low-light crop removal** (`CropBlock.canSurvive`, `hasSufficientLight`:
  `getRawBrightness(pos, 0) >= 8`): a crop (wheat, carrots, potatoes,
  beetroots, torchflower crop) or the pitcher crop's lower half beside a
  changed block breaks, with its loot, when its raw light is below 8
  (`VegetationBlock.updateShape` returns air; `updateOrDestroy` drops).
  Stems have no light rule. Seeds can't be planted below light 8. The
  check reads the light as it stands when the update goes out; 26.3's
  `canSurvive` has no sky-access exception (the brief's "or sky access" is
  older Java).
- **Sapling-grown pale oaks** (`TreeGrower.PALE_OAK`, whose only feature is
  `pale_oak_bonemeal`): four pale oak saplings now grow
  `pale_garden::pale_oak_bonemeal`, the generation 3 tree's
  `DarkOakTrunkPlacer(6, 2, 1)`, `DarkOakFoliagePlacer(0, 0)` and
  `ThreeLayersFeatureSize(1, 1, 0, 1, 2)` without decorators, so no
  hanging moss. Before, they grew the older dark-oak-style shape with moss.

### FOO-01a: foods

- `tools/extract_java_food_fixtures.py` reads every `Items` registration
  that calls `Item$Properties.food` from the jar's bytecode (40 foods) with
  its `Foods` and `Consumables` entries, into
  `tests/fixtures/java/26.3/foods.json` (see the fixture README).
- Every nutrition, saturation modifier, `alwaysEdible`, consume time,
  eat/drink animation, status effect (effect, amplifier, ticks, chance),
  removed effect, teleport, stack size and remainder already matched.
- **Mismatch fixed:** saturation was stored as decimal literals (`3.6`),
  while Java computes `nutrition * modifier * 2.0F` in floats. For carrots
  (3 × 0.6), the stews and soups (6 × 0.6) and others the float differs in
  the last bit (carrot 3.6000001 against 3.5999999). Foods now store the
  modifier and compute saturation as Java does
  (`item::saturation_by_modifier`).

### ENV-03: lightning, snow and the weather random

- **Lightning on entities** (`Entities::thunder_hit_objects`, from
  `LightningBolt.tick` and each class's `thunderHit`/`hurtServer`): every
  tick the bolt is visible, entities whose box meets
  `(x ± 3, y − 3 .. y + 9, z ± 3)` are hit:
  - dropped items and experience orbs take 5 damage from their 5 health
    and are gone. Nothing resists it: `damage_resistant` (netherite, the
    Nether star) names `#is_fire` or `#is_explosion`, and
    `lightning_bolt` is in neither (`#is_lightning` only);
  - boats, chest boats and minecarts take `VehicleEntity.hurtServer`'s
    5 × 10 = 50 damage, over 40, so they break into their item and chest
    vehicles spill their contents; a TNT minecart moving at 0.1 a tick or
    more is primed instead, one standing still drops (`MinecartTNT.destroy`,
    lightning isn't fire or a blast);
  - cushions break into their item (`Cushion.thunderHit`);
  - End crystals explode (`EndCrystal.hurtServer`);
  - item frames, paintings, leash knots and armor stands are untouched:
    `BlockAttachedEntity.thunderHit` and `ArmorStand.thunderHit` are empty.
  As in Java, entities are listed before any hit, so what the hits drop
  (a turtle's bowl, a boat's item) is hit on the bolt's next tick.
- **Snow pushes items and vehicles:** `ServerLevel.tickPrecipitation`
  calls `Block.pushEntitiesUp` for each added layer, and that moves every
  entity the new slice meets. The queue that lifted players, agents and
  mobs (`World::take_block_pushes`, now carrying the slice's bottom and
  top and shared with farmland) also lifts dropped items, orbs, boats,
  minecarts, cushions, primed TNT and armor stands
  (`Entities::apply_block_pushes`), in the desktop game, the headless LAN
  harness and the tick benchmark.
- **Weather random** (`ServerLevel.advanceWeatherCycle`,
  `MinecraftServer.setWeatherParameters`, `WeatherCommand`,
  `ServerLevel.resetWeatherCycle`, `UniformInt.sample`,
  `Mth.randomBetweenInclusive`): spells are `nextInt(max − min + 1) + min`
  draws from a `java.util.Random` LCG (`LegacyRandomSource`), from the
  `UniformInt`s `RAIN_DELAY` 12000–180000, `RAIN_DURATION` 12000–24000,
  `THUNDER_DELAY` 12000–180000 and `THUNDER_DURATION` 3600–15600 ticks.
  The timers now run Java's per-tick logic: a new world starts with every
  timer at 0 and draws thunder then rain on its first tick; a timer counts
  down, flips its weather at 0 and draws the next spell on the following
  tick. Java's clear-weather hold is new: `/weather clear` holds clear
  weather for a `RAIN_DELAY` sample with both timers at 0, and while it
  holds each tick sets the thunder and rain timers to 1 (both off), so the
  tick after it ends turns rain and thunder on, as Java's code does.
  `/weather rain` and `/weather thunder` draw one `RAIN_DURATION` or
  `THUNDER_DURATION` spell for both timers. Waking from sleep while it
  rains (`isRaining`, the faded level) now resets the cycle
  (`resetWeatherCycle`: both off, timers 0, so the rain fades out over 5
  seconds) instead of drawing a fresh clear spell.
- **Saved weather:** the level file's `weather` entry gains a fifth field,
  the clear hold in seconds, and keeps its timers to the tick (two
  decimals). Older entries load with no hold, and entries with no thunder
  timer still get a fresh spell.

## Differences from Java and remaining gaps

- **Solid block on farmland:** Java's `updateShape` schedules a tick one
  game tick later; VoxelCraft turns the farmland to dirt during the same
  update. Nothing between the two can observe the difference except a
  block that is placed and removed within one tick.
- **Mob trampling:** Java lets any living entity over 0.512 cubic blocks
  trample under `mob_griefing`. VoxelCraft mobs keep no general fall
  distance, so only players trample; the shared `World::trample_farmland`
  takes the permission and body size for when they do.
- **Light at the moment of the update:** Java's light engine runs after
  the block change, so the shape update a roof sends a crop below it
  still sees the old light, and the crop breaks only on a later update
  beside it. VoxelCraft knows the new skylight at once, so the roof's own
  update breaks a crop it darkens below 8. Block light from a removed
  torch does lag, as in Java.
- **Unknown skylight:** where a column's skylight isn't worked out yet (a
  partly loaded border), a crop is left standing rather than broken over
  unknown light. Java always has the light.
- **Weather random seed:** Java seeds `Level.random` from the clock and
  shares it with every other draw the level makes, so its spells can't be
  repeated. VoxelCraft draws spells in Java's order and way from its own
  `java.util.Random`, seeded from the world seed (`seed ^ 0x3EA74E12`), so
  a world's weather repeats. The rain and thunder fade still steps by dt
  (5 s for a full change, Java's 0.01 a tick), and VoxelCraft's `/weather`
  still snaps the sky at once; neither command takes a duration.
- **Destroyed containers:** a shulker box or bundle item that lightning
  destroys doesn't spill its contents (`ItemContainerContents.onDestroyed`);
  explosions don't either today.
- **Lightning on the rest:** primed TNT, falling blocks and projectiles
  ignore bolts, as their Java hurt ignores lightning; display and other
  entities VoxelCraft lacks aren't covered.
- **Snow push on item frames and paintings:** they hang on faces and never
  meet a floor slice, so they aren't moved.
