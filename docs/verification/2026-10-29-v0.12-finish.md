# Finishing v0.12: the remaining gaps, frame time and release prep (2026-10-29)

[Documentation index](../README.md) · [Roadmap v0.12](../roadmap.md#v012-scope-building-materials)

The v0.12 track records left gaps that belong to v0.12's own blocks and
need nothing from a later release. This record covers them, the owed
`materials` frame-time runs and profile, a memory leak those runs found, and
the 0.12.0 release prep. Implemented, unverified: no owner play yet.

- Requirements: BLD-01a and BLD-05b (sign editing on every input path,
  lodestone compasses), BLD-01b (slime blocks for mobs and items), BLD-01d
  and BLD-05b (arrows hitting cushions and decorations), BLD-05b
  (cauldrons putting out fires), PERF (frame time, memory).
- Reference: Java 26.3, official server artifact, SHA-1
  `33680f5f2ac32864d6d7cf5e56a705fdb3e05f4c`. Base commit: `098857f`.
- Automated checks on the finished tree: `cargo fmt`, `cargo clippy
  --release --all-targets -- -D warnings` and `cargo test --release` pass
  (1,788 tests, 13 ignored).

## What changed

**Slime blocks and beds for mobs and dropped items.** Only the player used
them before. `Entity.restituteMovementAfterCollisions` now throws a mob or
an item that lands faster than one tick of gravity back up at the block's
restitution (slime 1.0, beds 0.75), and an item at 0.8 of it
(`getBlockBounciness`). `SlimeBlock.stepOn` slows a nearly level item to
`0.4 + |dy| * 0.2` a tick. Mobs move by target speed rather than Java's
acceleration and friction, so a walker on slime keeps 0.270 of its speed:
Java's steady-state step `a / (1 - f)` with slime's 0.8 friction and the
extra 0.4 against ordinary ground, `(0.546 / 0.728)³ * (1 - 0.546) /
(1 - 0.4 * 0.728)`. A mount landing on slime takes no fall damage
(`SlimeBlock.fallOn`). Items now take their block's friction, so they slide
on ice (`ItemEntity.tick`).

**Arrows hitting things that aren't mobs.** An arrow now hits the nearest of
any mob, dragon part, minecart, boat, cushion, item frame, painting or armor
stand along its path. Following `AbstractArrow.onHitEntity`, the arrow is
gone after a hit unless it pierces (through `pierce level + 1` entities of
any kind, mobs included), and a target that refuses the damage turns it back
at a fifth of its speed.

- Item frames drop their item and stay (`ItemFrame.hurtServer` checks this
  before anything else, so a mob's arrow does it even without
  mobGriefing). An empty frame, a painting or a cushion breaks and drops
  itself (`BlockAttachedEntity`). An armor stand breaks at once
  (`ALWAYS_KILLS_ARMOR_STANDS`), dropping itself and its armor.
- Boats and minecarts add ten times the damage and break past 40
  (`VehicleEntity`). A burning arrow sets off a TNT minecart, with a blast
  that grows with the arrow's speed (`MinecartTNT.hurtServer`), and a moving
  TNT cart is primed instead of dropping.
- A creative player's arrow breaks things without drops. Without
  mobGriefing, frames (once empty), paintings, armor stands and cushions
  refuse a mob's arrow.

**Lodestone compasses.** Using a compass on a lodestone makes a lodestone
compass (`CompassItem.useOn`): a lone compass outside creative changes in
place, and otherwise one copy splits off into the inventory (or drops),
keeping its name and enchantments. Item stacks gained an optional tracker,
saved as an eighth stack field (`x_y_z_dimension`, or `-` once lost; older
saves load unchanged). Each tick, `LodestoneTracker.tick` checks every
compass a player carries, and one whose lodestone in this dimension is gone
loses its target. The needle points to the lodestone in its dimension and
spins elsewhere or once lost, in inventories, item frames, dropped items and
the first-person hand. It is named "Lodestone Compass" and has the
enchantment glint. The lock sound is the nearest shared one.

**Sign editing on every input path.** The host's keyboard editor already
existed.

- CLI agents: `sign x y z front|back ["line", ...]` writes one side within
  reach through `PlayerSession::edit_sign`, the rule the editor uses. Using
  a sign through `place` returns an error naming the command, as screens do.
- LAN clients: snapshots now carry the signs, shelves and chiseled
  bookshelves within 160 blocks. Clients drew blank signs and empty shelves
  before. A sign the client places or uses opens its editor, and Done sends
  the `sign` command, which the host accepts only for the side it opened,
  once (Java's `playerWhoMayEdit`).
- Controller players: an on-screen keyboard in the player's view (see the
  [agent guide](../agents.md#gamepad-players)). Java's screen has no
  controller layout, so this one is VoxelCraft's own. Lines stop at the
  sign's width as in the keyboard editor.

**Cauldrons putting out fires.** A burning player in a water cauldron is put
out and the cauldron loses a level. Powder snow also puts the fire out, and
melts into water a level lower (`LayeredCauldronBlock`). This applies to the
host and to CLI, LAN and controller players who may build.

**Memory while the window is hidden.** The occluded benchmark runs below
grew to 7–8.5 GB. Chunk meshes keep uploading through `Queue::write_buffer`
while no frame is drawn, and wgpu frees those staged copies only after a
submit, which a skipped frame never made. A skipped frame now submits an
empty batch and polls the device. With the window hidden on purpose (a
local, uncommitted change opening it invisible), the same 30-second
`materials` run peaked at **4,389 MiB before and 737 MiB after**, with every
frame skipped both times. Minimising the game while terrain streams no
longer grows it.

**Benchmark window.** A benchmark window now asks for focus and stays above
other windows. One started from a terminal or an agent opened behind the
active app and was counted occluded. A quick run after the change was
valid; the full runs below still fail once the display sleeps or the
screen locks.

## Frame time

`python3 tools/benchmark_scenes.py --scenes materials,building` on the
Apple M5, AC power, 1920 x 1080, enhanced graphics, `Immediate` present mode,
runtime source `9871993b…`:

| Run | Average FPS | 1% low FPS | p99 ms | Valid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| materials 1 | 538.9 | 137.8 | 4.54 | Yes; misses the gate |
| materials 2–3, building 1 | 44–50 (1% low) | — | 18.7–21.6 | No: window occluded |

The machine was in use for runs 2–3, and its display then slept and locked
(11:28), so no second valid windowed run was possible today.

Offscreen comparison runs used a local, uncommitted switch onto the
renderer's offscreen path (draw everything, present nothing). Each ran 30
seconds after a 10-second warm-up, same scene settings. They aren't gate
evidence:

| Scene | Average FPS | 1% low FPS | p50 ms | p99 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| building | 631 | 229 | 1.52 | 3.90 |
| materials | 584 | 200 | 1.59 | 4.55 |

Even the older `building` scene's 1% low is now under 240 (the 2026-10-16
windowed baseline was 261–273).

A 15-second `sample` of the offscreen `materials` run explains it. The main
thread is CPU-bound, and about 58% of its samples are in two places.

- `Entities::mesh` rebuilds every entity's vertices each frame (2,390
  samples). Most of that is mob models (`entity::model::build`, 1,611); the
  item frame wall is about 500.
- `Renderer::set_entities` copies the result into the GPU buffer (1,823
  samples, almost all `memmove`).

Chunk meshing on the workers, signs, banners and texture binding are small
by comparison. Both scenes carry about 273 mobs from natural herds. So the
cost is mostly mob models rebuilt and re-uploaded every frame, shared by
every scene, rather than v0.12's blocks or the second texture page. Caching
entity vertices, or building them on a worker, is the lead for the gate.
The gate belongs to v0.20, as for v0.8, v0.9 and v0.11; the three-run
windowed set moves there with this profile.

## Differences from Java and not done

- Mob movement keeps VoxelCraft's target-speed model, so a mob's slowdown on
  slime is the steady-state ratio above, not Java's tick-by-tick motion.
  Honey and soul sand don't slow mobs or items yet.
- Two arrows hitting the same decoration in one tick count once.
- A lodestone in an unloaded chunk is kept. Java's check reads the saved
  points of interest; nothing here removes a lodestone without loading its
  chunk.
- The controller keyboard has no accents or other characters outside its
  keys. The keyboard editor accepts any character Java allows.
- Splash water bottles have no water behaviour (putting out fire, candles
  and campfires, hurting endermen and blazes). Candles put out by
  explosions matter only for explosions that don't destroy blocks (wind
  charges, v0.16).
- Sounds: the lock sound and the new blocks' sounds are the nearest shared
  ones; dedicated sounds come with the v0.20 sound review.
- Not verified here: a sign edited in a live two-process LAN game, the
  controller keyboard with a physical controller, and the three valid
  windowed frame-time runs. The screen was locked from 11:28. Owner play
  remains the acceptance evidence for all of it.
