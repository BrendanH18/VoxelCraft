## Fixture

[`dispensing.json`](../../tests/fixtures/java/26.3/dispensing.json) is made
by [`tools/extract_java_dispense_fixtures.py`](../../tools/extract_java_dispense_fixtures.py)
([README](../../tests/fixtures/java/26.3/README.md#dispensing-and-hopper-sides-v017-track-c2)),
which compiles the original JDK oracle
[`tools/JavaDispenseVectors.java`](../../tools/JavaDispenseVectors.java)
against the jar and runs it after `Bootstrap.bootStrap()`, binding the
default item components (as the jar's own component report does) and the
jar's item and block tags. It reports, from the jar's live objects rather
than from reading code:

- every item whose `getDispenseMethod` isn't the plain drop (588 of 1658:
  the bootstrap registry's 82 entries, 82 equippable items, 336
  `#sulfur_cube_swallowable` items and 88 spawn eggs), with each
  equippable item's slot, `dispensable` flag and allowed wearers;
- each projectile's `DispenseConfig` (power, uncertainty, level event and
  its spawn point for all six facings), the boat width and
  `Hopper.SUCK_AABB`;
- every block whose block entity is a `Container` (or that is a
  `WorldlyContainerHolder`), grouped by rule set: its slots per face in
  order, and which of 21 probe items each slot accepts and gives up
  through each face of an empty container;
- the tags those behaviours read (`#sulfur_cube_swallowable`,
  `#furnace_fuel_bottom_takeable`, `#bookshelf_books`,
  `#does_not_block_hoppers`, `#convertible_to_mud`, `#beehives`, `#rails`,
  and the entity tags `#can_equip_saddle`, `#can_equip_harness`,
  `#can_wear_horse_armor`, `#can_wear_nautilus_armor`).

The brewing stand's `canPlaceItem` reads the level's recipes for slots 0-3,
so the level-less oracle accepts nothing there; the fixture says so and the
test checks Java's rule for those slots directly.

## What was built

- **The dispense table** ([`world/dispensing.rs`](../../src/world/dispensing.rs)):
  `behaviour(item)` resolves an item the way `getDispenseMethod` does
  (bootstrap registry, then equippable, then sulfur-cube swallowable),
  with `equippable(item)` (slot and allowed wearers) and
  `projectile(item)`/`projectile_offset` (the `DispenseConfig`).
  `World::dispense` runs each behaviour. Behaviours that only touch blocks
  finish in the dispenser's scheduled tick: buckets, bone meal, TNT,
  shulker boxes, the wither skull and carved pumpkin block halves,
  glowstone into respawn anchors, honeycomb onto copper, water bottles onto
  dirt (leaving the glass bottle in the same slot, `consumeWithRemainder`).
  Behaviours that look for entities (equipment, shears, the brush, chests
  onto mounts, flint and steel, glass bottles at hives) are queued with
  their slot and finish in `World::tick_automation_entities`, later in the
  same game tick; the item leaves the dispenser only then, and is skipped
  if a player took it meanwhile. Spawned entities (projectiles, boats,
  minecarts, armour stands) leave at once and appear in that pass.
- **The entity half** ([`entity/dispensed.rs`](../../src/entity/dispensed.rs)):
  equipment onto mobs that can pick up loot, saddles and body armour onto
  mounts under Java's age and taming rules, armour onto armour stands, and
  armour, heads, pumpkins, elytra and shields onto players (the app lends
  the host and agent players through `World::tick_automation_entities_with`);
  shears cutting leads and fence knots, shearing sheep, mooshrooms, bogged,
  sulfur cubes and copper golems; brushing armadillos; chests onto tamed
  donkeys, mules and llamas; flint and steel priming sulfur cubes; eggs,
  snowballs, wind charges, experience bottles and splash and lingering
  potions at their configured power and uncertainty; armour stands turned by
  `Direction.toYRot`. Mob targets come from the mob index, not a walk over
  every mob.
- **Boats and minecarts** go through the existing cart API
  (`Entities::spawn_cart`, Track D1's), at Java's spawn points: boats
  `0.5625 + width/2` out, a block up over water in front or on the surface
  of water below; minecarts 1.125 out onto a rail (0.1 up, 0.6 on a slope)
  or into the air over one.
- **Sided transfers** ([`world/container_sides.rs`](../../src/world/container_sides.rs)):
  one `Kind` per container rule set with `slots_for_face`, `can_place` and
  `can_take`, and `World::container_kind`, `container_stack` and
  `set_container_stack`. Hoppers, droppers and hopper minecarts now try the
  face's slots in Java's order (a hopper under a furnace takes the output
  before an empty bucket), apply each container's `canPlaceItem` (a furnace
  refuses a second bucket in its fuel slot; a dropper below a furnace
  fuels it through the bottom; a brewing stand's bottom face takes bottles
  and the ingredient but not fuel), and reach two new containers: shelves
  (three plain slots) and chiseled bookshelves (one book a slot, updating
  the block and its last-used slot). Track C1's crafter has a `Kind::Crafter`
  seam. `#bookshelf_books` now includes written and writable books, for
  players too.
- **Hopper pickup** collects through a sorted cell index of item entities,
  built once per tick that has a collecting hopper, from `Hopper.SUCK_AABB`
  (11/16 up the hopper to the top of the cell above); a full block above
  stops collection unless it is a hive or nest (`#does_not_block_hoppers`);
  a composter at level 7 counts as a container above and stops it too; a
  stack only partly taken stays and the next item is tried, as in Java.
- **Saves**: queued dispenses are saved (`d,...` entries) and the entity
  output kinds extend the old item/shot flag (codes 2-4 for boats, carts and
  stands); older saves load unchanged.

## Containers, face by face

`tests/hopper_sides.rs` checks every 26.3 container rule set (chest, trapped
chest, the copper chests, barrel, dispenser, dropper, hopper, shulker
boxes, furnace, smoker, blast furnace, brewing stand, composter at each
level, jukebox, chiseled bookshelf, shelves) against the fixture: the same
slots in the same order for all six faces, and the same probes accepted
and given up through each. Not modelled: the decorated pot (it holds no
item in VoxelCraft yet) and the crafter (Track C1's).

## Differences from Java

- Dispenses that look for entities finish in the entity pass of the same
  game tick, not inside the block tick. Another block change in between
  (a player taking the item) skips the dispense.
- Equipment targets are tried mobs (in mob order), then armour stands, then
  players, rather than Java's entity-section order; VoxelCraft's armour
  stands have no hand slots, so a shield never goes onto one.
- A dispensed boat starts at yaw 0; a dispensed carved pumpkin is placed in
  its default state, and a copper golem base counts only as a copper block
  directly below the pumpkin.
- A dispenser's glass bottle or shears at a full hive release its bees even
  at night or in rain.
- Snow golems can't be sheared (they have no pumpkin state).
- Dispensers play none of Java's level events (the click, the fail click,
  the smoke puff or the per-projectile sounds); that predates this track.
- Java's `canTakeItem` for jukeboxes and chiseled bookshelves also asks the
  taker for room; the insertion that follows refuses a full taker anyway.

## Follow-up: hopper timing (2026-11-23)

On `v017-c2-hoppers`, from the merged `v017-redstone-automation`
(`1f0952e`), the two hopper builds of Track F's
[contraption corpus](2026-11-23-v0.17-reference-contraptions.md) were
fixed and checked against Java itself.

**Cause.** Hoppers ran as scheduled block ticks. In Java they are block
entities: `ServerLevel.tick` runs `tickBlockEntities` after the scheduled
ticks and block events, and `HopperBlockEntity.pushItemsTick` counts a
per-hopper cooldown down every tick. Two things followed. A hopper unlocked
by a scheduled tick (the clock's repeater turning off) waited a tick for
its own scheduled tick, where Java's ticker moves the item later in the
same tick. And the cooldown was a due tick rather than a counter, so an
emptied receiver that hadn't ticked yet this tick got 8 ticks where Java's
receiver still counts its first tick down (`8 - k`, k = 1 only if it
already ticked), passing the item on 7 ticks after it arrived. Together
they gave the hopper clock period 13 instead of 11 and let a carrier hopper
in the sorter keep a buffered item whose successors passed the filter.

**What changed** (`world/automation.rs`): hoppers enrol in a ticker list
in creation order when placed (or when an older save's pending hopper tick
runs); `World::tick_hoppers` runs each loaded, ticking hopper's
`pushItemsTick` after block events, from both `simulation::tick_world_rules`
and `World::tick_redstone`; the cooldown is the ticks left, counted down by
the hopper's own ticks (so a hopper in a chunk that isn't ticking keeps it
unchanged, replacing the old due-tick shifting); `tryMoveInItem` keeps a
custom cooldown above 8; a full hopper doesn't collect; and a hopper's
container change updates comparators at once instead of at the start of
the next tick (without that, a restart between ticks dropped the update).
Saves keep `c,x,y,z,ticks` (the same numbers as before: ticks left) and
add the ticker order as `h,x,y,z`; older saves load.

**Checked on the vanilla server.** `tools/java_server_trace.py` runs the
pinned 26.3 jar headless in a superflat world, freezes the tick, builds a
spec with `/setblock` (block entity data included), steps one tick at a
time and probes after each. The specs are
[`hopper_clock.json`](../../tests/contraptions/java/hopper_clock.json) and
[`item_sorter.json`](../../tests/contraptions/java/item_sorter.json), the
builds of `tests/contraptions/builds.rs` in Java's block states (a diode
faces its input; wires get explicit connections). Run on a Boat sandbox
with a Temurin 25 runtime and the jar from Mojang (SHA-1 checked):

- Hopper clock: the repeater turns on at ticks 5, 16, 27 ... and off at
  12, 23, 34 ...: period 11, on 7. Track F's expectation holds; the test
  now also checks the first turn-on at tick 5.
- Item sorter: iron reaches the output chest at ticks 22, 30, 38, 46, 134,
  142, 150, 158 and 166, and the run ends with **9** iron in the output
  chest, **one in the lock hopper**, 10 diamonds and 10 gold at the end of
  the line and the filter back at 41 iron. Track F expected all ten iron in
  the output chest and an empty lock hopper; that was wrong (the lock
  hopper drains only when a 42nd iron trips the comparator, and after the
  last iron none does). The test now expects Java's end state and the nine
  arrival ticks; VoxelCraft matches both.

`hopper_clock_period` and `item_sorter_filters` are no longer ignored.

**Differences.** The ticker order after an unload is kept (Java appends
reloaded block entities at the end of its list); hopper item pickup still
happens in `tick_automation_entities`, before entities move this tick,
where Java's hopper collects after the entity phase; crafters tick in
Track C1's own pass inside the scheduled-tick phase, not interleaved with
hoppers in one creation-ordered list.

## Follow-up: hoppers and carts in one tick (2026-11-23)

On `v017-c2-hoppers-merged`, `v017-c2-hoppers` was merged into
`v017-redstone-automation` at `0c137bb`, which had Track D1's cart line
(`v017-d1-cart-line`): rails on hoppers, and hoppers moving items to and
from chest and hopper minecarts. D1 ran a cart-facing hopper's whole
transfer in the entity pass after rescheduling the hopper's block tick
(`automation.cart_hoppers` with `schedule_redstone(p, 1, 0)`), from cart
cells recorded in the previous entity pass.

Now the hopper phase runs once per game tick, in
`World::tick_automation_entities`, after the dispensers' output and the
container carts' own work (`exchange_minecarts`: the hopper minecart's
pickup and the record of `cart_cells`). That is Java's order: entities,
then block entities. Each hopper does Java's whole `tryMoveItems` in its
own tick (`try_move_items`): it ejects into a block container in front,
else into a chest or hopper minecart there (`getAttachedContainer` falling
back to `getEntityContainer`, a random one of several), then, unless full,
sucks from a block container above, else a cart there
(`getSourceContainer`), else the items it reaches. One cooldown covers both
moves, there is no rescheduling, and no second cart pass. The hopper sees
carts where they stand after this tick's cart movement (`tick_minecarts`
runs earlier in the tick) and items where they lay before this tick's item
physics (the one lag left: Java's items move before its hoppers tick).

World-only callers (`World::tick_hoppers`: `tick_redstone` and the headless
harness) run the same phase against blocks. A hopper whose front holds a
cart (from the last pass's `cart_cells`) leaves its whole move, and one
with a cart or items above leaves its pull, to that tick's entity pass,
with the cooldown already counted; without an entity pass that tick, the
move is dropped. `tick_world_rules` no longer ticks hoppers itself.

D1's unit test `hopper_cart_collects_stacks_and_container_hoppers_obey_cooldown`
now expects Java's order: in the first tick the hopper minecart takes a
diamond from the chest above and the hopper under it takes that diamond
from the cart; in the next tick the hopper waits out its cooldown while the
cart takes the next diamond. `minecart_storage_line` (checked by D1 on the
vanilla server), `hopper_clock_period`, `item_sorter_filters` and
`crafter_autocrafts` pass, with their persistence tests.
