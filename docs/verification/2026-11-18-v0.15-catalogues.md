## Java sources

All facts come from the pinned Minecraft Java Edition 26.3 server jar
(`server-26.3.jar`, SHA-1 checked by the extractor).
[`tools/extract_java_structure_catalogues.py`](../../tools/extract_java_structure_catalogues.py)
writes [`tests/fixtures/java/26.3/structure_catalogues.json`](../../tests/fixtures/java/26.3/structure_catalogues.json)
from:

- `worldgen/structure/{village_plains,village_desert,village_savanna,village_snowy,village_taiga,trial_chambers,ancient_city}.json`:
  start pool, size, distance, heightmap projection, terrain adaptation,
  start height, start jigsaw name, pool aliases, spawn override categories;
- every `worldgen/template_pool/{village,trial_chambers,ancient_city}/**`
  file: 116 pools and 927 elements (village 62 pools / 649 elements,
  trial chambers 47 / 213, ancient cities 7 / 65), each element's kind,
  location or feature, weight, projection and processor list;
- the 727 template files those pools name (village 478, trial chambers
  191, ancient cities 58), read only for facts: size; every jigsaw
  connector's orientation, name, target, pool, joint type and placement
  and selection priorities; residents (entity id, villager profession and
  type, baby age, cat and horse variants, persistence); counts of bells,
  bed heads, job site blocks, chests, barrels, decorated pots, cobwebs,
  trial spawners and vaults; each container's loot table; each trial
  spawner's configuration and each vault's loot table. No block layout is
  recorded or copied;
- the 20 `worldgen/processor_list` files the pools name.

Classes read with `javap` and Vineflower (outside the repository):
`JigsawPlacement` and `JigsawPlacement$Placer` (`tryPlacingChildren`: inside
and outside free space, the expansion hack, junctions, the placement
priority queue), `JigsawBlock.canAttach` (joint types; a nameless child
connector attaches to any target), `StructureTemplate$JigsawBlockInfo`,
`FeaturePoolElement` (a zero-size box and one default connector: down, top
south, no name, target and pool `empty`, rollable), `ListPoolElement`,
`RuleProcessor`, `ProcessorRule`, `RandomBlockMatchTest`,
`BlockRotProcessor`, `ProtectedBlockProcessor`. Tags read:
`#doors`, `#features_cannot_replace`. Loot tables added to
`src/world/loot_tables.json` by
[`tools/extract_java_structure_loot.py`](../../tools/extract_java_structure_loot.py):
the ten village profession chests (`village_armorer`, `_butcher`,
`_fisher`, `_fletcher`, `_mason`, `_shepherd`, `_tannery`, `_temple`,
`_toolsmith`, `_weaponsmith`) and `pots/trial_chambers/corridor`. The
extractor also now lists the trial chamber tables v0.16 had added to the
file by hand, so it reproduces the file exactly.

## Implemented

### The catalogue contract (FE-02)

Each structure has a catalogue file
([`village_v4.txt`](../../src/world/jigsaw/village_v4.txt),
[`trial_chambers_v4.txt`](../../src/world/jigsaw/trial_chambers_v4.txt),
[`ancient_city_v4.txt`](../../src/world/jigsaw/ancient_city_v4.txt)) with
one `piece` line per Java template location holding Java's facts, and
Java's pools element by element (processor list and projection per
element, feature and list elements, fallbacks). The lines were seeded from
the fixture once; the tests hold them to it. A painter per structure
([`village/catalogue.rs`](../../src/world/village/catalogue.rs),
[`trial_chambers/catalogue.rs`](../../src/world/trial_chambers/catalogue.rs),
[`ancient_city/catalogue.rs`](../../src/world/ancient_city/catalogue.rs))
draws an original template for every line at Java's exact size, with every
connector Java gives it (face or top, name, target, pool, joint,
priorities) at a place of its own design; the builder
([`jigsaw/catalogue.rs`](../../src/world/jigsaw/catalogue.rs)) refuses a
template whose connectors differ. Where along a face a connector sits, and
every block, is VoxelCraft's.

### Jigsaw engine additions (inert for generations 1–3)

In [`jigsaw.rs`](../../src/world/jigsaw.rs): templates can carry a
processor list and an overhang (feature pieces); `Base::LootIn` places a
loot container of a given block (barrels, dispensers, decorated pots);
pool aliases (`assemble_with_aliases`, Java's `PoolAliasLookup`); Java's
expansion hack for libraries that ask for it (villages); aligned joints
for vertical connectors; selection priority (connectors sorted after the
shuffle) and placement priority (Java's `SequencedPriorityIterator`); a
nameless child connector attaching to any target (feature elements);
`Palette::process_at`, which sees the world block a rule's location test
reads. Generation 1–3 libraries have no priorities, aligned joints,
nameless connectors or processor lists, so their assembly draws the same
random numbers in the same order; the golden hashes confirm it.

[`jigsaw/rules.rs`](../../src/world/jigsaw/rules.rs) holds Java's processor
lists as data and runs them like `StructureProcessor.processBlock`: each
step in order, `RuleProcessor` and `BlockRotProcessor` each seeding
`RandomSource.create(Mth.getSeed(pos))`, `random_block_match` drawing only
when its block matches, the first passing rule winning, protected blocks
kept.

### Villages

- Java's start pool per style (`village/<style>/town_centers`), size 6,
  distance 80, `beard_thin` through the existing terrain blend.
- Profession buildings for all 13 professions in all five styles, each
  with Java's fixed job site blocks in Java's counts (cartography tables,
  lecterns, looms and stonecutters included, which v0.15 Track B made
  work), beds, bells and loot chests with Java's tables (the ten
  profession tables added), small, medium and big houses, temples and
  libraries.
- Town centres (fountains and meeting points) with their bells, wells with
  the well bottom piece, streets (terrain matching, three wide, a spur to
  every building entrance, decor on the verges), terminators, small and
  large farms (wheat that the farm processors turn into carrots, potatoes,
  beetroots, melon and pumpkin stems), animal pens and stables, meeting
  points off the streets, lamps, taiga decorations, and the 13 feature
  elements (oak, spruce, pine and acacia trees; hay, melon, pumpkin, snow
  and ice piles; cactus, flower, berry and taiga grass patches); trees and
  piles only fill air and plants.
- Residents from Java's spawn pieces: villagers (unemployed, nitwits,
  babies at `Age -21359`), zombie villagers, cats of the ten variants Java
  names (`cat_jellie` holds an `all_black` cat in Java's file, kept),
  iron golems in town centres, cows, pigs, sheep and horses of Java's coat
  and markings in pens and butcher yards, camels in desert houses. Each
  spawns once when its cell first ticks (`Entities::populate_village_residents`);
  the spawn cell joins the saved `villager_births`, so nothing respawns
  after a reload. Zombie villagers wait out Peaceful. Generation 4 houses
  spawn no one by bed: Java's villages are peopled by their spawn pieces.
- The abandoned variant: Java's four zombie town centres (weight 1 each
  against 50 per ordinary centre, 4 in 204 ≈ 1.96%) lead only to the zombie
  pools, whose `zombie_<style>` processors remove doors and torches, turn
  cobblestone mossy, scatter cobwebs over planks, logs, terracotta and
  glass panes and change the crops, as Java's lists do; their residents are
  zombie villagers.
- Java's processors throughout: `mossify_10/20/70_percent`, the five
  `farm_*`, `street_plains`, `street_savanna`, `street_snowy_or_taiga`
  (planks over water, grass patches) and the five `zombie_*` lists.

### Trial chambers

Java's full catalogue: corridor ends (the start), floor plates carrying
four corridor slices each, the corridor slices with their decor, addon,
spawner and hallway connectors, intersections, atrium with its reliefs and
grand staircases, entrances with their displays, every hallway (straight,
corners, staircases up and down, caches, rubble, encounters, the corridor
connectors and the trapped staircase), the eight chambers (`chamber_1`,
`_2`, `_4`, `_8`, assembly, eruption, slanted, pedestal) with their
centres, slices, quadrants, arms, ramps, staircases, covers, hanging
pieces and addons, entrance caps, spawner plates and contents, vaults and
ominous vaults, supply chests, decorated pots (Java's corridor pot loot),
candles, beds, barrels, disposals and dispensers. Spawner contents carry
Java's trial spawner configurations and cobwebs; the structure's pool
aliases pick the ranged, melee and small melee contents once per chamber.
The copper bulb degradation runs from the rules engine, every bulb lit.

### Ancient cities

Java's full catalogue: the three city centres, each with the reinforced
deepslate portal frame on a stepped platform and a hidden room inside the
platform (a lectern, Java's plain chest in `city_center_2`, soul light,
entered through wool); the ten pieces walling the centre in; the entrance
connector and five entrance paths; the city walls (intact and ruined
corners, the intersection, the L, horizontal walls with stairs, passage
and bridge) including the `no_corners` pool, whose
`intact_horizontal_wall_stairs_5` the jar lacks (Java places nothing for
it, and neither does VoxelCraft); and the structures (barracks, three
chambers, sauna, statue, ruins, tall ruins, pillars, the three-camp list
element and the ice box) with Java's chests and tables. The start is
placed by its `city_anchor` connector at the start position (height -27),
as `start_jigsaw_name` does; the walk level is -51 as before. Java's three
degradation lists run from the rules engine; sculk patches grow from
Java's sculk pool as generation 3's do.

## Differences from Java

- **Templates are original (FE-02).** Sizes and connector graphs are
  Java's; layouts are not, so a Java seed's village, chamber or city does
  not reappear block for block. Connectors keep Java's faces but sit at
  the middle of their face (equal connectors share a cell), so nested
  pieces tile only as far as their sizes allow; some of Java's connector
  attempts fail where Java's would fit, and the reverse.
- Legacy and single pool elements are not told apart (Java's legacy
  elements never place air); the catalogue templates say where air goes.
- The rules engine's `blockstate_match` (the abandoned villages' brown
  glass panes) matches any glass pane: VoxelCraft panes have no connection
  states. Doors in templates are oak whatever the style.
- Beds are red whatever Java's colour (the trial chambers' sixteen bed
  pieces keep their names and weights).
- The armor stands of the taiga armorer's house are not placed.
- Java's flower, berry, cactus and taiga grass patches scatter plants over
  the ground round the connector; VoxelCraft's place one in the
  connector's own cell (a spread copy could float or bury itself on a
  slope). Trees and piles spread round it.
- Residents spawn when their cell first ticks rather than with the chunk,
  and Java's random sequences (structure seeds, alias choice, pot loot
  rolls) are VoxelCraft's hashes.
- The expansion hack and the joint, priority and alias rules follow
  Java's code; the free-space test uses boxes, as generation 3's does.
- The terrain blend round generation 4 villages can leave a one-block
  overhang beside a piece, as generation 3's could where it met the same
  ground (seed 7's desert village has one two blocks from its meeting
  point). The surface rule gives such an overhang its ceiling form (sand
  hangs as sandstone, as in Java's deserts);
  `blended_surfaces_and_small_plants_keep_their_top_rules` now expects
  that form over open air instead of failing on it.
- Trial chamber connector shafts: Java's upper and lower hallway connectors
  enter and leave on the same face, so VoxelCraft's climb over the
  corridor's roof (upper) or drop under its floor (lower), and staircases
  climb or drop by their height less a hallway's; Java's own heights are
  not known (no layouts are read).
- Ancient cities keep generation 3's carve-and-fill for `beard_box` and the
  hashed sculk patch growth; trial chambers keep the solid shells for
  `encapsulate`.
