## Sources

as Tracks 7b to 7d), fetched from Mojang's version manifest in a Boat
sandbox (`voxelcraft-t7e-java`) and decompiled there with Vineflower

`full` (the other types have no metadata, `none`); the profession hats
are `full` for the farmer, fisherman, fletcher, librarian and shepherd
and `partial` for the butcher, the same for zombie villagers; the zombie
villager's type textures have no metadata.

## What changed

### 1. Villagers on Java's parts, with the outfit layers (MOB-06)

- **The villager mesh.** The adult villager and wandering trader are now
  `VillagerModel`'s part tree (a new `entity::model::villager_outfit`
  module, on Track 7c's rig): an 8 x 10 x 8 head (this game's was 8
  tall) with its nose hanging from the eye line to a pixel under the
  chin, the 20-pixel robe (`jacket`, grown by 0.5) over the body, the
  folded `arms` part (two sleeves and the hands between, turned -0.75
  about x at Java's pivot) and 12-pixel legs walking at half stride,
  all drawn at `villagerLikeScale`'s 0.9375 about the feet (the
  sleeper's slide to the pillow is unchanged, as the renderer applies it
  before the mesh's scale). The face (green eyes, unibrow) is this
  game's own, set just under the hat's brim line.
- **The zombie villager mesh.** `ZombieVillagerModel` is a humanoid, not
  the villager's mesh: the adult now has humanoid arms (a sleeve with
  the rotted hand out of it) raised by `animateZombieArms` and the full
  stride, the villager's 10-pixel head and the robe grown by 0.05,
  unscaled (34 pixels tall, 0.175 over its 1.95 box, as Java's). It
  shakes while it is cured, as before.
- **Type layer.** Each of Java's seven types (`desert`, `jungle`,
  `plains`, `savanna`, `snow`, `swamp`, `taiga`, the villager's
  `map_variant`, `plains` until its biome is read) has its own outfit:
  a palette for the robe, hem, belt, legs and accents, touches on the
  robe (the desert sash and collar, the jungle's leaf shoulders and hem,
  the savanna's beaded collar and red bands, the snow coat's fur collar
  and hem, the swamp's moss, the taiga's fur shawl; plains keeps the
  plain brown robe villagers had) and headwear on the hat shell (the
  desert head wrap with its gem, the jungle leaf band, the savanna's red
  band and feather, the snow knitted hat with fur, ear flaps and bobble,
  the swamp's broad reed hat; none for plains and taiga). All original
  art in the established box style; the robe boxes carry colour keys the
  draw replaces with the type's palette. Zombie villagers wear the same
  outfits faded and greened. Babies (villager children and the zombie
  villager cub) wear the type's baby outfit: its colours and smaller
  headwear (`baby/<type>`).
- **Profession and level layers.** For adults with a job (never for
  babies, never for `none`): the profession's apron (the colours
  villagers already had; jobless villagers no longer wear one) and its
  hat (the farmer's straw hat, moved onto the new head, and new
  original hats for the fisherman, fletcher, librarian and shepherd and
  the butcher's headband), then, unless a nitwit, a level badge on the
  belt in stone, iron, gold, emerald or diamond (level clamped 1 to 5).
- **`typeHatVisible`.** The type's headwear shows only while the
  profession's hat metadata is `none`, or `partial` while the type's is
  not `full`: a farmer's hat hides every type's headwear, a butcher's
  headband hides only the villager's desert and snow hats (and never a
  zombie villager's, whose type textures have no metadata). For babies
  the profession's metadata still counts, though the profession isn't
  drawn, as in Java.
- **LAN.** Guests get the villager's type and level in its look, next to
  the profession, so they draw the same layers.

### 2. The adult zombie villager's armor (MOB-06)

`HumanoidArmorLayer` with `ZOMBIE_VILLAGER_ARMOR`: on the new humanoid
parts the adult zombie villager wears its armor (this game's armor
pieces in their texture layout), with the helmet on the taller head
(Java's 8-pixel helmet box at the top of the 10-pixel head) and the
body's and legs' boxes grown a further 0.1, as
`ZombieVillagerModel.createBaseArmorMesh` grows them. As a humanoid it
also holds its main-hand item (`ItemInHandLayer`), which Java's
`HumanoidMobRenderer` draws; this game drew none on zombie villagers.

### 3. The fox's states and the shown trade (MOB-06)

- **Adult fox.** Rebuilt on `AdultFoxModel`'s parts (it shared the wolf's
  layout before): the 8 x 6 x 6 head with ears and snout, the body box
  turned a quarter to lie along the fox with the tail hanging from its
  end, 6-pixel legs, unscaled (the old model was squashed to 0.7467).
  Colours, the white cheeks and tail tip, eyes and nose are this game's.
- **`FoxModel.setupAnim`** on Track 2e's state (`Mob::fox`), shared by the
  adult and the kit with each model's overrides: the head rolls with
  `getHeadRollAngle` while interested; crouching (`crouchAmount`, eased)
  tilts the body 6 degrees, drops the head by the crouch times the age
  scale and the body by the crouch (a sixth for the kit), with the
  `cos(ageInTicks) * 0.05` wiggle on body and legs; sleeping hides the
  legs, rolls the body on its side and turns the head back (its
  `cos(ageInTicks * 0.027) / 22` sway); sitting sits the body up and folds
  the hind legs (the adult's and the kit's numbers); the adult's pounce
  lowers body and head by half the crouch; faceplanted, the head stops
  looking and the legs paddle. The look applies unless it sleeps, lies
  faceplanted or crouches. `FoxRenderer.setupRotations` pitches the whole
  fox about its feet by its look while it pounces or lies faceplanted:
  nose up as it leaps, down as it lands. A sleeping fox closes its eyes
  (`fox_sleep`), here a dark slit.
- **The shown trade.** `CrossedArmsItemLayer`: a villager's
  `Mob::shown_trade_item()` (and a wandering trader's main hand) is held
  up in front of the folded arms: `translateToArms`, a 0.75 turn, a
  1.07 scale, `translate(0, 0.13, -0.34)` and a half turn, in the item's
  `ground` display transform, drawn by the renderer's item model pass.

### 4. Capture tooling

`--spawn villager,x,y,z,type[:job[:level]]` (also for zombie villagers,
with `baby`, an armor material or a held item in another field) dresses a
spawned villager for capture scenes, and two scenes use it
(`mobs-still-villager-types`, `mobs-still-villager-jobs` in
`tools/capture_scenes.json`).

## Remaining differences

- **Textures, not boxes.** Java paints each layer as a texture over the
  same boxes (the type's outfit, the profession's clothes, the level's
  badge); here the outfits are this game's own colours on the robe plus
  shaped boxes for accents and hats. The professions keep their simple
  aprons (now only for villagers with a job) and five new hats; their
  full Java-style clothing (a librarian's or cleric's robe, the smiths'
  aprons, the cartographer's monocle, and so on) is not drawn.
- **The faceplanted paddle.** Java's `legMotionPos` gains 0.67 per
  rendered frame, shared by every fox drawn; here it runs on each fox's
  own clock at 0.67 a tick.
- **Not in scope:** the fox's held item (`FoxHeldItemLayer`, held-item
  drawing belongs to Track 6d), the villager's unhappy head shake
  (`isUnhappy`; this game has no unhappy counter), and the items listed
  as unchanged in Track 7d's record (the guardian, horses' head pivot,
  bamboo, the armadillo, sniffer and camel adults' walk keyframes, LAN
  guests' piglin fighting state). The baby zombie and zombie villager
  cub's armor stays as Track 7d left it.
