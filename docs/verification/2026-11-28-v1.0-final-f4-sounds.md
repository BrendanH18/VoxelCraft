Status: **Implemented, unverified.** Branch `t3/v1.0-final-f4-sounds`,
from `2b6bf95` (the integration branch `t3/prep-release-1-0`, version
1.0.0), built overnight by Claude Opus 5.5 as a track of the final
gap-closing wave. Rows: UX-02 (sound) and AUT-01b (the goat horn's game
event). Nobody has heard the new sounds or played it, and no agent ran the
game: Boat has no audio device or GPU. Every new sound is original and
procedural; nothing is sampled or copied from Mojang's recordings.

## Scope

1. Goat horns played as Java's `InstrumentItem`, on every input path, with
   eight original horn calls; obtained as in Java.
2. Brushing's own sounds per block (`item.brush.brushing.*`), and LAN
   guests seeing the find rise out of the block.
3. Music discs heard to Java's range with Java's attenuation, and other
   music fading while a disc plays (`MusicManager`).
4. The mobs that borrowed another's voice get their own ambient, hurt,
   death and step sounds.

## What changed

### 1. Goat horns

- `InstrumentItem.use` on the shared session (`session/interact.rs`), so
  the keyboard, controller seats, LAN guests and CLI agents all blow a
  horn the same way: the held horn's instrument plays, `instrument_play`
  posts at the player's position from the player, and every goat horn
  cools down for 140 ticks (`Mth.floor(7.0 * 20)`; the cooldown is the
  `goat_horn` item's, so another instrument waits too). The horn isn't
  used up and takes no damage (`GOAT_HORN_INSTRUMENT_DAMAGE` 0). The
  hotbar shows the cooldown veil Java draws. A CLI agent's horn reaches
  the host's speakers through its `Horn` event.
- The instrument is the item ID's offset from `Item::GOAT_HORN`, in
  Java's registry order (`content/goat_horn.rs`; IDs 1008 to 1015, all
  already registered).
- Sound: `play` plays at the player in `RECORDS` at volume `range / 16`
  (16), pitch 1. Java clamps the gain to 1 and hears it linearly to 256
  blocks; the mixer now does the same for this sound
  (`Sound::java_range`, `mixer::linear_attenuation`), and LAN guests get
  it to 256 blocks (they used to get sounds to 64 only).
- Eight original calls (`audio/horns.rs`): the shared voice model in an
  open, brassy vowel that brightens as it swells, each with its own pitch
  line (ponder's low call sinking a third, sing's two slurred notes,
  seek's rising glide, feel's wavering fall, admire's bright leap,
  call's short blast then a long rising one, yearn's wandering note,
  dream's airy drift), a breathy edge and a long valley echo. 3.4 to
  6.8 seconds each.
- Obtaining: goats already dropped horns when ramming and pillager
  outposts already carried them. Ramming now follows `RamTarget` and
  `Goat.dropHorn`: the block ahead **or the one above it** must be in
  `snaps_goat_horn`, which now includes coal ore and only the ten
  overworld woods' logs (stripped logs and nether stems no longer snap a
  horn); babies never drop one; the horn spawns at the goat's body with
  Java's random velocity (±0.2, 0.3 to 0.7, ±0.2 a tick). A goat's horn
  is one of its group's four instruments (`#regular_goat_horns` or
  `#screaming_goat_horns`), as before.

### 2. Brushing

- Each stroke plays the brushed block's brush sound in `BLOCKS` at volume
  and pitch 1: `item.brush.brushing.sand` on suspicious sand,
  `.gravel` on suspicious gravel and `.generic` on anything else; the
  finishing stroke plays `.sand.complete` or `.gravel.complete` in
  `PLAYERS` (level event 3008). Five original sounds: a bristle sweep,
  grainy for sand and crunchy for gravel, and a pour of grains for the
  completion. CLI agents' strokes are now heard too (`Event::Brushed`).
- LAN guests draw the find rising out of the block: the host sends each
  partly brushed block's reward and hit direction within 64 blocks
  (`World::emerging_near`, as `BrushableBlockEntity.getUpdateTag` syncs
  them), and the guest's renderer draws it from that
  (`World::apply_emerging`).

### 3. Music discs and music fading

- A jukebox's tune plays as `Sound::Disc` (sharing the note block's
  buffers) with `forJukeboxSong`'s numbers: volume 4 (gain 1), linear
  attenuation to 64 blocks. LAN guests get it to 64 blocks too.
- **Java 26.3 fades no music for a jukebox.** `MusicManager.tick` fades
  only towards `Minecraft.getMusicVolume`, which is 1 under a screen with
  menu music and otherwise the camera's `EnvironmentAttributes.MUSIC_VOLUME`;
  `playJukeboxSong` starts the song, sets the Now Playing text and tells
  nearby entities, and no client class but the options, the login packet,
  the toast and the credits stops the music. The known limitation
  ("other music doesn't fade while a disc plays") describes Java's own
  behaviour, so nothing was added for it. What was missing was the
  attribute itself: 26.3 sets `MUSIC_VOLUME` to 0 in the pale garden
  (`OverworldBiomes.darkForest(.., true)`), and the music now fades out
  there through the existing `MusicInfo` fade (`Audio::update_music`).

### 4. Mobs' own voices and steps

New voices (`audio/creatures.rs`), each with its own ambient, hurt and
death recipe:

| Mob | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iron golem | cow | metal clank and groan; **no ambient call** (Java has none) |
| Llama, trader llama | cow | nasal humming bleat |
| Camel | cow | deep gurgling grumble |
| Snow golem | slime | hollow "hoo" with a snow crunch |
| Phantom | skeleton | thin wavering screech (`HOSTILE`) |
| Silverfish | spider | dry scratchy chitter |
| Turtle | fish | low creaking grunt, calling only as an adult on land (`Turtle.getAmbientSound`), every 200 ticks or so |
| Baby turtle | fish | its own higher `hurt_baby` and `death_baby`; no call |
| Copper golem | skeleton ambient | **no ambient call** (Java has none; its hurt, death and step were already its own) |

The other mobs that borrowed a kin's voice but have their own events in
Java get their kin's recipe made their own: transposed, filtered and
layered with a texture of their own (`creatures::kin`): husk (sand),
drowned (water), zombie villager, zombified piglin, stray (frost), bogged
(moss), wither skeleton (hollow), magma cube (sizzle; its `squish`),
wandering trader (hum), skeleton horse, zombie horse, donkey, mule, piglin
brute, zoglin, glow squid and elder guardian. The brute's and zoglin's
old pitch-down is gone. Cave spiders and mooshrooms keep the spider's
and cow's voices, as in Java. Cod, salmon, tropical fish, pufferfish and
tadpoles still share one fish voice (cod's events); see below.

Steps (`entity/mob_steps.rs`): iron golems (volume 1), llamas and trader
llamas (0.15), camels (1; `step_sand` on sand, red sand, suspicious sand
and concrete powder, `camel_sand_step_sound_blocks`), silverfish (0.15)
and turtles (`shamble`, `shamble_baby`, 0.15) play their own footstep as
Java's `Entity.move` does: `moveDist` grows by 0.6 of each horizontal move
on the ground, and a step plays each time it passes `nextStep`, one every
1.67 blocks. Seven original step sounds.

## Remaining differences

- **The horn's use pose and slowdown.** Java starts using the horn
  (`startUsingItem`, `TOOT_HORN` for 140 ticks): the player holds it to
  the mouth and walks at a fifth of the speed while the button is held.
  VoxelCraft plays the call and the cooldown but draws no pose and doesn't
  slow the player.
- **Statistics.** `awardStat(ITEM_USED)` for the horn isn't counted.
- **The horn's tooltip** doesn't name its instrument (Java shows "Ponder"
  and so on under the name).
- **The mixer's falloff for every other sound** is still VoxelCraft's
  smooth fade from 2 to 24 blocks, not Java's linear `16 * max(volume, 1)`;
  only the horns and discs use Java's linear range. Changing every sound
  is outside this track.
- **Fish voices.** Cod, salmon, tropical fish, pufferfish and tadpoles
  share one voice under cod's events; Java gives each its own hurt, death
  and flop (and tadpoles no ambient).
- **Other mobs' steps.** Only the listed mobs play their own steps; every
  mob that plays its block's step in Java (cows, pigs, zombies and the
  rest, and the snow golem) still plays none, and zombies, skeletons,
  spiders and the other mobs with their own `*.step` events don't play
  them yet.
- **The disc's sound event.** Each disc's tune is VoxelCraft's own note
  score through the note voices; its event is reported as
  `music_disc.13` for every disc (it has no subtitle, as in Java).
- **The iron golem's ambient buffer** is rendered but never plays; it
  names `entity.iron_golem.repair` only so that every buffer has a Java
  event.
- The kin voices are a kin's recipe transformed, not separate recipes;
  nobody has listened to any of the new sounds.
