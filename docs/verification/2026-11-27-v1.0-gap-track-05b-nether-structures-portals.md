## Scope

Track 5's "Remaining differences and open points" that need no scope
decision:

1. Generation 7: the Nether's structures placed in their own decoration
   steps, each piece clipped to the chunk being decorated, so later
   features see them and they no longer cut through features.
2. Generation 7: ancient debris placed at its place in step 7.
3. Every generation: Nether portal blocks carry Java's `axis` state, set
   when lit or built and kept on reload, with older saves' portals given
   theirs on load; a spectator's trip with no exit portal is cancelled and
   no portal built.

Generations 1 to 6 must not move; Track 4b edits generation 7's End in
parallel, so this track stays in Nether and portal code.

## What changed

### Generation 7: structures in their decoration steps

`ChunkGenerator.applyBiomeDecoration` runs, for each decoration step, the
step's structures (`StructureStart.placeInChunk` with the chunk's writable
area) and then the step's features. `world::nether_order` now does the
same for each 16 × 16 Java chunk (cell) it decorates (`decoration()`
lists the sequence):

- **Steps and order.** Bastions (`bastion_remnant`) and Nether ruined
  portals (`ruined_portal_nether`) are `surface_structures` (step 4);
  fortresses and Nether fossils are `underground_decoration` (step 7).
  Within a step, structures run in the structure registry's order, which
  `RegistryLoadTask` registers sorted by id: bastion, then ruined portal;
  fortress, then fossil (`STRUCTURES`). So step 4's structures come
  before deltas and basalt columns, and step 7's before the blobs, springs,
  fire, glowstone, mushrooms and ores.
- **Clipping.** `getWritableArea` is the chunk's 16 × 16 columns from one
  above the bottom (y 1) to the top. A bastion, fortress or fossil paints
  only its pieces' blocks in those columns (`Bastions::paint_region`,
  `Fortresses::paint_box`, `Relic::paint_box`, the painters older
  generations use per 32³ chunk, now over any box). A ruined portal goes
  whole into the cell holding the middle of its template's box
  (`RuinedPortalPiece.postProcess`: `chunkBB.isInside(box.getCenter())`,
  then `encapsulate`), which always lies inside the cell's region.
- **Reading the region.** Structures read and write the cell's region as
  the earlier steps left it: fortress pillars (`fillColumnDown`, through
  air and fluid) stop on what is under them then, bastion processors see
  the blocks there, and a ruined portal's processors, netherrack spread,
  drips and chest check (`RuinedPortals::layout` and `Layout::process`,
  split out of the old builder) read the region instead of a chunk
  snapshot. Later features read the structures: glowstone hangs from
  bastion blackstone, fire can lie on a portal's netherrack, ores and
  blobs find no netherrack in fortress bricks.
- **Where regions overlap.** Track 5 keeps, where two cells changed a
  block, the change of the cell first by z then x. A block a structure
  changed is now marked (`CLAIMED`) and wins over every other cell's
  change there; then the rest apply as before. That is Java's result when
  the structure's chunk was decorated after the other: its pieces replace
  what they cover. Structures write only into their own cell's columns, a
  ruined portal into its region.
- Older generations' passes are gone from generation 7:
  `NetherGen::generate` returns after decoration (no fortress, bastion,
  fossil or debris pass), the generator doesn't paint ruined portals over
  its Nether chunks, and their chests come from the cells
  (`nether_order::structure_chests`). `RuinedPortals::get` gives only the
  layout for generation 7's Nether, where `locate` asks.

### Generation 7: ancient debris in step 7

`ore_ancient_debris_large` and `ore_debris_small` are in Java's order
among the step 7 features (after the other ores), with their placed
features' modifiers (`in_square`, trapezoid 8–24 or uniform 8 to 119,
`biome`). `scattered_debris` is `ScatteredOreFeature` with
`AbstractOreFeature.canPlaceOre`: `nextInt(size + 1)` tries, each
`round((nextFloat - nextFloat) × min(i, 7))` from the origin on every
axis; the target is `#base_stone_nether` (netherrack, basalt,
blackstone; generation 6 took netherrack only), and with
`discard_chance_on_air_exposure` 1 no random number is drawn and a block
with air on any side is skipped, read from the region as it stands then.
So debris can sit in basalt and blackstone blobs and bastion walls, as in
Java, and is never beside a corridor placed before it.

### Every generation: the portal's `axis` state

`NetherPortalBlock.AXIS` (`HORIZONTAL_AXIS`, default `x`) is now two block
states from Track 5's reservation: **14022** (`axis=x`) and **14023**
(`axis=z`), `portal::AXIS_X` and `AXIS_Z`. Both answer every property as
the portal (`Block::alias`, `base`: light 11, unbreakable, no drop, glass
sounds, immovable, not targetable, the portal texture and self-culling),
and `Block::is_nether_portal` covers them and the old state.

- **Set when lit or built.** `light_portal` fills the frame with its
  axis's state (`PortalShape.createPortalBlocks`), `create_portal` with the
  entry portal's (`createPortal(pos, axis)`). `setblock nether_portal`
  places Java's default, along X.
- **Used.** Travel reads the axis from the state
  (`getDimensionTransitionFromExit`, `createDimensionTransition`), and
  measures a portal with `getLargestRectangleAround` over blocks in the
  same state, as Java does. A portal block stays while held above, below
  and on both sides along its own axis (Java's `updateShape` ignores
  neighbours across its axis); before, either axis would do. Particles
  still pick their side from neighbouring portal blocks of either axis, as
  `animateTick` does.
- **Kept on reload.** The states are saved like any other (state ids fit
  `VXC3`/`VXC4` and region records unchanged).
- **Older saves.** State **104**, the portal of saves before this, stays
  registered as an axis-less portal. When a saved chunk loads
  (`World::install_chunk`, from `chunks.bin` or a region record), each
  of its 104 blocks gets the axis the game gave it then: along X when
  portal or obsidian holds it on both sides along X, else along Z
  (`inferred_axis`), reading neighbours across chunk borders from loaded
  or saved chunks; a neighbour in a chunk never loaded or saved counts as
  holding it, so a portal at a chunk's edge keeps its axis. A 104 block
  met any other way is read the same way at use.
- **Spectators.** `getExitPortal`: with no portal found near the target
  and the entity a spectator, `getPortalDestination` gives no transition,
  so the spectator stays and no portal is built (the portal cooldown is
  set and the portal retried). VoxelCraft decides arrivals after the
  destination loads, so `travel` now first asks the destination's save
  (`saved_portal_near`: its `chunks.bin` and region records, read without
  loading the dimension; `find_portal_in` is `findClosestPortalPosition`'s
  search over them, 16 blocks into the Nether, 128 out). With none, the
  trip is cancelled and the player waits until they step back in. A
  spectator's arrival never builds a portal either.

## Remaining differences and open points

- **Overlapping regions** (Track 5's open point for the owner): each cell
  is still decorated over undecorated neighbours. A block a structure
  changed now wins over the other cell's change; a block a structure set
  to what was already there (a fortress corridor's air in an open cave) is
  not marked, so a neighbouring cell's feature can stand in it, Java's
  result when that cell was decorated after the structure's.
- **Randomness** is VoxelCraft's (FE-01): structures keep their layouts'
  own random sources (a ruined portal's processors go on with its layout's
  sequence, not `setFeatureSeed(decorationSeed, index, step)`), and the
  debris draws from the cell's feature stream.
- The structures themselves are as before: generation 6's bastion
  catalogue and Java fortress layouts, the procedural fossils (not Java's
  fourteen templates), the ruined portal templates. Only where and when
  they are placed changed.
- Structures are clipped at the region's top, y 128; Java's writable area
  goes to the dimension's top (255), but no Nether structure reaches above
  the roof.
- **Spectators** decide before leaving from the destination's save; Java
  asks again every 10 ticks while the spectator stays in the portal. Alone
  in a world nothing can build a portal meanwhile, so the outcome is the
  same; the player steps out and in to try again. A LAN client's trip is
  unchanged (the host's save isn't the client's to read).
- **Portal look**: the portal is still drawn as a translucent full cube;
  Java's model is a 4-pixel-thick plane along the axis. The state now
  makes that possible (not in this track's scope; it would need a capture
  on the Mac).
- **Compatibility**: a build from before this one opening a world saved by
  it sees states 14022 and 14023 as unknown blocks (older builds already
  can't open states they don't know), and an older LAN client draws them
  so.
- **Not run:** the game, captures, frame-time suites. Nobody has looked at
  a generation 7 bastion, fortress or ruined portal, travelled through a
  portal, or loaded an older save with portals in a running game.
