## Scope

Run the dimensions the player isn't in the way Java 26.3 runs them, so 1.0
ships without FE-05:

1. Establish Java's behaviour for a level nobody is in from the 26.3
   bytecode.
2. Keep each dimension that holds live tickets (or is inside its empty-time
   grace) as a background level with its own world and entities, loading
   only what its tickets reach, ticked each game tick in Java's level
   order with no player in it.
3. Arrivals through portals into it placed (and their exit portal found or
   built) at the time of the trip, moving under the portal ticket, frozen
   in saved chunks when it expires; pearls flying on in their own level
   and taking their owner across when they land; the owed-arrival,
   owed-pearl and owed-portal mechanisms replaced where the background
   level does the job, their save keys still read.
4. Saving every level; LAN hosts' background levels running too; memory
   and tick cost bounded and measured.

All done; what remains is [listed below](#remaining-differences).

## Java's behaviour for a level nobody is in

**Every level ticks.** `MinecraftServer` ticks each `ServerLevel` every
server tick in the order they were registered: the Overworld, the Nether,
the End. Whether a player is in it doesn't decide whether it ticks; its
chunk tickets decide what of it does.

**Tickets.** `TicketType` in 26.3 (timeout in ticks; flags `PERSIST` 1,
`LOADING` 2, `SIMULATION` 4, `KEEP_DIMENSION_ACTIVE` 8,
`CAN_EXPIRE_IF_UNLOADED` 16):

| Type | Timeout | Flags | Placed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `player_spawn` | 20 | loading | `PrepareSpawnTask`, radius 3, at login |
| `spawn_search` | 1 | loading | `PlayerSpawnFinder`, radius 0 |
| `dragon` | none | loading, simulation | `EnderDragonFight.tick`, radius 9 at (0, 0), while players are in the fight's area |
| `player_loading` | none | loading | a player's view (`PlayerTicketTracker`) |
| `player_simulation` | none | simulation, keep active | each player's chunk, level 31 minus the simulation distance |
| `forced` | none | all but expire | `/forceload` (level 31) |
| `portal` | 300 | persist, loading, simulation, keep active | `Entity.placePortalTicket`, radius 3 |
| `ender_pearl` | 40 | loading, simulation, keep active | `ServerPlayer.placeEnderPearlTicket`, radius 2 |
| `unknown` | 1 | loading, expire if unloaded | `ServerChunkCache.getChunk` (a synchronous load) |

There is no `START` ticket: **26.3 has no spawn chunks.** Nothing keeps the
spawn area loaded without a player or a ticket.

A ticket of radius `r` has level `33 - r`. A chunk is entity-ticking at a
simulation level of 31 or less, block-ticking at 32 or less, and loaded
(`FULL`) at a loading level of 33 or less. So a **portal ticket** (level 30)
entity-ticks its chunk and the 8 around it, block-ticks a 5 × 5 square and
loads a 7 × 7 one; a **pearl ticket** (level 31) entity-ticks the pearl's
chunk alone, block-ticks the 3 × 3 around it and loads 5 × 5.

**Ticket time.** `purgeStaleTickets` runs at the start of each level's
chunk-source tick: a ticket with a timeout counts down only while its chunk
is loaded (`canTicketExpire`: its holder is ready for saving, unless it may
expire unloaded), and goes once `ticksLeft` is below 0 — 301 loaded ticks
for a portal ticket. Adding a ticket of the same type and level at the same
chunk resets its time. Portal tickets are saved with the level
(`chunk_tickets`, with `ticks_left`) and come back when the world loads;
pearl tickets aren't saved (the pearl places a new one).

**Where a portal ticket comes from.** `TeleportTransition.PLACE_PORTAL_TICKET`
and `Entity.placePortalTicket` (radius 3) at the arrival: through a Nether
portal at the exit portal the search found (`getExitPortal`: the found
portal block), or where the traveller came out when one was built; into
the End at the arrival; through an End gateway at the arrival (players
and other entities alike); out of the End for anything but a player at
the arrival (a player's respawn transition places none). Every arrival in
another level also calls `newLevel.resetEmptyTime()`
(`teleportCrossDimension`). A pearl that goes through a portal places one
where it comes out (`ThrownEnderpearl.teleport`).

**What runs in a level each tick** (`ServerLevel.tick`), players or not:

1. The world border, weather (`advanceWeatherCycle`), the sleep check and
   time (`tickTime`). (VoxelCraft keeps one game-wide clock and weather,
   which the host's tick advances; unchanged.)
2. Scheduled block and fluid ticks (`blockTicks.tick`, `fluidTicks.tick`)
   wherever `isPositionTickingWithEntitiesLoaded`: block-ticking chunks
   whose entities are loaded.
3. Raids (`Raids.tick`): a raid whose centre's chunk isn't loaded stops
   being active and waits (`Raid.tick`).
4. The chunk source (`ServerChunkCache.tick`): tickets purged, levels
   updated, then `tickChunks`: natural spawning and lightning
   (`tickThunder`) only in chunks a player is close enough to for spawning
   (`collectSpawningChunks`, `anyPlayerCloseEnoughForSpawning`), and the
   custom spawners (phantoms, patrols, cats, the wandering trader) only
   with players; **random ticks and precipitation (`tickChunk`) in every
   entity-ticking chunk** (`forEachBlockTickingChunk` walks
   `forEachEntityTickingChunk`), players or not. Then chunk unloading
   (`ChunkMap.tick`, `processUnloads`): chunks no ticket reaches unload and
   save, their entities with them.
5. Block events (`runBlockEvents`).
6. `hasActiveTickets` (`shouldKeepDimensionActive`: a ticket with
   `KEEP_DIMENSION_ACTIVE`) resets `emptyTime` to 0; `emptyTime` then
   counts up, and **only while it is below 300** do the dragon fight
   (`EnderDragonFight.tick`), the entities (each only in an entity-ticking
   chunk, `inEntityTickingRange`) and the block entities (each only in a
   block-ticking chunk, `shouldTickBlocksAt`) tick. So entities and block
   entities run on the tick the last active ticket goes and for 298 ticks
   after it.
7. Entity management (`entityManager.tick`) whatever `emptyTime` says.

**So a level nobody is in runs briefly and locally.** Checking the
expectations the task set against this:

- *A mob that comes through a portal walks on in its destination for the
  portal ticket's lifetime:* yes. It arrives at once, the ticket makes its
  chunk and the 8 around it entity-tick for 300 loaded ticks, then they
  stop ticking and unload, the mob saved in them.
- *A pearl keeps flying, loading chunks, and teleports its owner when it
  lands:* yes. Each tick the pearl renews its owner's pearl ticket where it
  is when its timer runs out (every 39 ticks) or it has moved into another
  chunk (`ThrownEnderpearl.tick`, which also resets its level's
  `emptyTime`), so its own chunk keeps entity-ticking wherever it flies, as
  long as its owner is a player in the game (any dimension). Landing,
  `onHit` takes an owner in another level across when
  `isAllowedToTeleportOwner` (`canUsePortal(true)`: alive and, for a
  living owner, not sleeping): the owner goes to the pearl's
  `oldPosition` with no motion (`TeleportTransition`, `Relative.ROTATION`,
  `DO_NOTHING`), leaving any vehicle, takes 5 `enderPearl` damage, and the
  teleport sound plays there; 5% of the time an endermite spawns, in the
  pearl's level at the owner's old coordinates.
- *The level the player just left keeps ticking its entities for
  emptyTime:* **not as such.** The player's own tickets
  (`PLAYER_LOADING`, `PLAYER_SIMULATION`) go with them
  (`removePlayerImmediately`), so the chunks they kept stop
  entity-ticking at once and unload; `emptyTime` only gates entities that
  some other ticket still keeps ticking. Every ticket that simulates keeps
  the level active except the dragon fight's, so in 26.3 `emptyTime`
  matters only for the End's arena after the last player leaves it (the
  fight drops its ticket at its next player scan, within 20 ticks), and
  for nothing a player can build. What the level left does keep ticking is
  what other tickets reach: the portal ticket of the player's own recent
  arrival there (300 ticks), pearls in flight, travellers arriving.

## What changed

### Tickets ([`world::tickets`](../../src/world/tickets.rs))

`Tickets` is a level's `TicketStorage` for the types a level can hold here:
`Portal` (300 ticks, radius 3, persistent, keeps the level active),
`EnderPearl` (40 ticks, radius 2, keeps it active) and `Load`, a
loading-only ticket with no timeout that holds ground for work waiting on
it (below). Same-type-and-level tickets reset rather than stack; `purge` is
`purgeStaleTickets` with `canTicketExpire`; `simulation_level` is the
tracker's level; `save` and `load` are `chunk_tickets` with `ticks_left`.
`EmptyTime` is `ServerLevel.emptyTime`. Players don't hold tickets here:
the world's existing player ranges (the simulation distance, agents',
LAN players' and split-screen views) are their `PLAYER_*` tickets, as
before.

### The world ([`World`](../../src/world/mod.rs), [`activity`](../../src/world/activity.rs))

- `World::set_tickets`: the simulation tickets join the players in
  `is_entity_ticking` and `is_block_ticking` (and the storage columns
  random ticks, precipitation and cauldron drips walk), the loading
  tickets join what streams and stays loaded. In the host's level that is
  new too: a pearl flying past the simulation distance loads and ticks its
  own chunks (Track 8f's pearl flew on only where the terrain happened to
  be loaded).
- `World::set_background`: the host's view stops loading, keeping and
  meshing anything; only what the tickets reach stays loaded. Back in
  view, every loaded chunk is meshed afresh. `World::update_background`
  streams it with no host position.
- `simulation::tick_unattended_world_rules`: a tick's world part with no
  players: scheduled block and fluid ticks, random ticks, precipitation,
  fire, leaf decay and block events where the tickets reach; block
  entities (furnaces, brewing stands, campfires, jukeboxes, lids, bells,
  moving pistons) and falling blocks only while `emptyTime` runs. The game
  clock stays the host's level's to count.
- Pearls tick only in an entity-ticking chunk and renew their ticket as
  `ThrownEnderpearl.tick` does (`Pearl::ticket`, `Entities::pearl_tickets`).
- The End fight's dragon moves only in an entity-ticking chunk when no
  player is in the fight's area (`Fight::dragon_ticking`): without a player
  there Java has no `DRAGON` ticket.

### Background levels ([`app::levels`](../../src/app/levels.rs))

Design (also in [architecture](../architecture.md#levels-the-host-isnt-in-v10-final-f11)):
the host's level stays in `Game::world` and `Game::mobs.entities`; every
other dimension that has been loaded is a `Level` with its own `World`,
`Entities`, tickets and empty time. Each game tick the background levels
before the host's in Java's order tick, then the host's level with the
host, then those after it. A background level's tick swaps it into
`Game::world` and `Game::mobs.entities` (`with_level`; the game-wide maps
and loot sequences stay put), sets `Game::unattended`, suppresses the
audio (nothing it plays reaches the player, a LAN guest's tap or the
captions) and runs `tick_unattended`:

1. its tickets counted down and handed to its world, which streams;
2. `emptyTime`;
3. the world rules as above, block and piston pushes, stalactites;
4. while `emptyTime` runs: the automation entities (hoppers, carts,
   dispensers), trial spawners, beacons, conduits, potent sulfur, respawn
   anchors, then the entity update with no players (`update_mobs`; its
   entity events handled by the same code as the host's level's), with
   portal departures and arrivals; otherwise entity management only
   (`sync_chunk_entities`) and waiting arrivals;
5. world drops become items, pearls' tickets are taken, and its
   particles and sound queues are dropped.

The swap was chosen over factoring a level tick out of `Game::tick`
because the entity events are handled by `Game` methods on `self.world`
and `self.mobs.entities`; swapping runs exactly the host's level's code.
Every use of the host or the other players on that path is gated on
`unattended`: no customers, riders, blast damage or pushes, beacon,
conduit or potent sulfur effects on players, trial spawner players, raid
omens or hero effects, and the entities' context has no players and no
spawning.

**Travelling.** `switch_dimension` still saves first. The level left
becomes a background level, with its tickets; a destination already
running in the background is swapped in for good, its world back in view;
otherwise it loads from its save as before. A LAN client runs no
background levels, and a world whose region files couldn't be opened
drops the level left, as before.

**Arrivals.** A traveller bound for another dimension (Track F2's
`PortalArrival`) is now resolved in its destination as soon as that level
ticks: the destination opens in the background if it isn't running
(`open_waiting_levels`, after each tick), a loading ticket holds the ground
its arrival needs (`hold_ground`, the readiness Track F2 used), and once it
has streamed in the traveller makes Java's arrival there (the
`PortalForcer` search, or `createPortal`; the End platform; the world
spawn's top), joins the level, gets a `PORTAL` ticket at the exit portal
found or where it came out (none for a carried vehicle's spot), and resets
the level's empty time. It then moves for 300 ticks and freezes with its
chunk when the ticket goes. The host's own arrivals place portal tickets
the same way (Nether portal, End portal, End gateway, a vehicle out of the
End), and so do entities through End gateways.

**Portals owed to the Nether.** A trip the Nether rule refuses still has
its exit portal built (Track 8c's `OwedPortal`), now as soon as the Nether's
ground there has streamed in (`build_ready_owed_portals`), in the
background, with no ticket after (Java's refused transition never runs its
post step).

**Pearls.** A pearl stays in the level it flies in when its owner changes
dimension (Track 8f's `leave_level` is gone from travel), keeps its own
chunks loaded and ticking there, and landing for an owner in another level
sets a pearl trip (`pearl_owner_away`); after the levels tick the host
goes there (`take_pearl_trip`: alive, not sleeping, not arriving or in the
credits; any vehicle left behind) with `Arrival::Pearl`: the pearl's
`oldPosition`, no motion, the rotation kept, the teleport sound and 5
`enderPearl` damage. Pearls still leave the level with an owner who leaves
the game and come back with them (Track 8f); a saved pearl of an owner in
the game whose level isn't running opens it.

**Saving.** Every save writes the background levels first, each as the
host's level is saved: stored entities and loaded chunks to its region
records, then its level file (with `chunk_tickets` when it has portal
tickets), waiting until it is on disk. A background Overworld's state goes
into the root level file with the player's. Each owner's pearls are saved
from every level.

**Opening a world.** Dimensions whose level files hold portal tickets, and
those something waits for (travellers, owed portals, pearls of a player in
the game), open in the background.

**LAN.** The host runs its background levels; guests stay in the host's
dimension as before, and a guest's pearls are saved from every level.

## IDs used

None: no block states, items, entity kinds or texture layers. New saved
key, written only when there is something to keep, so a save without it
(the v0.8 fixture among them) resaves unchanged: `chunk_tickets` in a
dimension's level file (the root one for the Overworld). Track F2's
`portal_arrivals`, Track 8c's `owed_nether_portals` and Track 8f's
`ender_pearls` (root level file and profiles) are still read and written
as before; the first two are now written only while something is in
transit at the moment of a save.

## Remaining differences

- **Loading is asynchronous.** Java loads the ground an arrival or an owed
  portal needs at once, inside the tick of the trip (`getChunk` with an
  `UNKNOWN` ticket). Here chunks stream in on worker threads, so a
  traveller into a dimension whose ground isn't loaded arrives a few ticks
  later (out of both levels meanwhile, held by a loading ticket that
  ticks nothing), and an owed portal is built a few ticks after the
  refused trip. Nothing in that ground runs in between, so the search and
  `createPortal` see what Java's saw.
- **A pearl of another player** (a LAN guest, a controller seat, an
  agent) that lands in a level that player isn't in doesn't take them
  across: players other than the host follow the host between dimensions
  here. The pearl is used up. Through an End gateway in a level its owner
  isn't in, a pearl lands at the gateway's mouth for its owner rather than
  going through (Java's pearl goes through and places a portal ticket at
  the exit). Pearls still don't use Nether or End portals (Track F2).
- **The pearl's endermite** (5%, in the pearl's level at the owner's old
  coordinates) isn't spawned on a trip across levels; within a level it is
  as before.
- **The dragon fight** isn't ticked as `EnderDragonFight.tick` is in a
  level nobody is in: the dragon moves only in an entity-ticking chunk
  with no player in the fight's area, from the first tick (Java's `DRAGON`
  ticket goes at the fight's next player scan, up to 20 ticks after the
  last player leaves).
- **Random ticks reach one ring further** than Java's: VoxelCraft has
  random-ticked block-ticking chunks (level 32) since before F11, for
  players' ranges as for tickets' now; Java's `forEachBlockTickingChunk`
  random-ticks entity-ticking ones (level 31).
- **Order inside a level's tick**: tickets are purged at the start of the
  level's tick rather than after its scheduled ticks and raids; block
