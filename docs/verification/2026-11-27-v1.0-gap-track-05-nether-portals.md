# v1.0 gap Track 5: Nether generation and portals — 2026-11-27

Status: **Implemented, unverified.** No owner play, and no agent played the
game. Branch `t3/v1.0-gap-track-05-nether-portals`, from `53505dd` (the
gap-closing plan), built by Claude Opus 5.5 as Track 5's implementation
agent ([plan](../roadmap.md#v10-gap-plan-2026-11-27)). Rows: GEN-04a and
GEN-04c.

## Scope

From the plan: in a new world generation only, the Nether's features in
Java's decoration order on decorated terrain, and the gravel and lava edges
of floors by the lava sea (FE-01 keeps the Nether's density and biome noise
original, so Java's surface rules and features apply on top of it). For
portals, in every generation: the relative position inside the exit portal,
the world border in the portal search and creation, and portal linking and
arrival in the generation corpus. Generations 3 to 6 must not move.

## What changed

### Generation 7

`Generation::V7` (`worldgen=7` in `level.txt`) is what new worlds use. It
changes only the Nether; the Overworld and End generate as in generation 6.
Saved worlds keep their generation. Generation 6 had no golden pin, so this
track added `generation_6_chunk_hashes_stay_identical` (the same sets as
generation 5's, all three dimensions, both corpus seeds), pinned at
`53505dd` before any generation change; it and the generation 3 to 5 pins
pass unchanged at the branch head. Track 4 (The End) also needs a new
generation; this track added the variant in its minimal form and gated
everything on `>= Generation::V7`, for the integrator to reconcile.

### GEN-04a: the gravel and lava edges (Java's surface rules)

26.3 renamed surface rules to material rules and made the Nether's data:
`worldgen/material_rule/nether.json`. `world::nether_surface` ports it and
`NetherGen::classify` uses it in generation 7 for every rock block, as
`MaterialSystem.buildSurface` applies it: a block's stone depth above (rock
up to the first air; lava doesn't end the run, so the floor under the lava
sea is a floor) and below (rock down to the first air or lava), the
column's surface depth (`surface` noise × 2.75 + 3 + a random quarter,
truncated toward zero; a hole at 0 or less), the biome, and five 2D noises.
That gives exactly Java's edges by the lava sea:

- **lava**: a floor's top block below y 32 in a hole becomes lava, the
  ragged edges of the sea;
- **gravel**: on the wastes' floors from y 31 whose surface is below y 35
  (`gravel_layer`), and under the floors of soul sand valleys and basalt
  deltas whose surface is at y 30–34 (`patch`);
- the wastes' soul sand only under floors whose surface is at y 30–34
  (`soul_sand_layer`); generation 6's soul sand patches up to y 90 and its
  gravel shores were original;
- the rest of the table (nylium and wart blocks by the `netherrack` and
  `nether_wart` noises from y 31, soul sand or soul soil and basalt or
  blackstone by `nether_state_selector`, basalt under delta ceilings, the
  bedrock gradients, netherrack from y 122) as Java's.

The noises keep Java's octaves and amplitudes with VoxelCraft's
permutations (FE-01). 26.3 stores each noise in a new form (`base_amplitude`,
`base_octave`, `octave_count`, `amplitude_modifiers`); its base amplitudes
are `NormalNoise.createParity`'s, so they give the 1.21 layouts'
values, which `NormalNoise` already implements (the biome source uses it).
Bedrock now replaces rock only, as in Java (generations 1–6 also put it in
open cells at y 1–4 and 123–126). The biome is the plain quart's, as for
every surface and feature (the fuzzy zoom remains a known gap,
[nether biomes](../nether-biomes.md#known-gaps)).

### GEN-04a: features in Java's decoration order on decorated terrain

`world::nether_order` decorates one 16 × 16 Java chunk (a cell) at a time as
`ChunkGenerator.applyBiomeDecoration` does:

- over a region of the 3 × 3 cells around it (`FEATURES`' block-state write
  radius is 1 in `ChunkPyramid`); writes outside it are dropped, as
  `WorldGenRegion.ensureCanWrite` drops them;
- the placed features that a biome of those nine cells lists
  (`possibleBiomes`), step by step, in the order `FeatureSorter` gives the
  biome source's biomes in the `nether` preset's order (wastes, soul sand
  valley, crimson, warped, deltas): `JAVA_ORDER`. It differs from generation
  6's table order in three places: `patch_crimson_roots` comes after
  glowstone; the deltas' `spring_closed_double` and ores come right after
  `ore_magma`; and the warped forest's step 9 features come before the
  crimson forest's;
- each placed feature runs its modifiers as `FeaturePlacer` does: each
  modifier maps a position to all its outputs before any goes further,
  depth first in order (so `count_on_every_layer` finds all its floors
  before placing any), and its modifiers and feature share one random
  stream;
- every read is of the region as earlier features left it: the floor search
  (`findOnGroundYPosition`, air or lava counting as open), the heightmap,
  every block predicate, the environment scan and the features themselves.
  Glowstone (`RandomNeighborSpreadFeature`) now counts the glowstone already
  in the level, as Java's does, instead of only its own blocks.

So later features build on earlier ones: ores replace only the netherrack
the blobs left, glowstone spreads from either glowstone feature's blocks,
twisting vines skip spots the warped vegetation took (they are rarer than
in generation 6, as in Java), fungi grow among the vines placed before them.
The "tall features first, first write wins" painting order is gone.

A cell's changes go into a cache shared by all chunks, at most 4 million
changed blocks (32 MB), oldest out first: a basalt delta cell changes about
16,600 blocks (its blobs), other biomes' cells 450 to 1,400 (measured on
seed 12345's bench patches). A 32³ chunk takes the changes of the sixteen
cells whose regions reach it. Open lava springs record where they went and
are scheduled to flow as in generation 6.

**Where regions overlap.** In Java a chunk's features may write into its
eight neighbours, and which of two overlapping chunks decorates first
depends on how the player moved; the later one sees the earlier one's
blocks. Every chunk being a pure function of the seed rules that out here
without decorating a large neighbourhood in a fixed order (a valid Java
order where every cell sees its earlier neighbours chains through nine
phases, about sixteen cells out, too much to compute or cache per chunk).
So each cell is decorated over undecorated neighbours, and where two cells
changed the same block, the cell first by z, then x, keeps its change. That
is Java's result whenever that cell was decorated first and the other's
features didn't depend on its blocks; Java's later features rarely replace
another's blocks (ores take only netherrack, plants and fire only air).
Within each cell's region the order is Java's. **For the owner:** this is
the one place generation 7 can't follow a single Java order; it is recorded
here rather than decided as a scope change.

### GEN-04c: portals (every generation)

Following `NetherPortalBlock.getPortalDestination`, `getExitPortal`,
`getDimensionTransitionFromExit` and `createDimensionTransition`:

- **Relative position.** On travel, the entry portal is measured with
  `BlockUtil.getLargestRectangleAround` (21 each way) around the portal
  block the player touches, and `PortalShape.getRelativePosition` records
  where the player stood: across it and up it as fractions of their room
  (Java's float entity dimensions), and their distance from its middle
  plane. On arrival the exit portal is measured the same way around the
  portal block the search found (the search now returns that block, not the
  lowest under it), and the player is put at the same place in it, then
  moved by `findCollisionFreePosition`: to the nearest point at most half
  their width across and one block up where no block's collision box,
  grown by half their size, holds their centre (unchanged if there is none
  or they are over 4 blocks either way). Before, the player always landed
  in the middle of the exit's bottom-left cell.
- **Turn and motion.** `createDimensionTransition` keeps the player's look
  and motion (`Relative.ROTATION` and `Relative.DELTA`; the respawn packet
  keeps both on the client) and adds a quarter turn when the two portals'
  axes differ. Before, the player was turned to face out of the portal,
  levelled and stopped.
- **World border.** VoxelCraft has no `/worldborder`, and in 26.3 each
  dimension's border is its own saved data created from
  `WorldBorder.Settings.DEFAULT`, so every dimension's border is the default
  one: centred on 0, 0 and 59,999,968 across (±29,999,984).
  `world::portal::BORDER` holds it. The travel target is clamped to it
  (`clampToBounds`); the search skips portals outside it
  (`findClosestPortalPosition`); `createPortal` skips columns that are, or
  whose next block along the portal is, outside it, clamps its fallback
  ledge into it, and builds nothing if the ledge can't fit between y 70 and
  9 below the top (never at these dimensions' heights; the player then
  lands at the target).
- VoxelCraft's portal blocks have no `axis` state, so a portal's axis is
  still inferred from its neighbours, and the rectangle test is "a portal
  block" rather than "the same state".

### Portal linking and arrival in the corpus

`terrain::portal_corpus` (`portals_link_and_arrive_on_the_corpus_seeds`)
runs a trip on both corpus seeds (12345 and 42): a portal built on the
Overworld surface by (8, 8); a player stepping in off-centre; the trip set
out as travel sets it out; a fresh generation 7 Nether with no portal in
16 blocks, so `createPortal` builds one within 16 blocks along the entry's
axis; the arrival in the same place, clear of blocks, standing on the
frame; then linking both ways (the Overworld's 128-block search from the
way back finds the portal left by; a second trip in finds the portal built
within 16). `PORTAL_CORPUS` pins each seed's two portals and the arrival.

## Remaining differences and open points

- **Overlapping regions** (owner): described above; within a cell's region
  the order is Java's.
- **Structures** are still painted after every feature, as in generation 6.
  Java places each structure's pieces in their own step, clipped to the
  chunk being decorated (bastions and Nether ruined portals in step 4,
  fortresses and Nether fossils in step 7), so in Java later features see
  them: glowstone can hang from bastion blackstone and fire can sit on
  structure floors there, and here features never touch structures and
  structures cut through features.
- **Ancient debris** keeps its own pass after the structures (unchanged; it
  follows Java's scattered ore and exposure but not its place in step 7).
- **Randomness** is VoxelCraft's (FE-01): counts, heights, shapes,
  thresholds and order are Java's.
- **Portal axis** is inferred from neighbours (no `axis` state).
- Java builds no exit portal for a spectator (the trip is cancelled);
  VoxelCraft's arrival doesn't check the game mode, as before.
- **New worlds' Nethers look different** from generation 6's (Java's
  surfaces: less soul sand in the wastes, gravel and lava edges by the sea,
  bare forest patches from the `netherrack` noise; fewer twisting vines).
  Capture scenes and the `nether` benchmark scene generate fresh worlds, so
  they show generation 7; the `--dimension nether` start now lands in the
  middle of the arrival portal rather than its west column.
- **LAN**: a client of an older build joining a generation 7 world falls back
  to its own latest generation (existing behaviour for unknown numbers).
- **Not run:** the game, frame-time suites, captures. Nobody has looked at
  a generation 7 Nether or travelled through a portal in a running game.
