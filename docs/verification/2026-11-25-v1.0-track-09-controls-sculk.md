## What changed: controls (PLY-06a)

1. **Options** ([`settings.rs`](../../src/app/settings.rs)), under Java's
   keys and defaults: Attack/Destroy and Use Item/Place Block, Hold or
   Toggle (`toggleAttack`, `toggleUse`, both Hold); Auto-Jump (`autoJump`,
   **off**, as `Options.autoJump` defaults in 26.3); Attack Indicator
   (`attackIndicator`, saved as `AttackIndicatorStatus`'s id 0 OFF, 1
   Crosshair, 2 Hotbar; default Crosshair; an id out of range wraps like
   the `LEGACY_CODEC`). View Bobbing now also loads and saves Java's
   `bobView`; the older `view_bobbing` line is still written and read so
   earlier builds keep the setting.
2. **Screens.** Controls lists Sneak, Sprint, Attack/Destroy, Use
   Item/Place Block and Auto-Jump in `ControlsScreen.options` order with
   Java's captions ("Attack/Destroy: Hold", "Auto-Jump: OFF"); Sprint
   Window, quit shortcuts and the operator items tab stay out. Video
   Settings' Preferences gain "Attack Indicator: Crosshair / Hotbar / OFF"
   (Java lists it after the autosave indicator and vignette, which
   VoxelCraft lacks), before VoxelCraft's own FPS counter.
3. **Attack/Use Toggle on every input path.** `ToggleKeyMapping.setDown`
   flips the held state on each press and ignores releases, while
   `KeyMapping.click` still counts the press; `handleKeybinds` acts on the
   click before the held state. So a press that turns Attack on clicks and
   holds; one that turns it off clicks once more, then `continueAttack(false)`
   stops mining; Use turning off releases an item in use (bow, food, shield,
   spyglass) without a new use, otherwise uses once more and stops
   repeating. The table is `toggled_presses` in
   [`app/mod.rs`](../../src/app/mod.rs); the keyboard and mouse (`press`,
   `on_release`), the controller's triggers (`Seat::trigger_edges`, with
   the toggled state let go when a screen opens, as `ToggleKeyMapping`
   releases on `setScreen`) and the LAN client (through the same button
   path) use it. The CLI agent has no held buttons, so the toggles don't
   apply there.
4. **Auto-Jump** ([`player/travel.rs`](../../src/player/travel.rs)): Java's
   `updateAutoJump` after each move (`LocalPlayer.move`): `canAutoJump`
   (option on, on the ground, not sneaking, not riding, a movement key held,
   block jump factor at least 1); the move or, when it was blocked, the key
   vector times the movement speed; facing within the 0.15 dot of the
   movement; headroom in the two cells above the box top; the 7-tick
   look-ahead box and the two side segments at 0.51 up, tested against the
   block collision boxes in `Cursor3D` order; the obstacle raised by the
   shapes stacked on it up to the jump height (1.2, plus 0.75 per Jump Boost
   level), with the extra headroom checks; and a jump on the next tick when
   the obstacle is more than 0.5 and at most the jump height above the feet
   (`autoJumpTime = 1`, `input.makeJump()`). Each local player copies the
   option in: the host, every controller seat, and the LAN client, which
   sends the auto-jump to the host as its jump press (the host simulates
   that player). CLI agents keep it off.
5. **Camera** ([`camera.rs`](../../src/camera.rs)):
   - Third-person distance is `Camera.alignWithEntity`'s: the larger of the
     player's `camera_distance` (4) and a living mount's — 8 on a happy
     ghast (`Entities::camera_distance`); carts, boats and cushions aren't
     living and keep 4. Back and front views were already Java's (the front
     view reverses yaw and pitch and backs off along the new forward).
   - `getMaxZoom`'s `ClipContext.Block.VISUAL` ray now passes through the
     blocks whose visual shape is empty: glass, stained and tinted glass and
     copper grates (`TransparentBlock`), iron and copper bars and glass
     panes (`IronBarsBlock`), and powder snow (`see_through`).
   - The camera's eye height eases halfway to the pose's each tick
     (`Camera.tick`'s `eyeHeight`), so crouching and standing glide as in
     Java (`Player::camera_eye_offset`).
   - The crosshair and its attack indicator draw in first person only
     (`Hud.extractCrosshair`).
6. **View bobbing and hurt tilt.** `bobView` now reads Java's bob: once a
   tick, before travel, `ClientAvatarState.updateBob` eases 0.4 of the way
   to min(0.1, the horizontal `deltaMovement`) on the ground and not
   swimming (0 otherwise), and riding resets it (`rideTick`). The bob is
   applied whenever View Bobbing is on, as `GameRenderer.renderLevel` does,
   so it fades out over a few ticks when swimming starts instead of
   stopping at once. `bobHurt` (the ten-tick quartic tilt by Damage Tilt and
   the death roll) already matched and was re-read.
7. **Hotbar attack indicator** (`Hud.extractItemHotbar`): with the option
   on Hotbar, an 18-pixel gauge six pixels right of the hotbar (the off-hand
   side is the left for a right-handed player) fills from the bottom in
   19ths while the charge is below full (original art, no Mojang sprite).

## What changed: game events (AUT-01b)

Every Java call site the game has the action for now posts its event,
except the nine listed under [remaining gaps](#remaining-gaps). Posts made
where only the entity simulation is at hand are queued (`Sculk::post`) and
reach listeners at the next entity update, as the v0.17 record describes.

- **Mob behaviours, found tick by tick** (`mob_actions` and `flapping` in
  [`entity/sculk_events.rs`](../../src/entity/sculk_events.rs), from each
  mob's state against the last tick's): armadillos rolling up and out
  (`entity_action`); camels sitting, standing and dashing; nautiluses
  dashing; sulfur cubes priming (`prime_fuse`); creakings activating,
  deactivating, freezing or unfreezing (`canMove`) and twitching when hit
  while protected; turtles digging every fifth count of a clutch on sand;
  equipment changes in every slot (`equip` for an item with an
  `EQUIPPABLE` component, otherwise `unequip`, never on the first tick);
  mobs boarding a cart or boat (`entity_mount` at the vehicle, from the
  mob); bounces off slime blocks and beds (`Mob::bounces`); and flapping
  over air (`processFlappingMovement`): bats awake every 10 ticks, bees in
  the air every 2, vexes every 4, phantoms every 25 from `getId() * 3`,
  allays whenever airborne, chickens and parrots by flown distance with
  their `flapSpeed`.
- **Mob actions posted where they happen:** the armadillo's scute drop
  (`entity_place`) and brushing (`entity_interact`), the ravager's roar,
  the sniffer digging (`entity_action` at its head block every tenth tick),
  pandas, horses and camels eating (`eat`), evoker fangs and vexes
  (`entity_place`, from the evoker), shulkers opening and closing
  (`container_open` / `container_close`), shulker bullets destroyed
  (`entity_damage`), the dragon's death (`entity_die`), monster and trial
  spawners and creaking hearts spawning (`entity_place`) and creaking
  heart resin (`block_place`).
- **Players:** held uses post `item_interact_start` and
  `item_interact_finish` (`causeUseVibration`): eating and drinking
  (finishing was already posted), bows and crossbows (`start_draw`,
  `release_draw`, a cancelled or loaded draw), shields and spyglasses
  (app hands); a totem of undying posts `item_interact_finish`
  (`World::player_totems`, from `Vitals::totems_used`); shears on a
  mooshroom, snow golem, copper golem or sulfur cube, or on a nautilus's
  saddle or a happy ghast's harness (`Mob.shearItem`), post `shear` before
  `entity_interact`; a fish or sulfur cube bucket posts `entity_place`
  after `fluid_place`; beehive harvests post `shear` or `fluid_pickup`
  (and no longer `block_change`); a decorated pot's wobble posts
  `block_change`; Frost Walker posts `block_place` per ice block from the
  walker; trampling farmland posts `block_change` from the player.
- **Decorations and vehicles:** a punch or arrow on a framed item posts
  `block_change`, on an armor stand's first hit `entity_damage`, and a break
  `entity_die` (also from explosions); a frame losing its wall posts
  `block_change`; hits on boats and minecarts post `entity_damage` from the
  attacker; item entities hurt by explosions post `entity_damage`; closing
  a chest boat's or chest minecart's store posts `container_close` per
  viewer (`Entities::set_vehicle_viewers`).
- **Block ticks** (all from no source): eyeblossoms opening or closing,
  dried ghasts hydrating and drying, big dripleaf tilting (not the unstable
  step, which doesn't `causesVibration`), sweet berry bushes growing,
  turtle eggs cracking (`block_change`) and hatching (`block_destroy`),
  cauldrons filling from rain, snow and drips, mud drying to clay over
  dripstone, campfires finishing each food, bees entering and leaving
  hives (from the bee), powered shelves (`block_activate` /
  `block_deactivate`), sniffer eggs placed or advanced (`block_place`),
  farmland drying or covered (`block_change`) and suspicious sand or gravel
  breaking after a fall (`block_destroy`).
- **Reclassified as absent** (the game lacks the action): foxes picking
  berries, rabbits raiding carrots, `/kill` on anything but the player
  (`Entity.kill`, `EnderDragon.kill`), goat horns, water bottles making
  mud, shears trimming plant tips, and paths reverting to dirt.

## Differences from Java

- Auto-Jump tests block collision boxes only; Java's `getCollisions` also
  sees entities with collision boxes (boats, shulkers) and the world
  border. It runs in the simulation's tick (the player's own move), as
  Java's client does, but with a controller's analog stick the key vector
  keeps its length instead of being normalized.
- Mob behaviours found tick by tick post at the next entity update after
  the change, from the mob's position after its move; Java posts inside
  the behaviour. Camel dashes are found by their cooldown starting, so a
  dash Java starts but `canJump` then refuses isn't posted.
- The flap rhythms count ticks from when the game first saw the mob, not
  from its `tickCount`, so the phase differs (the rate is Java's). The
  dragon's flaps aren't posted.
- A loaded crossbow posts `item_interact_finish` when it loads; Java posts
  it when the player lets go.
- A totem posts on the player's next tick after the save.
- Decorations hit by the player post from the player, but a break from an
  explosion or a fall into the void posts from no one, where Java names
  the attacker or the decoration.
- `entity_damage` for an item in a blast isn't posted for items that burn
  in fire or lava (they vanish at once in VoxelCraft).
- Vehicle store closings post from no one (Java names the player).
- The camera smoothing uses the previous tick's pose for the first frame
  of a teleport.

## IDs and appended orderings

No block, item, texture, sound, particle or game event IDs. Appended to
`options.txt`, after `entityShadows` and before the key bindings:
`toggleAttack`, `toggleUse`, `autoJump`, `attackIndicator`, `bobView`

eye fields are never saved.

## Remaining gaps

The `gap` rows of `COVERAGE`:

- Leads: `Entity.dropAllLeashConnections` (shearing off leads),
  `Entity.interact` (leashes on non-mob entities),
  `LeashFenceKnotEntity.interact` and `LeadItem.bindPlayerMobs`. Track 6
  owns leads and fence knots in v1.0, so these are left to that track's
  code (seam: post `shear` at the mob, `block_attach` at the knot).
- `AbstractBoat.onAboveBubbleColumn` (`splash`): Track 7 reworks boats
  tick by tick.
- `ArmorStand.causeDamage`: armor stands don't burn in VoxelCraft yet.
- `CampfireBlock.douse` from splash potions (shovels already post).
- `Level.destroyBlock`: mob breaks go through `World::animal_block`, but
  other callers (scaffolding, chorus plants, cactus, the Wither, door
  breaking) haven't been checked one by one.

Other open items for PLY-06a: Sprint Window (`sprintWindow`, the
double-tap window, fixed at Java's default 7), the sleeping camera's bed
rotation and 0.15 lift, the dragon's flaps, and the controller's analog
Auto-Jump input length.

## Open decisions for the owner

None blocking. Two Java-faithful defaults were taken: Auto-Jump is off by
default (Java 26.3's `options.autoJump` default), and the attack and use
toggles also apply to a controller's triggers, which Java has no
equivalent of.

The game itself (no hands-on session, no screenshot), the frame-time
suites and the headless benchmark. The per-mob comparisons add a few field
