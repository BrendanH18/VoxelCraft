## What changed

### 1. Java's face UVs on every face (UX-01, BLD-05c)

Java maps an element's face without explicit UVs by `FaceBakery`'s
default UVs: east `(16 - z, 16 - y)`, west `(z, 16 - y)`, up `(x, z)`,
down `(x, 16 - z)`, south `(x, 16 - y)` and north `(16 - x, 16 - y)`, so
every face's texture is upright and **unmirrored seen from outside**. The
terrain shader's planar UVs ran the same way on opposite faces, so the
**east, north and down** faces showed their textures mirrored (Track 6c's
record named north and east; the bottom face was mirrored too).

- `chunk.wgsl`'s `face_uv` is Java's mapping in planar form (`-z` for
  `16 - z`, which tiles identically), for greedy, detail and fine quads.
  [`mesh::face_uv`](../../src/mesh.rs) is the same mapping for the CPU
  models; held, dropped, framed, shelved and slot blocks, falling blocks,
  chests and barrels with moving lids, signs' boards and the item models'
  boxes all use it, so a block's faces look the same in the world and in
  every item view.
- **Doors** keep Java's look from both sides: Java's door models give one
  broad face an explicit reversed UV (`door_bottom_left`'s east face,
  `door_bottom_right`'s west face, swapped again by the `_open` models),
  so the panel reads as one board seen through. A side detail quad's lower
  u bound is at most 15, so its top bit (word 0 bit 25) now mirrors the
  face; the mesh sets it on the face Java mirrors: the one on the cell's
  edge when the door is hinged right or open (not both), the inner one
  otherwise ([`shape::door_mirrors_edge_face`](../../src/world/shape.rs)).
  No quad grew; no other block uses the bit.
- Every other block family keeps its layers and in-plane orientation; only
  the faces Java draws unmirrored stopped being mirrored. The game draws
  directional blocks (furnaces, logs on their side, pistons, glazed
  terracotta) with layers that are not rotated within a face, so nothing
  depended on the mirror.

### 2. Block items through their whole display transforms (BLD-05c)

- **First person**: a block item is now drawn by
  `firstperson_righthand` of its own model (`item_display::drawn`), after
  `ItemInHandRenderer`'s arm chain: `block/block` is still 45 degrees at
  0.4, but an orientable block (furnace, pumpkin, dispenser, hive, loom,
  glazed terracotta) turns 135 degrees, stairs and fences theirs, a chest
  315 degrees, a head, statue, shelf or conduit its own transform. It was
  45 degrees for every block with Track 6's scales.
- **Dropped**: `ItemEntityRenderer`'s chain, the item's `ground` transform
  and the model raised so its lowest point (`getModelBoundingBox`, after
  the transform) hovers 1/16 over the feet. It was a cube at Track 6's
  scale without the transform's turn or offset.
- **Framed**: `ItemFrameRenderer`'s chain: the model's +Z into the wall,
  the item plane 7/16 out from the cell's centre, the frame's rotation,
  half size, then the item's `fixed` transform (`block/block` another
  half; chests and heads turned half about y; fences, walls and anvils a
  quarter). Blocks sat on the frame's backing at 0.375 of a block with only
  the quarter turn.
- All these, the third-person and mob hands and shelves now draw a block
  item's **slot model** (`ui::block_item_quads`): Java's face UVs and a
  head's 8 × 8 × 8 skull box, so a head held, dropped, framed or shelved is
  the skull, as in a slot (it was a full cube in those views).

### 3. In-hand models: the trident and the spyglass (BLD-05c)

Java's item definitions switch both outside slots, the ground, frames and
shelves (`display_context` select): the **trident** to `TridentModel`
(`item/trident_in_hand`, through the special model's `scale(1, -1, -1)`:
a 25-pixel shaft, a 3-wide base and three spikes) and the **spyglass** to
`item/spyglass_in_hand` (the narrow tube over the wide one). Both are now
drawn with those geometries and display transforms in first person, in
third-person and mob hands (a drowned's trident too), with the glint
(`item_models::in_hand_mesh`, `item_display::held`). Their textures are
original art on two new layers in Java's layouts (`SPYGLASS_MODEL`,
`TRIDENT_MODEL`). They were the flat sprite in a hand.

### 4. The allay's and the copper golem's grips (BLD-05c)

Both now hold the item's real model through Java's `ItemInHandLayer`
(their `translateToHand` on the game's models, then -90 degrees about x,
180 about y, the (1, 2, -10) offset and the item's
`thirdperson_righthand` transform), drawn by the item model pass, so the
item **glints** and **translucent blocks blend**, as for players and the
humanoid mobs. `AllayModel.translateToHand`: the body (at its Java pivot,
4.5 pixels up the game's body), 1/16 down and 3/16 back in Java's frame,
the right arm's x turn, 0.7 of the size and 1/16 aside.
`CopperGolemModel.translateToHand` (idle): the right arm, a quarter turn
about y and 2/16 forward; a statue keeps its pose. They were small boxes in
the entity pass. Only the held item's drawing changed (their models are
Track 7c's).

### 5. Fences' item geometry (BLD-05c)

A fence item is Java's `fence_inventory` model: two 4 × 16 × 4 posts at
z 0–4 and 12–16 and two 2 × 3 bars (rows 12–15 and 6–9) running from
z -2 to 18, each piece with its explicit UVs
(`content::item_models::element_quads`, which lays an explicit UV
rectangle over a face as `FaceBakery` does). Model coordinates are
floats, so the bars reach 2/16 beyond the block as Java's do. It was the
game's post with bars (only its direction matched).

### 6. Bamboo's four stalk models (UX-01)

Java's bamboo blockstate picks one of four stalk models per age at random
(`bamboo1_age0` to `bamboo4_age1`); each samples a different column of the
stalk texture: the sides columns 0–2, 3–5, 6–8 or 9–11 (two wide for a thin
stalk, three for a thick one), the top the square at (13, 0) and the
bottom the one at (13, 4). The choice is `ModelBlockRenderer`'s: its
random source seeded with `Mth.getSeed(x, y, z)`, one `nextLong` for the
multipart model, reseeded with it for the stalk part, `nextInt(4)`
([`offset::bamboo_model`](../../src/world/offset.rs), checked against a
separate Java program's output). The mesh stores the choice in a fine
quad's spare word 0 bits 29–30 (each chunk's mesh input now carries its
world origin), and the shader maps the stalk faces to that model's part of
the texture. The stalk's **original art is redrawn in Java's layout**:
four canes with their nodes at different rows, and the cut ends with a
pale pith. The leaves are unchanged.

### 7. Pointed dripstone drips from its offset tip

The game had no dripstone drip particles. A free-hanging pointed dripstone
tip (pointing down, a tip, not waterlogged) now runs
`PointedDripstoneBlock.animateTick` in the ambient particle pass: on 12%
of its ticks it looks for the fluid above the block its run of dripstone
hangs from (at most 11 up, `getFluidAboveStalactite`; mud is water
outside the Nether); a source of water or lava drips, and with no fluid
2% of ticks drip the dimension's default (lava in the Nether, water
elsewhere). The drop starts at the tip's **offset** centre
(`getOffset`, Java's speleothem offset of `world::offset`), 4/16 up the
cell (`STALACTITE_DRIP_START_PIXEL` less a pixel). Water drops are a new
particle kind (`WaterDrip`: Java's (0.2, 0.3, 1) hanging 40 ticks, then
falling and landing as a splash, gone in water); lava drops are the
existing cooling lava drip.

### 8. LAN guests' held stacks

A LAN player's lite state already carries the held stack's components
(`stack_appearance`, `stack_to_string`: enchantments, lodestone, name and
the rest), and since Track 6c the appearance draws the whole stack; the
"plain stack" in 6c's record no longer happens. A test now pins it: an
enchanted sword in another player's hand and off hand keeps its glint
across the lite state (`app::lan::tests::other_players_held_stacks_keep_their_glint`).

### 9. Chat as Java's: the debug line and fading

- **Fading**: each chat line fades as `ChatComponent.AlphaCalculator.timeBased`
  computes it from its 200-tick life: fully shown for 9 seconds, then over
  the last second as the square of what is left, text and background
  together (the background at `textBackgroundOpacity`'s 0.5), **in a LAN
  game as in a single-player one** (a LAN game showed its last four lines
  without fading).
- **Layout**: the unfocused chat's: up to 10 lines
  (`chatHeightUnfocused`'s 90 pixels of 9-pixel lines) from 40 pixels
  above the bottom, on a 332-pixel band, text 4 pixels in (it was four
  12-pixel lines 64 pixels up).
- **The debug prefix**: "[Debug]:" is Java's yellow (`ChatFormatting.YELLOW`,
  0xFFFF55) and bold (each glyph drawn again a pixel right, advanced a pixel
  more), the message after it white (it was all white).

## Inventory changes

What a player sees differently in inventory and container screens:

- **Fence icons** are Java's `fence_inventory`: two posts with two bars
  reaching a little past them, instead of one post with bars; every other
  slot icon is unchanged (slots already used Java's face UVs).
- Nothing else in a slot changes: the trident and spyglass keep their flat
  icons in slots (Java switches models only in hands), and the chat drawn
  over the hotbar is hidden while an inventory is open, as before.

Outside the inventory: east, north and bottom faces of every block show
their texture the right way round; doors look the same from both sides as
Java's do; held, dropped and framed blocks turn as Java turns them; heads
are skulls in every view; the trident and spyglass are 3D in hands; the
allay and the copper golem hold real, glinting items; bamboo stalks vary
along a grove; dripstone drips; chat lines fade.

## Remaining differences

- **Per-model UVs in the world**: the world mesh draws every face with
  Java's default UVs and doors with Java's mirrored face; the block models
  that give other faces explicit UVs keep the default mapping. A scan of
  the 26.3 models finds explicitly reversed or rotated UVs on, among
  others, glazed terracotta (each side face rotated a different quarter),
  trapdoors' and doors' thin edges (strips taken from other texture
  columns), glass panes' and bars' sides (`_alt`), the stonecutter,
  grindstone, anvil, campfire, lantern, chain, scaffolding, vine,
  tripwire, the hanging signs, `cube_mirrored` and
  `cube_north_west_mirrored` (sand and the like in Java's random
  rotations), stems and crops. The game's random model rotations (Java's
  random `y` turns of stone, grass, sand and others) are not drawn either.
- **The trident while thrown back** (`item/trident_throwing`, when the
  item is in use) keeps the in-hand transform; Java turns it point down.
- **A drowned's raised trident** uses the in-hand model too (Java's mob
  `using_item` is false unless it is charging).
- **The allay's grip** sits on the game's allay body, whose proportions
  and pivots are Track 7c's, not Java's; the golem's idle grip is used in
  every state (the game's golem has no interaction states). The held-item
  outline under Glowing doesn't include items drawn by the item pass.
- **Dripstone drips** play no landing sound (`DripstoneFallAndLandParticle`'s
  `POINTED_DRIPSTONE_DRIP_*`), and the water drop's splash is the drop
  turned into the game's splash particle rather than a new `SPLASH`.
- **Chat** keeps the game's font metrics (Java's 9-pixel line with the
  game's glyphs) and draws no scroll bar or tags; the chat's opacity, line
  spacing, height and width options are Java's defaults, not settings.
- **Mob in-hand items** use the adult offset for the allay and golem
  (neither has a baby form).
- The glint is Track 6's original pattern, not Java's texture.
- Bamboo's `stage` (when a stalk stops growing) is still the game's height
  roll (unchanged from 6c).
