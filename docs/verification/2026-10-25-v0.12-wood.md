## Delivered

### Logs, wood and stripped blocks

- The nine overworld logs and the bamboo block gain X and Z states; their
  existing ids keep meaning Y. Logs, `*_wood`, stripped logs, stripped wood
  and the block of stripped bamboo take the clicked face's axis
  (`RotatedPillarBlock.getStateForPlacement`); end textures follow the
  axis and side textures turn a quarter where Java's model turns the grain.
- An axe strips every log, wood and bamboo block in place, keeping the axis
  and wearing the axe one point (`block_transformer/axe.json`), through the
  shared `use_held` chain every input path uses.
- Properties: hardness 2.0, axe, fire 5/5, lava, drop themselves.
  `is_log` (Java's `#logs`) covers wood and stripped forms, so leaves near
  them don't decay, they smelt to charcoal, burn for 300 ticks and cocoa
  grows on all four jungle forms (`#jungle_logs`). The bamboo blocks are
  `#bamboo_blocks`: 300-tick fuel, 2 bamboo planks.
- Recipes: four logs make three wood and four stripped logs three stripped
  wood; planks take each wood's whole log tag; the smoker takes any.
- Fixed on the way: the bamboo block's fire odds were 5/20; Java's are 5/5.

### Trapdoors, buttons and pressure plates

Spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, mangrove and cherry trapdoors,
buttons and plates, and the polished blackstone button and plate. Like the
existing crimson, warped, pale oak and bamboo ones, each state is the twin
of an oak state (the polished blackstone ones of stone states), so every
redstone rule, mount (all twelve button mounts), waterlogged trapdoor,
placement and toggle applies unchanged, and `keep_wood` keeps the family.
Recipes: two trapdoors from six planks, a button from one, a plate from
two. Fuel: wooden buttons 100 ticks, plates and trapdoors 300; polished
blackstone none. Wooden switches (pale oak and bamboo included) now sound
like wood.

### Signs

Standing (16 rotations), wall, hanging (16 rotations, attached or not) and
wall hanging signs for the twelve woods, dry and waterlogged.

- Placement follows `StandingAndWallBlockItem`: the player's look order
  picks floor or wall (sign) and ceiling or wall (hanging sign), with
  Java's `canSurvive` rules (`isSolid` below or behind; centre support
  above; a wall hanging sign needs a sturdy face or another wall hanging
  sign along its plank, and never faces along the clicked face's axis). A
  hanging sign under another hanging sign of the same axis hangs straight;
  under a block without a full bottom face (or sneaking) it hangs from
  the middle. Standing, wall and hanging signs break when their support
  goes, and a sign resting on a sign breaks with it; hanging signs hold
  hanging signs (Java's `getBlockSupportShape` is their outline). A wall
  hanging sign checks its sides only when placed (`canPlace`) and stays
  when they go, as Java's inherited `canSurvive` does. Signs stack to 16.
- Outline shapes from the block classes (the wall sign's 4.5–12.5 is
  rounded out to whole pixels); no collision except a wall hanging sign's
  plank. Hardness 1.0, axe; drops the sign item; no fire odds; lava for all
  but crimson and warped. Fuel 200 ticks, hanging signs 800 (new
  `FuelClass::HangingSign`).
- Text: four lines per side in a block entity, saved under the level's
  `wood` property (hex-encoded lines). Right-click with an empty hand (or
  an item that does nothing) opens Java's edit screen for the side the
  player faces (`getSlotPlayerIsFacing`); placing a sign opens it for the
  front (`setPlacedBy`). Dyes recolour a side with text, glow ink lights it
  and ink sacs unlight it, spending one outside creative; a dye of the
  current colour falls through to the editor. The edit is accepted within
  block reach plus 4 (`playerIsTooFarAwayToEdit`).
- Recipes: three signs from six planks and a stick; six hanging signs from
  six stripped logs (stripped stems, stripped bamboo) and two chains.
- Drawing: sign bodies and text are drawn each frame for signs within 64
  blocks, outside the chunk meshes (Java's block entity view distance).
  Bodies use the model coordinates and planks (stripped logs for hanging
  signs), turned by the rotation; text uses Java's transforms (1/96 block
  per font pixel, 1.35/96 on hanging signs), line heights 10 and 9, widths
  90 and 60, dyed text at 40% brightness and lit by the world, glowing
  text at full colour and brightness with the dark outline within 16
  blocks (always for black).
- The edit screen is a menu-toolkit screen: four text fields as wide as
  the sign's lines, Up and Down to move between them, Done or Escape to
  keep the text. A dev flag, `--sign x,y,z,line|line|line|line`, writes a
  sign's front text for scripted screenshots.

### Shelves

Twelve shelves (Java 26.3 `ShelfBlock`), facing, powered, side chain and
waterlogged states.

- Placement faces the player; Java's back-board and shelf shapes for
  outline, collision and light occlusion (`useShapeForLightOcclusion`).
- Three slots; right-clicking a slot on the front face swaps the whole held
  stack with it (`swapSingleItem`, creative keeps a copy); an empty hand on
  an empty slot passes.
- Redstone power sets `powered`; powered shelves facing the same way chain
  left to right up to three (`SideChainPartBlock`), and unpowering
  unchains a shelf and its neighbours. A powered chain swaps its slots with
  the end of the hotbar (`swapHotbar`).
- Comparators read the filled-slot bits from behind the shelf.
- Hardness 2.0, axe, fire 30/20 (not crimson or warped), drops itself and
  spills its items, 300-tick fuel. Recipe: six stripped logs, two rows.
- Items show on the shelf as `ShelfRenderer` places them, a quarter block
  each.

### Chiseled bookshelf

Facing and six slot-occupied flags (256 states). Books and enchanted
books go into the empty slot under the click; an empty hand takes a book
out into the inventory (or drops it at the player). Comparators read the
last slot used plus one. Hardness 1.5, axe; drops itself only with silk
touch, and its books spill; 300-tick fuel; recipe: six planks around three
wooden slabs. The front shows each slot full or empty (64 front textures).

### Shared integration

Pistons can't move signs, shelves or chiseled bookshelves (block
entities). The block entities are created and spilled with their blocks
(`track_wood`), saved in the level file and reloaded. Every block is in the
creative palette and named for the registry audit.

## Differences from Java

- Sign text uses the UI's proportional 8×8 font, not Java's font, so line
  widths differ slightly; long lines are cut by character, not by Java's
  word splitting.
- Signs and shelf items are rebuilt each frame within 64 blocks; Java
  draws block entities to its view distance setting.
- Hanging sign chains are thin boxes, not Java's crossed planes, and side
  textures of sign posts are planks.
- Quarter-turned side textures only turn the grain; Java's UV rotation of
  each face differs in detail.
- Sounds reuse existing wood sounds (no shelf, chiseled bookshelf, dye or
  ink sounds), and shelves play nothing when powered.
- The sign editor pauses an unshared world like every menu (Java's
  doesn't), Enter doesn't move to the next line, and its board is a flat
  colour rather than the sign model.
- The edit screen isn't available to LAN clients or controller players
  (their uses report the editor and nothing opens), and LAN doesn't send
  sign text or shelf items to clients.
- Hoppers don't feed or empty shelves and chiseled bookshelves.
- Writable and written books, knowledge books (`#bookshelf_books`) don't
  exist yet; books and enchanted books do.
- Sign waxing waits for honeycomb (v0.15); the waxed flag is saved.
- Support checks run when a player or a falling block changes a block,
  not after redstone edits (the engine's existing rule for ladders,
  lanterns, torches and plants too): a hanging sign under a trapdoor that
  redstone opens stays until the next player edit beside it.
- Review fixes (2026-10-26): hanging signs chain, signs on signs break
  together, wall hanging signs survive losing a side, signs stack to 16,
  a hotbar swap on a powered shelf chain updates every shelf's
  comparators, and a held item is used when aimed at a shelf's or
  chiseled bookshelf's sides or back.
