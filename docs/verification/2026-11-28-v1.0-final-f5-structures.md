## 1. Mineshaft chest minecarts (closed)

Java: each corridor support rolls `nextInt(100) == 0` on its two sides
(local `(2, 0, z - 1)` and `(0, 0, z + 1)`); `createChest` then, when the
cell is air over a non-air block inside the chunk, places a rail whose
shape is `nextBoolean() ? NORTH_SOUTH : EAST_WEST` and adds a
`MinecartChest` at the cell's centre (`setInitialPos(x + 0.5, y + 0.5,
z + 0.5)`) with `setLootTable(abandoned_mineshaft, nextLong())`.

Generation 7 lays the rail of a random axis in that cell and records a
`MineshaftCart` feature in place of the chest
(`src/world/mineshaft.rs`); the world keeps the loaded carts still to
spawn (`World::mineshaft_carts`, derived from generation and never
saved), and the entities spawn each one once when its cell ticks
(`Entities::populate_mineshaft_carts`), at the rail's height, filled from
`abandoned_mineshaft`. The cart is an ordinary saved chest minecart; its
cell joins the saved generation receipts (`villager_births`, the same set
village residents use), so a broken or ridden-off cart never spawns again
when its unedited chunk regenerates. No save format changed.

Differences: the decision and the loot seed come from VoxelCraft's
per-cell hashes, as the rest of the corridor decoration does (GEN-05c's
"decoration randomness from VoxelCraft streams"); the contents are rolled
when the cart spawns, not when it is first opened (Java keeps
`LootTable` and `LootTableSeed` until then, so the opener's luck can't
change them here); the cart spawns on the rail rather than half a block
above it; the cell below must be solid, as for the older chests, where
Java takes any non-air block.

## 2. Stronghold biome search (closed)

Java's `generateRingPositions` forks the ring random for each position
(already consumed by generation 5's sequence) and runs
`findBiomeHorizontal(chunk centre, y 0, 112, #stronghold_biased_to,
fork)`: with `findClosest` false it walks only the outer radius, 28
quarts, as a full square (z outer, x inner, step 1, 57 × 57 cells),
keeping the first match without a draw and each later one when
`nextInt(found + 1) == 0`. The kept cell's block (`quart << 2`) gives the
chunk (`>> 4`); no match keeps the ring chunk.

Generation 7 does exactly that over VoxelCraft's biome at `(x, 0, z)`
(`Generator::biome_at`, so cave biomes count as Java's noise biome at y 0
does) with Java's 38-biome tag (`stronghold::BIASED_TO`, `sulfur_caves`
included) in `src/world/stronghold.rs`. `Strongholds::starts` still
returns the ring positions (the M3 corpus checks them against Java's
ring maths); `Strongholds::start` gives the moved corner. Each search
runs when its stronghold is first needed (painting, features, eyes of
ender and `/locate`), not for all 128 at world load; `nearest` resolves
only the ring positions that could still be nearest once moved.

Difference: VoxelCraft's biomes are its own (FE-01), so the moved chunks
are not Java's for the same seed; the algorithm, radius, tag and random
draws are.

## 3. Ancient cities and cave water (closed)

Java doesn't keep water out of cities through the template's
`liquid_settings` (the default `apply_waterlogging` only waterlogs
template blocks that already stand in water) or the beardifier
(`beard_box` lowers the density round the boxes before the aquifer runs,
and `updateShapeAtEdge` schedules ticks for fluids at a template's
edge, so water next to a piece would flow in). What keeps Java's cities
dry is the aquifer: `noise_settings/overworld.json`'s
`aquifers.exclusion` is `min(-0.225 - erosion, max(depth - 0.9, 0))`, and
`computeSurfaceLevel` gives every aquifer cell whose centre samples a
positive exclusion floodedness -1, so it holds no fluid. That region,
erosion below -0.225 at a depth parameter above 0.9, holds the deep dark
(erosion -1 to -0.375 at depth 1.1), and so every ancient city's
surroundings. The lava sea below y -54 is checked before that and stays.

Generation 7 applies the same rule to VoxelCraft's aquifers
(`caves::Aquifer::with_exclusion`, used by the density terrain's carver
in `src/world/terrain/density.rs`): an aquifer cell centred at least ten
blocks under the surface (shallower cells hold the sea, as Java's
`computeFluid` returns the global fluid first) whose climate's erosion is
below -0.225, more than `deep_dark::DRY_DEPTH` blocks deep, is dry
(`deep_dark::aquifer_excluded`). The depth is the deep dark's own
`MIN_DEPTH` (Java's 1.1) less 0.2 at Java's 128 blocks per unit.

Differences: VoxelCraft's terrain, climate and aquifer noise are its own
(FE-01), so the depth threshold is the conversion above rather than
Java's `depth` density function; the city still carves its margin after
the terrain (the older `beard_box` painter) rather than in the density.
Springs (`spring_water` features) are not part of this change.

## 4. Villages and outposts on steep ground (closed)

Java's `JigsawPlacement.tryPlacingChildren`: a rigid child of a rigid
parent keeps the parent's plane (`parentMinY + delta`); every other child
is placed at `getFirstFreeHeight(connector x, z, WORLD_SURFACE_WG) -
childJigsawY`, horizontal or vertical connector alike. Only the free-space
voxel shape rejects a candidate. The start's floor is projected to the
heightmap at its box's centre. `beard_thin` then adds the Beardifier's
kernel round each rigid piece and junction; Java adds no foundation.

Generation 7's villages, pillager outposts and abandoned camps assemble
with `jigsaw::assemble_adapted` (`Mode::Java` in `src/world/jigsaw.rs`):
Java's height rule above, with no generation 3 envelope and no combined
density check, so no piece or start is dropped for steep ground. They
paint through `jigsaw::NoFoundation`, which removes the fallback
foundation under rigid pieces. The existing `beard_thin` density term
still shapes the terrain. Generations 3 to 6 keep `assemble_enveloped`
and their foundations.

Differences: the templates are original (FE-02); the beard kernel uses
an exact square root rather than Java's fast inverse square root, as
before. On ground steeper than the kernel's reach a building now
overhangs or sinks into the hill as Java's does.

## Inventory changes

- GEN-05a: generation 7's surface jigsaws (villages, outposts, camps) use
  Java's `JigsawPlacement` child heights with no envelope and no fallback
  foundations; `beard_thin` alone adapts the ground. The 2026-11-09
  envelope note applies to generations 3 to 6 only.
- GEN-05b: generation 7 villages keep every building on steep ground (no
  piece dropped); the release notes' "on steep ground some villages and
  outposts are smaller or don't appear" no longer applies to new worlds.
- GEN-05c: remove "chest minecarts in mineshafts" and "the stronghold
  ring biome search" from Remaining: generation 7 places Java's chest
  minecarts (contents rolled at spawn) and moves ring positions by
  Java's `findBiomeHorizontal` over VoxelCraft's biomes.
- GEN-05e: generation 7 outposts as GEN-05a.
- GEN-05f: generation 7 abandoned camps as GEN-05a.
- Ancient cities (GEN-05, deep dark): generation 7 aquifers follow Java's
  `aquifers.exclusion`, so no cave water floods a city; the release notes'
  "water from a nearby cave can pour into an ancient city" no longer
  applies to new worlds.

## Remaining

- Mineshaft carts roll their loot at spawn rather than on first open.
- The city's terrain carving is still the older painter rather than
  `beard_box` in the density; springs near a city are untouched.
