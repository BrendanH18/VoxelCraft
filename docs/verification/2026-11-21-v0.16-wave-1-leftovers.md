## What is implemented

**Potent sulfur** (`world::potent_sulfur`). `validBlockState` on
placement and whenever the cell above or below changes (water flowing in
or out, a block placed or broken): dry without a water source above
(water, a waterlogged block or a bubble column), continuous over a lava
source, dormant over magma (an erupting block keeps erupting, and a block
becoming a geyser resets its countdown), wet otherwise. Generated or
loaded potent sulfur settles on its chunk's first tick (Java's template
and feature placement run `updateShape`). Each block has a block entity
kept by position and ticked in block-ticking chunks only when it isn't
dry (Java's `getTicker`):

- the gas source is the first open cell over at most four blocks of water
  (`findNoxiousGasSourceBlock`); wet and dormant blocks give Nausea for 4
  seconds (ambient, visible) every 10 game ticks to living players and mobs
  in the source's cell grown 2.5 sideways whose eyes are in an open cell
  within 3 blocks of it, just over a water source, with a clear line under
  the surface (`canBeReachedByNoxiousGas`);
- dormant and erupting blocks count down every 20 game ticks; a dormant
  one waits `10 × (water − 1) + 15..=30`, an erupting one
  `water − 1 + 1..=2` (drawn from `XoroshiroRandomSource(seed ^ -904011478)`
  forked at the block, as Java; checked against the jar's own draws), then
  flips; erupting starts play the eruption sound and post
  `block_activate`, ending posts `block_deactivate`; the countdown is
  saved (the dimension's `potent_sulfur` property);
- erupting and continuous blocks lift every tick: players (not flying, not
  riding), mobs (not riding), items and experience orbs whose box meets the
  passable column above (six cells per block of water,
  `getUnobstructedBlockCount`) and that rise slower than
  `0.3 + 0.1 × water` blocks a tick gain 0.2 a tick; a lifted player's fall
  is capped at one block (`checkFallDistanceAccumulation`);
- the client side: a yellow-green gas cloud at the source every 20 ticks,
  a plume of spray and steam every 20 ticks of an eruption (taller over
  deeper water) and its sound every 40, two sulfur bubbles in the water
  above and, one animate tick in ten, the noxious gas sound
  (`animateTick`).

Sounds are original synths (`audio::sulfur_cube::render_geyser`).

**Sulfur spikes** (`world::speleothem`, Java's shared `SpeleothemBlock`).
A held spike now takes its thickness from its neighbours when placed (tips
merge unless the player sneaks) and holds water placed into a source. When
the cell above or below a speleothem changes, its thickness is recomputed
(`calculateSpeleothemThickness`): a growing or broken column reshapes
from base to tip. This is the shared code path, so pointed dripstone
follows it too. Hanging spikes under sulfur grow on random ticks (0.011377778
a tick, `getMaxGrowthLength` 2, so `findTip` looks one block past the run's
top): an even split between a new tip below the free-hanging tip (or merged
tips where a stalagmite waits) and a stalagmite raised from the first floor
within ten blocks. A fast trident now breaks sulfur spikes as it breaks
dripstone (`onProjectileHit`). In 26.3 only `PointedDripstoneBlock`
overrides `fallOn` (the fixture lists the declaring classes), so landing on
a sulfur spike hurts no more than the fall, as in Java; dripstone's own
landing damage is a different code path and is left as it was.

**Sulfur springs** (`world::sulfur_springs`, generation 5). Java's
`rooted_sulfur_spring` placement: per Java chunk one or two tries at a
height from the bottom to 256; from air, an upward scan of at most 12
finds a solid ceiling and the cell under it is the origin, which must be
in the sulfur caves. The root system climbs from there (at most 184 cells,
never above the surface) to the first air cell with five more air above
it, a solid non-lava floor and level ground eight blocks out on four sides
(two below solid, two above open). There `sulfur_spring` picks a size by
weight (small 200, medium 90, large 20, extra large 5), scatters tuff over
the solid ground around (64, 80, 96 or 128 tries at trapezoid offsets of
±7..±10 and ±3, each scanning four down) and sets one of the size's
templates seven blocks down, centred and turned a random quarter
(`TemplateFeature`). The rock between the lower cave and the spring turns
to sulfur where it is `#azalea_root_replaceable` (20 tries a layer within
radius 3) and a sulfur block hangs from the lower cave's ceiling.
Springs are laid out against the pristine terrain and clipped into every
chunk they reach (like geodes), ahead of geodes, ores and Track D's pools
and spikes, as Java's lakes step comes first.

The ten templates are original and generated (FE-02) at Java's sizes
(8×11×9 to 16×11×16): a rounded body seven layers deep of tuff, granite,
stone, cinnabar and sulfur, a pool whose surface is level with the cave
floor, dormant potent sulfur over magma under its deepest water (two vents
in the extra large one), a sulfur rim with spikes of one to three, and
open air over the footprint. Each spring is a working geyser.

**The Wither** (`entity::wither`).

- Skulls in flight are saved with their position, motion, `dangerous` and
  owner (the level's `wither_skulls` property) and reload flying. They are
  no longer dropped after 1200 ticks: as in Java a skull flies until it
  hits, is discarded where no chunk is loaded and waits where entities
  don't tick.
- Goal selection follows `Mob.serverAiStep`: the target and goal
  selectors choose every other tick (`(tickCount + id) % 2 == 0`, and the
  first tick); running goals tick every tick. The target selector runs
  `HurtByTargetGoal` (priority 1) over `NearestAttackableTargetGoal` (2): a
  target is kept while it is alive, attackable and within 40 blocks; a
  newer hurt (within Java's 100-tick memory) by an attackable player or
  **mob** takes over unless the hurt goal already runs; otherwise the
  nearest visible non-undead target in range. Mob hits reach it through
  the shared mob-hit path (melee of nether mobs, polar bears, pandas,
  endermites and tamed animals, mob arrows) and iron golem and zombie
  villager blows; hits by `#wither_friends` and hits while charging are
  refused there, as `hurtServer` refuses them.
- `RangedAttackGoal` (2) and `WaterAvoidingRandomFlyingGoal` (5) share the
  `MOVE` flag: the attack runs while there is a target and flies a
  `FlyingPathNavigation` path (the flying node evaluator, reach 1) to the
  target's block until it is within 20 blocks and seen for 5 ticks, then
  stops; the path's next node is the `FlyingMoveControl`'s wanted spot.
  Without a target the flight goal starts one time in 60 selection ticks
  (`reducedTickDelay(120)`) with `HoverRandomPos.getPos(8, 7, view, π/2, 3, 1)`:
  ten tries toward a spot over solid ground ahead (a stable destination),
  raised one to three blocks above it (`moveUpToAboveSolid`), not in water
  and without a path malus, best walk value first; else
  `AirAndWaterRandomPos.getPos(8, 4, −2, view, π/2)`; it runs until the
  path is done. `aiStep`'s own steering toward the target is unchanged.

**Dispensers** (`world::automation`).

- A wither skeleton skull goes into the empty cell in front only where
  `WitherSkullBlock.canSpawnMob` holds (two above the world's bottom, not
  Peaceful, and a Wither's base `   `/`###`/`~#~` around the cell), as a
  floor skull turned the dispenser's way (`RotationSegment`), with
  `block_place`; the placement then checks the full pattern and builds the
  Wither as a player's would. Otherwise the skull stays in the dispenser
  (it is no longer dropped as an item).
- `SulfurCubeBlockDispenseItemBehavior`: a `#sulfur_cube_swallowable` item
  without a behaviour of its own (carved pumpkins keep theirs), and TNT
  under `tnt_explodes`, go into the first sulfur cube in the cell in front
  that takes them (`equipItem`: adults, not the block they hold); with no
  such cube they are dispensed or primed as before.

## Differences from Java

Potent sulfur:

- Particles reuse the engine's bubble, smoke and splash kinds with sulfur
  colours (no `sulfur_bubbles`, `noxious_gas_cloud` or geyser particle
  kinds); the plume's look is original.
- Lifts reach players, mobs, items and experience orbs; arrows, tridents,
  minecarts, boats, falling blocks and primed TNT aren't lifted.
  `canSimulateMovement` isn't modelled. Mob eyes are at 0.85 of their
  height for the gas test.
- Countdowns are saved by position in the dimension (Java keeps them in the
  block entity), and the plume's phase restarts from the load time as
  Java's `eruptionTick` does. The headless LAN server and LAN clients don't
  run the block entities (as with beacons): a client sees the states change
  and the animate-tick bubbles, not the plume or the geyser sounds.
- "Passable" means no collision in the engine's block table.

Sulfur spikes:

- `updateShape`'s skip while a falling stalactite's tick is scheduled
  isn't modelled (the engine drops unsupported stalactites at once).
- Pointed dripstone shares the thickness updates but doesn't grow (its
  growth needs water two above and the dripping rules; not built), and
  `PointedDripstoneBlock.fallOn`'s landing damage is still not built.

Sulfur springs:

- Templates are original and generated (FE-02); random draws are the
  engine's (FE-01), so a Java seed doesn't put springs where Java does.
- The placement is decided against the pristine terrain
  (`Generator::natural_block`: carving and fluids, no aquifers, lava lakes
  or sulfur bands), and the surface is the column's height or the sea; the
  roots are judged against each chunk's blocks when painted. A
  climate test skips columns that can never be sulfur caves before any
  terrain probe. The template's water doesn't schedule fluid ticks, and a
  template never replaces bedrock.

The Wither:

- Java's entity id is the engine's mob uid in the selection parity.
- `HurtByTargetGoal` sees mob hits that go through the shared mob-hit path
  or iron golem and zombie villager blows; other mob damage (snow golem
  snowballs, explosions a mob caused, thorns) doesn't retarget it.
  `LookAtPlayerGoal` and `RandomLookAroundGoal` still aren't modelled.
- `HoverRandomPos` treats a solid block as a stable destination
  (`entityCanStandOn`) and uses the path malus of the engine's flying
  evaluator.
- A skull flying out of the world (above the top or into an unloaded
  chunk) is discarded; Java keeps one above the build height.
- A dispenser that can't place a wither skeleton skull doesn't equip it on
  an entity in front (`EquipmentDispenseItemBehavior` isn't modelled for
  any item); it keeps the skull.
