The 26.3 client jar, from Mojang's version manifest onto a Boat sandbox

or unpickable), `getQuickCraftPlaceCount` (floor of count / slots, 1, the
stack limit), `canItemQuickReplace`, `mouseClicked` (an empty cursor acts
on press and skips the release; a held stack starts quick crafting) and

`doubleClick` flag and the same hovered slot, with no `lastClickTime`
reset after a placing release; the game keeps Java's 250 ms window.
`getHeldProjectile` looks at the off-hand, then the main hand.

## What changed

1. **Drag (`QUICK_CRAFT`)** — [`inventory/clicks.rs`](../../src/inventory/clicks.rs)
   holds the rules over any screen's slots in menu order
   ([`Cells`](../../src/inventory/clicks.rs)); [`app/clicks.rs`](../../src/app/clicks.rs)
   lists every screen's slots in Java's menu order (inventory, crafting
   table, furnaces, brewing stand, chests and vehicles, ender chest,
   enchanting table, anvil and grindstone, smithing table, stonecutter,
   loom, cartography table, beacon) with each slot's `mayPlace`,
   `mayPickup` and stack limit. Left drag shares the held stack out evenly
   (`count / slots`), right drag puts one in each, middle drag (creative
   only, `hasInfiniteMaterials`) fills each to a full stack without using
   the held stack; each slot is capped at its own limit and the cursor
   keeps the rest. A slot joins only when it is empty or holds the same
   item with room, takes the item, isn't a result, and (left and right)
   while the held count exceeds the slots taken. A one-slot drag is an
   ordinary click with that button. With a stack held, the click lands
   on release, as in Java; a press with an empty cursor still acts at
   once. While dragging, the slots show what they will hold over Java's
   white highlight and the cursor shows what will be left.
2. **Double click (`PICKUP_ALL`)** — a second left press on the same slot
   within 250 ms with a stack held gathers matching stacks onto the cursor
   in menu order, part stacks first, then full ones, never from results
   (furnace output is taken, paying its experience, as Java's
   `FurnaceResultSlot.onTake`). It does nothing while the slot still holds
   something it could pick up.
3. **Shift double-click** — every filled slot of the clicked slot's
   section (`slot.container`: the player's slots, armour and off-hand
   together; the crafting grid; the container) holding the stack the first
   shift-click moved is quick-moved in turn.
4. **`SWAP`** — number keys over any slot of any screen (no longer only
   storage and inventory slots) and **F over a slot** (button 40, the
   off-hand), with nothing held (`checkHotbarKeyPressed`), with Java's
   split when the slot holds less than the stack and the displaced stack
   added to the inventory or dropped; a result slot empties only into an
   empty key slot, running its take.
5. **`THROW`** — Q (Ctrl+Q) over any slot of any screen with nothing held;
   on a result slot it crafts and throws, repeatedly with Ctrl while the
   same item comes out.
6. **`CLONE`** — the middle button clones any slot's stack in creative on
   every screen, not only the creative screen; survival's middle button
   does nothing (it used to act as a left click).
7. **Input paths** — the host's mouse and keys; LAN guests send `drag KIND
   SLOT...`, `collect SLOT RIGHT`, `slot_swap SLOT KEY` and `slot_throw
   SLOT WHOLE` commands, run on the host's copy of their screen (their
   shift double-click sends one shift click per matching slot, as Java's
   client sends one `QUICK_MOVE` each); controller seats hold A (even
   share) or X (one each) with a stack held and move the cursor to drag
   (the click waits for the button's release, as the mouse's does), and
   double-press A (within Java's 250 ms) to gather and Y to move every
   matching stack. Java has no controller input; this is the mapping.
8. **Off-hand bows and crossbows** — `PlayerSession::start_offhand_draw`
   ([`ranged.rs`](../../src/session/ranged.rs)): when the main hand's use
   passed (it holds nothing that draws, eats, drinks, scopes, blocks or
   charges), a bow or crossbow in the off-hand draws, loads or fires,
   under Java's slot 40 (`inventory::OFFHAND`, which `Inventory::get`,
   `wear` and the new `slot_mut` accept). The draw goes on while the
   hotbar selection changes, as Java's used hand does. The host, seats,
   LAN guests (through the shared hands) and the CLI `draw` command use it.

9. **The off-hand item drawn in the hands** — first person
   ([`render/hand.rs`](../../src/render/hand.rs)): `Hand::off` carries the
   off-hand stack (from `map_hand`, so the host and every split-screen
   view), drawn in a second pass with the view frame mirrored across x
   (`renderArmWithItem` with `side = -1`) and the display transform
   mirrored about the model's centre (`ItemTransform.apply` with
   `leftHand`), so a sword or torch keeps its own handedness. Blocks, flat
   items and 3D item meshes (shield, banners, beds) all go through it; it
   isn't drawn while a map takes that side. Third person
   ([`player_model.rs`](../../src/entity/player_model.rs)):
   `PlayerAppearance::off_stack`, held in the left arm with Java's
   `ItemInHandLayer` offset of (-1, 2, -10) sixteenths and the mirrored
   third-person display transform, for the host's third-person views,
   split-screen seats and the host's view of LAN guests.

## Not closed (remaining differences)

- The off-hand's first-person item has no swing or equip animation of its
  own (Java's `offHandHeight` and the off-hand swing when it places or
  uses), no compass or clock dial frame, and an off-hand bow isn't drawn
  pulled back (first or third person; the arm pose follows the main hand).
- A LAN guest's view of other players shows their off-hand only if the
  host's player snapshot carries it (not checked).
- Controller seats have no middle (creative fill) drag, and no number,
  swap-hands or drop key over a slot.
- **CLI agents**: the CLI has no slot-click screen model (its `chest`,
  `furnace`, `bundle` and workstation commands move whole stacks), so the
  new click types aren't offered there.
- An off-hand bow takes arrows from the off-hand (never, it holds the
  bow) and then the inventory in order; Java looks at the main hand before
  the inventory.
- The drag preview's count isn't turned yellow at a slot's limit, and the
  cursor shows nothing (not a yellow "0") when a drag uses it all up.
- A LAN guest's drag isn't previewed (the host checks the slots).
- Merchant screens have no payment slots to drag into (trades are bought
  from their offer at once; existing behaviour).

## Inventory changes

- **UX-03c** (every container screen): drags, double-click collect, shift
  double-click, `SWAP` with the off-hand and any slot, `THROW` and `CLONE`
  on every screen, for the mouse, LAN guests and (double-press) controller
  seats. Closes the release-note limitation "You can't drag a stack across
  slots to share it out, or double-click to collect a stack."
- **The off-hand rows** (whichever rows own off-hand use and held-item
  drawing; UX-03 in this track's scope): the off-hand item is drawn in
  first and third person, and bows and crossbows draw from the off-hand.
  Closes the release-note limitation "The off-hand item isn't drawn in
  your hand, and bows can't be drawn from the off-hand", with the
  animation differences above left.
