## What is implemented

**Trade table.** `src/entity/villager_trades.rs` is rebuilt from the 26.3
default data: all 13 professions' five levels (in each level tag's order,
`#common_smith` expanded) and the wandering trader's `buying`, `uncommon`
and `common` sets, 391 entries in all, with Java ids, items by registry name,
counts, `max_uses`, villager XP, `reputation_discount`, the
`merchant_predicate` villager types and the result modifier. Before this,
the table was the older 1.14-era list: many offers were missing, and some
prices, uses and XP differed from 26.3's.

**Offer construction** (`TradeDef::offer`, Java `VillagerTrade.getOffer`):
an offer is not made when the villager's type fails the predicate, the
modifier discards the result or a cost falls below one. Modifiers:
`enchant_with_levels` (5–19 levels over `#on_traded_equipment`, the levels
added to the price, discarded unless enchanted); `enchant_randomly` for
librarian books (`#tradeable`, `2 + nextInt(5 + 10 × level) + 3 × level`
added to the price, doubled for `#double_trade_price`); exploration maps
(resolved when the merchant screen opens, as before); `set_random_dyes`
(`1 + binomial(2, 0.75)` random dyes mixed into leather); `set_potion`;
`set_random_potion` over the `#tradeable` potion tag (tipped arrows); and
`set_stew_effect` (the farmer's stew, one of six effects). Costs are
clamped to the item's stack size (`TradeCost.toItemCost`), so the
librarian asks one book and quill, not two.

**Drawing offers** (`addOffersFromItemListingsWithoutDuplicates`): each
level draws its trade set's `amount` (two, three for the master
librarian) from the tag, removing each tried entry and skipping those that
yield no offer. Offers append after the last one, as Java's list does.
The wandering trader draws two `buying`, two `uncommon` and five `common`
offers in `updateTrades` order (it used to offer five common and one rare
from a 1.21 list).

**Prices** (`Offer::price_with`, `special_price_diff`): the base price plus
`max(0, floor(base × demand × multiplier))` plus the special price
difference, clamped once to 1–stack size (the old code clamped twice, so a
discount on a price over 64 came off 64). The special difference is
`-floor(reputation × multiplier)` and, for Hero of the Village,
`-max(floor((0.3 + 0.0625 × amplifier) × base), 1)`; the game passes no
Hero yet ([hook below](#hooks-for-other-tracks)). Wandering traders have
none.

**Levelling.** 26.3's `rewardTradeXp` promotes at once when the trade's XP
reaches the level's threshold (0/10/70/150/250) and grants Regeneration for
200 ticks; the 40-tick `updateMerchantTimer` of earlier versions is gone,
so the game's timer is removed. Saves holding a pending promotion promote
on load.

**Restocking** (`restock`, Java `WorkAtPoi` and `shouldRestock`): while the
schedule says WORK, at most every 300 ticks and on a coin flip, a villager
within 1.73 blocks of its job site's centre works it. A new day (12000
ticks after the last restock, or a later day number) first runs
`catchUpDemand`: the restocks missed that day still update demand, which
is how unused offers get cheaper. Then a restock needs a used offer and
either no restock yet today, or fewer than two and more than 2400 ticks
since the last. The last restock is now saved as an absolute tick
(`last_restock`); older saves convert from the day and tick of day.

**Profession lock** (`ResetProfession`): losing the job site fires a
villager only with no XP and at level 1.

**Schedules** (`schedule_activity`): adults idle from tick 10, work from
2000, meet from 9000, idle from 11000 and rest from 12000 through midnight;
babies idle, play from 3000, idle from 6000, play from 10000 and rest from
12000. Work, rest, farmer harvesting and restocking follow the activity.
Breeding runs only in the idle activity, as `VillagerMakeLove` is only in
the idle package.

**Meeting points.** Adults claim the nearest bell within 48 blocks as their
meeting point (`PoiTypes.MEETING`: 32 villagers per bell), release it when
the bell goes, save it, and during MEET walk until within six blocks of it
(`getMeetPackage`'s `SetWalkTargetFromBlockMemory`).

**Gossip** (`villager_gossip.rs`, Java `GossipContainer`): minor positive
is capped at 25 (it was 200); adding uses Java's merge (a sum over the cap
keeps the larger of the cap and the old value) and drops values below two;
decay runs once 24000 ticks after the last (ticks spent unloaded no longer
multiply it); sharing follows `TradeWithVillager`: a villager within
√5 blocks of another hears up to ten weighted picks of its gossip, each
less its type's transfer decay, when neither gossiped in the last 1200
ticks, and both then wait. Curing adds major positive 20 and minor
positive 25 every time; the caps keep repeats from stacking.

**Golems** (`spawnGolemIfNeeded`): villagers that slept in the last 24000
ticks and haven't seen a golem count each other in the box grown by 10
blocks around the asking villager (it was a sphere); babies count. A
panicking villager checks every 100 ticks with three needed, and a
villager that just gossiped checks with five (it was a minute-wide check
over every villager).

**Cures** (`ZombieVillager`): `nextInt(2401) + 3600` ticks, Weakness
removed and Strength granted for the whole conversion, the special-block
scan unchanged (1% a tick, the 8×8×8 cube from −4 to +3, 14 blocks, 30% each),
and the cured villager has Nausea for 200 ticks.

**Hostiles.** Villagers flee zoglins at 10 blocks, the last
`VillagerHostilesSensor` entry Track A left to this track. Track A's
other distances and the wandering trader's avoid goals match Java (see
the [review](#review-of-track-as-flee-distances)).

**Wandering trader spawning** (`WanderingTraderSpawner`): the roll is
`nextInt(100) <= chance` (26, 51 or 76 percent; it was 25, 50, 75); the
player is drawn from living players only; with none, the chance resets
to 25 as Java's `spawn` reports success.

## Differences from Java

- **Random sequences.** Java draws each trade set from a world-wide random
  sequence named after it (`trade_set/<profession>/level_N`); the game
  seeds each villager's draw from its own seed, so a seed doesn't
  reproduce Java's offers.
- **Missing items.** An entry whose item the game lacks yields no offer
  and another is drawn, so the remaining offers come up more often. Six
  trade items are missing, each listed in `registry_gaps.json`: the
  wandering trader's golden dandelion, poplar log, poplar sapling, shelf
  mushroom and sulfur spike (v0.16), and the woodland mansion map (v0.15,
  Track F). Every other item of the 391 trades resolves, so the old
  table's filtered families (fish and buckets, campfires, banners, item
  frames, suspicious stew, glazed terracotta, tipped arrows) are offered.
- **Explorer maps.** The woodland mansion map (master cartographer) is
  Track F's; until it registers, that entry is redrawn. The trade-rebalance
  pack's extra maps aren't in 26.3's default trades.
- **POI acquisition.** Claims run once a second for the nearest free POI
  without Java's path check, the five-candidate batch or the jittered
  40–80 tick retry (`JitteredLinearRetry`); beds are not checked for
  `occupied`. Jobs and beds keep their single tickets and the 48-block range.
- **Brain.** The rest of the brain is not ported: strolling around the job
  site or bell, `SocializeAtBell`, `ShowTradesToPlayer`, `GiveGiftToHero`,
  babies' tag and bed-jumping play, and `InteractWith`. The hostile sensor
  has no line-of-sight check. Work checks run once a second rather than
  every tick, so a restock can come up to a second later.
- **Food.** Villagers pick up only food (bread, carrots, potatoes,
  beetroot); Java's `#villager_picks_up` also takes seeds, wheat and
  pitcher pods, and farmers keep seeds to plant.
- **Golem spawn search.** Ten tries within 8 blocks across and 6 up or
  down, as before, not `SpawnUtil`'s exact `LEGACY_IRON_GOLEM` placement.
- **Wandering trader.** The bell nearest the player is used where Java
  takes the first meeting POI `PoiManager.find` reaches; `setHomeTo(pos,
  16)` isn't modelled; the spawn wait counts 24000 ticks whole rather than
  in 1200-tick steps (the first attempt and the period are the same).
- **Gossip.** Weighted picks use the game's random generator, not the
  villager's Java random.
