## Scope

1. Nether portal blocks drawn as Java's thin plane along their `axis`
   (the `nether_portal_ns` and `nether_portal_ew` models), with Java's
   culling between portal blocks, its outline and collision shapes, the
   animated texture on both faces, and the portal's particles and sounds
   checked against Java.
2. A spectator standing in a portal with no exit retrying every 10 ticks.
   **Not done: it needs a scope decision** (see
   [below](#2-spectators-in-a-portal-scope-decision-needed)).
3. Night Vision's lightmap as Java 26.3's `lightmap.fsh` and
   `LightmapRenderStateExtractor`, with Java's fade, combined with Track
   1's Darkness pulse, for LAN guests and split screen.

## What changed

### 1. Nether portals as Java's thin plane

- **Model** (`src/mesh.rs`). A portal block meshes as Java's model: the two
  broad faces of a 4/16-thick box through the middle of its cell, along X
  (faces at z 6/16 and 10/16) for `axis=x` and along Z (x 6/16 and 10/16)
  for `axis=z`. The model has no edge faces and no `cullface`, so nothing is
  drawn between portal blocks or against the frame, and a neighbour never
  hides a face; the obsidian's faces toward the portal are drawn, as Java
  draws them beside a non-full shape. The faces go in the translucent pass
  (the texture's alpha puts the portal in Java's translucent layer), with
  back faces culled as Java's are. An older save's axis-less portal (state
  104) takes its axis from the blocks east and west of it
  (`portal::along_x`), as travel does.
- **Light.** `ModelBlockRenderer` uses ambient occlusion only for blocks
  that emit no light; the portal emits 11, so its faces are drawn flat with
  the light of its own cell (no AO), as the faces sit inside the cell.
- **Outline and collision** (`portal::plane`, `shape::outline_with`,
  `Block::is_targetable`). Java's `SHAPES` (`Block.column(4, 16, 0, 16)`
  turned by axis) is the outline: the crosshair now meets the portal at
  that plane (before, portals weren't targetable at all and the crosshair
  went through them to the frame or what was behind). As in Java, the
  plane stops the crosshair: looking through a portal targets the portal,
  in survival it can't be broken (strength −1, like bedrock), and in
  creative it breaks and takes its portal with it. It still has no
  collision, and the player is in it anywhere in the cell, as before
  (`getEntityInsideCollisionShape` is the full block).
- **Animated texture** (`chunk.wgsl`, `fs_translucent`). Java's
  `nether_portal.png` runs 32 frames of one tick each. The game has one
  original portal layer, and this track was allowed no new layers, so the
  translucent shader animates that layer on Java's clock: two copies
  scrolled diagonally opposite ways, a texel every second tick, averaged,
  repeating every 32 ticks; both faces show it. The art is the game's own;
  only the clock is Java's.
- **Particles.** Already Java's `animateTick`: four `portal` particles a
  display tick, on the side the neighbours east and west choose. Unchanged.
- **Sounds** (`src/audio/sounds.rs`, `Sound::Portal`). The game had none of
  the portal's three sounds: it played a low creeper fuse on stepping in and
  a pitched-down glass placing on every arrival. Now, as Java:
  - `block.portal.ambient` ("Portal whooshes"): `animateTick` one time in
    100, at the block's centre, `BLOCKS`, volume 0.5, pitch 0.8–1.2;
  - `block.portal.trigger` ("Portal noise intensifies"): when the portal's
    effect starts from nothing, local `AMBIENT`, volume 0.25, pitch 0.8–1.2;
  - `block.portal.travel` ("Portal noise fades"): on arriving through a
    Nether portal or the End portal into the End
    (`TeleportTransition.PLAY_PORTAL_SOUND`, level event 1032), local
    `AMBIENT`, volume 0.25, pitch 0.8–1.2. Leaving the End, respawning and
    gateways play none of it (they played the glass sound before).

  The three are new original synthesized sounds (a wavering low drone under
  swirling band noise: a whoosh, a rising swell and a falling one), appended
  to the sound bank's keys (no existing key moved), with Java's subtitles.
- **Capture tooling.** `--place x,y,z,fire` inside an obsidian frame now
  lights the portal, as Java's `BaseFireBlock.onPlace` does, so capture
  scenes can build one (placing portal blocks one by one breaks them).

### 2. Spectators in a portal: scope decision needed

The brief said Java keeps a spectator with no exit in the portal state and
retries every 10 ticks. The 26.3 jar says otherwise: a spectator never
enters the portal state at all. `Player.tick` sets `noPhysics` for
spectators, `Entity.isAffectedByBlocks` is `!noPhysics`, and so
`checkInsideBlocks` never calls `NetherPortalBlock.entityInside` (nor the
End portal's or gateway's) for a spectator. `getExitPortal`'s
`isSpectator()` check only matters for a player who became a spectator
while already counting down in a portal. Mojang's tracker agrees:
MC-46421, "Cannot use nether portals, end portals and end gateways while in
spectator mode", listed as affecting versions through 1.21.7.

So in Java a spectator can't travel through a portal, while VoxelCraft lets
spectators travel (Track 5b only stopped one with no exit portal). Matching
Java means taking an existing ability away from spectators, which is a
scope decision for the owner; this track left it alone. Either answer is
small: stop spectators entering portals (Java), or keep the game's
behaviour and record it as a difference.

### 3. Night Vision as Java 26.3's lightmap

`lightmap.fsh` builds each light level from
`max(AmbientColor, NightVisionColor · NightVisionFactor)` plus sky and
block light, then takes off Darkness's `DarknessScale`, clamps and applies
the Brightness. The game's older formula scaled the clamped light toward
full white, which lit unlit caves at full strength; Java's lifts them to
`#999999` (0.6) before the Brightness and leaves light already above that
alone.

- **Shaders** (`chunk.wgsl`, `entity.wgsl`, `block_model.wgsl`):
  `ambient_light()` is `max(g.ambient.rgb, 0.6 · g.effects.x)` and goes
  where the ambient colour went, in the Overworld's lightmap and in the
  Nether's and End's; `lightmap(raw)` is now just Darkness off the
  unclamped sum, then the Brightness (which clamps). With no night vision
  the result is exactly the old one (`clamp(raw) + max(raw − 1, 0) = raw`).
  Particles and rain take the same ambient term (the Overworld's particle
  and rain light keep their own approximations, with night vision raising
  their floor to the same 0.6 times its strength).
- **Strength** (`Effects::night_vision`, `Effects::lightmap_vision`). Java's
  `nightVisionScale`: 1, and over the last 200 ticks
  `0.7 + sin((duration − partialTick) · π · 0.2) · 0.3`, a flicker between
  0.4 and 1 every 10 ticks. The game's had an absolute value (0.7 to 1,
  twice as often) and used the game's clock instead of the partial tick.
  The lightmap takes it while Night Vision is on, else Conduit Power's
  underwater vision with the eyes in water (now the client's ramped
  `getWaterVision`, from 0 to 1 over 30 seconds, where the game used 1),
  else 0, as `LightmapRenderStateExtractor.extract` does. The fog's night
  vision brightening uses the same corrected scale (Java's `FogRenderer`
  uses `nightVisionScale` too).
- **With Darkness.** Both effects now come straight from `lightmap.fsh`:
  night vision raises the ambient term, Darkness's pulse comes off the sum
  before the clamp, and Darkness's blend lowers the Brightness. A test runs
  them together against Java's order.
- **Split screen and LAN guests.** Each view's strength comes from
  `App::atmosphere` (the main view and every split view, each with its own
  player's effects, partial tick and underwater time). A LAN guest's
  effects are the host's copy, refreshed by snapshot; the strength needs
  only the remaining ticks, so a guest sees the same flicker.

## Remaining differences and open points

- **Spectators and portals** need the owner's decision (above).
- **The texture animation** is the game's own art moved on Java's clock,
  not Java's 32 drawn frames; the layer count was fixed for this track.
  Its UVs follow the game's planar mapping (u along +Z on the X faces),
  like every other block, where Java's model maps each face's u from its
  own left edge, so the east face is mirrored relative to Java's. The
  portal art is symmetric enough that this doesn't show.
- **Face shading** is the game's directional shade for every block (0.8 on
  X faces, 0.68 on Z faces), not Java's (0.6 and 0.8).
- **The sounds** are original recipes no one has listened to yet, like the
  rest of the bank. A LAN guest hears the ambient whoosh from its own
  particle pass; portal travel stays host-controlled for guests (unchanged),
  so a guest hears no trigger or travel sound.
- **Particles and rain in the Overworld** keep their own light
  approximations (the ambient's 0.04 floor), now raised by night vision
  the same way; Java samples the full lightmap for them.
- **Java's lightmap updates once a tick**; the game computes it each frame
  (under a tick of easing, as Track 1 noted for Darkness).
- **Not run:** the game on the Mac, scripted scenes, frame-time suites.
  Nobody has looked at a portal or Night Vision in a running game; the
  captures are software renders on Boat (appearance only).
