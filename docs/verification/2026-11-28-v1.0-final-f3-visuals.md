## What changed

### 1. Random block model variants (UX-01)

A blockstate entry listing several models is a `WeightedVariants`:
`ModelBlockRenderer.tesselateBlock` seeds a `SingleThreadedRandomSource`
(Java's 48-bit LCG) with `BlockState.getSeed(pos)`, which is
`Mth.getSeed(pos)` for every block here, and `WeightedList.getRandomOrThrow`
takes `nextInt(totalWeight)`. A scan of all 1,288 blockstates in the jar
finds these entries with several models:

| Group | Blocks (Java's entry) | Variants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turned | `grass_block[snowy=false]`, `dirt`, `sand`, `red_sand`, `mycelium[snowy=false]`, `podzol[snowy=false]`, `rooted_dirt`, the 16 `*_concrete_powder` | the model turned `y` 0, 90, 180, 270 |
| Mirrored | `stone`, `infested_stone`, `bedrock`, `sculk`, `deepslate[axis=y]`, `infested_deepslate[axis=y]` | the model, `*_mirrored` (`cube_mirrored_all`: every face's UVs `[16, 0, 0, 16]`), each turned `y` 0 and 180 |
| Netherrack | `netherrack` | `x` 0 to 270 for each `y` 0 to 270 (16) |

All weigh 1, so the pick is the top two (or four) bits of the LCG's first
step. **Gravel has a single model in 26.3** and isn't turned. For each
variant `tools/extract_java_block_variants.py` emulates `FaceBakery` (the
Track 6e tool's `quads`) and writes each world face's UV symmetry against
the planar default into `tests/fixtures/java/26.3/block_variants.json`.
The textures don't change; only some faces turn or mirror (a grass
block's sides stay, its top and dirt bottom turn).

**Mesh and shader** (still 12 bytes a quad, no extra quads): the mesher
puts the block's group (`mesh::variants::group`) in the merge key's four
spare bits above the 12-bit layer, so faces of different groups never
merge but same-group faces still do, and stores it in word 1 bits 25-26
of a normal quad (free until now). The chunk instance's world origin gains
its y (`Sint32x3`). The fragment shader (`fs_opaque` through
`sample_opaque`) finds its cell from the chunk-local position and the
face, runs `Mth.getSeed` and the LCG step in 64-bit arithmetic built from
`u32` pairs (`java_variant`), looks up the variant's symmetry for the face
(`variant_symmetry`, the three groups packed 3 bits a face) and turns the
planar UVs and their screen gradients alike (`textureSampleGrad`, so the
mip level stays smooth across cell edges). Opaque normal quads only;
detail, cross and fine quads carry no group.

### 2. Fire's two textures (UX-01, BLD-05c)

`blockstates/fire.json` and `soul_fire.json` are multipart: the floor is
`fire_floor0` (`fire_0`) or `fire_floor1` (`fire_1`), the sides
`fire_side0`, `_side1`, `_side_alt0`, `_side_alt1`. `MultiPartModel.collectParts`
draws one `nextLong` from the position's random and reseeds with it before
every part, so all of a block's parts pick from the same state. The game
draws fire as a crossed pair of planes with no connection states (Java's
floor case), so each fire and soul fire block now takes **Java's floor
pick**: `fire_1` when `nextInt(2)` after the reseed is 1
(`chunk.wgsl`'s `java_fire_floor`, computed per vertex for fire crosses).

`fire_1` is original art in the style of `fire_0`
([`fire_1_textures.rs`](../../src/render/fire_1_textures.rs)): narrower
single-column tongues leaning the other way, reaching higher, with more
embers in the upper half, seven frames at ten a second like `fire_0`; soul
fire's second texture shares its tongues in soul fire's ramp. The track
has four texture layers, so the seven frames are **packed**, one a colour
channel (`tex::FIRE_1` 4088-4090, alpha 255 so mip levels average
plainly): 0 where see-through, else `128 + heat * 127`; the chunk and
entity shaders decode the sRGB byte and colour it (`fire_1_color`).

A burning entity's flame (`FlameFeatureRenderer`) now alternates `fire_0`
and `fire_1` quad by quad (material 7, the packed layer and channel); it
drew `fire_0` three frames on for the `fire_1` quads before.

### 3. Bells swing (BLD-05c)

`BellBlockEntity.onHit(direction)` / `triggerEvent(1, direction)` start a
shake of `DURATION` 50 ticks; `BellRenderer` passes `ticks + partialTicks`
and the direction to `BellModel.setupAnim`, which turns `bell_body` (pivot
(8, 12, 8)) by `sin(t / pi) / (4 + t / 3)` radians: `xRot` minus that for a
north strike and plus for south, `zRot` minus for east and plus for west
(`ModelPart`'s `rotationZYX`). In the game (`world::bell`):

- a ring from any source starts the shake from the bell's facing (Java's
  `attemptToRing` with no direction): VoxelCraft's single bell state is
  Java's `bell_floor` model unturned, `facing=north`. A player's use then
  restarts it from the face struck (`hitResult.getDirection()`); ringing
  again restarts the count, as `onHit` does;
- `World::tick_bells` counts the ticks (in the simulation tick, after the
  lids) and ends the shake at 50;
- a shaking bell is left out of its chunk mesh and drawn as a block-entity
  model with its body and lip swung (`BlockModel::swing`), as chest lids
  are; entering and leaving remeshes its chunk once;
- LAN guests receive shaking bells in the snapshot (`bells`: position,
  ticks, direction), as they receive lids.

## Inventory changes

Nothing in an inventory or container slot changes: Java's item models are
the unturned block models, so stone, grass, sand, netherrack and the rest
keep their icons; fire and bells have no item drawn from these models (the
bell's item is its sprite). Outside the inventory: the blocks above show
their tops (and for stone, bedrock, sculk, deepslate and netherrack their
sides) turned or mirrored per block as Java's do; fire and soul fire blocks
show one of two textures by position; flames on burning entities alternate
both; rung bells swing.

## Remaining differences

- **Other random variants** are not drawn: `lily_pad`, `sea_pickle` and
  `turtle_egg` (`y` turns of detail models), `dirt_path` (a 15/16 detail
  block turned `y`), `chorus_plant`'s weighted `noside` models (weights 2,
  1, 1, 1, the only non-power-of-two total), and `deepslate`'s `axis=x` and
  `z` variants (the game has no deepslate on its side; Java's `axis=x` list
  repeats `x90 y90` twice, recorded in the fixture).
- **Falling blocks** (`FallingBlockRenderer` seeds by the start position)
  and block-breaking particles keep the unturned model.
- **Fire's geometry** is the game's crossed planes, not Java's four tilted
  floor planes and side planes; the side models' pick (`fire_side1`,
  `_alt0`, `_alt1`, which mirror) and `fire_up*` aren't drawn. The screen
  overlay when burning keeps `fire_0` (Java's `ScreenEffectRenderer` draws
  `fire_1`).
- **fire_1 and soul_fire_1 share tongues** (four layers can't hold 14
  frames); each is its own colour ramp. `fire_1` has no distinct mip art:
  its mip levels average the packed heat, which rounds the far tongues
  slightly differently from `fire_0`'s alpha-weighted mips.
- **The bell's body** is the game's existing boxes (cup 5-11 × 3-12, lip
  4-12 × 2-4), not `BellModel`'s (6 × 7 × 6 at 6-13, lip 8 × 2 × 8 at
  4-6), swung about Java's pivot (8, 12, 8); its faces keep the shade of
  their unturned direction. The game's bell has no facing or wall/ceiling
  attachments, so a redstone or villager ring always swings as a north
  strike. Projectiles don't ring bells in the game.
