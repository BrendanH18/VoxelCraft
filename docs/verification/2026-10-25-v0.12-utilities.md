## Design

`src/world/utilities.rs` owns the range and is the registry the shared
tables call (`block.rs` hooks for name/kind/textures, `shaped`, `base`,
`drop`, mining, palette, `can_stay_on`, emission; `shape.rs`,
`waterlog.rs`, `mesh.rs`, `mining.rs`, `growth.rs`, `redstone.rs`).
Behaviour is in submodules: `campfire`, `cauldron`, `chests`, `dripleaf`,
`dripstone`, `anchor`, `interact` (every block use, shared by all input
paths through `session::interact`) and `ticks`. Per-dimension block-entity
state (campfire slots, a decorated pot's stack, pending drips, trapped
chest viewers, tilt timers, landed stalactites) is `utilities::State`,
saved under the level's `utilities` property. The ender chest is a
27-slot inventory on `PlayerSession`, saved as the player's `ender_chest`
property.

The decorations are entities in `src/entity/decor.rs`, kept in
`Entities::decor`, stored per chunk with the other entities and saved
under the dimension's `decor` property. Placement, use and attacks join
the shared session chains (`session::interact`, `session::combat`), so the
desktop, controller and CLI-agent paths get them. A framed item is drawn
with the free-standing block model renderer (`BlockModel::orient`), the
frames, paintings and stands as entity cuboids.

## Delivered behaviour

- **Cauldrons**: buckets of water, lava and powder snow fill any cauldron
  and a full one fills a bucket; water bottles add and glass bottles take a
  level. Rain fills empty or water cauldrons and snow powder snow ones
  (`handlePrecipitation` chances). A stalactite under a water or lava
  source drips into the first cauldron below its tip (17.6% / 5.9% a random
  tick, arriving 50 + fall ticks later); mud above dries into clay.
  Content heights follow `getContentHeight`; lava cauldrons give light 15.
- **Campfires**: four slots cook Java's nine campfire recipes in 600 ticks,
  cooling by two a tick when out; food spills when the fire breaks. Lit
  campfires hurt 1 (soul 2) without setting the player alight, give light
  15 (soul 10), smoke (11% of ticks) and become signal fires over hay.
  Flint and steel or fire charges light a dry one; shovels and water put it
  out. Drops: two charcoal (soul: soul soil), the block with silk touch.
- **Flower pots** take every pottable plant that exists and give it back;
  pick-block gives the plant. **Decorated pots** (no sherds) take one
  stack, wobble otherwise, shatter into four bricks when broken with a
  tool (not silk touch) and drop themselves by hand.
- **Coral wall fans** place from the fan item on a sturdy wall face (Java's
  nearest-looking-direction order), fall off without their wall, and die
  60–99 ticks after losing water, like every coral.
- **Falling stalactites**: pointed dripstone left hanging from nothing
  falls as falling blocks from the unsupported block to the tip, breaks
  into items where it lands, and the tip hurts what it lands on by
  `min(floor(ceil(distance - 1) × max(length, 6)), 40)` (players through
  armor, and mobs).
- **Big dripleaf tilting**: standing on a level leaf makes it unstable,
  then partial after 10 ticks, full after 10 more (no collision) and level
  again 100 ticks later; redstone power holds it level.
- **Trapped chests** store like chests and give their viewer count as
  redstone power (strong into the block below). **Ender chests** open the
  player's own ender inventory (shut under a conductor), drop eight
  obsidian (themselves with silk touch) and give light 7.
- **Lodestones** place and mine (3.5, pickaxe). **Respawn anchors** take
  glowstone (up to 4 charges, light 0/3/7/11/15). Used in the Nether, a
  charged anchor becomes the respawn point, replacing a bed; respawning
  there uses a charge and stands the player at Java's first free offset
  (`RESPAWN_OFFSETS`); an empty, missing or boxed-in anchor sends them to
  the world spawn. Used anywhere else, a charged anchor explodes (power 5,
  fiery).
- **Item frames and glow item frames** hang on any face of a solid block
  (side faces of repeaters and comparators too), take one of the held
  item, turn it an eighth per click, pop it out on a hit and break on the
  next, dropping themselves; two frames can't overlap facing the same way.
  The glow frame and its item are drawn fully lit. They drop when their
  wall goes (checked every 100 ticks).
- **Paintings** hang on walls only; the item picks, at random, one of the
  largest placeable variants that fits (solid wall behind every block,
  nothing in the way, no overlapping painting).
- **Armor stands** stand against any face but the underside, facing the
  player in 45° steps, where nothing (block, mob, player or decoration) is
  in the way. Armor and carved pumpkins go on (and come off, by the height
  clicked); other items find no arms. Two hits within five ticks break it,
  dropping the stand and its armor; creative breaks at once without drops.
  Stands fall under gravity. Explosions break decorations (stands drop
  only their armor).
- **Recipes**: every Java 26.3 recipe producing these blocks and items,
  including `#logs` for campfires and `#wool` for paintings. The lodestone
  and armor stand use the stone track's chiseled stone bricks and the
  smooth stone slab.

## Differences from Java and not done

- **Dye washing** in water cauldrons is not done: dyed leather armour and
  banners don't exist in this tree (banners are the colour track's).
- **Lodestone compasses** are not done: the compass has no target data.
- Stalactites aren't broken by projectiles (tridents don't exist), and
  falling stalactites only hurt players and mobs.
- Trapped chests are single only, like plain chests; comparators don't
  read item frames.
- Item frames don't show maps; the framed item turns in eighths but is
  drawn as a flat icon or small block, not Java's item model. Paintings
  are drawn as four tiles a block.
- Armor stands have no arms, poses, small or invisible variants and no
  hand items; their armor is drawn as coloured shells.
- The new entities aren't sent to LAN clients (multiplayer is maintenance
  only); CLI agents can place and use them through the shared chains but
  can't set an anchor respawn (their respawn path is the overworld one).
- CLI, LAN and controller players (agents) take lava and contact-fire
  damage without armor, unlike the host (an existing difference in
  `Agent::tick_rules`, not this track's; stalactite damage now goes
  through armor for them). Java's `damages_helmet` for falling
  stalactites isn't modelled for anyone.
- Ender chests open only for the keyboard player: controller players get
  a message and CLI agents an error, because neither has an ender chest
  screen or protocol yet (as with the wood track's sign editor).
- Review fixes (2026-10-26): anchors any player uses outside the Nether
  explode (the host drains a world queue each tick); big dripleaves tilt
  under the host and agents in play; pending drips, tilting dripleaves and
  a stalactite falling through a save are saved (falling entries gain
  `hurt,from_y`, and five-field entries still load); an open ender chest
  is never saved as a chest; `/spawnpoint` clears the anchor; players are
  hit by a stalactite that lands in their cell (taken as it lands, applied on their next tick through armor; mobs in the same tick); the headless LAN server does the same and explodes anchors (by code reading: `tests/lan_loopback.rs` drives that harness, but not its stalactite or anchor paths yet; it simulates no dropped items or TNT, so it discards both); one unreadable armor slot
  no longer drops the whole stand; pistons can't move ender chests or
  campfires (block entities) and break flower pots and decorated pots.
- Sounds use the nearest existing procedural sounds; there are no
  dedicated frame, painting or anchor sounds.
