## IDs allocated

Block states 22528–23372 of the track's 22528–24575 (845 laid out, 732
registered); 23373–24575 stay free. No item IDs: every copper block is a
block item (`Item::from_block`), and the copper nugget and ingot already
existed. Texture layers 2128–2200 of 2128–2383, as offsets from

The stairs and slabs are `slab_stair_states` families 53–56 (one per
stage, `copper::STAIR_FAMILY` = 39 legacy + 14 stone families). That
module supports appended families laid out contiguously in their owner's
range (`LEGACY_COUNT`, `FAMILY_COUNT = 57`, under the 6-bit limit of 64);
its own range and the 39 legacy families are unchanged.

Following the existing door and trapdoor families: a door's hinge lives on
the lower half (the upper half stores facing and open) and `powered` is the
saved redstone edge set, so a door has 24 states per stage instead of
Java's 32. Copper trapdoors also keep `powered` in the edge set (16 dry
and 16 wet). The reserved double-chest and powered-rod locals are left
unregistered so they can be added without renumbering.

## Delivered behaviour

- **Registry**: Java's display names (`exposed cut copper stairs`,
  `lightning rod`, …); the wall torch states are named after Java's block
  id (`copper wall torch`) and drop and pick the torch. `registry_audit`
  lists no copper block of this track.
- **Mining** (`Blocks` properties, `mineable/pickaxe`, `needs_stone_tool`):
  hardness 3, pickaxe, stone tier for full blocks, cut, chiseled, stairs,
  slabs, grates, bulbs, trapdoors, chests and rods; doors 3, pickaxe, any
  tool drops; bars and chains 5, any pickaxe; lanterns 3.5, any tool drops;
  torches instant. Every state drops its item; a door's upper half drops
  nothing and a double slab two slabs.
- **Light**: lit bulbs 15/12/8/4 by stage, lanterns 15, torches 14. Dry
  grates pass light and skylight like glass; wet ones dampen by 1.
- **Waterlogging**: grates, stairs, slabs (not double), trapdoors, bars,
  chains, lanterns, chests and rods have dry/wet pairs through
  `waterlog::pair`.
- **Oxidation** (`ChangeOverTimeBlock.changeOverTime`): on a random tick, a
  0.05688889 chance to try; abort if any aging copper within Manhattan
  distance 4 is younger; otherwise age with chance
  `((older + 1) / (older + same + 1))²`, × 0.75 while unaffected. Every
  property carries over (`withPropertiesOf`). Only a door's lower half
  ticks, and its upper half follows (`DoorBlock.updateShape`). The block of
  copper (231) now ages too; torches don't.
- **Scraping** (`block_transformer/axe.json`, `scrape` entries): using an
  axe on copper (sneaking, for doors, trapdoors and chests, as Java's block
  use comes first) moves it one stage back keeping its properties, both
  door halves, costs one durability and shows teal particles
  (`Used::Scraped`). Unaffected copper isn't scraped.
- **Bulbs** (`CopperBulbBlock.checkAndFlip`): each change of neighbour
  signal is stored as POWERED; a rising edge toggles LIT. Comparators read
  15 when lit. Bulbs aren't redstone conductors.
- **Doors and trapdoors**: copper doors open by hand and by redstone
  (`BlockSetType.COPPER` opens by hand), hinge like other doors and keep the
  hinge. Copper trapdoors act as oak trapdoors for use, redstone, placement
  and shapes, on their own copper (`copper::trapdoor_reskin`, hooked into
  `keep_wood`).
- **Placement**: lanterns hang or stand by Java's nearest vertical look
  direction; torches stand or go on the first sturdy wall in look order
  (`StandingAndWallBlockItem`); lightning rods point out of the clicked
  face; chains follow the clicked axis; chests face the placer; stairs and
  slabs use the shared half/shape/double rules. Hanging lanterns and wall
  torches break when their support goes; standing ones when their floor
  goes.
- **Shapes**: lanterns as `LanternBlock`, lightning rods drawn as
  `template_lightning_rod` (rod and tip) and colliding as `RodBlock`'s 4×4
  column, chains as `ChainBlock`, bars joining bars, panes and walls,
  chests colliding like chests.
- **Chests**: 27-slot chests; contents stay as the chest ages or is
  scraped and spill when it breaks.
- **Recipes** (crafting JSON): per stage cut copper ×4, stairs ×4, slab ×6,
  chiseled ×1, grate ×4, bulb ×4; unaffected only door ×3, trapdoor, bars
  ×16, chain, lantern, chest, lightning rod and torch ×4 (coal or charcoal).
  The 32 unwaxed stonecutting recipes are data in
  `crafting::copper_stonecutting()` for the v0.17 screen. Java has no
  smelting for these blocks.
- **Creative palette**: every family's item at every stage, once.
- **Textures**: original procedural art (`render/copper_textures.rs`), four
  stages from bright copper through brown and green patina to teal.

## Differences from Java and not done

- **Double copper chests** are not implemented: VoxelCraft has no chest
  pairs for any chest yet. Copper chests are single; the `type` states are
  reserved. Copper golem interaction and statues wait for v0.15.
- **Lightning** (ENV-03, v0.15): rods don't attract strikes, give a
  redstone pulse or strip oxidation; their powered states are reserved.
- **Waxed** copper and honeycomb waxing wait for bees (v0.15).
- Copper chests age even while open (Java waits until nobody has one open).
- Sounds use the nearest shared materials (stone for copper, wood for the
  torch; scraping a high stone step); doors and trapdoors use the wooden
  door sound. Java has dedicated copper, bulb, grate, door and chest sounds.
- A lone copper bars block draws only its post caps, as lone iron bars
  already do (shared pane renderer; see Checks).
- The wall torch is drawn upright (Java's model tilts 22.5°), and its
  outline is 6 wide (Java's is 5).
- Placement is blocked by the player's whole cell for rods, chains and
  lanterns, as for other shaped blocks (Java tests the collision shape).
- Explosions remove copper like other ordinary blocks (VoxelCraft has no
  per-block blast resistance).
- Mob pathing treats copper doors as the existing door rules do; not
  checked against Java's `mob_interactable_doors`.
- Copper chests, like wooden chests here, are drawn as full cubes. Neither
  conducts redstone, as in Java (fixed in review: wooden chests conducted
  before v0.12).
- Copper chests can age while a player has them open. Java's
  `WeatheringCopperChestBlock.randomTick` skips a chest with viewers, but
  VoxelCraft tracks no container viewers yet; that comes with chest lid
  animation. The contents are kept either way.
- Review fixes (2026-10-26): oxidising or scraping a powered door or
  trapdoor keeps its powered state, pistons break copper lanterns and wall
  torches, redstone dust climbs past blocks that don't conduct (copper
  bulbs, chests), and chunks stored as all-copper still age.

Unverified: ordinary play by the owner, frame time in a scene full of
copper on the M5, and the cost of texture paging now that layers pass 2048.
