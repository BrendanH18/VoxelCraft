## What is implemented

**Stonecutter.** The screen (`Container::Stonecutter`) lists the input's
recipes in Java's order: recipe id order, which `RecipeManager` registers
from the sorted data listing and `StonecutterMenu` filters by input
(`crafting::stonecutting::menu`; every 26.3 id is
`<result>_from_<input>_stonecutting`). Four columns, three rows visible,
scrolled with the wheel. The input takes anything (Java's plain slot); the
choice resets when the input item changes; taking the result uses one input
and keeps the choice; shift-click on the result crafts into the inventory
again and again while input lasts, dropping what doesn't fit; shift-click
from the inventory sends cuttable items to the input.

**Loom and banner patterns.** Banner, dye and pattern-item slots with
`LoomMenu`'s rules: no pattern item offers the 32 `#no_item_required`
patterns in tag order, a pattern item offers its one pattern (chosen at
once), the choice survives a list change only while still offered, a
banner with six layers takes no more, and taking the result uses one banner
and one dye (not the pattern item). All 43 patterns have original masks
(`content::banner_patterns::mask`, two texture layers each). Patterns are
the stack's `banner` payload and, placed, the banner's block entity
(`world::workstations`, with its custom name); they draw on standing and
wall banners (`banner::pattern_quads`, each layer tinted and lifted over
both faces of the flag), on banner and shield icons, in the loom's list and
its preview. Breaking a banner drops it with its patterns and name. The ten
pattern items (ids 1619–1628) have their rarities, original icons, recipes
(paper and the charge, five of six: see below) and loot (piglin from
`bastion_other`, guster and flow from trial chamber vaults; those tables
already named them). The cartographer's globe pattern trade now resolves.
`BannerDuplicateRecipe` and `ShieldDecorationRecipe` work in the grid (the
patterned banner stays as a remainder). A water cauldron washes the top
layer off a banner (`bannerInteraction`: one level, Creative keeps the
original). `banner_patterns::ominous_banner()` builds the raid banner: white,
eight layers, its own name and uncommon rarity, saved as an `ominous` flag
in the banner payload, and it survives placing and breaking.

**Cartography table.** Map and additional slots (`mayPlace`: any map with an
id; paper, an empty map or a glass pane). An empty map makes two copies;
paper zooms an unlocked filled map out one scale up to 4; a glass pane
locks an unlocked map. Zooming and locking happen when the result is taken
(`MapItem.onCraftedBy` with the `map_post_processing` component, carried in
the stack's `entity_data`): a new map id with `MapItemSavedData.scaled()` (a
blank map snapped to the wider grid) or `locked()` (the image and markers
kept, frozen). Locked maps stop updating (`MapData::update`), and the lock
is saved. `MapExtendingRecipe` (eight paper around a filled map) works in
the crafting grid, which now reads saved maps (`Grid::result_in`).

**Books and the lectern.** The book and quill (1557) opens an editor on use:
pages of up to 1024 characters and 14 lines of 114 pixels, up to 100 pages,
Page Up/Down and the page buttons (a new page past the last), Done (empty
trailing pages erased, as `saveChanges`) and Sign (title box of 15
characters; Sign and Close needs a non-blank title). Escape leaves without
saving, as Java's screen does. Signing makes a written book (1558,
stacks to 16, glint) titled and authored, generation 0; its name is the
title. Book cloning copies originals and copies of originals one
generation on, keeping the original. The written book opens a reader. A
lectern takes a held book (`#lectern_books`), switches to its `has_book`
state, opens the reader with page turning and Take Book (refused when the
player can't build), reports `floor(page / (pages - 1) * 14) + 1` to
comparators, and on each page change powers for two ticks (weak to every
side, strong down) with a page-turn sound. Breaking it drops the book. The
librarian's book and quill trade resolves.

**Spyglass.** Item 1556 (copper and amethyst recipe). Use raises it after
any block use declines (as `SpyglassItem.use`), for up to 1200 ticks; while
scoping in first person the field of view is a tenth (not scaled by FOV
Effects, as Java), the hand is hidden and the scope overlay eases from 0.5
toward 1.125 of the screen's short side. Controller seats share the same
hands adapter; split-screen views zoom and hide the hand.

**Paths.** Desktop screens for all three workstations, the book editor and
the readers. Controller tabs for the three workstations (inputs, then the
choices nine to a row, then the inventory). LAN: the three containers and
their slots (550–558, 600–699, 700–799), the choice and input sent with the
container, banner and lectern entities near each peer in the snapshot, a
numbered `book_open` request that opens the client's lectern reader or book
screen (as `sign_edit` does for signs), and the book and lectern actions as
commands. CLI agent: `stonecut item`,
`loom pattern dye`, `cartography copy|zoom|lock`, `book write [...]`,
`book sign "title" [...]`, `lectern x y z page n|take`; the LAN host routes
`book` and `lectern` through the same commands.

**Saves.** Banner entities and lectern books in each dimension's
`workstations` property; book, banner and shield payloads in stack strings;
map locks and scales in the map file.

## Differences from Java

- The creeper banner pattern has no recipe: creeper heads aren't registered
  (listed in `registry_gaps.json` for v0.16), so the item exists (creative,
  commands) but can't be made in survival yet.
- Pattern items are named after their ids ("flower banner pattern") so each
  name is unique; Java names eight of them "Banner Pattern" with the charge
  on a second tooltip line. Banner, shield and written-book tooltips don't
  list layers, author or generation.
- The pattern designs are original masks at 16 × 32 texels per flag, not
  Mojang's textures; gradients are ordered-dithered because the block model
  shader alpha-tests; "mojang" is an original emblem. Icons draw the layers
  over a simplified cloth or shield face; dropped patterned items and the
  shield held in first person show the plain sprite.
- No dedicated sounds: existing click and place sounds stand in for the
  stonecutter, loom and cartography take sounds, `BOOK_PUT`,
  `BOOK_PAGE_TURN` and the spyglass sounds. No stats are awarded.
- The cartography preview shows what the result does as text, not Java's
  map preview. The loom preview draws the result banner, not Java's model.
- Java's cartography table has no `facing`; it now always places its first
  state, while the three other legacy states stay registered for old saves.
- The book and quill can be opened from the main hand only (Java also opens
  it from the off-hand); signing uses "Player" as the host's author name.
  The edit screen types at the end of the page (no caret movement or
  selection) and only ASCII, which the UI font draws.
- Lecterns don't draw a book model (the top texture shows one), and taking
  the book uses the reader's button; a `has_book` lectern placed from an
  item with block entity data (operators only in Java) isn't supported.
- Banners on maps (`MapItem.useOn` markers) and item-frame map images are
  not part of this track.
- The spyglass overlay is drawn only in the desktop view, not in other
  split-screen views; third-person arm poses aren't drawn.
- Lectern and book screens aren't on the controller menus (desktop, LAN and
  CLI only).
