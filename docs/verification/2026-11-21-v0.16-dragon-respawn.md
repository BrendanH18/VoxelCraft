## What is implemented

**End crystals** (`entity::crystal`). Crystals are one entity kind in every
dimension, saved with their dimension: the fight's pillar crystals (now
entities, not a mask inside the fight) and placed ones. The item is crafted
from seven glass around an eye of ender over a ghast tear, has Java's
glint, and goes only on obsidian or bedrock with air above and no entity in
the 1x2x1 box over it (players, mobs, decorations, dropped items, carts,
TNT and other crystals, whose 2x2x2 boxes reach over), standing at the
block's centre with no bedrock plate (`setShowBottom(false)`); it is spent
in survival. Placing works from every input path that uses an item on a
block: desktop, controller, LAN guests and CLI agents share
`Session::use_on`. Any blow but the dragon's destroys a crystal: melee
(credited to the player, so its blast's kills count as theirs), arrows (the
shooter credited), and blasts. A destroyed crystal explodes at its feet
with power 6, breaking blocks with no fire (`ExplosionInteraction.BLOCK`,
under `block_explosion_drop_decay`), unless a blast destroyed it: then it
just goes, as Java's `hurtServer` skips the second blast for
`#is_explosion` damage. Crystals are fire immune, keep a fire burning in
their own air cell in the End every tick (not under `mobGriefing`; the End
isn't a portal dimension, so the fire lights no portal), and can carry a
beam to a block. Invulnerable crystals (the ritual's) take only creative
players' hits. A placed crystal heals a dragon within its box grown by 32
like a pillar's, and its destruction hurts a dragon it was healing.

**The fight** (`entity::dragon`, `app::mobs`). Crystal destruction follows
`onCrystalDestroyed`: the pillar count (`aliveCrystals`, rescanned every 100
ticks and on each loss, from the crystals in the pillars' columns) feeds
the holding pattern's odds; the dragon's healer takes 10 to the head; a
circling dragon turns on the player behind the blow, or the nearest within
64. A new dragon now appears at Java's (0, 128, 0) (it was 110).

**The respawn ritual.** A crystal placed in the End lets the next fight
tick try `tryRespawn`: with the dragon dead and no ritual running, a
crystal in each of the four cells three out from the exit portal's pillar
and one above its rim (Java's north, east, south, west order, by box
intersection) starts it with every crystal found. The exit portal shuts
(`respawnDragon`: its bedrock and portal blocks become end stone, then the
inactive podium is built over them). Then, with Java's timings: START (one
tick) turns the crystals' beams to (0, 128, 0); PREPARING_TO_SUMMON_PILLARS
growls at 0, 50–52 and 95–99 and moves on at 100; SUMMONING_PILLARS beams at
each pillar's top at `40 i` and at `40 i + 39` rebuilds it: every block
within 10 of its cap goes, a power 5 blast with no source goes off at the
cap (breaking blocks), the spike is rebuilt by `placeSpike`'s rules with its
cage and fire, and a new invulnerable crystal stands on it beaming at (0,
128, 0); at 400 SUMMONING_DRAGON beams back at the centre, growls for the
first four ticks and from 80, and at 100 the dragon appears at (0, 128, 0)
facing a random way, the pillar crystals lose their beams and
invulnerability, and each ritual crystal blows up with power 6 breaking no
blocks (`ExplosionInteraction.NONE`) and is gone. The ritual advances only
while a living player is within 192 of (0, 128, 0) and the pillars and
portal are loaded (Java's `tick` gate). Destroying a ritual crystal, or
losing them all, aborts it (`abortRespawnSequence`): the exit portal opens
again and the rebuilt pillars' crystals lose their beams and
invulnerability. The ritual saves with the fight (stage, time and its
crystals' positions, matched to the reloaded crystals) and resumes on
time. Later kills pay 500 experience instead of 12,000, leave no egg and
open the next gateway; the 20 gateway spots run out as in Java.

**Generation 5 End** (`world::end`). New worlds' End uses Java's spike
layout for the seed (`EndSpikeFeature`: the cache key from the seed's first
`nextLong`, sizes shuffled by `Util.shuffle`, spikes at `floor(42 cos)` and
`floor(42 sin)`, `dx² + dz² <= r² + 1` and the square cleared above y 65),
Java's gateway order for the seed (`0..20` shuffled by the seed, used from
the end), and puts the exit portal on the island's surface block as
`spawnExitPortal` does (one lower than generations 1–4). Generations 1–4
keep their pillars, gateway order and podium, pinned by their hashes, so
saved gateway counts still name the same spots.

**End gateways** (`world::end_portal`, `app::dimension`). A new gateway's
exit is found by `findOrCreateValidTeleportPos` against the End as it
stands (loaded or saved chunks, else the generator's chunks, End cities
included): 1024 blocks straight out, back past chunks with any block (up to
16 chunk steps) and on past empty ones (up to 16); in that chunk the end
stone from y 30 up with two non-full blocks above nearest the world origin
in 3D, first found in Java's x-then-y-then-z order; if none, Java's
`EndIslandFeature` island at y 75, shaped by `RandomSource.create` of its
position's `asLong` exactly as Java's; then `findTallestBlock` within 16
(its own column included, x outer and z inner, strictly taller replaces),
and the exit gateway ten above. Arrivals stand on `findExitPosition`'s
tallest full, non-bedrock block within 5 of the exit gateway's cap (its own
column skipped). Players keep their motion through a gateway
(`Relative.DELTA`), so an elytra flight carries on.

**Nether portals** (`world::portal`, `app::dimension`). The far side is
`PortalForcer`'s: the scaled position at the traveller's own height; an
existing portal within 16 blocks horizontally going into the Nether or 128
coming out, at any height, nearest by Java's `distSqr` and lower first on
a tie, searched over loaded and saved chunks (a chunk that was never
changed holds no portal); if it lies outside the loaded area, the arrival
waits for it to load. With none, `createPortal`: columns spiral out 16
blocks east then south (`BlockPos.spiralAround`), from the
`MOTION_BLOCKING` height down, and the bottom of every run of replaceable,
fluid-free cells (one cell, or three or more) that can hold the 4x5 frame
on solid ground is a site; the nearest with room on both sides wins, else
the nearest with room for the frame alone; with neither, an obsidian ledge
three wide at y 70 to 9 below the top. The new portal runs along the axis
of the one entered, with Java's frame and 2x3 portal.

**Progression fix found by the audit.** Ghasts dropped tears only to
players; Java 26.3's `entities/ghast` has no `killed_by_player` condition
on the tear (only on the music disc). Tears now drop on any death, as the
End crystal recipe needs.

## Differences from Java

- The crystal entity has no portal travel (`handlePortal`), and no game
  events but the blast's; placing it plays no sound, as in Java, but posts
  no `ENTITY_PLACE` vibration. Snowballs, eggs, ender pearls, tridents and
  wind charges pass through crystals (Java hurts them on contact); arrows,
  melee and blasts destroy them. A hit crystal goes at the next tick
  rather than within the hit.
- The ritual starts at the fight's next tick after a placement rather than
  during it, and its growls (level event 3001, volume 64) play as the
  dragon's growl at (0, 128, 0) at normal range. At its end the ritual
  crystals are removed before their blasts land, so (unlike Java) those
  blasts don't hit the other ritual crystals and turn the new dragon on
  the nearest player.
- In generations 1–4 the respawn rebuilds the original round pillars at
  their own positions (the ritual follows the world's pillars), and their
  podium stays one block above the surface; only generation 5 has Java's
  layout. Neither generation places the podium's four wall torches.
- The exit gateway search reads unloaded chunks from the save or the
  generator (no player edits are lost: saved chunks are read as saved),
  and an island is built when the traveller arrives (the arrival waits for
  16 blocks around it). A gateway's 40-tick cooldown, its 200-tick spawning
  beam and the 2,400-tick flash aren't modelled. A pearl thrown into a
  gateway takes its thrower through at once instead of travelling and
  landing; agents' pearls land at the gateway.
- The portal search ignores the world border, and the traveller arrives at
  the found portal's lowest cell nearest the search point, not Java's
  relative position within it (`PortalShape.findRelativePosition`); mobs,
  items and carts don't use portals.
- The 26.3 `music_disc_tears` from a ghast killed by its own fireball isn't
  dropped (no such disc is registered).
