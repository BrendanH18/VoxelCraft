## What changed

### Camels ride on `Camel`'s code (FOO-04c)

- **Pose clock** (`entity::mounts::camel`, `State::camel_sitting` and
  `pose_time`): Java's `LAST_POSE_CHANGE_TICK` as a pose and the ticks
  since it changed. `sitDown`/`standUp` restart the clock;
  `isInPoseTransition` is 40 ticks sitting down, 52 standing up;
  `refuseToMove` is sitting or changing pose; `standUpInstantly` (water,
  any hit, a fall that hurts) sets a full stand (53). Spawned camels start
  fully standing. The old `sit_ticks`/`stand_ticks` countdowns are gone.
- **Dimensions** (`Mob::shape`): 1.7 × 0.945 sitting
  (`2.375 − SITTING_HEIGHT_DIFFERENCE`), calves 0.95 × 1.4 standing and
  0.95 × 0.425 sitting.
- **Idle sitting** (`Mob::camel_idle`, from the idle branch of `think`):
  `CamelAi`'s priority-4 `RunOne`, while there is no walk target: an even
  pick among `RandomStroll.stroll(2.0)` (unless it refuses to move),
  `RandomSitting(20)` (pose time at least 400, out of water, not leashed,
  on the ground, no steering rider or mob rider, room for the other pose)
  and `DoNothing(30, 60)`. This replaces the earlier 1-in-2400-a-tick,
  600–1200-tick schedule.
- **`CamelMoveControl` and `Camel.travel`** (`Mob::camel_hold`): a seated
  camel that wants to walk (not leashed, room to stand) starts standing
  up; while it sits or changes pose it is held still on the ground.
- **Ridden** (`Mob::equine_ridden_tick`, now for camels and camel husks
  too): no input and no turning while it refuses to move; forward input
  stands a seated, saddled camel up (`Camel.tickRidden`); ridden speed
  `MOVEMENT_SPEED` 0.09, plus 0.1 while the rider sprints with the dash
  ready (`getRiddenSpeed`); step height 1.5; fall damage as horses.
- **Dash:** the rider's charge follows `LocalPlayer` (below), only while
  the camel can jump (saddled, not refusing to move) and its dash cooldown
  is 0; the release counts only from the ground (`Camel.onPlayerJump`).
  `executeRidersJump` adds `22.2222 × scale × 0.09 × block speed factor`
  along the flattened look and `1.4285 × scale × getJumpPower()` (0.42 ×
  block jump factor + Jump Boost) up, to the existing motion; the cooldown
  is 55, counted down after the move (`Camel.tick`), and `DASH` clears
  under 50 once the camel is down. The camel's jump attribute is the fixed
  0.42 (older saves stored 0.5; they load 0.42).
- **Seats** (`camel::attachment`, used for players by
  `Entities::mount_seat` and for husk riders by `camel_husk::rider_seat`):
  `getPassengerAttachmentPoint`: alone or in front 0.5 forward, behind 0.7
  back (an animal 0.2 further forward), times the age scale (0.6 for
  calves), at `getBodyAnchorAnimationYOffset`'s height through both
  transitions (front and rear seats have their own flex points), less the
  rider's vehicle attachment (a player's 0.6, a zombie-type's 0.7). The
  earlier seats were the middle alone and ±0.5 with two.
- The model's body sinks and rises with the anchor
  (`camel::sit_fraction`); LAN replicas carry the pose and its clock;
  sculk sensors hear `sitDown`/`standUp` as before.
- **Save:** a camel whose pose has changed saves `camel_sitting` and
  `pose_time` instead of `sit_ticks` and `stand_ticks`; every other mount,
  and a camel still in its spawn pose, writes the old zero `sit_ticks` and
  `stand_ticks`, so their records save byte for byte as before (the v0.8
  entity fixture checks this). Older saves load seated (with pose time
  400, free to stand) when they were sitting, partway up when standing
  up, else standing.

### Horses ridden in water (FOO-04c)

The steered horse no longer falls back to the continuous ride in water or
lava. One Java tick now does, in order:

1. `Entity.baseTick`: the fluid the box touches (`player::fluid::contact`,
   shared with players and boats) and the water current's push.
2. `LivingEntity.aiStep`: motion under 0.003 is dropped.
3. `travelRidden` as before, then `travel`: `travelInWater` (0.02 a tick
   of input, drag `getWaterSlowDown`: 0.8, a skeleton horse's 0.96;
   Dolphin's Grace 0.96; `getFluidFallingAdjustedMovement`) or
   `travelInLava`, `jumpOutOfFluid` (0.3 up where the bank is free) and
   `floatInLiquidWhileRidden` (0.04 up while deeper than 0.4, for
   `#can_float_while_ridden`: every horse, donkey, mule, zombie horse,
   camel and camel husk, not the skeleton horse).
4. Fall distance resets in water.

The mount's own AI float jump (`FloatGoal`, the camel brain's `Swim`)
isn't applied to a steered mount: in Java the rider's client simulates the
vehicle it controls (`isLocalInstanceAuthoritative`), where the mount's AI
doesn't run, so its `jumping` flag stays false.

Two rider-side corrections found on the way:

- **The riding jump charge** follows `LocalPlayer.aiStep`: only for a
  controlled vehicle that `canJump` and whose `getJumpCooldown` is 0; the
  press tick resets the charge to 0, each held tick adds 0.1 to 0.9, then
  `0.8 + 0.2 / (ticks − 9)`; the release sends the jump and blocks the next
  charge for ten ticks. The nautilus's dash cooldown now gates its charge
  the same way. The old charge started at 0.1 on the press tick.
- **In the air** a steered mount moves with `getFlyingSpeed`, a tenth of
  its riding speed, not 0.02 (horses' jumps, camels' dashes).

### The nautilus inventory screen (FOO-04c)

`Entities::vehicle_slots`, `vehicle_container` and `vehicle_title` now
cover tame adult nautiluses and zombie nautiluses
(`mounts::has_mount_screen`), with their saddle and body-armor slots and
`NautilusInventoryMenu`'s rules. Every path the horse screens use reaches
it unchanged: sneak-use (`PlayerSession::use_held`, keyboard and
controller), the inventory key or use while riding (desktop, controller
and LAN), the controller's vehicle tab, LAN peers and the CLI's `chest
take/put`. As `openCustomInventoryScreen` requires, nobody else may be
riding it; the same check now applies to horses, donkeys, mules and llamas
(`AbstractHorse.mobInteract`), but not camels, which open regardless
(`Camel.mobInteract`). Shift-clicking a stack no mount slot accepts now
moves it between the hotbar and the main inventory
(`AbstractMountInventoryMenu.quickMoveStack`) for every mob vehicle; chest
carts and chest boats keep `ChestMenu`'s behaviour.

### Baby dolphins (MOB-03c)

- After the first dolphin of a natural pack, a dolphin is a calf with
  probability 0.1 (`Dolphin.finalizeSpawn`'s `AgeableMobGroupData(0.1F)`,
  in `Entities::spawn_natural`). A dolphin spawn egg used on a dolphin
  already made one.
- Calves age a tick a tick to adulthood at 24000 (`AgeableMob.aiStep`); a
  fish grows a calf that can age by a tenth of the time left
  (`ageUp(getSpeedUpSecondsWhenFeeding(-age), true)`) instead of setting
  `GotFish`. A golden dandelion locks or unlocks a calf's age (dolphins
  aren't in `#cannot_be_age_locked`).
- A calf's box is 0.65 of the adult's (`BABY_DIMENSIONS`), and it draws an
  original model on `BabyDolphinModel`'s proportions at full scale (Java's
  renderer swaps the model rather than scaling it).
- Calves don't retaliate (`canAttack`), as the existing
  `dolphin_hurt_by` already had it.

### Guardians target squid and axolotls (MOB-03c)

`GuardianAttackSelector` accepts players, squid (and glow squid) and
axolotls more than 3 blocks away. `Entities::aquatic_sense` gives each
guardian the nearest squid, glow squid or axolotl within 16 blocks (twice
a second, and its current target's position every tick); `aquatic::upkeep`
keeps the current target while it stays valid (an elder's even within 3
blocks, `GuardianAttackGoal.canContinueToUse`), else takes the nearest
valid player or mob (within 16, more than 3 blocks, in the home, in sight).
The charge is unchanged; at its end a mob takes one hit of the larger of
the physical and magic damage (6, an elder's 8) with a 0.4 push away
(`EntityEvent::MobHit`). Acquiring a player now also needs more than 3
blocks for elders, as the selector does.

### Water currents (MOB-03c)

`Mob::pushed_by_fluid` is Java's `isPushedByFluid`: false for fish and
tadpoles (`WaterAnimal`), squid, glow squid and dolphins
(`AgeableWaterCreature`), turtles, axolotls, frogs and nautiluses; a
drowned only while it isn't swimming; true for every other mob, guardians
and elder guardians included. So the "swimmers" the earlier records listed
are, apart from guardians, correctly never pushed. Pushed mobs now get
`CurrentAccumulator.applyTo`'s 0.014 a tick along the normalised flow
(`FluidContact::entity_water_impulse`): exactly per tick on Java ticks
(guardians, polar bears in water, ridden horses and camels), and as the
steady drift that push gives against water drag (four times the push a
tick) in VoxelCraft's continuous water model for other land mobs.

## Differences and remaining gaps

- **Camels:** `SetWalkTargetFromLookTarget` is left out of the idle
  `RunOne` (VoxelCraft keeps no look-target memory), so a standing camel
  picks among three behaviours where Java sometimes has four; the `RunOne`
  waits for the stroll's path rather than Java's walk-target memory.
  `CamelMoveControl` is read as "the AI produced a movement this tick".
  Standing up on a leash pull (`onElasticLeashPull`), the head-yaw clamp
  (`MAX_HEAD_Y_ROT` 30) and `CamelBodyRotationControl` aren't modelled.
  The sit, stand, dash and dash-ready sounds aren't played, and the model
  lowers its body rather than playing Java's sit, sit-up and dash
  animations. A calf's model still draws at VoxelCraft's generic 0.5
  baby scale (Java's age scale is 0.6).
- **Horses in fluids:** the climbing lift (`onClimbable`'s 0.2) and lava
  currents aren't applied to mounts; a sprinting camel's 0.9 water
  slow-down isn't used (the mount's own sprint flag isn't modelled).
  Horses' rearing (`standIfPossible`) is still not modelled.
- **Nautilus screen:** it uses the shared chest-style vehicle screen with
  its two slots, as horses do, not Java's dedicated mount layout and slot
  sprites (an existing horse difference).
- **Baby dolphins:** `forcedAge` (carried into adulthood) and its
  happy-villager particles aren't kept; air and drying use the adult's
  height for the eyes (Java's calf eyes are at 0.09375).
- **Guardians:** candidate mobs are sensed twice a second;
  `NearestAttackableTargetGoal`'s 1-in-5 roll per selector update isn't
  modelled for any target; the hit on a mob is one combined hit rather
  than Java's magic hit then `doHurtTarget` inside the same invulnerability
  window (same total damage).
- **Currents:** land mobs in VoxelCraft's continuous water model reach
  Java's steady drift, not its tick-by-tick transient; lava currents
  don't push mobs.
