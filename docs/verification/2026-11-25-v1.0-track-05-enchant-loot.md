## What changed

### PRO-03: Soul Speed

- **The enchantment** (`src/enchant.rs`): `Enchantment::SoulSpeed`, the 43rd
  and last Java enchantment, with Java's definition (max level 3, weight 1,
  costs 10 + 10/level to 25 + 10/level, anvil cost 8, `#enchantable/foot_armor`,
  no exclusive set), in `#treasure` (so never from the table, villagers'
  `#tradeable`, `#on_random_loot` or mob spawn equipment), in
  `#double_trade_price` and in `#tooltip_order` between Aqua Affinity and
  Swift Sneak. It's compatible with Frost Walker and Depth Strider, as in
  Java.
- **Its save bits.** `Enchants` packs three bits a level into a `u128`, and
  42 enchantments used 126 bits, which is why Soul Speed waited
  (EFX-ENCH-17's EFX-ENCH-18). Soul Speed takes the last two bits (126–127),
  which hold levels 0–3, Java's maximum. Every other enchantment keeps its
  bits, so the save format (one hex `u128`) is unchanged and older saves
  read as before; a level above III (only reachable through a hand-edited
  save) clamps to III. Every bit is now in use.
- **Movement** (`src/player/soul_speed.rs`, `src/player/travel.rs`): Java's
  `location_changed` effect, evaluated where `LivingEntity.onChangedBlock`
  runs (a new block position, or landing from a fall) and when the boots
  change: not riding, not flying, and either on `#soul_speed_blocks` (soul
  sand and soul soil, at `getBlockPosBelowThatAffectsMyMovement`) or, once
  active, in the air. While active the player has `movement_speed` +0.0405
  (+0.0105 a level past the first) as an `add_value` modifier and
  `movement_efficiency` +1, so `getBlockSpeedFactor` lerps soul sand's 0.4 to
  1: no slowdown. It goes at the first block change on any other ground.
- **Boot wear**: on the same check, on the ground on those blocks, chance
  0.04 per level of one point (`change_item_damage`), through Unbreaking's
  armor chance, none in Creative; boots at full damage break with the usual
  "… broke" popup and break sound.
  `PlayerSession::tick_soul_speed` applies it; every adapter (the game and
  the agents) calls it next to Frost Walker.
- **Particles and sound**: the `tick` effects every fifth tick on the
  ground on those blocks while moving (horizontal movement ≥ 1e-5) and not
  flying: one `soul` particle at a random point across the width, 0.1 above
  the feet, moving at −0.2 × the player's movement horizontally and 0.1 up
  (`SpawnParticlesEffect`), and with chance 0.35 `particle.soul_escape` at
  volume 0.6 and pitch 0.6–1.0 (`PlaySoundEffect`, the `PLAYERS` category).
  The particle is a new kind, `Kind::Soul` (`EmissiveRisingParticle`: rising
  physics, friction 0.96, lifetime `8 / (0.2..1.0) + 4`, scale 1.5, full
  bright); the sound a new `Sound::SoulEscape`, whose subtitle ("Soul
  escapes", range 12) comes from the sound fixture.
- **Sources**: piglin bartering's first two entries now give an enchanted
  book or iron boots with Soul Speed I–III (`enchant_randomly` limited to
  `minecraft:soul_speed`; the empty rolls are gone), and `bastion_other`
  chests' Soul Speed books and boots, rolled from the jar's table, now carry
  it (the table already named it; the enchantment was missing). Nothing
  else gives it, as in Java.
- **Frost Walker** now also runs when the boots are put on, as Java runs a
  newly equipped slot's `location_changed` effects (a difference the v0.15
  record listed).
- **The anvil name box** was added by v0.18 Track 2 on the desktop and the
  controller screens ([shared UI](2026-11-23-v0.18-shared-ui.md), item 6):
  nothing was missing, and this track didn't change it.

### PRO-04: trail ruin archaeology, End city and mansion loot

- **Loot tables.** `archaeology/trail_ruins_common` (31 entries, weights 2
  and 1, total 45) and `archaeology/trail_ruins_rare` (12 equal entries:
  seven sherds, the host, raiser, shaper and wayfinder templates and the
  Relic disc) were added to `src/world/loot_tables.json` from the jar by
  `tools/extract_java_structure_loot.py` (two new `TABLES` entries;
  `archaeology/` tables sit outside `chests/`). The other tables in the file
  are byte-identical to the jar's; the file also holds three abandoned camp
  tables the extractor doesn't list, which were kept as they were.
  `loot::sequence_key` keeps the `archaeology/` folder, as Java's
  `random_sequence` does.
- **Every item resolves.** The seven sherds the rare table names (burn,
  danger, friend, heart, heartbreak, howl and sheaf) are registered as items
  1950–1956, with the original procedural sherd icon (`Sprite::PotterySherd`
  7–13), and their seven `registry_gaps.json` entries (DEF-02) are gone.
  Every other name in both tables already resolved.
  `tests/java_registry.rs` checks every loot name resolves or is owned.
- **Suspicious gravel in trail ruins** (`src/world/trail_ruins.rs`). The
  ruins' processor lists' capped rules
  (`worldgen/processor_list/trail_ruins_*_archaeology`, mapped to pieces by
  the `trail_ruins/*` template pools): houses (the tower, its additions,
  buildings, grouped buildings and decor) turn up to 6 gravel blocks into
  suspicious gravel with the common table, then 3 more with the rare one;
  roads 2 (common); the tower top 2 (common). Each `CappedProcessor` shuffles
  the piece's processed blocks (after the gravel and mud brick rules) with
  the world seed forked at the piece, both caps of a piece drawing the same
  order as Java's do, and `AppendLoot` seeds each block's loot with the
  first long of its position's processor random, exactly as the ocean ruins
  do. The blocks are painted in every chunk the ruin crosses and their
  loot table and seed reach the block entity through
  `Generator::structure_features`.
- **Brushing** (`src/world/archaeology.rs`): `Table::TrailCommon` and
  `TrailRare` roll the JSON tables (`BrushableBlockEntity.unpackLootTable`
  keeps the first stack), with the block's seed or the table's world random
  sequence.
- **End city and mansion chests.** Both tables were already the jar's,
  with the spire template in `end_city_treasure`'s second pool (1 in 15)
  and the vex template in `woodland_mansion`'s fourth pool, and both
  templates are registered (v0.15 Track D); End city chests (v0.16 Track A)
  and mansion chests (v0.15) generate. The PRO-04 note was stale; nothing
  changed.

### MOB-02d: mob armor against fire, lava and falls

(`src/entity/mob/hazards.rs`, `src/entity/mob.rs`)

- Fire and lava now hurt mobs as Java's damage types: `lava` (4 every half
  second), `in_fire` (the first touch and 1 every half second) and
  `on_fire` (burning and sunburn). `take_damage` already ran
  `getDamageAfterArmorAbsorb` and `getDamageAfterMagicAbsorb` for typed
  hits, so worn armor now reduces lava and fire contact, `on_fire` keeps
  its `#bypasses_armor`, and Protection and Fire Protection reduce all
  three.
- **Fall damage for mobs.** Before this, only mounts, wolves and frogs took
  any. Now every mob moved by the ordinary physics does, after
  `Entity.checkFallDamage` and `LivingEntity.causeFallDamage`: the distance
  fallen, reset in or through water (`updateFluidInteraction` and the move's
  water clip), on climbable blocks, under Slow Falling and in a cobweb, is
  `floor((distance + 1e-6 − safe_fall_distance) × modifier)` on landing.
  The safe distance is 3 (5 for foxes) plus the Jump Boost level; the
  landing block's `fallOn` sets the modifier (hay bales and honey 0.2,
  slime and powder snow none; 26.3's `BedBlock` has no `fallOn`, so beds
  don't soften it); goats take 10 less and frogs 5. Mobs in
  `#fall_damage_immune` (iron, snow and copper golems, shulkers, allays,
  bats, bees, blazes, cats, chickens, ghasts, happy ghasts, phantoms, magma
  cubes, ocelots, parrots, the Wither and breezes) take none. It lands as
  `minecraft:fall` damage: armor points don't soften it (`#bypasses_armor`);
  Protection and Feather Falling do. Wolves' and frogs' existing falls now
  carry the same type.
- **Armor durability.** Java 26.3's `Mob` doesn't override `hurtArmor`
  (only `Player`, `Horse` and `Wolf` do; checked in the jar), so a mob's worn
  humanoid armor never wears from damage, and the game matches that. The
  one way it wears is `Mob.burnUndead`, now implemented: an undead mob with
  anything on its head doesn't catch fire in daylight, and on each sun-burn
  tick (`isSunBurnTick`'s chance, `(brightness − 0.4) × 2 / 30`) a damageable
  helmet takes 0 or 1 points through Unbreaking and breaks at full damage.

### PLY-03b: mobs in cobwebs, and the rest of the effect list

- Mobs are held by cobwebs (`WebBlock.entityInside`,
  `Entity.makeStuckInBlock`): inside one a mob moves (0.25, 0.05, 0.25) of
  its motion, or (0.5, 0.25, 0.5) under Weaving, its motion stops and its
  fall is forgotten. Spiders and cave spiders (`Spider.makeStuckInBlock`)
  and the Wither aren't held. Weaving's web speed now applies to mobs as
  well as the player.
- The rest of PLY-03b's list was checked: every 26.3 effect is registered
  with its icon, save and instance rules, and acts. The one remaining gap is
  Darkness, which is fog only: Java also darkens the lightmap. That is
  shader work in the chunk, entity and block-model shaders, which Track 2
  is changing (the chunk shader's sRGB and fog math), so it wasn't cheap
  here and is left as an open item.

## Differences from Java and open decisions

- **Soul particle art**: Java's `soul` particle is an animated wisp sprite;
  the game draws the soul flame's sprite with the wisp's physics, size and
  glow. **Sound**: `particle.soul_escape` is an original procedural wisp,
  like the other v0.16+ stand-ins.
- **Mobs with Soul Speed boots** get nothing: only players run the
  location-changed effects. Piglins and zombified piglins can be given or
  pick up boots in Java and would run them. Open: whether to extend the
  effect to mobs.
- **Fall damage scope**: mobs moved by their own Java-tick movement
  (swimmers, ghasts, bats, ocelots, pandas, polar bears in water and frogs
  mid-jump) keep their own fall handling, which this track didn't change.
  Creepers'
  `causeFallDamage` fuse increase and pointed dripstone's stalagmite damage
  aren't modelled. A mob's fall distance isn't saved (Java saves
  `fall_distance`): a mob saved mid-fall lands without damage after a
  reload. Mob collision leaves a body 1e-5 above the floor, so a fall that
  ends exactly on a whole number of blocks counts that much short (this is
  the existing physics, not Java's).
- **Behaviour change to watch in play**: mobs now die from long falls
  (zombies off cliffs, mobs knocked off ledges, animals falling into
  ravines). That is Java's behaviour, but it hadn't existed in the game.
- **Sun protection** uses the game's continuous sunburn condition
  (`daylight > 0.45` and exposure) to decide a mob would burn, then Java's
  per-tick chance for the helmet's wear; Java draws the same chance for the
  ignition too.
- **Cobwebs on mobs** use the mob physics' per-second integration: the
  multiplier scales that step's motion and stops it, as Java does per tick.
  Sweet berry bushes and powder snow still don't hold mobs.
- **Trail ruins** keep the original reduced templates (DEF-02/GEN-05f), so
  where the suspicious gravel lands can't match Java's; the processors,
  limits, tables, shuffle and seeds are Java's.
- **Darkness** (PLY-03b) still darkens only the fog; left for after Track 2's
  shader work. Open: schedule it.
- The anvil name box needed nothing; PRO-03's note about it was stale.
