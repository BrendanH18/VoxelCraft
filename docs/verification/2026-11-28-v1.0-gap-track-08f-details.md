## Scope

What [Track 8e](2026-11-28-v1.0-gap-track-08e-fire-tnt-credits.md#remaining-differences)
and [Track 7f](2026-11-28-v1.0-gap-track-07f-villager-details.md#remaining-differences)
left in damage messages, projectiles, fire, the credits and sound:

1. Death messages naming players by their profile name, as Java's do,
   wherever a message names an attacker or a victim.
2. Ender pearls saved with their owner (and what they do while the owner
   is elsewhere); eyes of ender saved; the mob behind a TNT blast made the
   attacker of what it hurts; fire charges starting fires on blocks, used
   and dispensed (`FireChargeItem`).
3. The chunks around a player watching the End credits no longer
   ticking; the villager's `entity.villager.no` with its head shake.

All of them are done; what remains is [listed below](#remaining-differences).

## What changed

### Players named in death messages (item 1)

Every message that named a player said "a player", and the dying player
was "Player" on their own death screen. Java names both by their display
name, a player's being their profile name. Now:

- **The killer.** A hit a player caused carries them (`Cause::by`, or
  `Cause::names` for an End crystal a player broke, whose blast has no
  indirect source and so primes TNT for no one). When the hit kills, the
  victim's `Vitals::killer` keeps that player, and the game puts their
  profile name where "a player" was: "was slain by Alex", "was shot by
  Alex", "was impaled by Alex", "was blown up by Alex" (TNT they lit, a
  crystal they broke), "was killed while trying to hurt Alex" (Thorns),
  and for a player's wind charge Java's `mob` message, "was slain by
  Alex" (it said "was blasted by a wind charge", which an ownerless one
  still says).
  The host is named by their own profile, each split-screen seat, agent
  and LAN player by theirs. The named text is what the save keeps.
- **The victim.** The death screen reads "<profile> fell from a high
  place" (on a LAN guest's screen, the guest's profile), as does the log.
- **Chat.** With `show_death_messages`, the message goes to every
  player's chat as `ServerPlayer.die` broadcasts it (the host's console
  and the LAN guests'), once per death: an agent killed by a blast or
  another player's hit between its own ticks is announced too
  (`Bot::announced`; before, only deaths inside the agent's own tick were
  logged). A profile loaded dead isn't announced again.
- **Thorns' wording.** "was killed trying to hurt a player" becomes
  Java's "was killed while trying to hurt" them; an old save's text reads
  back as that cause (`Cause::RETIRED`), and the fixture test no longer
  lists the wording as a difference.

### Pearls with their owners (item 2)

Track 8e saved pearls in the dimension's level file. Java keeps a pearl
with its thrower: it's saved in their player data (`ender_pearls`, each
with its `ender_pearl_dimension`) and is in its level only while they
are in the game (`PlayerList.remove` takes it out with them). Now
(`OwnedPearls`):

- The host's pearls are saved with the host's data in the root level
  file and each profile's with that profile (`ender_pearls`, each pearl
  with its `dimension`), written only when there are some. The level
  file no longer writes `ender_pearls`; one Track 8e wrote still loads.
- **The owner leaves** (a LAN guest disconnects, a controller seat or an
  agent leaves): their pearls leave the level with them, frozen in
  flight, and come back where they were when they return.
- **The owner (or the pearl) changes dimension:** the pearl stays with
  the level it flies in, waiting with its owner's data, and flies on when
  the host is back there.
- **Far from players:** a pearl keeps flying wherever the terrain is
  loaded, as Java's pearl keeps its own chunks ticking for its owner
  (`ENDER_PEARL` ticket); before, it waited at the simulation distance.

### Eyes of ender saved (item 2)

Thrown eyes are saved with the dimension (`ender_eyes`, with those in
unloaded chunks), as Java saves them: position and motion. Java doesn't
save their target, life or whether they'll survive, so a reloaded eye
flies straight on for another 80 ticks and shatters; here too.

### Whoever was behind a blast is the attacker (item 2)

`ServerExplosion` hurts each entity with the blast's damage source, and
`hurtServer` makes the source's entity the attacker. Now every blast
with an entity behind it (`Cause::by`: primed TNT's owner, a ghast for
its fireball's blast, a player for a punched fireball) does that:

- a mob it hurts takes a mob behind it as its attacker
  (`setLastHurtByMob`): polar bears, pandas, armadillos, endermites,
  raiders, nether mobs, the warden and the Wither (not its friends) turn
  on it, and a tamed animal's owner's wolves go for it;
- a mob it hurts takes a player behind it as its attacker, as a
  projectile they shot does: `HurtByTargetGoal`, villager gossip, nether
  mobs' and the warden's anger, and the player's wolves joining in;
- a player it hurts takes a mob behind it as their `lastHurtByMob`:
  their wolves and the foxes that trust them defend them.

A creeper's own blast blames no one (it is discarded with it).

### Fire charges set fires (item 2)

`FireChargeItem.useOn` on a block that isn't a campfire, candle or
candle cake (those were already lit) sets fire in the cell beside the
clicked face where fire can go, lighting a Nether portal frame there as
flint and steel does, with the charge used up outside creative and
`FIRECHARGE_USE` (the blaze's shot sound) at Java's pitch; where fire
can't go it does nothing and keeps the charge. Dispensed fire charges
already flew as small fireballs that set fire where they hit; that fire
was wrongly held back by `mob_griefing`, which Java's `SmallFireball`
only asks of a mob's fireball (`IgniteBlock::mob_owned`).

### The credits' chunks stop (item 3)

While the End credits roll, the host is no longer one of the players
whose chunks tick (`World::set_simulation_players_with_host`): with
nobody else in the world, no block, fluid, random or entity tick runs
near them; a LAN or split-screen player still keeps their own chunks
going. The world's own refreshes of the host's position don't bring
them back until the credits end. Time and weather go on, as Java's
level ticks without players.

### The villager says no (item 3)

A refused trade and a pestered child now play `entity.villager.no`
with the head shake (`setUnhappy`): an original synthesized sound in the
established villager voice, the same nasal tract as its "hmm" in two
short closed-mouth syllables, the second lower and falling ("hm-mm").
It plays as `NEUTRAL` at volume 1 and the voice pitch, half an octave
higher for a child.

## IDs used

None: no block states, items, entity kinds or texture layers. New saved
keys, each written only when there is something to keep, so a save
without them (the v0.8 fixture among them) resaves unchanged:
`ender_pearls` in the root level file (the host's) and in a profile (its
player's), and `ender_eyes` in a dimension's level file. The level file's
`ender_pearls` (Track 8e, never released) is read and no longer written.
The new sound (`Sound::VillagerNo`) takes the next index after the fox's
cues; sound indices aren't saved.

## Remaining differences

- **Mobs are still "a zombie"**, not Java's "Zombie": VoxelCraft names
  mobs with an article throughout its messages; only players got their
  names. Java's `.item` variants ("using [Sword]", for a custom-named
  weapon) and the kill-credit variants ("drowned while trying to escape
  Alex", `getKillCredit`) aren't there. A player's Harming potion or
  tipped arrow still kills with "died from magic" (typed `magic`), where
  Java's `indirect_magic` says "was killed by Alex using magic": the
  effect events carry no thrower.
- **A pearl in another dimension waits.** Java's pearl keeps flying in
  its own level while its owner is elsewhere and, landing, takes them
  across (`isAllowedToTeleportOwner`, `canUsePortal`); here only the
  host's dimension runs, so it waits there until the host comes back. Its
  ticket doesn't load terrain the game hasn't loaded either: past the
  loaded area it waits.
- **The credits' chunks stay loaded**, though they don't tick; Java's
  unload when the tickets go.
- **A fire charge used again on the spot it just set alight** lit a fire
  again in the test (a fire block's thin outline, which Java's crosshair
  stops at, may not be one here); not looked into.
- **The villager's "no"** is original synthesized audio, not Java's
  sample (as every voice here); nobody has listened to it.
- From earlier tracks, still open: a ghast fireball's blast says "was
  fireballed by a ghast" (even punched back by a player) where Java says
  "was blown up by" its owner; a rocket's block hit reaches target
  blocks and redstone ore only when it burns; only arrows and tridents
  read a player's hurt cooldown before hitting; one fire texture.
