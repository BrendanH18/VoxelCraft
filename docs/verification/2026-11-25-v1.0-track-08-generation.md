Status: **Implemented, unverified.** No owner play, and no agent played the

## What changed

### Generation 6

A new world generation, `Generation::V6` (`worldgen=6` in `level.txt`), is
what new worlds use. It changes only the Nether: Java 26.3's placed
features (GEN-04a) and the full bastion catalogue (GEN-05g). Generations 1
to 5 generate exactly as before: their code paths are untouched and the
generation 1–5 golden chunk hashes (`generation_5_chunk_hashes_stay_identical`
and the older ones) pass unchanged. A saved world keeps its generation, so
existing worlds don't change and there are no seams. This follows how
generation 5 was added (`terrain.rs`'s `Generation`, `from_prop`, `LATEST`).

### GEN-04a: the Nether against Java 26.3

The Nether's terrain and biome noise stay original under FE-01. Everything
else was checked against the jar:

- **Biome source.** Java's `nether` multi-noise preset is code, not data;
  `tools/JavaNetherVectors.java` runs it against the jar and prints its
  five parameter points. They match `NetherBiome::point` exactly
  (temperature, humidity and offset; continentalness, erosion, depth and
  weirdness zero). No change was needed.
- **Features and placement.** Java 26.3 made the Nether's features data:
  the five biomes name 37 placed features. Generation 5 had 13 of them, as
  ports of 1.21's feature classes, plus noise-painted glowstone, quartz,
  gold and delta blobs. Generation 6 has all 37 as a table
  (`world::nether_placed::PLACED`) of Java's placement modifiers and
  configured features, run by a placement interpreter in
  `nether_decoration::v6`. New: magma, gravel, blackstone, soul sand, gold
  and quartz ores (`OreFeature`); glowstone as `random_neighbor_spread`;
  open, closed and delta lava springs (`SpringFeature`, open ones scheduled
  to flow when their chunk loads); fire and soul fire patches; nether and
  roof mushrooms (Java's heightmap placement puts the overworld mushrooms
  on the bedrock roof, which generation 6 now generates); crimson roots in
  soul sand valleys; basalt and blackstone blobs (`ReplaceBlobsFeature`).
  Changed to 26.3's data: crimson, warped and sprout vegetation (64 tries at
  trapezoid offsets around a nylium spot), weeping vines (a wart spread and
  100 `block_column`s) and twisting vines (64 columns found by a downward
  `environment_scan`), each column a plant layer of weighted length and a
  head aged 17–25. Huge fungi, deltas, columns and the basalt pillar keep
  their ports, checked against the 26.3 configurations (26.3's
  `stepped_column_cluster` with weights 9:1 is the same rule as 1.21's 0.9
  chance). Java's overworld `spring_lava` is listed for four Nether biomes
  but has no valid block in the Nether, so it never places; the table keeps
  it as inert. Ancient debris keeps its existing pass, which already
  followed `ore_ancient_debris_large` and `ore_debris_small`.
- **Structure placement.** `nether_complexes` (spacing 27, separation 4,
  salt 30084232, fortress 2 : bastion 3) and `nether_fossils` (2, 1,
  14357921) were already Java's; they are now named constants checked
  against the fixture, as are the bastion's jigsaw settings and the
  `has_structure` biome tags (bastions everywhere but basalt deltas,
  fossils only in soul sand valleys, fortresses and Nether ruined portals
  everywhere).

### GEN-05g: Java's full bastion catalogue

Generation 6 bastions are built from all 60 of Java's `bastion/*` pools and
an original template for each of the 167 locations they name, to the FE-02
contract: Java's pool names, weights, fallbacks and processor lists; each
template at Java's size with Java's jigsaw connectors (name, target, pool,
joint, priorities) at the positions Java's template holds them, so the
pieces tile as Java's do; and exactly Java's count of chests (with their
tables: `bastion_treasure`, `bastion_bridge`, `bastion_hoglin_stable`,
`bastion_other`), the magma cube spawner, gold blocks, gilded blackstone,
lava and residents. `src/world/jigsaw/bastion_v6.txt` holds the facts (a
`piece` line per template, then the pools); `src/world/bastion/catalogue.rs`
draws the templates by kind (described in [bastions](../bastions.md)). The
existing jigsaw engine assembles them with Java's settings (start pool
`bastion/starts`, size 6, distance 80, start height 33). The processor
engine gained Java's `axis_aligned_linear_pos` position predicate (the
`high_rampart` list's falling-off with height) and an `apply_in` that
measures from the piece's position, as `RuleProcessor` does. Residents come
from the placed `bastion/mobs/*` templates (brutes with golden axes, sword
and crossbow piglins, hoglins). Generations 3–5 keep the compact graph.

### BLD-05b: maps in item frames

A frame holding a filled map whose data exists now draws as Java's
`ItemFrameRenderer` does: Java's `template_item_frame_map` model (a
full-size frame one sixteenth deep instead of the small frame) and the
map's 128 × 128 image over the whole face, 1/128 block a pixel, 0.4375 from
the cell centre toward the wall less one map pixel, turned by the frame's
rotation in quarter turns (`rotation % 4`), unshaded (Java's text render
type), lit by the frame's light (a glow frame's fully). The item icon is no
longer drawn over it. Only markers whose `MapDecorationType.showOnItemFrame`
is true are drawn on a framed map (no player, red or blue markers).

Java's frame markers are added: every 10 ticks each framed map that tracks
position gets a `frame` decoration at its frame's block x and z, turned
`Direction.get2DDataValue() * 90` (south 0, west 90, north 180, east 270,
floors and ceilings −90), and a marker whose frame no longer holds the map
is removed. Markers save with the map (`maps.json`'s decorations). They
show on held maps too, as a green arrow, and on framed maps.

### BLD-05c: wall amethyst buds

Java's amethyst buds and clusters use the `cross` model, turned by the
blockstate (`facing=north`: `x: 90`; east, south, west: `x: 90` with `y`
90/180/270; down: `x: 180`), read from the 26.3 client jar. All six
facings were already registered (up and down 1926–1933, walls 2400–2415),
waterlogging included, so no states were allocated. The four wall facings
drew as their outline box; they now draw as the cross turned onto its side,
pointing away from its wall. The block keeps its `Shaped` behaviour
(outline, collision, light, drops); only the mesher changes, sending wall
buds (dry or waterlogged) to the cross pass with two new quad bits (axis
and direction) that `chunk.wgsl` uses to lay the two diagonal planes along
x or z.

## Remaining gaps and open decisions

- **Look of the bastion templates and framed maps (owner).** The templates
  are original and nobody has looked at them in a running game; Track 8
  has no Mac or play. The owner may want a capture pass before the
  bastions merge, as Track 10's art does.
- **Generation number (integrator).** Generation 6 is new here. If another
  track also adds a generation, the two must merge into one (the
  integrator's call; this track's Nether and bastion changes are gated on
  `Generation::V6`).
- **Features read undecorated terrain.** As in generation 5, every
  feature sees the terrain before decoration plus its own writes, and the
  first write wins; Java's later features see earlier ones. The fungi and
  vines paint first so they stay whole. Recorded difference.
- **Decoration randomness** is VoxelCraft's (per position, so chunks are
  independent), as FE-01 allows; counts, heights and shapes are Java's.
- **Map frame markers** are reconciled every 10 ticks: a marker leaves at
  most half a second after its map leaves the frame (Java removes it at
  once), and two frames holding one map in the same column with the same
  facing share a marker. Decorations other than frames and treasure use
  original glyphs, not Java's sprites.
- **Light for roof mushrooms and Nether mushrooms** is not checked, as in
  Java's worldgen (no light yet when features run).
- **Debris exposure ignores bastion air** (pre-existing): ancient debris
  in a neighbouring chunk can sit against a bastion court. And at rare
  points the exposure sampler's point density disagrees with the terrain
  (seed 42's (32, 68, 14)); its test now allows one sample in a thousand.
- **Bastion heights.** Older generations keep bastions to y 33–70; generation
  6 checks each bastion's own box, since its catalogue climbs higher and
  its bridge legs hang lower (`generation_6_bastions_paint_outside_the_old_height_band`).
- **Not run:** the game, frame-time suites, captures.

## Measurements

`cargo run --profile ci --locked --example nether_patch_bench <generation>`
on Boat (x86_64 Linux, not the reference Mac; headless; median of seven
fresh generators, 100 chunks each, seed 12345), generation 5 → 6:
wastes 0.357 → 0.582 ms/chunk, soul sand valley 0.349 → 0.503, crimson
forest 0.427 → 0.585, warped forest 0.578 → 0.657, basalt deltas 2.429 →
2.396. Generation 6 adds 0.08–0.23 ms of chunk generation (on the worker
threads) for the ores, glowstone, springs and patches; deltas cost the
same, as real blobs clipped to the chunk replace the noise blobs. Chunk
generation is off the frame, but streaming in the Nether should be part of
the owner's frame-time runs.
